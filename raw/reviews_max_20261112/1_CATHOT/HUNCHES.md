## H1. A finite category whose classifying space is finitely dominated has the homotopy type of a finite complex

**Statement.** Let C be a finite category such that BC has the homotopy type of a finite-dimensional CW complex.
Then BC has the homotopy type of a finite CW complex; equivalently, of the order complex of a finite poset.
Equivalently: Wall's finiteness obstruction w(BC) ∈ K̃₀(Z[π₁(BC)]) vanishes for each component.

**Why it matters.** With G.1 of the return it would give: the finite-dimensional homotopy types in Grothendieck's
Hot₀ (V ms. 51–52, "types d'homotopie de type fini") are exactly the finite complexes. For one object this is
Theorem 2.2 of `OWN_RESULTS.md` (there π₁ = 1 and K̃₀(Z) = 0).

**Reasons.** (a) BC has finitely many cells in each dimension, so a finite-dimensional homotopy type makes it
finitely dominated and w(BC) is defined. (b) By Miller's Theorem B every subgroup of every endomorphism monoid of C
(and of its Karoubi envelope) maps trivially to π = π₁(BC) (this is the mechanism of G.12). (c) The cellular
chains of the universal cover are Zπ ⊗^L_{ZC} Z. Projective ZC-modules of the form ZC(x, −)e, e an idempotent
arrow, induce free Zπ-modules (an idempotent becomes 1 in π); a projective module over a group ring ZG_e of a
subgroup G_e ⊂ End(x) induces Zπ ⊗_Z (its coinvariants), again free, because G_e → π is trivial. So every natural
source of projectives induces stably free modules. (d) For n above the dimension, w(BC) = ±[Zπ ⊗_{ZC} Ωⁿ Z], the
class of the n-th syzygy of the constant module; what is missing is a dévissage showing that this class lies in the
subgroup generated by the modules in (c).

**What I tried.** The reduction (d); a search for a counterexample of the form C = C̃/G with G finite acting freely
on a finite category C̃ of type S³ (Swan complexes with π₁ = Q₈ and k-invariant ±3 have w ≠ 0): I found no way to
make the Swan module (3, Σ) appear.

**Smallest undecided instance.** π₁(BC) = Q₈ with universal cover of type S³; or π₁ = Z/23.

**Prompt.** `PRO_PROMPTS/FINMON/PROMPT.md`, Part D.

## H2. Is K(Z, 2) the classifying space of a finite monoid or a finite category? (G.17 of the return)

**No guess either way; three facts.** (a) Reformulation: BM ≃ CP^∞ if and only if U(M) = 1 and there is a 2-cocycle
u: M × M → Z such that the monoid M ×_u Z ((a, r)(b, s) = (ab, r + s + u(a, b))) has contractible classifying
space; the projection B(M ×_u Z) → BM is a principal fibration with fibre S¹ classified by [u]. (b) No monoid of
order ≤ 6 has the mod-p Betti numbers 0, 1, 0, 1, 0 in degrees 1–5 (`work/code/higher_homology_order6.out`).
(c) If M is regular, QM is quasi-hereditary (Putcha, J. Algebra 205 (1998); from memory, not opened), hence of
finite global dimension, so H_k(M; Q) = 0 for large k: no finite regular monoid has the type of K(Z, 2), nor of any
space with infinitely many non-zero rational Betti numbers. A candidate must be non-regular, like Sweeney's Example
5.2 (y² = x₀₀ ≠ y), whose quotient by the band is the dual numbers Q[y]/(y²).
**Algebraic target.** The trivial module must have a minimal resolution of period 2 with Ext^{odd} = 0, as the
simple module S_a of the Nakayama algebra with two simples and radical square zero. I found no monoid algebra with
the trivial module in such a block (none of order ≤ 4 has H₂ ≠ 0 with H₁ = 0).
**Smallest undecided instance.** Monoids of order 7.
**Prompt.** `PRO_PROMPTS/FINMON/PROMPT.md`, Part E.

## H3. [BC, S²] is uncountable for some finite category C (D.6 of the return has a negative answer)

**Statement.** For the six-element monoid M = {1, x₀₀, x₀₁, x₁₀, x₁₁, y} of Sweeney's Example 5.2
(x_ij x_kl = x_il, y x_ij = x_0j, x_ij y = x_i0, y² = x₀₀), the set [BM, S²] is uncountable. Hence X ↦ [BX, S²] on
finite categories is not a sequential colimit of representables by finite categories (D.4), and Grothendieck's
sequences "(K_n)" of finite categories (V ms. 43–46) cannot ind-represent the 2-sphere even on finite tests.
Stronger form: BM ≃ ⋁_{n≥2} Sⁿ.

**Reasons.** (a) H_k(BM; Z) = Z for all k ≥ 2, U(M) = 1 (Sweeney; my bar-complex computation agrees through degree
5 at p = 2, 3, 5, 32003). (b) P₂ ⊂ M is a retract by the homomorphism y ↦ x₀₀, so S² is a retract of BM.
(c) My computation (`work/code/cup_products.py`): the cup products x₂², x₂x₃, x₃x₂ vanish in H⁴, H⁵ with
coefficients F₂, F₃, F₃₂₀₀₃. (d) Rationally QM has two simple modules, S_t (trivial) and S_u, with ΩS_t = S_u and
QM/QB = Q[y]/(y²) (both checked). Heuristically (not proved): the positive-degree classes of Ext(S_t, S_t) factor
through Ext(S_u, S_u), and x₂² = 0 says that the composite Ext¹(S_u, S_t) ∘ Ext¹(S_t, S_u) is zero, which would
make all products of positive-degree classes vanish. (e) If Sⁿ is a homotopy retract of BM for infinitely many n = 2p, Proposition 4.1 of `OWN_RESULTS.md`
gives uncountably many classes.
**What is missing.** Sphericity of the generators of H_n(BM) for n ≥ 3, or any other route to infinitely many
independent maps to S³. Phantom maps do not help: Ph(BM, S³) = 0 because the generator of H² is pulled back from
S².
**Counter-scenario.** A vanishing theorem of Sullivan type for maps from BM to finite complexes; it cannot hold in
full because of (b).
**Smallest undecided instance.** Is the generator of H₃(BM; Z) = Z spherical? Is there a map BM → S³ of non-zero
degree on H₃?
**Prompt.** `PRO_PROMPTS/S2COUNT/PROMPT.md`.

## H4. E.6 of the return is false in general

**Statement (E.6).** A Thomason-fibrant, i: A → B a Thomason cofibration, p: B → A with [p][i] = id_A in Cat_hot.
Is there a functor r: B → A with ri = id_A and [r] = [p] in Cat_hot?
**Guess.** Not always. **Reasons.** The proof would be a homotopy extension property for zigzags of natural
transformations: an extension along (B × {0}) ∪ (A × Z) → B × Z for a fence Z. This map is not a pseudo-Dwyer
morphism when the upward closure of A in B is larger than A (I checked that no order-preserving retraction of the
required kind exists), and Raptis (Homology Homotopy Appl. 12 (2010), p. 221) shows that the Thomason structure is
not monoidal. Fibrancy of A gives fillers on c Sd²(Δ¹ × Δ¹), not on a product of fences.
**What is true.** (a) A strict retraction in the Hot-class of p exists (return). (b) If A is moreover local (C.3),
E.6 holds: both r and p are functors into a local category with the same class in Hot. (c) If A is a groupoid
(return).
**Smallest undecided instance.** A a fibrant poset model of S¹ or of the six-point S², B = A ∪ one c Sd² Δ¹ glued
at two points.
**Prompt.** `PRO_PROMPTS/INDLOCAL/PROMPT.md`, Part D.

## H5. The higher analogue of the main theorem

**Statement.** Let nCat_hot be strict n-categories with morphisms the strict functors modulo the equivalence
relation generated by oplax transformations (for n = 2: lax or pseudo-natural transformations and modifications;
in general the homotopy relation of the Gray cylinder). Then X ↦ [N_n X, Y] is representable on nCat_hot exactly
when Y has the homotopy type of the nerve of a strict n-groupoid. For n = 2 these are all 2-types; for n ≥ 3 they
are not all n-types (Simpson; the simply connected types of strict ∞-groupoids are products of Eilenberg–Mac Lane
spaces, as recalled in the introduction of OpenAI family 312, 24 Sep 2026, claimed and unrefereed).
**Reasons.** The proof of B.11 is by groups too large to act; the same groups, as one-object n-categories, see
only the "group-like" part of any small n-category. The positive half needs: the nerve of a strict n-groupoid G is
"local" for strict n-categories X, that is π₀ of the n-category of functors X → G is [N_n X, N_n G]; for n = 1
this is C.1.
**Connection.** OpenAI family 317 (25 Sep 2026, claimed, Lean for the main theorem) gives the Thomason model
structure on nCat for all n; its fibrant objects are not the local ones, as for n = 1.
**Prompt.** `PRO_PROMPTS/INDLOCAL/PROMPT.md`, Part E.

## H6. E.26 and E.27 hold for Σ = all arrows

**Statement.** For Σ = Mor(X) and every left-cofinal sequence of types t (VII ms. 99–102, 138), the colimit category
C_∞(t; a, b) has the weak homotopy type of the path space P_{a,b}(BX), compatibly with all transition maps induced
by morphisms of sequences, with the all-type projection (E.26), and with concatenation for every block τ₀ (E.27).
**Reasons.** For every type τ the constant section X → Fun(I_τ, X) is a weak equivalence, compatible with all
type morphisms; so on total categories every comparison is a weak equivalence over X × X by two-out-of-three. What
is needed is that the strict fibres over (a, b) are homotopy fibres in the colimit. In the return's E.21 this
comes from two cofinal families of stages (fibrations and opfibrations); for a general sequence the transition maps
compose arrows and are not full, and the lift categories need a new argument. The Dwyer–Kan hammock localization
for W = all arrows gives the all-type statement in the limit (Calculating simplicial localizations, §§2–3; opened
by the return, not by me).
**Smallest undecided instance.** The block (+, −, +) with left, right and symmetric padding (as the return says).
**Prompt.** `PRO_PROMPTS/INDLOCAL/PROMPT.md`, Part C.

## H7. Every finite monoid with classifying space of finite type is "aperiodic up to homotopy" in a strong sense

**Statement.** If M is a finite monoid and BM has finite homotopy type, then for every idempotent e and every
subgroup G of eMe the map BG → BM is null-homotopic (this much is Miller's Theorem A, since BM is simply connected
and finite-dimensional). Guess: there is a finite aperiodic monoid M′ and a homomorphism or zigzag of homomorphisms
between M and M′ inducing a homotopy equivalence of classifying spaces.
**Reason.** Theorem C of arXiv:2610.38760v1 gives an aperiodic M′ with BM′ ≃ BM, with no relation to M. A natural
candidate for a direct construction is not known to me.
**Prompt.** `PRO_PROMPTS/FINMON/PROMPT.md`, Part B.
