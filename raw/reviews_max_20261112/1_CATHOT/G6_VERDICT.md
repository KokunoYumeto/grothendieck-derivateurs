## Verdict

**Conjecture 7.1 of O. Ben-Bassat, *On a Conjecture of Fiedorowicz*, arXiv:2610.38760v1 (28 Oct 2026), exactly as stated in v1, is false, and the return's argument for this (G.4 and G.6) is correct.** RP² is a counterexample; so is every connected finite CW complex with non-trivial finite fundamental group. ## The statement refuted

arXiv:2610.38760v1, Section 7 "The finite fundamental-group question", TeX source lines 1282–1285 (PDF p. 17):

> **Conjecture 7.1.** Let X be a connected finite CW complex. If π₁(X) is finite, then X ≃ BM for some finite monoid M.

Conventions of the preprint: BM = |N M| for the one-object category M; "≃" is homotopy equivalence; a finite CW complex has finitely many cells. Only v1 exists (arXiv abstract page, fetched 12 Nov 2026).

## The theorem that refutes it

**Theorem.** Let M be a finite monoid. If BM is homotopy equivalent to a finite-dimensional CW complex, then the universal group U(M) = π₁(BM) is trivial.

**Counterexample to 7.1.** X = RP²: connected, three cells, π₁ = Z/2. If RP² ≃ BM with M finite, the Theorem gives π₁(RP²) = 1, which is false.

**Proof of the Theorem** (complete; each step checked against the sources named).

1. *π₁(BM) = U(M), and η: M → U(M) is onto.* N M is a reduced simplicial set; π₁ of its realization is generated by the non-degenerate edges [m], m ≠ 1, with one relation [m₁][m₂] = [m₁m₂] per 2-simplex: the defining presentation of U(M). For a ∈ M, aʳ = aˢ for some r < s, hence η(a)^{s−r} = 1 and η(a)⁻¹ = η(a^{s−r−1}); so every element of U(M) is η(m) for some m, and U(M) is finite. (Preprint §7; McDuff, Topology 18 (1979), Lemma 1, for π₁, quoted by the preprint and by Sweeney, arXiv:2502.05276v2, §2.3. I did not open McDuff's paper; the two-line proof above replaces it.)

2. *A finite group H inside M that surjects onto U(M).* Let K be the minimal two-sided ideal of M (the intersection of all two-sided ideals; non-empty), e ∈ K an idempotent (a power of any element of K), H = eMe. For x ∈ H: x ∈ K, MxM is a two-sided ideal contained in K, so MxM = K ∋ e, e = axb, and e = (eae)x(ebe) in the finite monoid H. In a finite monoid one-sided inverses are two-sided, so the three factors are invertible; H is a group. η(e)² = η(e) gives η(e) = 1, so η(eme) = η(m) and η|H: H → U(M) is a surjective group homomorphism.

3. *A map BH → BM that is η|H on π₁.* Kar(M): objects the idempotents, Hom(f, g) = gMf. The inclusion j of the object 1 (with End(1) = M) induces a homotopy equivalence BM → B Kar(M), because every object is a retract of 1 (Quillen's Theorem A: the comma category j/f is a retract of j/1, which has a terminal object; this is Lemma 2.4 of the preprint). End(e) = H. With r = e: 1 → e and s = e: e → 1 one has r∘s = id_e and s∘h∘r = h ∈ M for h ∈ H; so, transported to the base point 1 along r, the loop of h ∈ H is the loop of h ∈ M, whose class is η(h). The composite BH → B Kar(M) ← BM (second arrow an equivalence) therefore induces the surjection η|H on π₁.

4. *Miller's theorem.* H. Miller, *The Sullivan conjecture on maps from classifying spaces*, Ann. of Math. (2) 120 (1984), 39–87, p. 40: "**Theorem B.** Let X be a finite dimensional CW complex and G a torsion group. Then any pointed map BG → X induces the trivial map of fundamental groups." Restated as Theorem 10.1 and proved on pp. 84–85. Hypotheses in our case: G = H is finite, so torsion; X = Z is the finite-dimensional CW complex with BM ≃ Z; the map is BH → B Kar(M) → BM → Z (a homotopy inverse of Bj chosen; pointedness is not needed, 10.1 says "any map"). The 1985 correction to Miller's paper (Ann. of Math. 121, 605–609) concerns the proof of his Theorem C (the spectral sequence (2.5) and "Fact 3.9") and not Section 10.

5. *Conclusion.* The map of step 3 followed by BM ≃ Z is onto on π₁ (step 3) and trivial on π₁ (step 4). Hence π₁(Z) = U(M) = 1. ∎

**The proof of step 4, so that nothing is taken on authority.** Let f: BG → X with G finite, X a connected CW complex of dimension d, and suppose f_* = φ is non-trivial on π₁. Pick c ∈ G of prime-power order pᵏ with φ(c) ≠ 1 (a primary component of an element not in the kernel), C = ⟨c⟩, N = φ(C) ≠ 1, X′ → X the covering with π₁(X′) = N, f′: BC → X′ the lift, ψ a character of N of order p, L the flat line bundle of ψ on X′. Then f′^*L is the line bundle of χ = ψ∘φ|C on BC, and χ has order p. In K⁰(X′), ([L] − 1)^{d+1} = 0, because the skeletal filtration is multiplicative and X′ has dimension d (Miller argues with Lusternik–Schnirelmann category). So (χ − 1)^{d+1} restricts to zero in K⁰ of every finite skeleton of BC. By Atiyah, *Characters and cohomology of finite groups*, Publ. Math. IHES 9 (1961), Proposition (8.1) for cyclic groups (or Theorem (7.2), p. 46: α̂: R(G)^ → 𝒦*(B_G) is a topological isomorphism) and Proposition (6.11), p. 45 (for a p-group R(G) → R(G)^ is a monomorphism), (χ − 1)^{d+1} = 0 in R(C). But its value at c is (ζ − 1)^{d+1} ≠ 0 with ζ a primitive p-th root of unity. Contradiction. (Miller's own computation: K⁰(BZ_{mn}) = Z[[x]]/((1+x)^{mn} − 1) and the image (1+x)ⁿ − 1 of x_m is not nilpotent; I verified this by mapping x to ζ − 1 in Z_p[ζ] for ζ of order p^c, p^c the exact power of a prime p | m in mn.)

## Is the return's own argument right?

Yes. G.4 of the return has exactly steps 1–5 above: finiteness of U(M) and surjectivity of η; the group eMe at an idempotent of the minimal ideal, with the correct proof that it is a group; the typing of BH → BM through Kar(M) (needed because H → M does not preserve the identity), with Quillen's Theorem A; Miller's Theorem B with the page. I checked each sentence of its proof; I found no error and no gap. Two remarks of precision:
- The return cites "Theorem B, p. 40" and applies it to a *connected* finite-dimensional Z; the theorem does not need connectedness, and it is stated for pointed maps, which costs nothing here.
- G.6 says "this refutes Conjecture 7.1 in the verified version"; the conjecture is about homotopy equivalence X ≃ BM, and the refutation is of that statement.

G.5 ("among connected homotopy types with a finite CW model, the types realized by finite monoids are exactly the simply connected ones") is correct provided Theorem C of the preprint is; the return says so. My reading of the proof of Theorem C, with what I re-derived and what I tested by program, is in `EMAIL_CLAIMS_CHECK.md` (d).

## What the corrected statement is

For connected finite CW complexes X: X ≃ BM for a finite monoid M if and only if X is simply connected (⇐: Theorem C of the preprint; ⇒: the Theorem above). Sharper statements proved in this review (`OWN_RESULTS.md`): for a finite monoid M, "BM of finite homotopy type", "BM of finite-dimensional homotopy type" and "H_k(M; Z) = 0 for large k" are equivalent, and each forces U(M) = 1; if a prime p divides |U(M)|, then H_k(M; F_p) ≠ 0 for infinitely many k.

The one-object restriction is what fails: the category with objects 0, 1, 2, Hom(i, j) = Z/2 for i < j and composition by addition (3 objects, 9 arrows) has classifying space homeomorphic to RP² (return G.11; verified, see `EMAIL_CLAIMS_CHECK.md` (g1)), and no finite category of type RP² has fewer objects or fewer arrows (return G.12–G.13; proof re-derived).
