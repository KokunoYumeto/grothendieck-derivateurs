## Corrections to leads, drafts, and printed formulas

1. **R7 requires its pairwise hypothesis for the stated target.** In general the canonical quotient of Tor1 is `(aug K1 ∩ aug K2)/aug(K1 ∩ K2)`. It becomes `Z/aug(K1 ∩ K2)` when both pairs have affine equalizers. Under totality this condition holds, so the reviewer's intended use is valid.

2. **The two kernels are different.** The triple quotient H3 controls the syzygy kernel in `Pq ⊕ Pq`. Simultaneous affine equalization asks for the intersection of two kernels in `Pq`. Vanishing of H1 and H2 for H3 does not identify those objects. This is the exact gap in the attempted shortcut, not a counterexample.

3. **R8 has a disconnected and empty scope correction.** The Cat comparison property characterizes componentwise filteredness. A discrete two-object category and the empty category satisfy the comparison without being filtered. The connected nonempty theorem survives in full. The paper also settles the previously unproved abelian implication.

4. **A potential main counterexample cannot be sifted.** D.2 improves the prompt's initial restriction: any separator has infinitely many objects, no weakly terminal object, and fails siftedness.

5. **AKR B.5 has two sign misprints.** On p. 378, overlap cases (c) and (d) require `4k-i-j` and `4k+1-i-j`. Direct substitution gives these signs; part of the printed proof itself agrees.

6. **AKR B.6 has a target-type defect.** The map `β_i × id_I : n × I → s × I` needs the latter summand in its target. The displayed final summand does not have that type.

7. **AKR B.6's restriction assertion fails for property (11).** Restrict the n=3 formulas to a two-point path. The last and first copies agree on both points, while the proposed intersection should be the image of a one-point source. The repaired construction uses each path's actual vertex count with a common cyclic length. An explicit interval table proves the overlap properties uniformly, and every computed repaired quasi-filter also passes their exhaustive finite checks.

8. **The literal calculation is invalid as a C.1/(11) certificate, but valid for Part B.9.** It uses the defective common-n restriction and records every failure of (11). The opened B.7–B.9 hypotheses require the quasi-filter identities and (9), both verified by the code, but do not require (11). Its class death is therefore a valid finite counterexample to automatic permanence in the broader Part B construction. It does not decide totality of either infinite completion.

9. **R10 lacks an eventual-survival argument.** The cone attachment sequence both creates H2 from certain H1 links and permits later H2 links to kill existing classes. For the repaired first stage the paper proves full-stage survival through K2 and no further; the proof permits arbitrary B.9 second-stage targets over that repaired K1. Two different permitted numerical schedules are kept separate in the repaired selected K2 and K3 computations.

10. **The presheaf reflection is not always discrete.** The sign-orbit example computes the failure exactly. Its natural element category has a weakly terminal object and the unequalizable pair id, −id, so it is not a total indexing shape. Using it as a counterexample to D.5's restricted family would be invalid.

11. **The historical fixed-point equivalence is not an exactness criterion.** Adámek–Reiterman (1976), II.3, is explicitly corrected by AKR (1981), A.4. Pairs determine their fixed-point condition; finite simultaneous affine families determine exactness. Husainov (2002), Problem 2.5, repeats the fixed-point/exactness question despite AKR's earlier negative example. This does not settle totality/exactness.

12. **The manuscript's relevant typing corrections are explicit.** II ms. 36 needs `y\X` for the functor X→Y, not `y\Y`. XII ms. 88 needs `U'/H'`, not `U/H'`. The pushout at XII ms. 113 is `U' ⨿_U X`, not the self-referential expression with X' on both sides. No conclusion is drawn from the already marked-false Proposition 2.19.

## Remaining mathematical statements

The exact unresolved integral assertion is: for every total nonempty connected small A and every triple `a0,a1,a2:p→q`, does the augmentation image of `ker(a1−a0) ∩ ker(a2−a0)` in the integral representable Pq contain 1? The Tor quotient is proved, but its vanishing has not been established.

For Q the first unresolved triple asks for an integral simultaneous annihilator of nonzero augmentation, which can then be divided by its augmentation. These are the kernel tests after the indicated coefficient change; no exactness of reduction on integral kernels is assumed.
