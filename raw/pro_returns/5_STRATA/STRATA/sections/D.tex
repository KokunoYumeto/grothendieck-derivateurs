\section{Recollement and base change}

\begin{statement}{D.1}{Known}{Pointed derivators have the exceptional adjoints in the manuscript's variance}
Let $\D$ be a derivator satisfying Der~1--4 on all small categories, in presheaf variance, and suppose that $\D(e)$ has a zero object. For the sieve $j:A\hookrightarrow X$ and complementary cosieve $i:B\hookrightarrow X$ there are adjunction chains
\[
 j^?\dashv j_!\dashv j^*\dashv j_* ,
 \qquad i_!\dashv i^*\dashv i_*\dashv i^!.
\]
All four Kan extensions $j_!,j_*,i_!,i_*$ are fully faithful. The essential image of $i_*$ consists of objects zero on $A$, and the essential image of $j_!$ consists of objects zero on $B$. These assertions hold at every additional diagram shape and do not require the strongness axiom Der~5.
\end{statement}
\begin{proof}
The pointwise Kan-extension formulas and full faithfulness of the inclusions show that restriction of either Kan extension to its source is the identity. Outside $B$, the comma category computing $i_*$ is empty; hence $i_*$ has terminal, therefore zero, values there. The unit $F\to i_*i^*F$ is an isomorphism precisely when its values outside $B$ are isomorphisms to zero, by Der~2. The corresponding argument for $j_!$ uses initial values outside $A$. This proves the essential-image descriptions and full faithfulness without a diagram-lifting axiom.

For existence of the outer adjoints, apply \citet[Lemma~3.7 and Corollary~3.8]{CDGroth2013} to the opposite-indexing derivator $I\mapsto\D(I^{\op})$. To make the variance and the construction explicit, the proof there uses the full subcategory of $K\times[1]$ on $(k,0)$ and $(u(j),1)$ for a sieve $u:J\hookrightarrow K$. Its projection $q$ to $K$ and its inclusion $s$ of $K\times\{0\}$ give the reflector $q_!s_*$ onto diagrams zero on $J$; the dual cylinder gives the coreflector. The pointwise comma categories over $u(j)$ are $(J/j)\times[1]$ and have the indicated terminal objects, so the reflector vanishes exactly where required. Transporting this construction to presheaf variance gives a coreflector onto $\operatorname{im}i_*$ and a reflector onto $\operatorname{im}j_!$. Composing with the inverse restriction equivalences gives $i^!$ and $j^?$. The proof uses Kan extensions and pointwise conservativity only and applies unchanged after shifting by any small category. This is precisely the additional adjunction statement of I ms.~1--3, rather than an assertion that an arbitrary Kan extension has further adjoints.
\end{proof}

\begin{statement}{D.2}{Known}{The pointed sequences and the stable recollement}
Under D.1 there are canonical coherent fibre and cofiber constructions
\[
 i_*i^!F\simeq\operatorname{fib}(F\to j_*j^*F),
 \qquad
 j_!j^?F\simeq\operatorname{cofib}(i_!i^*F\to F),
\]
and the canonical square with sides $j_!j^*F\to F\to i_*i^*F$ and its fourth vertex zero is both homotopy cartesian and homotopy cocartesian. If $\D$ is stable, these give the coherent exact sequences
\[
 j_!j^*F\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow i_*i^*F,
 \qquad
 i_*i^!F\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow j_*j^*F.
\]
If in addition $\D$ is strong, their canonical triangles give the usual triangulated recollement
$(j_!,j^*,j_*;i^*,i_*,i^!)$ at every shape. Pointedness alone does not assert stability or a triangulated structure.
\end{statement}
\begin{proof}
The support coreflection in D.1 is represented by the fibre of the unit to $j_*j^*$; its restriction to $A$ is the fibre of an identity, hence zero. The universal support adjunction identifies it with $i_*i^!$. Dually the support reflection is the cofiber of the counit from $i_!i^*$ and identifies with $j_!j^?$. These are coherent derivator fibres and cofibers. They are not ordinary fibres or cokernels in $\D(X)$.

The special adjunction maps are themselves obtained coherently by the mapping-cylinder Kan extensions used in D.1. We can construct the square without a diagram-lifting axiom. In $X\times[1]\times[1]$, with coordinates $(x,t,s)$, let $S$ be the sieve defined by $t=0$ or $x\in A$, and let $J$ be the cosieve defined by $s=1$ or $x\in B$. Restrict the pullback of $F$ to $S\cap J$, extend by zero along the sieve $S\cap J\hookrightarrow J$, and then extend by zero along the cosieve $J\hookrightarrow X\times[1]^2$. In presheaf variance the resulting square, with vertices $(1,1),(0,1),(1,0),(0,0)$, is
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
j_!j^*F&\longrightarrow&F\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
0&\longrightarrow&i_*i^*F.
\end{array}
\]
It restricts at $a\in A$ to a square with an identity $F_a\to F_a$ and two zero vertices, and at $b\in B$ to one with the other identity $F_b\to F_b$. Both are bicartesian. Pointwise detection of homotopy cartesian and cocartesian squares proves the assertion in the shifted derivator. This reasoning establishes a particular coherent square and does not invoke the false assertion that every incoherent square lifts uniquely.

Stability identifies fibres and cofibers with the expected suspension shift. Under Der~5, Groth's canonical triangulation theorem and Example~4.25 therefore give the recollement \citep[Theorem~4.16, \S4.3]{CDGroth2013}. Full faithfulness and the kernel-image equality are D.1, while the two triangles are the displayed sequences followed by their suspension connecting maps. All these six functors are exact in the stable sense. Their coherent Mayer--Vietoris sequences are also covered by \citet[Theorem~6.1]{CDGPS2014}. The manuscript's ordinary abelian short exact sequence in I ms.~2 is its represented abelian specialization; it is not a short exact sequence of spaces. In particular we retain the editors' pointedness correction before its square~(2).
\end{proof}

\begin{statement}{D.3}{Proved}{Exact finite-poset tests for properness and smoothness}
Let $f:P\to Q$ be a monotone map of small posets. In the manuscript's $W_\infty$ terminology, $f$ is proper if and only if for every $p\in P$ and every $q\geq f(p)$ the poset
\[
 L_f^+(p,q)=\{p'\in P:p\leq p',\ f(p')=q\}
\]
has weakly contractible nerve. It is smooth if and only if for every $p\in P$ and every $q\leq f(p)$ the poset
\[
 L_f^-(p,q)=\{p'\in P:p'\leq p,\ f(p')=q\}
\]
has weakly contractible nerve. These tests apply to finite, locally finite and arbitrary small posets. Empty lifting posets fail the test. No condition is imposed for a pair $(p,q)$ for which the displayed base inequality does not hold.
\end{statement}
\begin{proof}
For a functor $f:X\to Y$ and an arrow $\alpha:f(x)\to y$, the outgoing lift category has objects $v:x\to x'$ with $f(x')=y$ and $f(v)=\alpha$, and morphisms $w:x'\to x''$ over $1_y$ satisfying $wv=v'$. This is the comma category $(x,\alpha)\backslash X_y$ for the canonical inclusion $X_y\to X/y$. The latter is homotopy final precisely when all these comma categories are weakly contractible. This is the properness condition of I ms.~53--54. Its dual is the incoming lift category with a fixed target upstairs, equivalently homotopy initiality of $X_y\to y\backslash X$. Maltsiniotis proves the equivalent universal base-change formulation \citep[Theorem~1.2 and its proper dual]{CDMaltsiniotis2005}. For posets an arrow is uniquely determined by its endpoints, and the two lift categories become exactly the two full subposets in the statement. This proves both directions without replacing contractibility by connectedness. The empty category is not weakly contractible, so existence of a lift is a necessary part of either test. Identity-arrow tests have $p$ itself as an initial or terminal object and are automatically satisfied.
\end{proof}

\begin{statement}{D.4}{Proved}{Fibre formulas and universal base change}
Let
\[
 \begin{array}{ccc}
 X'&\xrightarrow{g'}&X\\
 {\scriptstyle f'}\downarrow&&\downarrow{\scriptstyle f}\\
 Y'&\xrightarrow{g}&Y
 \end{array}
\]
be a strict pullback of small categories. For every presheaf-variance derivator satisfying Der~1--4 the specified mate
\[
 g^*f_*\longrightarrow f'_*g'^*
\]
is invertible if $f$ is $W_\infty$-proper or if $g$ is $W_\infty$-smooth. Its left-Kan counterpart
\[
 f'_!g'^*\longrightarrow g^*f_!
\]
is invertible if $f$ is smooth or $g$ is proper. Properness of a fixed $f$ is equivalent to the first comparison for every strict pullback and every space-valued coefficient; smoothness is equivalent to the dual assertion. For proper $f$ and smooth $f$, respectively, the fibre comparisons are
\[
 (f_*F)_y\simeq\holim_{X_y^{\op}}F|_{X_y},
 \qquad
 (f_!F)_y\simeq\hocolim_{X_y^{\op}}F|_{X_y}.
\]
\end{statement}
\begin{proof}
The ordinary pointwise index for $f_*$ is $(X/y)^{\op}$; D.3 says that the opposite of $X_y\to X/y$ is homotopy initial. Thus restriction to the fibre computes the same homotopy limit. This cofinality is valid for all derivators, by \citet[Theorems~3.8 and~3.16]{CDGPS2014}. Similarly, for $f_!$ the index is $(y\backslash X)^{\op}$, and smoothness makes its fibre inclusion homotopy final. This proves the two displayed comparisons with their correct variances.

Under strict pullback the outgoing lift categories of $f'$ identify with those of $f$ at the prescribed base arrow: a lift of an arrow in $Y'$ is a lift of its image in $Y$ together with its already specified $Y'$ component. Morphisms identify as well. Hence properness is preserved by strict pullback, as is smoothness by the dual argument. For proper $f$, the strict fibres of $f$ over $g(y')$ and of $f'$ over $y'$ are isomorphic. The two fibre formulas therefore identify the right mate with an isomorphism at every $y'$, and Der~2 proves right base change. Smooth $f$ gives the left statement by the same argument. If $g$ is smooth, transpose the square and apply this left statement to obtain $g'_!f'^*\to f^*g_!$; the transformation between their full right adjoints is exactly $g^*f_*\to f'_*g'^*$ and is invertible. The proper-$g$ assertion is dual. This uses the adjunction correspondence for whole composite adjoints, which preserves invertibility, rather than an unrestricted assertion about partial mates. Thus no additional topological assumption is hidden in the word ``smooth.''

For the converse to properness it suffices to test fibre squares. The adjoint mate of their right comparison is
\[
 k_!p^*\longrightarrow f^*y_!,
 \qquad k:X_y\hookrightarrow X,\quad p:X_y\to e.
\]
Evaluate it on the terminal space. At $x\in X$ the target is the discrete representable $\Hom_Y(f(x),y)$; the source is the coproduct, indexed by $\alpha:f(x)\to y$, of the nerves of the outgoing lift categories. The map collapses each summand to its index. Its being an equivalence forces every such lift category to be weakly contractible. This is exactly D.3's general-category criterion. Passing to opposite indexing categories proves the smooth converse. The proof covers empty fibres and nonsurjectivity on components: an empty indexing family gives the corresponding initial or terminal coefficient, and an existing base arrow with no lift is detected by an empty summand over a nonempty representable.
\end{proof}

\begin{statement}{D.5}{Refuted}{Topological properness and a stratified submersion do not characterize these functors}
For finite posets, every induced map $|Nf|$ is proper as a map of topological spaces. This does not imply categorical properness. Categorical properness does not imply that strict fibres are homotopy fibres. Categorical smoothness does not imply local product structure on realizations.
\end{statement}
\begin{proof}
A finite-poset nerve realizes to a compact Hausdorff polyhedron, so every continuous map between such realizations is proper. Let $f:e\to[1]$ select $0$. It is smooth: its incoming lifting categories are singletons whenever a base arrow has target $0$. It is not proper, since the arrow $0\to1$ has no outgoing lift. Its realization is the endpoint inclusion $\{0\}\hookrightarrow[0,1]$, a proper map that has no local product description as a submersion onto a neighborhood of $0$. This gives both the properness and the smooth-submersion separation, with the source and target completely specified.

The dual inclusion $h:e\to[1]$ selecting $1$ is proper and not smooth. Its strict fibre at $0$ is empty, while its homotopy fibre at $0$ is contractible, because $[0,1]$ is contractible and nonempty paths join $1$ to $0$. Its over-comma transition is $\varnothing\to e$, which is not a weak equivalence. Thus categorical properness guarantees the strict-fibre formula for contravariant right Kan extensions, not the Quillen-Theorem-B fibre comparison or locally constant transport without their further hypotheses. This independently verifies the particular proper-cosieve correction to row Ib-C003 in the supplied GALOIS D.9.

The first example also displays failure of right base change. Pull it back along the inclusion of $1$. The strict pullback is empty. For a discrete two-point space $M$, the value of $f_*M$ at $1$ is $M$, since $e/1$ is terminal, whereas right Kan extension from the empty fibre has value $*$. The base-change map is $M\to *$ and is not invertible. The program checks the posets, both lift tests, and these cardinalities. There are no omitted endomorphisms or compositions in these posets.
\end{proof}

\begin{statement}{D.6}{Proved}{Proper and smooth is strictly weaker than a strict Grothendieck bifibration}
Let $P$ have four objects $x,a,b,c$ and order relations $x<a<c$, $x<b<c$, with $a,b$ incomparable. Let $f:P\to[1]$ send $x$ to $0$ and $a,b,c$ to $1$. Then $f$ is proper and smooth, and is a Grothendieck fibration, but is not a Grothendieck opfibration. Its realization is not a locally trivial topological fibre bundle.
\end{statement}
\begin{proof}
The only nonidentity outgoing lifting test not confined to one fibre is $L_f^+(x,1)=\{a,b<c\}$. It has terminal object $c$, hence contractible nerve. Every other outgoing test has its fixed source as an initial object. The incoming lifting category for $0\to1$ at each of $a,b,c$ is the singleton $\{x\}$; the remaining incoming tests have their fixed target as terminal object. D.3 proves properness and smoothness.

For a poset over this two-element base, a cartesian lift of $0\to1$ with fixed target exists exactly when the incoming lift poset has a greatest element. Indeed every base arrow into $0$ is its identity, so the full cartesian factorization test is exactly the greatest-element test in that fibre. All the incoming lift posets just listed have the required greatest element $x$. Identity arrows of the base have identity lifts. Dually an opcartesian lift from $x$ over $0\to1$ would be a least element of $\{a,b<c\}$. There is none, since $a,b$ are incomparable minima. Thus $f$ is not an opfibration. The program exhausts every lifting poset and checks these greatest/least-element tests over $[1]$. For bases with more composable arrows, greatest elements in exact incoming lift posets alone would only establish the weaker precartesian test; we make no such general cartesian criterion.

The realization is a cone on the interval-shaped realization of $\{a,b<c\}$, projected to $[0,1]$ by its cone coordinate. The fibre at $0$ is a point and each fibre at $t>0$ is a nontrivial interval-shaped tree. These fibres are not homeomorphic, so there is no local trivialization at $0$. They are all contractible, which also shows why weak contractibility of lift categories cannot supply a geometric bundle structure. Part~E uses this distinction when auditing VIc-C008.
\end{proof}

\begin{statement}{D.7}{Proved}{Inclusions, components, and empty sources}
For a full inclusion $P\hookrightarrow Q$ of posets, categorical properness is equivalent to $P$ being a cosieve, and categorical smoothness is equivalent to $P$ being a sieve. Every product projection $P\times Q\to Q$ is both proper and smooth. A proper map has upward-closed strict image; a smooth map has downward-closed strict image. A map that is both has image a union of connected components of the underlying comparability graph. The empty-source map satisfies both lift conditions vacuously.
\end{statement}
\begin{proof}
For an inclusion, an outgoing lift from $p$ to $q\geq p$ exists exactly when $q\in P$; when it exists its lifting poset is the singleton $\{q\}$. This is precisely the cosieve condition. The dual calculation gives the sieve condition. For a product projection, the outgoing lift poset from $(p,q)$ to $q'$ is $\{p'\geq p\}\times\{q'\}$, with initial object $(p,q')$; the incoming one has terminal object $(p,q')$. For a general map, nonemptiness of the relevant lifting categories proves the asserted closure of its strict image. An upward- and downward-closed subset is constant along every edge of the comparability graph, hence along its connected components. If the domain is empty, there is no fixed upstairs object at which to test a lift; the conditions are vacuous and its Kan extensions have the prescribed empty-limit or empty-colimit values. Thus no unstated surjectivity on $\pi_0$ is present.
\end{proof}

\begin{statement}{D.8}{Proved}{The precise constructible base-change statement and a geometric class where it is literal}
Under the finite-poset constructible-coefficient dictionary established in Part~A, intrinsic restriction and its two adjoints satisfy D.4. This statement holds for any coefficient theory covered by that dictionary, and formally for the restriction of any derivator to finite posets. For a pointed theory the open--closed functors are those of D.1--D.2. For geometric coefficients covered by exodromy, the closed-cosieve inclusion is the literal closed inclusion $BB\hookrightarrow BX$; its right Kan extension is ordinary closed direct image. A sieve is represented geometrically by the open subset $BX\setminus BB$, with its constructible equivalence to $BA$, rather than by the generally non-open inclusion $BA\hookrightarrow BX$. Product projections of finite posets give a further class of literal proper, locally trivial stratified projections. For spaces, and for compactly generated or stable presentable coefficients covered by Part~A, their right direct image and proper base change agree with D.4. Arbitrary monotone maps do not support an unqualified identification of intrinsic right Kan extension with ambient topological direct image.
\end{statement}
\begin{proof}
Transport of an adjunction through the specified coefficient equivalences identifies its left and right adjoints uniquely, including their units, counits, and Beck--Chevalley mates. D.4 therefore gives the claimed intrinsic theorem, not just an abstract equality of its two sides. For closed cosieves the realization is the closed subcomplex $BB$. Ambient extension by terminal objects away from this closed subspace has exactly the support characterization of $i_*$ and preserves the given constructibility; hence it is that right adjoint. For a sieve the open complement is the one calculated in C.2. Its deformation retraction onto $BA$ preserves the induced stratification, and the exodromy equivalence identifies its constructible coefficients with those on $A$. The geometric open restriction and its adjoints are consequently the specified intrinsic functors under this identification.

For product projections the natural homeomorphism $B(P\times Q)\cong BP\times BQ$ carries the minimum-vertex stratification to the product stratification. Since $P$ is finite, the fibre $BP$ is compact. Nonabelian proper base change applies because these polyhedra are locally compact Hausdorff \citep[Corollary~7.3.1.18]{FLurieHTT2009}; for the stated larger coefficient class we use \citet[Corollary~3.2, Proposition~3.8, and Subexample~3.15]{CDHaine2022}. It identifies restriction of direct image to a target stratum with direct image of the restricted product family. Exodromy on that stratum, which is contractible, identifies the family with a constant diagram of constructible coefficients on $BP$; its sections are the limit over $P^{\op}$. Thus direct image is constructible. Since ambient pullback restricts to the intrinsic restriction functor, uniqueness of its right adjoint identifies this direct image and its mate with the intrinsic ones in D.4. Base change of the projection is again the same product projection. The underlying realization square is a genuine topological pullback for finite posets: nerves preserve limits, and realization preserves them on underlying sets; a continuous bijection from the compact realized pullback to the Hausdorff pullback is a homeomorphism \citep[Theorem~3.6.2.1]{CDKerodon013D}. This establishes a precise geometric class without calling every categorical smooth map a differential submersion.

For the final warning use $f:e\to[1]$ selecting $0$. In the minimum-vertex stratification the open stratum of $B[1]=[0,1]$ is $[0,1)$, and its closed stratum is $\{1\}$. For a two-point space $M$, intrinsic $f_*M$ is the constant diagram $M\xrightarrow{1}M$. Ambient topological direct image along $|f|$ is instead the skyscraper at $0$: its stalk is $M$ at $0$ and terminal at points $0<t<1$. It is not locally constant on the open stratum. Therefore ambient direct image need not even preserve this constructible subcategory. Likewise, an intrinsic left adjoint $f_!$ is not, by its notation alone, a compact-support direct image. These distinctions are necessary corrections to the unrestricted six-operation reading of Lead~4.
\end{proof}

\begin{statement}{D.9}{Proved}{The identified transcription and terminology corrections}
At I ms.~54 the target of the fibre inclusion defining smoothness must be $y_0\backslash X$, not the printed $y_0\backslash Y$. The properness criterion of I ms.~53 uses outgoing lifts, and the smoothness criterion uses incoming lifts. At XII ms.~59 the displayed closure relation is incompatible with the simultaneously specified downset topology: with downsets open, $x\leq y$ implies $y\in\overline{\{x\}}$, not $x\in\overline{\{y\}}$.
\end{statement}
\begin{proof}
An object of the fibre $X_{y_0}$ maps to $(x,\id_{y_0})$, whose second datum has target $f(x)$; it is an object of $y_0\backslash X$. Mapping it into $y_0\backslash Y$ instead loses the upstairs object and cannot have the stated comma categories of lifts. The next displayed slice-fibre criterion in the manuscript has the correct incoming variance, which confirms the correction mathematically without attributing a scanning error. The outgoing criterion is verified in D.3. For the topological assertion, the closed subsets of a poset with downsets open are upsets, so the closure of $\{x\}$ is $\{y:x\leq y\}$. Taking the two-element poset already refutes the reverse formula. The same manuscript paragraph later gives this correct upward-closure description; hence its initial specialization-order formula and its open-set convention cannot both be retained. We retain the explicit sieve convention required throughout this paper.
\end{proof}
