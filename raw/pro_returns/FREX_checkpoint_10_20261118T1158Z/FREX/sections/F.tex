\begin{theorem}[Known and rechecked: the statement for II ms.\ 36ter]
\label{F:companion-chain}
Among nonempty connected small categories there are strict inclusions
\[
 \{\text{filtered}\}\ \subsetneq\
 \{\text{exact over }\Z\}\ \subsetneq\
 \{\text{total}\}\ \subsetneq\
 \{\text{quasi-filtered}\}.
\]
Successive separating witnesses are $\Delta_{\mathrm{inj}}$,
$\Cdelta=C(3,2,3)$, and $A_2(\F_2)$. The last witness may be replaced
by the repaired Ad\'amek--Koubek--Reiterman category of the supplied
TOTAL\_EXACT\_2 return. Thus the conjectured implication from total
asphericity to filteredness is false in two successive stronger forms.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We record the precise verified use of R1. Filtered colimits of abelian
groups are exact by their finite-witness description. If integral
colimits on $A$ are exact, then for every set action $L$ the direct-sum
functor
\[
 F\longmapsto\left(a\longmapsto\bigoplus_{x\in L(a)}F(a,x)\right)
\]
is exact and identifies colimits over $\operatorname{El}_A(L)$ with
colimits over $A$. Therefore every component of every category of
elements is integrally acyclic. This property passes to categories of
elements a second time. For any such component $E$, the locally
constant set action associated to a cyclic subgroup
$H\leq\pi_1(BE)$ gives its connected cover with fundamental group
$H$. Its first integral homology is $H$, so $H=0$. Hence $BE$ is
simply connected as well as acyclic and is weakly contractible by
Hurewicz. This rechecks the exact-to-total step of TOTASPH C.6.

For the final implication we use the supplied, checked all-action
characterization of quasi-filteredness in DF A.2: for a nonempty
connected category, conditions I and IV are equivalent to simple
connectivity of every action component. Its proof uses the doubled
cosieve test for common successors, the corresponding slice tests
for I, and the quotient of a representable identifying a parallel
pair for IV; the latter first-homology calculation is the quotient
of $\Z$ by the augmentation ideal of its integral equalizers.
Totality supplies precisely the required simple connectivity, so
the characterization applies without finiteness assumptions.

For strictness, finite linear orders are Ramsey, hence exact by
Theorem~\ref{A:compact-ramsey}; the two maps from a point into a
two-point order have no common post-equalizer. Part D proves
totality and nonexactness of $\Cdelta$ over every nonzero ring.
Finally Propositions~\ref{B:cocycle-audit} and
\ref{B:connectivity-gaps} give $BA_2(\F_2)\simeq K(\F_2,2)$ and
show that every action component is a point or has that weak type.
They are all simply connected, but one has nonzero second homology.
This proves the third strictness independently of the older repaired
Ad\'amek--Koubek--Reiterman witness.
\end{proof}

filtered, ``Tot'' total, ``Qf'' quasi-filtered, ``Ram'' the morphism

group. The columns $\Z,\Q$ refer to exactness of colimits. All entries
are proved; no bounded finite search is used to assert an entry.

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The totality, coefficient and amenability columns of the named
examples are Corollary~\ref{A:example-table}. Its proof also supplies
the nonfiltered column. The two uniform parameter rows are
Corollary~\ref{D:table}; in every parameter range two different point
embeddings into an edgeless two-point object cannot be post-equalized.
The quasi-filtered column follows from totality except for the cyclic
example, where Proposition~\ref{A:cyclic-classification} proves it
directly. For independently ordered equivalence relations and graphs,
the explicitly exhibited nontrivial finite support quotients give
action components with nontrivial fundamental group. For the two
translation examples even a parallel pair has no integral affine
equalizer. These prove every negative quasi-filtered entry.

For the Ramsey column, the convex-equivalence theorem is KPT
Corollary~6.8, checked in Part B. The high-parameter predimension
classes have the free component expansion and the Ramsey theorem
proved in Part D. All other rigid rows fail Ramsey because their
groups fail extreme amenability by their proved classification and
Theorem~\ref{A:compact-ramsey}. Finite graphs fail the morphism Ramsey
property already because a two-point graph has a nontrivial
automorphism. Thus every displayed entry has a cited numbered proof.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Three fixed-point properties.}
Under the hypotheses stated in Part A, the compact Hausdorff property
is equivalent to Ramsey and to extreme amenability; the compact convex
property is equivalent to amenability and to the strong convex Ramsey
property; and the compact abelian torsor property is equivalent to
integral exactness. The independently ordered poset example separates
the third property from the second and first. The translation examples
separate the second property from rational exactness. Finite cocycle
classes separate rational exactness with the finite-index condition
from integral exactness. Rational cocycle classes separate exactness
in every prime characteristic from rational exactness and from totality.
Independently ordered punctured vector spaces over $\F_2$ separate the
compact convex and torsor rows even when totality and all the additional
structural assumptions are imposed.

\paragraph{Source corrections.}
Moore's convex Ramsey equivalence is Theorem 7.1(5)--(6) in the
published paper, and Theorem 6.1 in the supplied preprint. Bodirsky's
Corollary 1.4(3), rather than the theorem for posets carrying only a
linear extension, supplies the Ramsey expansion of independently
ordered posets. The orbit-category tensor convention in Part B has
the constant module on the left; it must not be identified without
changing variance with the standard right-module definition of Bredon
homological dimension. L\"uck's cited survey also assumes local
compactness, which is not automatic for these automorphism groups.
