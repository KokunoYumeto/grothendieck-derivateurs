# Buffered incidence cycle: finite success and global-template exclusion

Status: exact finite candidate, followed by a proved obstruction to extending this particular factorization template. It is not a counterexample or a proof of the universal Part G assertions. Checked 2026-11-18, approximately 10:30 UTC.

Try to encode the original DF point-group families by fractions

u_x = A_x B_x^{-1},   v_x = C_x D_x^{-1},

where A,B,C,D are positive point-word families. For one ordered edge a<b<c the following eight positive words give the required relation:

A_b=pq, B_b=rq, B_a=rs, A_a=ts,
C_c=tu, D_c=vu, D_a=vw, C_a=pw.

Here p,q,r,s,t,u,v,w are distinct free letters; A_c,B_c,C_b,D_b are four further independent free letters. Then

u_b=p r^{-1}, u_a=t r^{-1}, v_c=t v^{-1}, v_a=p v^{-1},

so u_b u_a^{-1} v_c v_a^{-1}=1. All twelve point words form a bifix code. Their exponent-count matrix has rank11 and a11x11 determinant of absolute value1, so its integral kernel is exactly the primitive buffered incidence vector. Each four-word point map has rank4 and is injective. The core group generated by the eight two-letter words has rank7: the seven elements

are obtained successively from any seven words along the displayed cycle, and together with q form a basis of the ambient free group of rank8. ## Why the same template cannot extend globally

Suppose S is a smooth locally free Cdelta monoid, A_x,B_x,C_x,D_x are equivariant positive point-word families, and the displayed factorization exists over one strong edge and is transported equivariantly to every edge. Factors may have any finite strong support; they need not be supported on W itself.

The factor p prefixes A_b and C_a. Therefore G_b and G_a preserve its prefix-comparability component, so their generated group G preserves it. G.19 makes the whole G-orbit of p a chain. The same applies to t using A_a and C_c. Moving b to a produces a comparison between the p-chain and the t-chain, since the moved p and t both prefix A_a. The two-transitive-chain argument from G.20 makes every p-orbit point comparable with every t-orbit point, in particular p and t themselves.

If p and t are distinct, their strict prefix orientation is preserved under transport. Put transported factors for all six edges of X11, and their strict prefix quotients, in one finite free chart. Define integer point values

U_x=length(A_x)-length(B_x),
V_x=length(C_x)-length(D_x).

For each edge, its incidence difference is

U_b-U_a = length(p)-length(t) = V_a-V_c.

The six differences all have one strict sign. The exact X11 cycle identity tau1+tau2+tau4+tau5=0 from G.8 contradicts that sign. Thus p=t. The fraction identities then give u_a=u_b and v_a=v_c, and St makes both point-group families constant.

This excludes only the displayed eight-block cycle. An arbitrary equality of eight positive words can have other cancellation patterns; arbitrary elements of S^gp can also require more than one positive fraction. Neither situation has been reduced to this template. The main T1 and comparison questions therefore remain open.
