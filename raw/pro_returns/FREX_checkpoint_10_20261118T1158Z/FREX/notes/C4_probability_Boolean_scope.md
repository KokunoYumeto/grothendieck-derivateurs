# Probability Boolean-algebra route: source scope

The category of finite probability Boolean algebras with an independent arbitrary order remains an exploratory candidate for the universal rational gate. This note imports no Ramsey theorem for it and asserts neither totality nor failure of rational exactness.

## Opened primary sources

Kechris, Sokic, Todorcevic, *Ramsey properties of finite measure algebras and topological dynamics of the group of measure preserving automorphisms: Some results and an open problem*, Contemporary Mathematics690 (2017),69--85, public author copy: https://pma.caltech.edu/documents/5619/KechrisSokicTodorcevic01.pdf . Its homogeneous dual Ramsey problem concerns equipartitions and naturally ordered finite measure algebras, not an independently ordered expansion.

Mijares, *Homogeneous Dual Ramsey Theorem*, arXiv:1907.02675v2,13July2019,5pages, was opened in full at https://arxiv.org/html/1907.02675v2 and https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.02675 . The abstract claims a positive solution. That claim is not used here because the displayed proof has a concrete arithmetic failure.

For its k-class approximation, equation(9) adds one point to each class at each stage after k. Hence |union s(i,X)|-#a = k(i-k). Taking k=2,m=4, its equation(18) selects i0=6 and equation(19) gives n(X)=8. For the m-class partition Y, equation(22) selects l=m+1=5, so its subsequent n=|union s(l,Y)|-#a0 equals4. Thus the final assertion n(X)=n is false under the stated definitions. Moreover the selected n=m does not belong to the n>m range of bad colorings assumed at the start of the proof. These failures concern the proof as written; they do not constitute a disproof of the homogeneous dual Ramsey statement.

The finite natural-order marginal equations themselves are not a rational obstruction: order atom priorities by independent exponential variables with rates equal to their rational masses. Under coarsening, minima of independent variables again have exponential laws with the summed rates, so the natural atom-order distributions restrict consistently; finite order probabilities are rational. This observation supplies no Ramsey or all-module exactness conclusion without an additional argument.
