\section*{Conventions}
We fix Grothendieck universes $\mathbb U\in\mathbb V$ and take $\Cat$ to mean the category of $\mathbb U$-small categories. Localizations and categories of coefficients are formed in $\mathbb V$. We write $BA=|NA|$, let $W_\infty$ be the functors whose nerves are weak homotopy equivalences, and put $\mathrm{Hot}=\Cat[W_\infty^{-1}]$. A fundamental localizer $W$ contains identities, satisfies two-out-of-three, and satisfies the following weak-saturation clause: if $i:A\to B$, $r:B\to A$, $ri=\id_A$ and $ir\in W$, then $i,r\in W$. It sends every category with a terminal object to the point and satisfies relative Theorem A: for a strictly commuting triangle $A\xrightarrow{u}B\to C$, if $A/c\to B/c$ belongs to $W$ for every $c\in C$, then $u\in W$. We explicitly use the relative form, printed Loc~(3 bis) in Chapter XVI; A.1 compares this modern convention with the literal historical variants. A category $A$ is $W$-aspherical if $A\to e$ belongs to $W$.

For $u:A\to B$, $A/b$ has objects $(a,u(a)\to b)$ and $b\backslash A$ has objects $(a,b\to u(a))$. The word final denotes ordinary covariant finality (nonempty connected comma categories $b\backslash A$); homotopy finality requires their weak contractibility. Coefficients on $S$ have presheaf variance: $\D_{\mathcal V}(S)=\Ho\Fun(NS^{\op},\mathcal V)$. Accordingly $f_!$ and $f_*$ mean the left and right adjoints of restriction $f^*$. A sieve is closed under sources of arrows, and a cosieve under targets. We distinguish categorical equivalence, natural-transformation homotopy, weak homotopy equivalence of nerves, and equivalence of enhanced localizations.

Unless explicitly qualified, pullbacks in $\Cat$ and in relative categories are strict pullbacks. Infinity-categorical pullbacks are stated as such. A statement about raw pullback is never inferred solely from an equivalence between localizations. Every numbered statement bears one of the labels Proved, Refuted, Known, or Open. The latter records a limitation of the present audit and does not assert that the problem is open in the literature.
