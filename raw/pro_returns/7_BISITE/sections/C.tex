\section{Presheaf models, test categories, and strict higher categories}

\begin{claim}{C.1}{Proved}{What a presheaf model supplies before choosing a direction}
Let $A$ be any small category, $\widehat A=\operatorname{Set}^{A^{\op}}$,
and $i_A X=A/X$ its category of elements. For a fundamental localizer $W$
put $W_A=i_A^{-1}W$. For each $S\in\widehat A$ there is a canonical
isomorphism
\[
 \widehat A/S\cong\operatorname{Set}^{(A/S)^{\op}}.
\]
There are two specified, weakly saturated classes on this slice obtained
from B.1:
\[
 W^{\mathrm{pre,el}}_S=i_A^{-1}W^{\mathrm{pre}}_{A/S},\qquad
 W^{\mathrm{co,el}}_S=i_A^{-1}W^{\mathrm{co}}_{A/S}.
\]
Both are contained in the total class $W_A$ over $S$. These define
localizations for any $A$, but test-category theory alone does not identify
them with the two simplicial theories of Part~B.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
The displayed isomorphism sends $X\to S$ to the presheaf
$(a,s)\mapsto\{x\in X(a):p(x)=s\}$. Its inverse takes the disjoint union
of these fibres over $s\in S(a)$, with the specified restriction maps.
The two constructions are inverse on objects and arrows, and the resulting
category of elements over $A/S$ is canonically $A/X\to A/S$.
Pulling back the two functorial comma tests of B.1 consequently gives the
asserted classes, their weak saturation and their containment in the total
class. This constructs actual candidates without assuming an opposition
functor on $\widehat A$.
If $W$ is accessible and $A$ a $W$-local test category, the absolute
class $W_A$ supports the monomorphism model structure of
\citet[Corollary 4.2.18]{BCCisinski2006}. If $A$ is a $W$-test category,
its absolute homotopy theory presents $\Spaces_W$ by that book's
Theorem~4.2.15 and the test-category comparison. Neither assertion mentions
or chooses directed slices. C.2--C.3 give an explicit reason that the latter
choice cannot be silently inferred from the absolute comparison.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{C.2}{Refuted}{The two category-of-elements candidates need not be dual, even on a test category}
Assume $W\subseteq W_0$. For every small $A$ and $S\in\widehat A$,
$W^{\mathrm{pre,el}}_S$ consists exactly of isomorphisms.
For $A=\Delta$, $S=1$, and $W=W_\infty$,
$W^{\mathrm{co,el}}_1$ is instead the ordinary class of weak homotopy
equivalences of simplicial sets. Their enhanced localizations are not
equivalent. Thus transporting both fine tests through the category of
elements does not give an automatic extension of Part~B to presheaves.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Fix $(a,s)\in A/S$. An object of $(a,s)\backslash(A/X)$ is
$(b,x,h:a\to b)$ with $S(h)p(x)=s$. Its invariant
$X(h)x\in X(a)_s$ is preserved by every morphism. In the subcategory
with invariant $x_0$, the object $(a,x_0,1_a)$ is initial: its unique
arrow to the displayed object has underlying arrow $h$. Hence this comma
category is the disjoint union of contractible categories indexed by
$X(a)_s$. A map on such categories is in $W\subseteq W_0$ if and only
if it is bijective on these indices. Necessity follows from components;
sufficiency follows because a bijection of the contractible components
is already a $W_\infty$-map. Requiring the condition for all $(a,s)$
says exactly that $X\to Y$ is objectwise bijective, hence an isomorphism
of presheaves over $S$.

For the other comma orientation there is a strict isomorphism
\[
 (A/X)/(a,s)\cong A/(X\times_S h_a),
\]
where $h_a\to S$ is the map represented by $s$. An object on either side
is $(b,x,h:b\to a)$ satisfying $p(x)=S(h)s$, with the same triangle
condition on arrows. For $A=\Delta$ and $S=1$, these tests are the
weak equivalences $X\times\Delta^n\to Y\times\Delta^n$.
The last-vertex comparison identifies the classifying space of their
simplex categories with their realizations. The case $n=0$ is $X\to Y$,
and projection from a product with $\Delta^n$ is a weak homotopy
equivalence, proving the asserted characterization.
Localization at isomorphisms is the nerve of the ordinary category of
simplicial sets, with discrete mapping spaces. Localization at weak
homotopy equivalences is $\Spaces$, which has, for example,
$\operatorname{Map}(*,S^1)\simeq S^1$. An equivalence preserves mapping
spaces and cannot identify these two infinity-categories.
This is a counterexample to the specified category-of-elements extension,
not a denial of the simplicial opposition in B.22: that opposition uses
the directed simplex structure, which this transport of the two tests
has failed to preserve.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{C.3}{Refuted}{Elementwise smoothness and properness do not admit a general exchange involution}
Define a map $u:X\to Y$ in $\widehat A$ to be elementwise $W$-smooth
or $W$-proper when $i_Au:A/X\to A/Y$ has that categorical property.
Every presheaf map is elementwise $W$-smooth, for every fundamental
localizer. If $W\subseteq W_0$, a map is elementwise $W$-proper exactly
when all its naturality squares are pullbacks of sets.
Already for the test category $A=\Delta$, no autoequivalence of
$\widehat A$ can exchange these two classes.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
The functor $A/X\to A/Y$ is a discrete fibration. Given
$h:(b,y')\to(a,y)$ and a lift $(a,x)$ of $(a,y)$, its unique cartesian
lift has source $(b,X(h)x)$. Thus its incoming lift categories have
terminal objects and it is smooth for every $W$.
An outgoing lifting category, for $x\in X(a)$ and
$h:(a,u(x))\to(b,y)$ in $A/Y$, is the discrete set
\[
 \{x'\in X(b):X(h)x'=x,\ u(x')=y\}.
\]
There are no nonidentity vertical arrows, since the category-of-elements
map is a discrete fibration. This set is $W$-aspherical for
$W\subseteq W_0$ exactly when it is a singleton. For all choices this
is exactly bijectivity of
$X(b)\to X(a)\times_{Y(a)}Y(b)$, or the cartesian naturality condition.
For $A=[1]$, the presheaf $X(0)=\{*\}$, $X(1)=\varnothing$ mapping
to the terminal presheaf gives a nonsingleton, indeed empty, outgoing
lifting set over $0\to1$. For the test category $\Delta$, take
$\partial\Delta^1\hookrightarrow\Delta^1$, the vertex $0$, and
the arrow in the element category from that vertex to the nondegenerate
edge. It has no outgoing lift in the boundary. Thus the proper class is
strictly smaller than all maps, whereas the smooth class is all maps.
An autoequivalence takes all maps to all maps and cannot exchange them.
This proves a precise negative answer for a natural pair on a presheaf
category and identifies the missing directional input.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{C.4}{Proved}{The extra structure that does transport a duality}
Let $M$ have pullbacks and a weakly saturated class $W$, and let
$\mathcal L_+,\mathcal L_-$ be two pullback-stable classes.
Suppose $D:M\to M$ is an involutive equivalence preserving $W$, together
with its canonical pullback comparisons, and carrying $\mathcal L_+$
to $\mathcal L_-$. Then the universal-test classes satisfy
\[
 D(W_S^{\mathcal L_+})=W_{DS}^{\mathcal L_-},
\]
and $D$ induces an equivalence of the corresponding enhanced slice
localizations. It also exchanges their universal raw-pullback-preserving
classes. Such data are sufficient; being a test category supplies none
of the required exchange isomorphisms by itself.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
For $u:X\to Y$ over $S$ and $a:T\to S$ in $\mathcal L_+$, the
pullback comparison of the equivalence $D$ identifies
$D(a^*u)$ with $(Da)^*Du$. Membership in $W$ is equivalent on the two
sides. Every map in $\mathcal L_-$ to $DS$ is, up to the equivalence,
of this form. This proves the displayed equality in both directions.
An equivalence of relative categories induces an equivalence of their
infinity-localizations. Applying the same calculation after a further
arbitrary base change proves the last exchange assertion. This proof
uses no model structure.
For simplicial sets the involution is induced by the automorphism
$\rho:\Delta\to\Delta$,
$\rho(h)(i)=n-h(m-i)$ for $h:[m]\to[n]$; it is an automorphism of
$\Delta$, not a claimed equivalence $\Delta\simeq\Delta^{\op}$.
B.22 supplies the exchange of its smooth and proper tests.
For a general indexing category an involution must be chosen and its
exchange property proved. Replacing $A$ by $A^{\op}$ instead changes
the presheaf category to copresheaves and need not give an equivalence:
for the fork $a\leftarrow b\to c$, the ordinary presheaf category
recovers the idempotent completion of $A$ as its noninitial indecomposable projectives.
Indeed a projective is a retract of a coproduct of representables and an
indecomposable one is a retract of one representable; a finite poset has
no nonsplit idempotents. The fork and its opposite have different numbers
of minimal and maximal objects, so the two reconstructed categories are
not equivalent. The analogous infinity-categorical distinction is used
in Part~G.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{C.5}{Known}{Theorem A for the required strict higher slices}
For $1\leq n\leq\omega$, write $V^{(n)}$ for strict $n$-functors whose
Street nerves are weak homotopy equivalences; this notation is distinct
from the truncation localizer $W_n$ of B.10. Use the under-slices of
\citet[\S4]{BCAraMaltsiniotis2018} and their total-dual over-slices.
For a strictly commuting triangle $X\xrightarrow{u}Y\to S$, either
of the conditions
\[
 s\backslash u\in V^{(n)}\quad(s\in S_0),
 \qquad\text{or}\qquad
 u/s\in V^{(n)}\quad(s\in S_0)
\]
implies $u\in V^{(n)}$. The same result allows the specified oplax
triangle in the cited theorem.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}[Exact reference and conventions]
The under-slice assertion is
\citet[Theorem 5.2 and Remark 5.3]{BCAraMaltsiniotis2018},
\emph{Un th\'eor\`eme A de Quillen pour les $\infty$-cat\'egories
strictes I: la preuve simpliciale}. The over-slice assertion is
\citet[Corollary 6.8]{BCAraMaltsiniotis2022}, with the total-dual slice
isomorphism in its proof. The constructions restrict to strict $n$-categories;
they do not require a model structure. This resolves the titles and
variance of the result cited from memory in the prompt. The 2-categorical
predecessors include Chiche's \emph{Th\'eories homotopiques des 2-cat\'egories};
the all-dimensional statement used here is the stated Ara--Maltsiniotis
theorem, rather than an inference from the existence of a Street nerve.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{C.6}{Proved}{Two strict higher relative theories and a definite duality}
For a strict small $n$-category $S$, define $V^{\mathrm{pre}}_S$ and
$V^{\mathrm{co}}_S$ by the two slice tests in C.5. They are weakly
saturated and contained in the total class $V^{(n)}$. Let
$D_{\mathrm{tot}}$ reverse every positive-dimensional cell. Then
\[
 (n\Cat/S,V^{\mathrm{pre}}_S)
 \cong(n\Cat/D_{\mathrm{tot}}S,V^{\mathrm{co}}_{D_{\mathrm{tot}}S})
\]
as relative categories, and hence their enhanced localizations are
equivalent. This conclusion holds for every $1\leq n\leq\omega$ and
uses none of the claimed September 2026 model structures.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
For each object $s$, the slice construction is a functor on the strict
slice category, so identities, retracts and two-out-of-three are inherited
from Street weak equivalences. C.5 proves containment in the total class.
There is a canonical strict isomorphism
\[
 D_{\mathrm{tot}}(X/s)\cong s\backslash D_{\mathrm{tot}}X.
\]
Theorem~6.6 of \citet{BCAraMaltsiniotis2022} proves invariance of Street
weak equivalences under every cell duality. The displayed identity and
this invariance prove the relative-category isomorphism, including both
directions; applying infinity-localization proves the claim.
These are the particular lax and colax conventions fixed in C.5. Changing
which higher coherence cell is reversed changes the slice convention and
must be accompanied by the corresponding cell duality.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{C.7}{Proved}{What replaces the single opposite in strict dimension $n$}
For each subset $J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$, or $J\subseteq\mathbb N_{>0}$
when $n=\omega$, reversing the $j$-cells for $j\in J$ defines an
involutive automorphism $D_J$ of strict $n$-categories and preserves
$V^{(n)}$. The generators commute and compose by symmetric difference.
Define $\Liss^{(n)}$ to be the maps whose every strict base change
preserves $V^{\mathrm{co}}$ under raw pullback, and $\Prop^{(n)}$ using
$V^{\mathrm{pre}}$. Then both classes contain isomorphisms and are stable
under composition and strict base change; $D_{\mathrm{tot}}$ exchanges
them. These definitions alone do not identify them with a higher
lifting-category criterion or with smooth/proper Street-nerve maps.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Reversal exchanges the selected source and target operations and the
corresponding order of composition. The globular identities and interchange
law are invariant under these simultaneous exchanges. Applying the same
reversal twice is identity, and independent exchanges commute. This proves
the automorphism and symmetric-difference assertions. Its preservation
of $V^{(n)}$ is the verified theorem cited in C.6.
For the preservation classes, composition of raw pullbacks is raw
pullback of the composite, up to its canonical isomorphism. A further
base change is another member of the universally quantified family.
These two facts prove the closure assertions. The slice isomorphism of
C.6 identifies the preservation test for $p$ with the opposite test for
$D_{\mathrm{tot}}p$, proving exchange. For $n=2$ the generators are
usually called $\mathrm{op}$ and $\mathrm{co}$, and their product
$\mathrm{coop}$ is the total dual. The formal result concerns those
specified tests; an unproved higher analogue of B.6 is not hidden in the
notation $\Liss^{(n)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{C.8}{Known}{The verified absolute model structure in dimension two}
The category of strict $2$-categories has the proper combinatorial
Thomason model structure with weak equivalences detected by the Street
nerve, and
$c_2\operatorname{Sd}^2\dashv\operatorname{Ex}^2N_2$ is a Quillen
equivalence with simplicial sets in the Kan--Quillen structure.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}[Exact opened theorem]
This is \citet[Theorem 6.27 and Corollary 6.32]{BCAraMaltsiniotis2014},
\emph{Vers une structure de cat\'egorie de mod\`eles \`a la Thomason
sur la cat\'egorie des $n$-cat\'egories strictes}. The distinction between
the Quillen adjunction in Theorem~6.27 and the Quillen equivalence in
Corollary~6.32 is retained. Neither theorem identifies the two fine
relative classes over a higher base. The ordinary nerve in Part~B and
the twice-extended Street nerve here are different comparison functors.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{C.9}{Proved}{The exact coarse consequence of an absolute higher model theorem}
Suppose, for a fixed $n$, that the model structure and Quillen equivalence
claimed in family~317 are valid \citep[claimed, unrefereed]{OpenAI2026Thomason}. Then the total-weak-equivalence
localization of $n\Cat/S$ presents $\Spaces_{/|N_nS|}$, the slice of
spaces over the classifying space of the Street nerve. It has the usual
derived postcomposition and pullback adjunctions. This is a coarse
statement. It does not imply recognition of C.6's fine localizations or
any universal base-change property for the classes in C.7.
For $n=2$ the premise is already verified by C.8.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
The assumed Quillen equivalence identifies the absolute enhanced
localization with spaces and the image of $S$ with $|N_nS|$, since the
natural extension map $N_nS\to\operatorname{Ex}^2N_nS$ is a weak
homotopy equivalence. The slice model category presents the corresponding
infinity-categorical slice. One may check a nonfibrant base by choosing a
fibrant replacement $S\to S^{\mathrm f}$: right properness makes the
postcomposition--pullback adjunction between these two model slices a
Quillen equivalence. For a fibrant base the ordinary slice-model
comparison computes the derived slice directly. This supplies the needed
comparison for every $S$, not just fibrant higher bases.
The resulting adjunctions are those in the slice of spaces. The fine
classes require tests by higher comma categories and retain directed
information; none of these tests occurs in the absolute model hypothesis.
Already for $n=1$ and $S=[1]$, the coarse category is $\Spaces$ whereas
the fine category is the arrow infinity-category of spaces. Thus the
absolute theorem cannot, by this slice argument, prove the requested fine
recognition. This conditional argument does not certify the unreviewed
2026 manuscript or its formalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{C.10}{Open}{The strict higher fine recognition and base-change problem}
For a general strict $n$-category $S$, $2\leq n\leq\omega$, the following
specific extension is not proved here: the Street nerve induces equivalences
from the two enhanced localizations in C.6 to the actual covariant and
contravariant localizations over $N_nS$, with the slice conventions
matched as in C.5; moreover C.7's preservation classes admit a local
higher comma-lifting criterion and satisfy the universal Kan base-change
axioms of Part~E. These are required before declaring every such
$(n\Cat,V^{(n)},\Liss^{(n)},\Prop^{(n)})$ an instance of the abstract
theorem. Accepting family~317 does not remove these requirements.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}[The missing comparisons]
C.5 proves only that the fine tests imply total weak equivalence.
It does not identify a higher comma nerve with a right or left fibrant
replacement of a vertex, nor provide inverse relative functors as in B.3.
For an ordinary nerve the strict simplex-category last-vertex functor
lands in the category because composites are equalities. For a general
strict higher target the relevant simplex data include nonidentity higher
cells, so that construction cannot simply be reused as a strict functor.
Furthermore $N_nS$ need not be a quasicategory; B.4 shows why literal
simplicial slices cannot be substituted without their own proof.
What is needed is a relative straightening comparison respecting those
higher slice tests, followed by the universal base-change comparison for
its specified pullback functors. The walking noninvertible $2$-cell is
already a base outside the ordinary-category proof.
C.6--C.7 settle the formal duality and its weak-equivalence invariance;
C.8--C.9 settle the verified or conditional absolute consequence.
No full higher fine theorem, and no derivator calculus indexed by all
strict $n$-categories, is asserted on the strength of an absolute model
structure alone.
\end{proof}
