\subsection{Uniform bounds for products and a primary product obstruction}
\label{sec:A4-products}

The following two results concern specified product constructions.
They do not assert that every class in the fibre of $T$ has generators
of this form.  All sets, products, and indexing posets below are taken
in the fixed universe of the question.

\begin{claim}{A4.3}{Proved}{Multiplicities in products from a fixed set of factors can be bounded}\label{A43}
Let $\{X_s:s\in S\}$ be a set of connected pointed spaces, and choose
an infinite cardinal $\kappa$ such that
$|\pi_nX_s|\leq\kappa$ for every $s\in S$ and $n\geq1$.
For an arbitrary family of sets $\{I_s:s\in S\}$, put
\[
 P=\prod_{s\in S}X_s^{I_s},\qquad
 \mu_s=\min\{|I_s|,\kappa\},\qquad
 P_{\leq\kappa}=\prod_{s\in S}X_s^{\mu_s}.
 \tag{A4.3.1}
\]
Empty powers are points.  For every class of pointed spaces closed
under weak equivalences, retracts, and filtered homotopy colimits,
\[
             P\in\mathcal C
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P_{\leq\kappa}\in\mathcal C.
 \tag{A4.3.2}
\]
In particular their least reversible fundamental localizers coincide.

Consequently products with factors in this fixed set give only a set
of reversible closures.  More generally, the reversible closure of
any class of such products is the reversible closure of a set of such
products with bounded multiplicities.  The assertion remains true
after adjoining any fixed set of additional generators.  For a set of
countable connected CW models one may take $\kappa=\aleph_0$.

If a single connected $X$ has finite $\pi_nX$ in every degree, every
power $X^I$ belongs to every reversible aspherical class containing
$X$, without first adjoining $X^{\aleph_0}$.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Choose a subset $J_s\subseteq I_s$ of cardinal $\mu_s$.  Projection
onto the coordinates in $J_s$, with the pointed inclusion obtained by
putting the basepoint in the remaining coordinates, makes
$P_{\leq\kappa}$ a retract of $P$.  This proves one implication of
\textup{(A4.3.2)}.

For each $s$, let $\mathcal P_s$ be the poset of partitions of $I_s$
into at most $\mu_s$ nonempty blocks, ordered by refinement.  If
$I_s=\varnothing$, this is the one-object poset consisting of the
empty partition.  Each $\mathcal P_s$ is a set and is filtered.  When
$\mu_s$ is infinite, the common refinement of two partitions has at
most $\mu_s^2=\mu_s$ blocks.  When $\mu_s$ is finite, necessarily
$|I_s|=\mu_s$, so every partition and every common refinement already
has at most $\mu_s$ blocks.  The set
\[
                    \mathcal P=\prod_{s\in S}\mathcal P_s
\]
is again a filtered poset: common refinements are chosen in each
coordinate.  This argument applies even when $S$ is infinite.

For $\mathscr P=(\mathscr P_s)\in\mathcal P$, define
\[
                 P_{\mathscr P}
                    =\prod_{s\in S}X_s^{\mathscr P_s}.
\]
Refinement gives the coordinate-repetition maps.  Every
$P_{\mathscr P}$ is a pointed retract of $P_{\leq\kappa}$, by the
same projection-and-basepoint construction.  There is a canonical map
\[
        \hocolim_{\mathscr P\in\mathcal P}P_{\mathscr P}
                  \longrightarrow P.
 \tag{A4.3.3}
\]
All these spaces are connected.  Homotopy groups commute with filtered
homotopy colimits, so in degree $n\geq1$ the source group of
\textup{(A4.3.3)} is the union of the groups of families of functions
\[
       a_s:I_s\longrightarrow\pi_nX_s
\]
that are constant on each block of some simultaneous partition
$\mathscr P$.  Every such family occurs: the nonempty fibres of
$a_s$ partition $I_s$ into at most
$\min\{|I_s|,|\pi_nX_s|\}\leq\mu_s$ blocks.  All transition maps
on these groups are injective, and the comparison is an isomorphism
onto $\prod_s(\pi_nX_s)^{I_s}$.  Thus \textup{(A4.3.3)} is a weak
equivalence.  Closure under the stated operations proves the other
implication of \textup{(A4.3.2)}.  No interchange of an infinite
product and a filtered colimit has been assumed.

There is a set $\mathrm{Card}_{\leq\kappa}$ of possible multiplicities.
Hence the representatives
\[
 \left\{\prod_{s\in S}X_s^{\nu_s}:
           (\nu_s)\in(\mathrm{Card}_{\leq\kappa})^S\right\}
 \tag{A4.3.4}
\]
form a set.  Replacing every proposed product generator by its capped
representative proves the assertions about arbitrary classes of
generators and about adjoining fixed generators.  This is a set of
generators \emph{for the reversible closure}; no separate assertion
that all such closures are accessible is needed for this conclusion.
If $P$ is primary, or belongs to a specified sphere-null class, then
its capped representative retains that property because it is a
retract of $P$.

For the last assertion use finite partitions of $I$, rather than
partitions into at most $\kappa$ blocks.  The same calculation works
because each group $\pi_nX$ is finite, although their orders need not
be bounded independently of $n$.  Thus $X^I$ is a filtered homotopy
colimit of finite powers of $X$.  Reversible aspherical classes are
closed under finite products: use the cofiber sequence
$X\vee Y\to X\times Y\to X\wedge Y$, smash closure, and the
cofiber criterion.  This proves the assertion.  If $I$ is nonempty,
$X$ is also a retract of $X^I$, so their reversible closures agree.
The empty power is just the point.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{A4.4}{Proved}{Products that remain primary characterize the upper endpoint}\label{A44}
Let $p$ be a prime, let
$W_0=\min\operatorname{Rev}(BC_p\to *)$, and let
$W_{\mathrm{prim}}=W_{\Z[1/p]}$.
There is a sequence of simply connected spaces $Y_n$, each ordinarily
$BC_p$-acyclic, such that
\[
 Y=\prod_{n\geq0}Y_n
 \quad\hbox{has $p$-primary reduced integral homology},\qquad
 \min\operatorname{Rev}(Y\to *)=W_{\mathrm{prim}}.
 \tag{A4.4.1}
\]
One may take $Y_n=P_nM$, the $n$th Postnikov section of
$M=M(\mathbb F_p,2)=\operatorname{cofib}(p:S^2\to S^2)$.
The space $M$ is a homotopy retract of $Y$.

Consequently, for a reversible localizer
$W_0\subseteq W\subseteq W_{\mathrm{prim}}$, the following are
equivalent: $W=W_{\mathrm{prim}}$; and whenever a countable family of
$W$-aspherical spaces has a product whose reduced integral homology
is still $p$-primary, that product is $W$-aspherical.
At $p=2$ the product $Y$ is not in $\Nul_2$.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Serre's prime-primary class theorem shows that every homotopy group
of $M$ is a finite $p$-group: use the class of finite $p$-groups in
\citet[Chapter I, \S6.4; Chapter III, Corollary 2, p.~274]{A4SerreClasses}.
The hypotheses hold because $M$ is simply connected and its only
nonzero reduced integral homology group is $H_2M=\mathbb F_p$.
Choose a reduced minimal Kan model $K$ of $M$.  It is finite in every
simplicial degree.  For completeness, minimality makes $K_0$ a
singleton; once $K_{<m}$ is finite, there are finitely many possible
boundaries of $m$-simplices, and the nonempty sets of fillers modulo
homotopy relative to their boundaries are torsors for finite
$\pi_mK$.  Minimality chooses at most one representative of each
such class.  This is the levelwise-finiteness argument in
\citet[Lemma 7.2.4 and its proof]{A4BarneaHarpazHorel}.

Put $K^{(n)}=\operatorname{cosk}_{n+1}K$.  These are Kan complexes
representing $P_nM$, and the natural map $K\to K^{(n)}$ is an
isomorphism in simplicial degrees at most $n+1$;
see \citet[Proposition 3.5.3.23 and Examples 3.5.7.24,
3.5.8.4]{A4KerodonPostnikov}.
Choose a nonprincipal ultrafilter $\mathcal U$ on $\mathbb N$.
For an $m$-simplex $(z_n)$ of $\prod_nK^{(n)}$, all sufficiently
large $n$ identify $z_n$ with an element of the finite set $K_m$.
Define $r_m((z_n))$ to be the unique element of $K_m$ attained on a
$\mathcal U$-large set of those indices.  For every simplicial
operator, both degrees involved agree with $K$ on a common tail.
The inverse image of the selected value is $\mathcal U$-large, so
the operation commutes with this finite-set ultralimit.  Thus the
$r_m$ define a pointed simplicial map
\[
       r:\prod_nK^{(n)}\longrightarrow K.
\]
The diagonal $j:K\to\prod_nK^{(n)}$ satisfies $rj=1_K$
strictly.  Products of Kan complexes compute homotopy products, so
this proves the claimed homotopy retraction.  This construction uses
an ordinary nonprincipal ultrafilter, available in ZFC; it uses no
countably complete ultrafilter or measurable-cardinal assumption.

Each $P_nM$ is ordinarily $BC_p$-acyclic.  Indeed A34.2 shows that
$BC_p$ kills every simply connected primary GEM.  The finite
Postnikov tower of $P_nM$ has fibres
$K(\pi_kM,k)$, $2\leq k\leq n$.  Ordinary acyclic classes are
closed under such fibration extensions.  One can check this directly:
if $F\to E\to B$ is a fibration with $F$ and $B$ ordinarily
$A$-acyclic and $Z$ is $A$-null, the natural constant-map equivalences
$Z\to\operatorname{Map}(F_b,Z)$ for its fibres identify
\[
 \operatorname{Map}(E,Z)
   \simeq\holim_{b\in B}\operatorname{Map}(F_b,Z)
   \simeq\operatorname{Map}(B,Z)\simeq Z.
\]
They are natural in the fibre, so this argument includes the
coherent transport around the base.  Induction up the finite tower
gives the assertion; $P_0M$ and $P_1M$ are points.

It remains to verify the primary condition on the \emph{whole}
product.  For every $k\geq2$,
\[
       \pi_kY\cong\prod_{n\geq k}\pi_kM.
 \tag{A4.4.2}
\]
The finite group $\pi_kM$ has a finite $p$-power exponent, which
kills the entire product in \textup{(A4.4.2)}.  Thus all homotopy
groups of the simply connected $Y$ are $p$-primary torsion groups.
Apply the other direction of Serre's class theorem, now for the class
of all $p$-primary torsion groups
\citep[Chapter I, \S7.2; Chapter III, Theorem 1]{A4SerreClasses}.
It gives $\widetilde H_*(Y;\Z[1/p])=0$.  Equivalently,
$\Sigma^\infty Y\in\loc(\mathbb S/p)$: its localization away
from $p$ is connective with zero integral homology, hence zero by
connective stable Hurewicz, and the kernel of that telescope
localization is $\loc(\mathbb S/p)$.  This is the homology-to-stable
comparison; primary homology has not been inferred merely from
primary factors.

We recall directly why a reversible class containing $M$ contains
every connected primary space $Z$.  Desuspension puts
$M(\mathbb F_p,1)$ in that class, and smash closure puts
$M(\mathbb F_p,1)\wedge Z$ there.  This is the cofiber of the
$p$-fold suspension map on $\Sigma Z$, which is therefore a
$W$-equivalence by A.2.  Its sequential telescope has zero reduced
integral homology, since that homology is
$\widetilde H_*(\Sigma Z;\Z)\otimes\Z[1/p]$.  The telescope is
simply connected, so it is contractible.  Homotopy-colimit closure
then makes $\Sigma Z$, and reversibility makes $Z$, aspherical.
The cofiber criterion identifies the resulting localizer with
$W_{\mathrm{prim}}$.  Applying this to the retract $M$ of $Y$
proves \textup{(A4.4.1)}.  This also reproves the particular finite
Moore-generator comparison used here.

The stated conditional product-closure property for an interval
class forces it to contain $Y$, hence to be the upper endpoint.
The converse follows from the definition of that endpoint.  The
primary qualification is necessary: the product
$\prod_{n\geq1}K(\Z/p^n,2)$ already has
$H_2=\prod_{n\geq1}\Z/p^n$, which contains an element of infinite
order and is not primary.  Thus even the upper endpoint is not
closed under unrestricted countable products.

Finally the quotient of $M$ by its $2$-skeleton gives a map
$M\to S^3\to S^3_{(p)}$ which is nonzero in
$H^3(M;\Z_{(p)})\cong\mathbb F_p$.  Composing with the retraction
$Y\to M$ gives a nonnull map $Y\to S^3_{(p)}$.  At $p=2$ this is
one of the defining sphere tests for $\Nul_2$.  The reduced cellular
chain complex $\Z\xrightarrow{p}\Z$ and this nonzero cohomology
class are checked exactly in the accompanying finite certificate.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope of the product results.}
A4.3 supplies a uniform set of representatives only after the set of
possible factors has been fixed.  It does not show that arbitrary
reversible generators in the primary interval are products of
countable spaces or are generated by such products.  A4.4 gives a
restriction on every proper interval class, and an explicit failure
of closure under countable products that remain primary.  It supplies
no third interval element.  The supplied REVLOC report mentions a
prior product-of-Postnikov-sections attempt in a note not included in
the attachment; accordingly this argument is recorded with its proof
and source audit without a claim of novelty or of movement on a
principal A.4 question.
