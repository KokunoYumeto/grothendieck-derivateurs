## 1. A finite mod-p cohomology source produces a finite primary cofiber

**Proved lemma.** Let X be a connected pointed CW homotopy type with p-primary reduced integral homology. Assume that its total reduced mod-p homology is nonzero and finite dimensional. Then the reversible aspherical class generated by X contains a noncontractible finite simply connected p-primary complex.

Put Y=Sigma X, and let p_Y:Y->Y be the p-fold sum of the identity under the canonical co-H-space structure of the suspension. On reduced integral homology this map is multiplication by p. Its homotopy cofiber C is simply connected and is in every reversible aspherical class containing X. The cofiber exact sequence gives, for every k,

    0 -> H_k(Y;Z)/p -> H_k(C;Z) -> H_(k-1)(Y;Z)[p] -> 0.

The two outer groups are finite dimensional over F_p, by the homology universal coefficient sequence for Y. They vanish for all but finitely many k, again by that sequence and the finite total mod-p homology hypothesis. Thus each reduced H_k(C;Z) is finite p-primary and only finitely many are nonzero. One can also see nonvanishing directly: p_Y induces zero on reduced mod-p homology, so the mod-p cofiber sequence gives an injection H_k(Y;F_p)->H_k(C;F_p). Some such group is nonzero.

Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology*, Proposition 4C.1, gives a CW model with one generator n-cell and one relator (n+1)-cell for each finite cyclic summand of H_n(C;Z). Here there are only finitely many such summands. The resulting model K is therefore finite and simply connected, has nonzero p-primary reduced homology, and is homotopy equivalent to C. No finiteness of the original integral homology of X and no finite CW model for X are assumed.

The exact source was opened at <https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~Hatcher/AT/ATch4.4.pdf>, web refs `turn185957352view0` and `turn453639849view0`; Proposition 4C.1 and its entire proof, printed pages 429--430, were read. The proof builds generator and relator cells by the relative Hurewicz isomorphism and ends with homological Whitehead. The already existing bibliography key is `A2Hatcher`. A separate opening of Wall's original at <https://math.uchicago.edu/~shmuel/tom-readings/wall%20finiteness%201.pdf> produced an image-only PDF (`turn185957352view1`); its proof has not been read and no Wall theorem is used here.

## 5. Finite iterated-loop presentations preserve the exact reversible class

**Proved structural observation, independent of a stable splitting.** For every connected pointed CW type X and every finite m>=1,

    min Rev(X -> *) = min Rev(Omega^m Sigma^m X -> *).

Let C_m be the little-m-cubes operad and C_mX its free based algebra. May's actual unstable filtration has F_0 C_mX=* and cofibration pairs (F_r C_mX,F_(r−1) C_mX), with quotient

    C_m(r)_+ smash_(Sigma_r) X^smash r.

If X lies in a reversible aspherical class, so does X^smash r and its smash with C_m(r)_+. The permutation action on the configuration factor C_m(r) is free. Hence the displayed based orbit is equivalent to the derived based orbit, which is a pointed homotopy colimit of objects in the class. (One may compare the pairs EG×_G(C_m(r)×X^smash r), EG×_G(C_m(r)×*) with their ordinary quotient pairs; both vertical maps are equivalences and the based orbit is their cofiber.) Cofiber extension closure then puts every filtration stage, and their sequential homotopy colimit C_mX, in the class. May's approximation theorem identifies C_mX with Omega^m Sigma^m X because X is connected.

Conversely, the actual Sigma^m/Omega^m adjunction triangle makes Sigma^m X a homotopy retract of Sigma^m Omega^m Sigma^m X. Retract closure and m applications of reversible desuspension recover X. This converse uses finite m; it does **not** justify reversing the infinite-loop construction QX.

The exact primary source is May, *The Geometry of Iterated Loop Spaces*, author-hosted 2007 transcription <https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~may/BOOKS/gils.pdf>. Proposition2.6(i)–(ii), transcription p9, gives the NDR pairs and the quotient formula; its full proof uses the equivariant NDR construction of AppendixA.4, p86. Corollary4.6 supplies the free symmetric action, and Theorem6.1 gives the connected approximation. These statements and the Proposition2.6 proof were opened at `turn813542925view1`, `turn813542925view0` and `turn286358308view0`, then checked against local `sources/A3_May_1972_retyped.txt`. Existing bibliography key `A3May1972` is reused; the original printed page images were not obtained in this block.

Thus every reversible class in the primary interval admits a presentation over BC_p by **simply connected double-loop spaces** Omega² Sigma³ X, replacing each connected generator X after one suspension. This statement is valid for set presentations and for class presentations in the outer universe. It provides no bound on the number or cardinality of the generators, and it does not give finite Steenrod-algebra generation. The latter obstruction is real: applying this construction to a finite primary Moore source gives a double-loop generator for the upper endpoint, whereas the CCS–Hemmi argument in A34.6 shows that a simply connected primary loop-on-H space with finitely generated unstable cohomology is ordinarily BC_p-acyclic. Therefore the finite-generation property cannot be preserved by this construction in general.

## 6. Remaining construction and detector boundary after A4.8

Over W_0 one can additionally replace each double-loop generator H by P_BCp H without changing the generated reversible class: the unit is inverted already by W_0. This replacement remains simply connected and primary by cone attachment, and it remains double-loop by the Bousfield loop/nullification comparison. Therefore a proper intermediate class would have a presentation by primary BC_p-null loop-on-H spaces whose unstable cohomology is not finitely generated. This gives a precise construction domain, but no cardinal bound or nontrivial separator is inferred from it.

The attempt to use large torsion groups was revisited specifically for nonlocally finite p-groups. It returns to the already recorded obstruction in `notes/A4_new_routes.md`: torsion group-cellularity does not give BC_p-cellularity of its classifying space, and the standard Burnside examples have nonprimary Schur multipliers. The published Flores2005 paper explicitly imposes locally finite torsion subgroups in Section5.2; its discussion on printed p1157 says the general p-group nullification question was not then known. That historical sentence is not treated as a statement of the current 2026 literature. No proof of primary homology in all degrees or source sphere-nullity for a nonlocally finite candidate was obtained. These searches revisit an existing failed route and count as no new gate reduction.

A different natural construction is to build a primary BC_p-null space from finite targets using homotopy limits, retracts and pointed mapping spaces. Such operations preserve BC_p-nullity, but they also preserve locality for the source sphere-null class: mapping out of a sphere-null source into a homotopy limit is a homotopy limit of the mapping spaces, and mapping into Map_*(K,T) is adjoint to mapping from the smash with K. Reversible source sphere-nullity is smash-closed. Hence a noncontractible source which is both primary and sphere-null cannot arise as an object local for that same sphere-null class. This explains why merely increasing the size of products or mapping constructions from the familiar finite targets cannot furnish the missing identity-map detector. It does not prove that all BC_p-null targets are generated by these operations.

The finite Goodwillie layers are similarly insufficient. For a primary input their spectrum layers are primary infinite-loop constructions, while recovering the input requires an inverse-limit step. A finite-loop replacement preserves the reversible class, but passing to the infinite-loop object QX only has the forward implication; reversing it would incorrectly identify the lower primary class with the upper endpoint. No interchange of the inverse limit or a uniform finite suspension bound was obtained here.

## 7. The complex-projective Moore-smash candidate collapses

A concrete infinite-cohomology candidate was tested:

    C = M(F_p,1) smash CP^infinity
      = cofib(p_co-H : Sigma CP^infinity -> Sigma CP^infinity).

It is primary with infinitely many nonzero mod-p homology groups, so the finite-total-cohomology extraction in A4.7 does not by itself apply. Nevertheless C is already W_0-aspherical. The H-space p map CP^infinity=K(Z,2)→K(Z,2) has connected fibre BC_p, and is therefore an ordinary BC_p-nullification equivalence. Its telescope is K(Z[1/p],2). Homotopy-colimit closure makes the comparison CP^infinity→K(Z[1/p],2) a W_0-equivalence. Smashing it with M(F_p,1) gives a W_0-equivalence from C to a simply connected integrally acyclic space, hence to a point. The target is acyclic because multiplication by p is invertible on the positive homology of K(Z[1/p],2).

The two p maps must not be confused: the co-H sum on the suspension multiplies every reduced integral homology group by p, whereas the suspension of the H-space p map on CP^infinity acts by p^k in degree 2k+1. The telescope-and-smash argument handles that distinction; it does not identify the maps. The fibre is connected in degree two, unlike the arithmetic p map on K(Z,1), so this result is consistent with the earlier upper-endpoint extraction for Moore smashes of torsion-free abelian classifying groups.

## 8. The finite-detection mechanism after checkpoint 07

The stronger question now being attacked is the already recorded one from `notes/A4_new_routes.md` §4:

> If a connected primary space X is not sphere-null, must every reversible class containing BC_p and X be the upper primary class?

An affirmative answer would place every proper interval class inside W_S intersect U_p. It would not by itself identify that class with W_0 or prove that the full fibre is a set. No proof or counterexample to this statement has been obtained. In particular, the existence of a map X→S^n is not identified with a finite primary object in the aspherical class.

There is an elementary **sufficient finite-factorization criterion**, useful for locating the missing step. Suppose K is a connected finite pointed CW complex and there are maps

    K --a--> X --b--> K

such that (ba)_* on the finite-dimensional vector space H̃_*(K;F_p) is not nilpotent. Then min Rev(BC_p wedge X)=U_p.

Indeed put u=ba and v=ab. The two alternating mapping telescopes give a canonical equivalence Tel(u)≃Tel(v): maps a and b intertwine u and v, and the two composites induce the shift maps on their telescopes, which are equivalences. Every reversible class containing X contains Tel(v), by homotopy-colimit closure, and hence contains Tel(u). Since u factors through the primary space X, the image of u_* on each integral homology group of K is finite p-primary. Its direct limit is therefore finite p-primary and vanishes in all but finitely many degrees. Mod-p homology commutes with the telescope, and a nonnilpotent linear endomorphism of a finite-dimensional vector space has nonzero direct limit. Thus Tel(u) has nonzero finite total mod-p homology and primary integral homology. The extraction and finite-addition theorem A4.7 applies after suspension if needed.

This criterion does **not** follow just from a nonzero sphere-valued map. For K=S^n, every composite S^n→X→S^n has degree zero, since the intermediate integral homology is torsion. Thus the direct attempt to turn a sphere map into a nonnilpotent finite endomorphism fails even at ordinary homology. A finite Moore-type test object and a complementary map would have to be constructed. Neither a homotopy-category image object nor stable compactness supplies it automatically.

I also considered using a CW model of bounded homological dimension to make stable maps uniformly unstable. Even after a finite-dimensional model is supplied, a compact spectrum in loc(Σ∞X) need not come with a finite construction from finitely many copies of the noncompact spectrum Σ∞X. Transfinite cell dependencies can involve maps from entire infinite-cell copies of X. No bounded-cellular-stage theorem removing this issue was proved. This is a proof gap, not an implicit use of compact generation.

## 9. Reduced-product double-loop candidates: two immediate obstructions

A concrete construction under investigation is

    R(H_i)=hocolim_(F finite subset I) product_(i not in F) H_i,

followed by BC_p-nullification. Here each H_i is intended to be a simply connected primary BC_p-null double-loop space. This homotopy colimit removes individual finite-coordinate factors; it is not silently identified with the cofiber of a weak product inclusion. Products and the homotopy colimit need not preserve primary homotopy or BC_p-nullity. Those two properties must be checked before invoking the interval construction. In particular BC_p is not a compact source, so filtered colimits of BC_p-null spaces cannot automatically be declared BC_p-null.

For a constant family H_i=H with a degreewise finite reduced Kan model, the nonprincipal-ultrafilter operation on each finite simplicial set gives H^I→H. It ignores any finite coordinate set, and hence factors compatibly through every tail product. The constant diagonal gives a section. Thus H is a homotopy retract of R(H_i). If H already generates the upper endpoint over BC_p, so does this reduced product and its BC_p-nullification. This is the same finite-set ultrafilter mechanism used in A4.4; it is not a new cardinality theorem.

Another tempting family has coherent loop depth tending to infinity. Tailwise E_m structures for each fixed m can then be compatible with the filtered colimit, producing a grouplike E_infinity object. **This observation requires compatible operadic structures, not merely an assertion that the underlying spaces admit unrelated deloops.** When the resulting connected infinite-loop space remains primary, Levy's Theorem3.2 puts it in the lower class, and its BC_p-nullification is contractible. Thus taking more and more compatible deloops is not itself a way to retain an intermediate class. The primary condition is indispensable: arbitrary products of finite p-groups can acquire elements of infinite order.

Genuinely varying E_2 types with no retained finite-type retract remain a possible construction domain, but no such family has been shown to yield a nontrivial proper interval class. Stabilizing finite skeleta of a known primary source can also merely reproduce that source's existing nullification question; that reformulation is not counted as a new smaller gate.

## 10. Finite chromatic nullification: a candidate and a precise composition gap

An additional candidate is obtained by choosing a simply connected finite primary complex V of chromatic type at least two, choosing a simply connected finite mod-p Moore space M, and forming

    H = P_V(Ω²Σ²M) ≃ Ω²P_(Σ²V)(Σ²M),
    Z = P_BCp H.

The loop/nullification equivalence is Bousfield1997 Theorem5.2, applied twice. Products and the cone construction show that these spaces remain primary; the displayed double-loop model is simply connected because the input and the attaching domains before taking two loops are at least three-connected. Thus Z is a simply connected primary BC_p-null double-loop space. No claim that Z is noncontractible, sphere-null, or a proper generator has been proved. The construction is an attempted family, not an exhibited third point.

There is a useful auxiliary joint-nullification consequence of A4.7. If V is any simply connected finite primary complex with nonzero reduced homology and A=BC_p wedge V, then

    P_A X ≃ *  for every simply connected primary X.

Here is the full connectivity argument. A34.2 gives the reversible comparison with one suspension, since A kills every simply connected primary GEM. The symmetric-product retraction proof of A4.5 applies verbatim to A instead of BC_p on simply connected primary H-spaces: P_A preserves the required connectivity, primary homology and H-structure; if P_AΣH=*, then the null H-space P_AH is a retract of its symmetric product, which A kills. Thus reversible and ordinary A-acyclicity agree on that H-space domain. By A4.7, Rev(A)=U_p.

Now put Y=P_AX. It is simply connected, primary and A-null. The loop space ΩY need not be simply connected, so the preceding criterion must not be applied directly to it. Instead let P=π₁ΩY=π₂Y and let H be the universal cover of ΩY. This cover is a simply connected primary H-space: the multiplication lifts uniquely after fixing the unit, and its higher homotopy groups are those of ΩY. The pointed mapping space Map_*(A,K(P,1)) is discrete because A is connected. Mapping A into the fibre sequence H→ΩY→K(P,1), and using the A-nullity of ΩY, therefore gives Map_*(A,H)≃*. Hence H is A-null as well as Rev(A)-aspherical. The H-space criterion makes it A-acyclic, so H=*. Consequently ΩY≃K(P,1). Since this space is BC_p-null, Hom(C_p,P)=0. A nonzero p-primary abelian torsion group has a nonzero element of order p, so P=0. Therefore ΩY and Y are contractible. Root independently identified and checked the same universal-cover repair; this is an auxiliary deduction, not a principal-question status change.

This does not identify the composite P_BCp P_V with P_(BCp wedge V). A nullification need not preserve the local objects of a different nullification. In the candidate above, neither this preservation nor a nonzero value of the composite has been obtained. Alternating the two localizations to form their join supplies no proof about the first composite.

Let M=M(F_p,2), H=Ω²Σ²M and V=M(F_p,3). Sullivan's theorem makes H BC_p-null, because Σ²M is finite and mapping spaces commute with loops. ## 11. Draft forward finite-suspension preservation argument (09:51 UTC; awaiting independent audit)

The following proof is a candidate route-exclusion theorem, not an integrated or independently accepted result. Put A=BC_p and P=P_A.

**Finite mapping lemma.** If F is a connected primary A-acyclic loop space and K is a finite pointed CW complex, every connected component of Map_*(K,F) is A-acyclic. The needed closure under coherent loop-space fibres follows from Bousfield1996 Theorem1.9, printed p36. The source's definition on p35 says that BC_p is 1-supported p-torsion. In particular the fibre between two A-acyclic coherent loop spaces has A-acyclic connected components. Any connected loop map deloops to a map between connected classifying spaces, so this applies to those maps. Components of iterated loops of F are A-acyclic by induction. A finite CW decomposition of K computes Map_*(K,F) by finitely many fibres of the pointwise loop maps induced by cell attachments; restricting to identity components and using the preceding closure proves the lemma. All components are equivalent by loop multiplication. The primary claim used here follows from finite obstruction theory: mapping a finite complex into a primary nilpotent space has primary higher homotopy groups.
