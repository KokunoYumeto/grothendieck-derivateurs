## Results and exact limits

**A4.3** proves a uniform multiplicity bound for products from a fixed set S of connected pointed factors. Choose one infinite cardinal kappa bounding the cardinalities of every homotopy group of every factor. Replace each multiplicity |I_s| by min(|I_s|,kappa). The original product and the capped product belong to exactly the same classes closed under weak equivalences, retracts, and filtered homotopy colimits; in particular they have the same reversible closure.

The proof uses the small filtered poset of simultaneous partitions of the index sets into at most the capped numbers of blocks. Empty index sets have the unique empty partition. For finite multiplicities all partitions are allowed. For infinite multiplicities, finite common refinements respect the bound because kappa squared equals kappa. On each homotopy group, the comparison from the filtered homotopy colimit is precisely the inclusion of functions constant on some partition into all functions; partitioning by fibres proves equality. No exchange of an infinite product with a filtered colimit is assumed.

Thus all products from this fixed set of factors are represented, for purposes of reversible closure, by a set of capped products. Even a class of product generators can be replaced by a set. Adjoining a fixed set of generators does not change that conclusion. If the factors have countable CW models, kappa=aleph_0 suffices. If a fixed factor has finite homotopy groups in every degree, finite partitions suffice, and all of its arbitrary powers already lie in any reversible aspherical class containing it.

This is a set of generators in the reversible sense. The statement does not silently identify that condition with accessibility of the underlying fundamental localizer. It also does not show that arbitrary generators in the full primary fibre are products of countable spaces or are generated by such products. The full fibre set-size question remains open.

**A4.4** constructs an explicit countable product that remains primary but leaves the lower class. Let M=M(F_p,2) and Y=product_n P_n M. Each finite Postnikov section is ordinarily BC_p-acyclic. A degreewise finite minimal Kan model K of M gives a strict simplicial retraction from product_n cosk_(n+1) K onto K: in each simplicial degree use a nonprincipal ultrafilter to take the unique finite-set limit on the tail where the coskeleta agree with K. All simplicial operators commute with this operation, and the diagonal splits strictly.

The whole product is primary for a specific reason: in degree k its homotopy group is a power of the single finite p-group pi_k M, hence has bounded p-power exponent. Serre's theorem for arbitrary p-primary torsion groups then gives primary integral homology of Y. Connective stable Hurewicz and the kernel of localization away from p identify its reduced suspension spectrum with an object of loc(S/p). The claim does not infer primary homology from primary factors alone.

Since M retracts from Y, adjoining Y reaches the upper primary endpoint. A direct proof of the Moore-generator comparison is included: smash closure makes the cofiber of p on Sigma Z aspherical for every primary Z, and the p-telescope of Sigma Z is simply connected and acyclic, hence contractible. Reversibility then makes Z aspherical.

Therefore an interval class is the upper endpoint exactly when it is closed under countable products **whose total product remains primary**. Every proper interval class fails this conditional closure. Unrestricted countable-product closure would be a vacuous condition here: even the upper endpoint fails it, as product_n K(Z/p^n,2) has H_2=product_n Z/p^n with an infinite-order element.
