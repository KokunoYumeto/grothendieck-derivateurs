## Mathematical gain

The Ramsey proof works over the integers. Failure of an affine equalizer gives a proper augmentation ideal E=dZ. A nonzero circle character annihilating E extends from the image of the simultaneous-difference map to its free target. It produces a finite-dimensional circle-valued function whose coordinate differences always sum to a fixed nonzero c. A sufficiently fine finite coloring and the Ramsey property make each difference small, contradicting the circle metric triangle inequality. Common successors combine finitely many augmentation contributions into a single affine arrow. The algebra agent checked this proof independently, including E=0, torsion d≥2, infinite hom-sets and the empty-difference family.

The graph consequences use exact opened primary Ramsey theorems. We also verified amalgamation explicitly, including preservation of triangle-freeness in the free amalgam and extension of the two finite orders. The named categories fail filteredness because every arrow is injective and the two singleton embeddings into a two-element object cannot be equalized.

The smooth-coinvariant comparison is elementary: S is filtered colimit over the inclusion poset of finite substructures, T takes pointwise-stabilizer fixed vectors, S⊣T and ST=id. S is exact and T preserves monomorphisms. The canonical identity colim_C F=(SF)_G proves both directions of exactness. This supplies an actual comparison, rather than relying on the distinct measure-dependent categories in Harman–Snowden.
