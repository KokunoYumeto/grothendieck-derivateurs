# Fraïssé comparison with ordinary smooth coinvariants

This is an independent elementary comparison, checked here to avoid conflating ordinary smooth representations with the measure-dependent categories in Harman–Snowden. It gives a reduction for homogeneous candidates and does not establish their totality. No priority claim.

## Data and statement

Let M be a set-sized ultrahomogeneous structure which is the union of its finite substructures. Let C be the small category whose objects are all finite nonempty substructures A⊂M and whose arrows are all structure embeddings; assume every finite collection of such substructures lies in a finite substructure. Put G=Aut(M), with pointwise stabilizers G_A of finite substructures as an identity-neighborhood basis. In the ordinary locally finite relational examples these hypotheses are automatic. Let R be a commutative ring with trivial G-action.

Write Sm_R(G) for ordinary R-modules with a G-action such that every element is fixed by some G_A. Let coinv_G:Sm_R(G)→R-Mod be V↦V_G, the quotient by the subgroup generated by gv−v. Then

\[
\operatorname{colim}_C:(R\text{-Mod})^C\to R\text{-Mod}
\text{ is exact}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{coinv}_G:\operatorname{Sm}_R(G)\to R\text{-Mod}
\text{ is exact}.
\]

Replacing C by a skeleton gives the same assertion. If the age contains the empty structure, including or excluding it makes no difference to this construction when M has a nonempty finite substructure; we use the filtered poset of nonempty finite substructures throughout.

## Construction of the comparison functors

Let I be the filtered poset of finite nonempty substructures of M with inclusion arrows, viewed as a subcategory of C. Define

\[
S:(R\text{-Mod})^C\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sm}_R(G),\qquad
S(F)=\operatorname{colim}_{A\in I}F(A).
\]

For g∈G and x∈F(A), set g[x]_A=[F(g|_A:A→gA)x]_{gA}. This is well-defined because the squares of restrictions and inclusions commute. The group law follows by functoriality. The class [x]_A is fixed by G_A, so the module is smooth. The functor S is exact: restriction to I is pointwise exact and filtered colimits of R-modules are exact.

Define

\[
T:\operatorname{Sm}_R(G)\longrightarrow(R\text{-Mod})^C,
\qquad T(V)(A)=V^{G_A}.
\]

If f:A→B is an embedding, ultrahomogeneity extends the isomorphism A≅f(A) to some g∈G. Put T(V)(f)(v)=gv. This is independent of the chosen extension, since two extensions differ on the right by an element of G_A. Also gv is fixed by G_B: for h∈G_B, the element g^{-1}hg fixes A pointwise. Composition and identities follow from multiplication of extensions. Thus T is a well-defined functor, and it preserves monomorphisms because fixed-point submodules do.

There is an adjunction S⊣T. An equivariant homomorphism φ:S(F)→V gives maps F(A)→V^{G_A}, x↦φ([x]_A), natural for all embeddings. Conversely, a natural transformation F→T(V) induces a map from the filtered colimit over I; naturality for g|_A makes this map G-equivariant. The two constructions are inverse and natural.

The counit S T(V)→V is an isomorphism: its source is the directed union of the submodules V^{G_A}, which is all V by smoothness. In particular T is fully faithful. Notice that this does not assert T is exact; generally fixed points are only left exact.

Let Δ_C and Δ_G denote constant C-diagrams and trivial smooth G-modules. We have TΔ_G(W)=Δ_C(W). The adjunction then yields natural isomorphisms

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Hom}_R(\operatorname{colim}_C F,W)
&\cong\operatorname{Nat}(F,\Delta_C W)\\
&\cong\operatorname{Hom}_G(SF,\Delta_G W)\\
&\cong\operatorname{Hom}_R((SF)_G,W).
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence, canonically and naturally,

\[
\operatorname{colim}_C F\cong(SF)_G.
\]

## Proof of the exactness equivalence

If coinv_G is exact, its composite with the exact functor S is exact. This composite is colim_C.

Conversely suppose colim_C is exact. Let V↪W be a monomorphism of smooth G-modules. Applying T gives a monomorphism T(V)↪T(W). Exactness of colim_C gives an injection

\[
\operatorname{colim}_C T(V)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{colim}_C T(W).
\]

Under S T≅id and the preceding comparison, this is precisely the canonical map V_G→W_G. Thus coinv_G preserves monomorphisms. It is a left adjoint between abelian categories and so is right exact; therefore it is exact. This proof does not need enough projectives in Sm_R(G), nor a theorem identifying any derived smooth coinvariants with derived C-colimits.

## Relation to opened literature

Caramello, *Topological Galois Theory*, arXiv:1301.0300, Theorem3.5 gives the corresponding atomic-topos equivalence Sh(C^op,J_at)≃Cont(G) for C nonempty with AP and JEP and a C-universal C-ultrahomogeneous ind-object. The exactness comparison above is proved directly instead of being inferred silently from that equivalence.

Harman–Snowden, arXiv:2204.04526v2, §§1.3.3–1.3.4 and10–11, defines a distinct measure-dependent completed algebra with Schwartz-space representations. Its semisimplicity statements are not needed and have not been transported to the ordinary category Sm_R(G).

## Optional compact-action observation

For R=Z, if G is extremely amenable (every continuous action on a nonempty compact Hausdorff space has a fixed point), ordinary smooth coinvariants are exact. Here is a direct verification. Let K⊂V be smooth G-modules and let χ:K→T=R/Z be a G-invariant homomorphism. The space of homomorphisms V→T extending χ is a nonempty closed subspace of T^V: it is nonempty by injectivity of the divisible group T. The G-action is continuous because every v∈V has an open stabilizer. Extreme amenability gives a G-fixed extension of χ. Thus Hom_G(−,T) sends every monomorphism to a surjection. Since T is an injective cogenerator of Ab, the adjunction Hom(V_G,T)=Hom_G(V,T) implies that V↦V_G preserves monomorphisms, hence is exact. This proves the claimed observation without importing any nonstandard representation theory.

The Ramsey-to-exactness proof in ramsey_exact.md is stronger in its categorical scope because it does not require existence of a Fraïssé limit. None of these statements proves total⇒extreme amenability or total⇒the Ramsey property.
