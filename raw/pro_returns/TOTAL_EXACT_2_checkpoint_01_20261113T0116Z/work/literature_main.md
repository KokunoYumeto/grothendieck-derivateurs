## Checks of relied-on established proofs

IM1976 Thm.3.1 uses the constant right module ΔZ, the projective sum of right representables surjecting onto it, and Cohn purity (Prop.1.1). Purity solves a finite system whose right-hand sides lie in the augmentation kernel. The resulting coefficients give affine annihilation, but their target objects initially vary; Lemma3.2 supplies I, allowing the finitely many summands to be transported to a common target without changing their objectwise zero sum. This step is explicitly present at p.295 and is necessary when comparing colimit augmentation with one affine arrow.

IM1976 Prop.3.3 uses finiteness of the union of the supports of two affine arrows. Condition III equalizes those ordinary support arrows, and augmentation zero makes their difference vanish after composition. Conversely, affine commutative squares imply ordinary I because a nonzero coefficient on one side must match a basis arrow on the other. This correctly proves the all-finite-family criterion; applying IV repeatedly to already affine arrows would not be valid.

For the monoid exclusion, Isbell1968 §2 and the shorter IM1976 Thm.4.1 work with periods of the endomorphism acting on a set, not with a priori invertibility. Positive and negative coefficients yield finite orbit cycles of total lengths n+1 and n. A least positive period and a coprime-period reduction force period1. IM1976 Cor.4.3 applies it to representables, and Cor.4.4 additionally uses I and a weakly terminal object. No unmentioned terminal-object hypothesis is needed in the period argument.

For Gabriel–Ulmer §15.2(c), the displayed criterion is exactly connectedness and nonemptiness of cocone categories. Translating their terminology: their “kofinal” functors are final in the present covariant-colimit convention; their “kofiltrierend” is filtered. The two-object criterion therefore says the diagonal is final, i.e. siftedness. It does not identify total asphericity with siftedness.

## Independent proof routes examined

1. **Injective model transfer.** R1 and I imply that colim:sSet^A→sSet preserves objectwise monomorphisms and weak equivalences, hence is left Quillen for the injective model structure. Composing with free simplicial abelian groups yields a left Quillen composite. The unsolved step is whether all injective trivial cofibrations in simplicial abelian A-diagrams are generated by free abelianization of injective trivial cofibrations of simplicial A-sets. This cannot be assumed: it is a reformulation of the missing permutation-to-arbitrary-module transfer.
2. **Goodwillie/stabilization.** Preservation of all discrete diagrams does not directly imply preservation of Eilenberg–MacLane diagrams. Even R1's equality of simplicial-set hocolimit with strict colimit does not identify strict set colimit with abelian colimit. Stabilization yields the known T_i(M) formulation and no vanishing theorem by itself.
3. **Free finite-coproduct completion.** Packing a finite family into one pair of arrows would work if totality passed to the free finite-coproduct completion. That transfer is false as a general route: the completion has finite coproducts and hence is sifted; for Δ_inj it is not filtered, so R6 rules out totality. This construction cannot be silently used.
4. **Dual injectivity.** The hypothesis says constant Q/Z has vanishing Ext from permutation modules in all positive degrees. Full injectivity would settle the problem. No embedding/resolution theorem was found that upgrades this relative injectivity to all modules. Arbitrary kernels of maps between representables need not have permutation bases.
5. **Rational augmentation filtration.** For a Q-module diagram M, the augmentation quotient I/I² of Q[UM] recovers M. Both Q[UM] and I lie in the acyclic class, but passing to I/I² is a cokernel and is not licensed. A logarithm/eigenspace splitting would require a completion or an inverse limit; acyclicity is only known to be stable under filtered colimits. This route currently stops at the same unsupported step.

6. **Ordinary smooth coinvariants.** We proved an actual comparison for a set-sized ultrahomogeneous locally finite structure M: the category C of finite substructures and all embeddings has exact R-module colimits if and only if ordinary smooth Aut(M)-coinvariants are exact. The proof, including the functors S and T, their adjunction, the isomorphism ST=id, and the canonical colimit comparison, is in `fraisse_coinvariants.md`. No derived smooth-coinvariant comparison is claimed. Harman–Snowden's measure-dependent Schwartz categories do not supply that missing comparison.
7. **Ramsey exclusion.** We proved that common successors plus the finite-family Ramsey property imply integral III, using a circle-valued separating certificate. Condition I then gives exactness. The proof is in `ramsey_exact.tex` and was independently checked by the algebra agent. Ordered finite graphs and ordered finite triangle-free graphs are therefore exact, total and nonfiltered. Their AP and JEP were verified directly, and the Ramsey theorems and their proofs were opened in Hubička–Nešetřil2019 and Hubička2025. This excludes the named C4 candidates; it does not prove totality implies the Ramsey property.
8. **Compact-action reformulation.** Extreme amenability of a topological group implies exactness of its ordinary smooth integral coinvariants. We proved this directly using the compact space of extensions of an invariant character K→R/Z across a monomorphism K⊂V. The group action is continuous because V is smooth; a fixed extension and the injective cogenerator R/Z detect injectivity on coinvariants. This is recorded as an optional observation in `fraisse_coinvariants.md`. No converse or implication from category totality to extreme amenability has been obtained.
9. **Nonadditive free-group and Dold–Kan attempts.** An objectwise contraction or extra degeneracy on a set/group resolution need not be additive after passing to a proposed module resolution. Likewise, for an acyclic chain diagram C the map from the strict set colimit of U DK(C) to the underlying strict abelian colimit is the unproved comparison, not a consequence of R1. These attempts stop before any transfer from permutation modules to arbitrary column cokernels.
