Approaches tried for Moore targets and the exact stopping points:

1. Scalar telescopes and phantom maps: finite p homotopy groups make the relevant inverse systems Mittag--Leffler/pro-zero. Countable phantom maps into the target vanish. A construction retaining an essential component while killing all positive mapping groups would need different, non-scalar bonding maps; none has been constructed.
2. Bousfield's GEM/nullification route: Homotopical localizations of spaces (1997), Key Lemma 5.3 requires the entire based mapping space to be homotopically discrete. The given hypothesis makes only its constant component contractible. Lemma 5.5 fills this gap for H-space targets, but a finite torsion Moore space cannot be an H-space by Browder's top-homology theorem. The cited Dwyer--Mislin example explicitly warns that components can differ. The 1996 ULP Theorem 1.8 gives GEM fibres between P_(Sigma^(i+1)X) and P_(Sigma^iX) only for i>=1; the lowest step P_(Sigma X) to P_X has the same obstruction.
3. Loop decompositions and resolving classes: CMN/Anick decompositions offer retracts of loops on higher Moore spaces and degree-p sphere fibres, not a Moore space itself. A finite Moore retract of a loop space would inherit an H-structure and is impossible. The exponent-p (r=1) Anick retraction must not be assumed; the opened literature retains it as an open case. Strom's 2003 Theorem 10 requires a full suspension/smash-closed target family. A single loop Moore space has not yet been shown to supply that family.
4. Stabilization of the targets: resolving/null-target classes preserve homotopy limits and fibre extensions, not arbitrary direct limits. Replacing Moore spaces by their Q-stabilizations is invalid for arbitrary source spaces; Sullivan's theorem versus nontrivial stable maps from classifying spaces shows the danger.
5. Finite-coefficient extension theory: Cencelj--Dydak--Smrekar--Vavpetic--Virk, Algebraic properties of quasi-finite complexes, Fund. Math.197 (2007), Section 2 and Lemmas 7.1--7.2 were opened. Their strong continuity and co-P-complex results establish useful source classes null to all finite torsion targets, but do not yet address the single-target desuspension implication.
