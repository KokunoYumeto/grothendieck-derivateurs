# The Prüfer ideal: circle-transfer and Thom audit

Research note, 2026-11-15, after checkpoint 05. This records actual obstructions
to specific proposed constructions. The historical failed constructions
remain useful, but the ideal has now been computed: **D.2.15 proves
J_p=loc(S/p) for every prime**, and in fact loc(Sigma-infinity CP-infinity)=Sp.
See `sections/D2_CPinf.tex` and `notes/EH5_CPinf_detector.md` for the full
proof and exact primary-source audit.

Write D=Sigma-infinity CP-infinity (reduced), M=S/p, and

    J_p = loc(D smash M) = loc(Sigma-infinity B(C_{p^infinity})).

The equality and the established lower bound are proved in EH5_D3.md. In
particular J_p contains T(n) for every n>=1 and HF_p. Consequently its finite
thick part is either zero, or J_p is all of loc(S/p). The second alternative
is now established by D.2.15; the earlier investigations below explain why
several simpler attempted arguments did not establish it.

## The p-map cofiber is already ordinarily Prüfer-acyclic

Let A=B(C_{p^infinity}). The map

    CP-infinity=K(Z,2) -> K(Z[1/p],2)

has homotopy fiber A. The acyclic-fiber criterion therefore makes it an
A-nullification equivalence. Its target is A-null: both
Hom(C_{p^infinity},Z[1/p]) and Ext(C_{p^infinity},Z[1/p]) vanish. To check the
second assertion directly, express C_{p^infinity} as colim_n Z/p^n. Every
Hom(Z/p^n,Z[1/p]) and Ext(Z/p^n,Z[1/p]) is zero, so the Milnor Hom/Ext exact
sequence gives the assertion without a finiteness assumption. Thus

    P_A CP-infinity = K(Z[1/p],2).

The map induced by multiplication by p on K(Z,2) becomes an equivalence
after P_A. Its pointed homotopy cofiber is therefore A-acyclic. It cannot
separate the ordinary or reversible class generated by A from its stable
closure. No claim about general cofibers in the reverse direction is used.

## What the complex Kahn--Priddy analogue actually supplies

The original paper by Graeme Segal, *The stable homotopy of complex
projective space*, Quart. J. Math.24 (1973), 1--5, was opened directly:

https://ncatlab.org/nlab/files/Segal72.pdf

Proposition1 gives a split surjection to complex K-theory, and its corollary
splits BU off QCP-infinity. It does not split a sphere infinite loop space
off QCP-infinity. The explicit comparison with Kahn--Priddy is Proposition2
of the same paper; the two targets are different. This source therefore
supports the already known K-theoretic lower bound, not extraction of a
finite spectrum from J_p.

Madsen--Schlichtkrull, *The circle transfer and K-theory*, was also opened:

https://data.math.au.dk/publications/pp/1999/imf-pp-1999-17.pdf

Theorem1.1 and the splitting results of Section7 compare the transfer
fibration with the Adams-operation fibration on U_(p), and split a U_(p)
factor at odd primes. Their Theorem7.8 is a splitting onto U_(p), not a
section of the circle transfer onto the sphere space. No such section is
asserted there.

## The unlooped circle transfer has no space-level section

Consider the usual dimension-shifting circle transfer

    partial: Q Sigma(CP-infinity_+) -> Q_0 S^0.

Galatius, *Mod p homology of the stable mapping class group*, Topology43
(2004), 1105--1132, was opened directly:

https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0401168

Theorem1.1 computes the Hopf-algebra cokernel of partial_*. It is nonzero at
every prime: it contains the even-index Dyer--Lashof generator in degree2
at p=2, and the generator Q^1 of degree2p-2 at odd p. Hence partial_* is
not surjective even as a map of vector spaces, and partial has no homotopy
section, either p-locally or after p-completion. This conclusion permits
an arbitrary space-level section; no H-map assumption is needed.

The exact computed indecomposable formula is also given in Galatius
Theorem4.1. Theorem1.3 shows that Omega partial is mod-2 homology-surjective.
Thus the preceding homology obstruction alone cannot be applied after
looping. The next argument checks that interface independently.

## Looping once still gives no section

The base component of the once-looped source is

    Q_0(CP-infinity_+) = Q_0S^0 times QCP-infinity,

and the target is Omega_0 Q_0S^0.

At p=2 the source's second homotopy group is

    Z_(2) plus Z/2,

while the target's second homotopy group is pi_3(S)_(2)=Z/8. The target
group cannot inject into the source group, so no map between these spaces
can exhibit the target as a retract. At p-completion, replace Z_(2) by
Z_2; the obstruction is unchanged. Simple connectivity of the reduced
QCP-infinity factor alone would already obstruct a section with only that
factor as source, but the second-homotopy-group argument also covers the
extra sphere factor in CP-infinity_+.

For odd p, set d=2p-4. The first p-primary stable sphere stem is
pi_(2p-3)S=Z/p, and the preceding positive stems are zero p-locally. The
connective Atiyah--Hirzebruch spectral sequence for reduced CP-infinity
therefore gives pi_d(D)_(p)=Z_(p): on total degree d the only nonzero term
is H_d(CP-infinity;Z_(p)), and no differential can enter or leave it in
this range. The sphere summand has pi_d(S)_(p)=0. Thus

    pi_d Q_0(CP-infinity_+)_(p) = Z_(p),
    pi_d Omega_0Q_0S^0_(p) = Z/p.

Again there is no injection of the second group into the first, hence no
section. Completion changes only the torsion-free Z_(p) to Z_p.

The classical stems used here were primary-source checked: Serre,
*Groupes d'homotopie et classes de groupes abeliens*, Ann. Math.58 (1953),
ChapterIV Proposition11, p.285, gives the first odd-primary stem and the
preceding vanishing; ChapterIV Proposition10 contains pi_(n+2)S^n=Z/2 in
the stable range. Adams, *On the groups J(X)--IV*, Topology5 (1966),
Example7.17 and its continuation on p.46, explicitly records pi_3(S)=Z/24.
Both originals were opened, the latter also visually checked on the page:

https://www.sas.rochester.edu/mth/sites/doug-ravenel/otherpapers/Serre-Classes.pdf

https://www.sas.rochester.edu/mth/sites/doug-ravenel/otherpapers/Adams-JX4.pdf

## The Moore-smashed transfer does not supply a section either

Smash the stable circle transfer with M=S/p and take base components:

    Omega-infinity_0[ Sigma((S wedge D) smash M) ]
      -> Omega-infinity_0 M.

At p=2 its source has pi_2=pi_1(M)=Z/2. The reduced D summand contributes
nothing in that degree because D smash M is 2-connective. The Moore
cofiber exact sequence gives a short exact sequence

    0 -> Z/2 -> pi_2(M) -> Z/2 -> 0.

Thus the target's pi_2 has order4, while the source's has order2; no
section exists. The argument does not require deciding the extension.

At odd p use degree d=2p-2. By the same stable-stem and AHSS bounds,

    pi_(d-1)(D smash M)=0.

Indeed both pi_(2p-3)D_(p)=0 and the p-torsion subgroup of
pi_(2p-4)D_(p)=Z_(p) vanish. The sphere summand in the source contributes
pi_(2p-3)M=Z/p, but the restriction of the transfer to Sigma S is zero
p-locally, since pi_1S_(p)=0. Consequently the entire map on pi_d is zero.
The target group pi_(2p-2)M is Z/p, by the Moore exact sequence and the
first-p-torsion calculation. The map is not surjective, so it has no
section. After looping once the same obstruction occurs in degree d-1
(and degree1 at p=2), still positive. These statements survive completion.

The source includes a sphere summand in this formulation. That summand
would itself not be available from the reduced ideal J_p; retaining it
here only strengthens the negative result for the proposed transfer.

## Thom membership does not follow from the mod-p Chern class

Let L be the tautological complex line over CP-infinity. The sphere bundle
of L tensor p is BC_p, so there is the stable cofibration

    Sigma-infinity BC_p+ -> Sigma-infinity CP-infinity_+
      -> Th(L tensor p).

In the Verdier quotient by J_p, after smashing with M, the middle term is
M. The first map is the identity on its M summand and has zero reduced
component, since that component factors through D smash M. Its cofiber
is therefore Sigma(Sigma-infinity BC_p smash M) in the quotient. It follows
that

    Th(L tensor p) smash M belongs to J_p
       iff Sigma-infinity BC_p smash M belongs to J_p
       iff J_p = loc(S/p).

The last equivalence uses the already checked Levy Theorem1.5 and smashing
its actual construction with M. Since M smash M generates the same ideal
as M, no smash-Bousfield-order inference is used. Thus proving the Thom
spectrum is in J_p would indeed settle the finite-part question, but its
membership is precisely a remaining substantive step. Vanishing of
c_1(L tensor p) mod p does not make the stable spherical twist trivial.

For comparison, the ordinary stunted projective spectrum
CP_k-infinity=Th(kL) is a different bundle construction: kL is a direct
sum, not a tensor power. If k>=2 and CP_k-infinity smash M were in J_p,
the cofibration CP^(k-1) -> CP-infinity -> CP_k-infinity would place the
nonzero finite spectrum CP^(k-1) smash M in J_p, and hence force the full
endpoint. This gives another exact test, not a verified new member.

## An exact reformulation using E-infinity rings

The following is a proved equivalence, not an additional hypothesis:

    J_p is a proper subcategory of loc(S/p)
      iff there exists a nonzero derived-p-complete E-infinity ring R
          with F(Sigma-infinity CP-infinity,R)=0.

The function spectrum uses reduced CP-infinity and vanishes in all degrees.
Vanishing only in degree zero is insufficient. In the equivalent unreduced
form the augmentation R^(CP-infinity) -> R is an equivalence of spectra
(and hence of E-infinity rings).

Here is the complete localization argument. A localizing subcategory of
spectra is automatically a tensor ideal: for A in the subcategory, the
class of spectra X with A smash X in the subcategory is localizing and
contains the sphere. The single generator D smash M makes J_p accessible,
so there is an exact reflective localization L with kernel J_p. Since an
L-equivalence is exactly a map whose cofiber belongs to this tensor ideal,
tensoring an L-equivalence with any spectrum preserves L-equivalences.

The category of L-local objects therefore has its localized symmetric
monoidal structure, with tensor L(X smash Y), and its inclusion in spectra
is lax symmetric monoidal. In particular its unit gives an E-infinity
ring R_0 in spectra with underlying spectrum L(S). This does not assert
that L is smashing or that R_0 smash R_0 -> R_0 is an equivalence.

The monoidal statement was checked in the original 2017 Higher Algebra,
Example2.2.1.7 and Proposition2.2.1.9, pp.196--197:

https://www.math.ias.edu/~lurie/papers/HA.pdf

If J_p is proper inside loc(M), then M is not in J_p, so

    R_0/p = L(M) != 0.

Now apply derived p-completion. For an arbitrary spectrum E, put
C=fiber(S -> S[1/p]). Then the triangle

    F(S[1/p],E) -> E -> F(C,E)

exhibits F(C,E) as the S/p-homology localization of E: the first term is
p-invertible, and F(C,E) is right orthogonal to S[1/p], since
S[1/p] smash C=0. Orthogonality to S[1/p] is the same as orthogonality to
all p-invertible spectra, because those spectra form loc(S[1/p]). Thus
F(C,E) is derived p-complete, its map from E is an S/p-equivalence, and
F(C,E)/p is E/p. Equivalently this completion is holim_n E/p^n; the
function-spectrum description avoids any convergence assumption.

S/p-equivalences are preserved by arbitrary tensor products. The same
monoidal localization theorem therefore gives the completion

    R = (R_0)^wedge_p

an E-infinity ring structure. No E-infinity structure on the individual
cofibers R_0/p^n is used. The spectrum R remains J_p-local, either because
the local subcategory is stable and closed under limits, or directly from
F(D smash M,F(C,R_0))=F((D smash M) smash C,R_0)=0. Also
R/p=R_0/p is nonzero, so R is nonzero.

Since R is J_p-local, F(D smash M,R)=0. The cofiber definition of M now
says that p acts invertibly on F(D,R). This function spectrum is itself
derived p-complete: for every p-invertible spectrum X,

    F(X,F(D,R)) = F(X smash D,R) = 0.

A p-invertible p-complete spectrum is zero. Hence F(D,R)=0, as required.

Conversely, if a nonzero derived-p-complete R has F(D,R)=0, it has
F(D smash M,R)=0 and so is J_p-local. If J_p were loc(M), locality would
make p invertible on R; p-completeness would then give R=0. This proves
the equivalence, including its nontriviality and completion clauses.

### The remaining ring-theoretic question

The exact unresolved assertion is whether every nonzero derived-p-complete
E-infinity ring has some nonzero reduced CP-infinity cohomology. A theorem
with this scope would prove J_p=loc(S/p); an explicit counterexample would
prove its finite thick part is zero by the established dichotomy.

A nonzero complex-oriented ring certainly has such a class, since the
orientation restricts to the unit on the bottom S^2. However, the nilpotence
theorem only guarantees that MU smash R is nonzero for a nonzero ring R.
It does not make the original R complex-oriented. In particular the
comparison

    F(D,R) smash MU -> F(D,R smash MU)

cannot be treated as an equivalence: D is not compact. A Morava-theory or
complex-oriented algebra receiving R has the same problem. None of these
base changes has yet established the assertion for arbitrary R.

The characteristic-p case is excluded by an opened primary theorem:
Mathew--Naumann--Noel, *On a nilpotence conjecture of J. P. May*,
arXiv:1403.2023v3, Theorem4.18, proves the Hopkins--Mahowald identification
of the free E_2 ring with a chosen nullhomotopy of p with HF_p. Thus a
nonzero E_2 ring with p=0 in pi_0 is an HF_p-module and has nonzero reduced
CP-infinity cohomology. Indeed HF_p smash D is a wedge of positive even
shifts of HF_p, so its HF_p-linear mapping spectrum into a nonzero module
is a product of shifts of that module. The source is

https://arxiv.org/pdf/1403.2023

This does not permit replacing a hypothetical R by R/p as an E_2 ring.
The spectral cofiber R/p is CP-infinity-cohomologically local, but it need
not have an E_2 multiplication. The free E_2 p-quotient uses infinite
colimits, which need not preserve cohomological locality. Proposition4.2
of the same paper concerns smash-homology K(n)_*R, and does not supply
this missing cohomological closure.

There is also a valid finite-base-change exclusion. If some nonzero finite
p-local type-zero spectrum F makes R smash F a weak MU-module, then
F(D,R smash F)=F(D,R) smash F=0, using dualizability of F. A nonzero weak
MU-module E has nonzero reduced CP-infinity cohomology: the orientation
D -> Sigma^2 MU, followed by a weak action MU smash E -> E, gives a
section Sigma^-2 E -> F(D,E) of restriction to the bottom cell. Finally
R smash F is nonzero because a finite type-zero F generates the finite
p-local sphere under thick constructions. This excludes the nonzero
Wood-type spectra in Carrick's terminology. The exact primary definitions
and identification with weak MU-modules were opened in Carrick,
*Chromatic defect, Wood's theorem, and higher real K-theories*,
arXiv:2402.17519v2, Definition2.30 and Theorem2.9:

https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17519

It does not exclude arbitrary E-infinity rings. Replacing the finite F
by MU or Ravenel's infinite X(n) restores the same noncompact tensor
problem. No boundedness, finite chromatic defect, or Wood-type hypothesis
has been established for the localized unit R constructed above.

### Scope of Bousfield's transitional-dimension discussion

Bousfield1997, printed p.1346, Lemma10.4 assumes a spectrum E with
tran_p(E)=n, 0<n<infinity, and reduced (E/p)-homology of K(Z,n+1) zero.
It concludes that a nilpotent generalized polyGEM X is E/p-acyclic
exactly when pi_n X is p-divisible and pi_i X is uniquely p-divisible for
i<=n-1. The next paragraph says that no spectrum satisfying those
hypotheses was known to the author. The original was reopened directly:

https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/bous1.pdf

This is a smash-homology statement. At n=1 it is a CP-infinity
smash-acyclicity question, which the later reported Hopkins smash-support
theorem would exclude. It does not name or settle the cohomological J_p
alternative here. The conversion in Bousfield's Section2.6 from an
R-cohomology equivalence class to a smash-homology equivalence class
requires every pi_i(R/p) to be finite. There is no such finiteness
statement for the arbitrary local-unit R above. The historical sentence
is therefore not evidence that the present J_p problem has been
identified with that published open case.

### The circle group algebra and the Eilenberg--Moore obstruction

Put A=R[S^1]. Its underlying R-module is R plus Sigma R, and the
augmentation module satisfies

    End_A(R) = F(BS^1_+,R).

Thus the proposed counterexample has End_A(R)=R. This is an exact
reformulation, but a double-centralizer conclusion requires an additional
descent or completion hypothesis. The second endomorphism ring in that
case is again R, and the double-centralizer map A -> R is precisely the
augmentation whose underlying fiber is Sigma R. The bare equality of
endomorphism rings does not show that this map is an equivalence.

The augmentation fiber is not the trivial S^1-module Sigma R. In
Fun(BS^1,Mod(R)), the Euler cofiber sequence for the standard complex
representation C is

    R[S^1] -> R -> R smash S^C.

Consequently its fiber is Sigma^-1(R smash S^C). It has underlying
module Sigma R, but its full circle action is the complex stable
J-twist. The underlying reduced multiplication, including its eta term,
does not determine a trivialization of this coherent action. Nilpotence
of eta alone therefore does not justify a nilpotent-augmentation or
Koszul-duality argument.

The precise primary comparison is Mathew--Naumann--Noel, *Nilpotence and
descent in equivariant stable homotopy theory*, arXiv:1507.06869v2:

https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.06869

Definition7.20 calls the comparison

    R tensor_{F(X_+,R)} R -> F(Omega X_+,R)

an equivalence when the EMSS is relevant. Proposition7.28 retains a
tensor-comparison condition even when Omega X is finite. Theorem7.29
identifies relevance for BG with unipotence of Fun(BG,Mod(R)).
Proposition7.31 requires the cochain base-change comparison explicitly;
Corollary7.32 obtains it for perfect base change. Theorem7.35 assumes
AHSS degeneration, while Theorem7.37 supplies a torsion-free coefficient
case. Theorem7.43 uses complex orientation to trivialize the full
representation-sphere action in its Euler sequence. None of these
hypotheses has been established for the local-unit R above.

In fact, for a hypothetical CP-infinity-null R, the EM comparison for
X=BS^1 becomes the constant-function inclusion

    R -> R plus Sigma^-1 R.

Its cofiber is Sigma^-1 R. Thus relevance would force R=0, but assuming
relevance merely inserts the missing assertion at this stage.

There is an explicit warning even with a connective coefficient ring
and finite connected loop space. Carlsson--Douglas--Dundas, *Higher
topological cyclic homology and the Segal conjecture for tori*,
Advances in Mathematics 226 (2011), Proposition6.1, Theorem6.2 and the
rank filtration in Section6.1 identify F(BS^1_+,S_p) as connective:
the positive-rank contributions have nonnegative homotopy, and the
rank-zero term is S_p. These statements and their page images were
opened directly at

https://www.sas.rochester.edu/mth/sites/doug-ravenel/otherpapers/CDD.pdf

The displayed products in Theorems1.4 and6.2 start at rank one and omit
the unavoidable rank-zero sphere summand; Section6.1 explicitly retains
it, and Theorem6.8 splits the rank filtration. This omission does not
affect the connectivity assertion. For R=S_p, both the cochain ring and
its augmentation module R are connective, so their derived relative
tensor product is connective. However

    F(S^1_+,S_p) = S_p plus Sigma^-1 S_p

has pi_-1=Z_p. The EM comparison is therefore not an equivalence.
Finite Omega X, simple connectivity of X, and connective coefficients
together still do not supply the desired convergence theorem.

## The odd-primary intermediate conclusion

The earlier transfer-section and EM shortcuts remain invalid, but a
different use of the same Euler triangle now resolves every odd prime.
The complete proof in EH5_Pruefer_odd.md shows that an E-infinity ring
with F(D,R)=0 and eta_R=0 is zero. It extends the unit over Th(-L),
checks that the extension is a genuine local-system orientation by the
Thom colimit adjunction and connectedness, and dualizes to orient L.
The local-unit criterion therefore gives J_p=loc(S/p) for odd p.
Root and the axioms/derivators agent independently checked the proof.

At this intermediate stage the p=2 finite-part alternative was undecided. A nonzero
CP-infinity-null local unit there would have eta_R nonzero. None of the
earlier failed transfer sections or EM comparisons supplies a way to
kill eta while retaining the required locality and ring structure.

## Final all-prime conclusion

D.2.15 now proves that every E1 ring with reduced CP-infinity cohomology
zero is itself zero. At 2, the bottom reduced transfer is an odd multiple
of nu, as follows from Sq4 on the negative-line Thom class. Its vanishing
with coefficients makes eta act trivially on R smash Ceta. The E1 ring
End_R(R smash Ceta) is still D-null by finite duality, so D.2.14 applies
to it; eta-nilpotence then forces R=0. Derived completion removes the
2-completeness hypothesis. Applying this detector to the monoidal local
unit gives actual loc(D)=Sp, and smashing the construction with S/p gives
J_p=loc(S/p) for every p. Full proof and source audit are in
`sections/D2_CPinf.tex` and `notes/EH5_CPinf_detector.md`.
