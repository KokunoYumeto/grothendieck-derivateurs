# D.2.4: zero finite stable part — proved reductions and remaining separation

## Rational stable part forces fixedness

**Proposition (proved).** If a reversible fundamental localizer W satisfies
\[
0\ne T(W)\subseteq\operatorname{loc}(H\mathbb Q),
\]
then \(T(W)=\operatorname{loc}(H\mathbb Q)\) and
\(W=R(\operatorname{loc}(H\mathbb Q))\). In particular such a W is RT-fixed. If \(T(W)=0\), the existing argument gives \(W=W_{\mathbb Z}\), also fixed.

**Proof.** Write \(\mathcal A_W\) for the pointed W-aspherical spaces. It is closed under weak equivalences, retracts, pointed homotopy colimits, suspension and desuspension, and two out of three for homotopy-cofiber sequences. Every suspension spectrum of a member of \(\mathcal A_W\) is rational, because it lies in \(T(W)\subseteq\operatorname{loc}(H\mathbb Q)\). Here \(\operatorname{loc}(H\mathbb Q)\) is precisely the rational stable category: localization of the sphere at all nonzero integers is \(H\mathbb Q\).

Because T(W) is nonzero, some \(X\in\mathcal A_W\) has nonzero reduced suspension spectrum. This spectrum is connective, so its first nonzero homotopy group is its first nonzero integral homology group. Consequently X has a first nonzero reduced integral homology group, which is a nonzero rational vector space. Also X is connected: the reduced integral H0 of a disconnected pointed space is a nonzero free abelian group and cannot be rational.

Suspend X sufficiently often that \(Z=\Sigma^rX\) is simply connected and its first nonzero homology occurs in an odd degree \(d\ge3\). All its reduced homology groups are rational vector spaces. The nilpotent rationalization criterion identifies Z as a rational space (May–Ponto, Theorems 6.1.1 and 6.1.2). Hurewicz identifies \(\pi_dZ\) with \(H_dZ\). Choose a nonzero vector \(a\in H_dZ\) and a rational-linear functional \(\ell:H_dZ\to\mathbb Q\) with \(\ell(a)=1\). The class a is represented by a map \(S^d\to Z\), which uniquely extends along rationalization to a map \(S^d_{\mathbb Q}\to Z\). Since Q is injective as an abelian group, the universal coefficient theorem realizes \(\ell\) by a map \(Z\to K(\mathbb Q,d)\). For odd d, \(K(\mathbb Q,d)\simeq S^d_{\mathbb Q}\) (May–Ponto, Corollary 6.7.3). The composite is the identity in the homotopy category, since it is the identity on the sole nonzero homotopy group Q. Thus \(S^d_{\mathbb Q}\) is a homotopy retract of Z and belongs to \(\mathcal A_W\).

The equivalences \(\Sigma S^n_{\mathbb Q}\simeq S^{n+1}_{\mathbb Q}\), for n>=1, follow from simple connectivity and the rational homology localization criterion; for n=1 one may use the telescope model of \(K(\mathbb Q,1)\). Suspension and desuspension closure therefore put every positive-dimensional rational sphere in \(\mathcal A_W\).

Every simply connected rational space Y now belongs to \(\mathcal A_W\). Here is a cellular justification, avoiding an unproved wedge-splitting assertion for rational suspensions. Choose a CW model of Y with one 0-cell and no 1-cells. Replace the boundary sphere of every cell by its rationalization. Inductively, the attaching map extends to the rational sphere because the previous stage is simply connected and rational. Form the pointed homotopy cofiber of the wedge of these rationalized attaching maps. The new stage is simply connected, and its integral homology consists of rational vector spaces by the cofiber homology sequence, so it remains rational. The map from the corresponding ordinary skeleton is a rational homology equivalence, again by that sequence. At the sequential union, compactness of spheres and exactness of filtered colimits identify the result with the rationalization of Y, hence with Y itself. Every rationalized cell boundary, wedge, cofiber and sequential homotopy colimit used in this construction belongs to \(\mathcal A_W\), proving the claim. At the 2-cell stage the boundaries are \(K(\mathbb Q,1)\); the cofibers are simply connected, so no assertion that a wedge of rational circles is nilpotent is needed.

Finally, let U be any pointed space with rational reduced suspension spectrum. Its reduced H0 forces U connected, and \(\Sigma U\) is simply connected with rational reduced homology. Hence \(\Sigma U\) is a simply connected rational space, belongs to \(\mathcal A_W\), and desuspension puts U in \(\mathcal A_W\). We have proved that the null class of W equals the null class of \(R(\operatorname{loc}(H\mathbb Q))\). The reversible cofiber criterion identifies the localizers themselves. Since rational spheres already occur among their null objects, their stable part is all \(\operatorname{loc}(H\mathbb Q)\). QED.

## A nonzero suspension-generated stable category with zero finite part

**Proposition (proved).** Fix a prime p and let
\[
\mathcal L_p=\operatorname{loc}(\Sigma^\infty K(\mathbb Z/p,2)),
\]
where the suspension spectrum is reduced. Then \(\mathcal L_p\ne0\), it is generated by a suspension spectrum, and
\[
\mathcal L_p\cap\operatorname{Ho}(\mathrm{Sp})^\omega=0.
\]

**Proof.** Lee's theorem says that for every simply connected pi-finite space A, the collapse A->* induces an equivalence
\[
\mathbb S\xrightarrow{\simeq}F(\Sigma^\infty A_+,\mathbb S).
\]
The exact modern primary statement is Allen Yuan, *The Sphere of Semiadditive Height 1*, Corollary 1.4.1 (Lee), arXiv:2208.12844, p.3. A pi-finite space has finite homotopy groups, only finitely many of which are nonzero, so A=K(Z/p,2) satisfies its hypotheses. Splitting off the basepoint gives \(F(\Sigma^\infty A,\mathbb S)=0\).

The full class \(\{E:F(E,\mathbb S)=0\}\) is localizing: the mapping-spectrum functor takes coproducts to products, cofiber sequences to fiber sequences, and shifts to inverse shifts. It therefore contains \(\mathcal L_p\). A finite spectrum F in this class has zero Spanier–Whitehead dual, and duality on finite spectra then forces F=0. Finally \(H_2(K(\mathbb Z/p,2);\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/p\), so the generating spectrum, and hence \(\mathcal L_p\), is nonzero. QED.

The smallest reversible localizer containing K(Z/p,2)->* is accessible by B.4 and has stable part exactly \(\mathcal L_p\). Thus a nonzero suspension-generated stable part with zero finite part is concrete. What remains unproved is that this smallest reversible localizer is not RT-fixed, or another construction giving that separation.

### Failed or insufficient witness routes

- Replacing K(Z/p,2) by QK(Z/p,2) cannot separate it from the reversible closure: K(Z/p,2) is an infinite loop space and a retract of QK(Z/p,2) by the suspension-spectrum/infinite-loop adjunction.
- B(Z/p) is not in \(\mathcal L_p\), since it has nonzero stable cohomotopy; it cannot witness failure of RT-fixedness for this stable part.
- A nonzero finite space cannot serve as the missing null object, since its nonzero suspension spectrum would contradict the zero finite part.
- Intersecting the known B(Z/p)-generated reversible localizer with \(R(\mathcal L_p)\) retains stable part \(\mathcal L_p\), because K(Z/p,2) is in the former null class. It would give the desired separation if one found a space Y with \(\Sigma^\infty Y\in\mathcal L_p\) but Y not null for the unstable sphere family. Such a Y has not yet been constructed.

## A finite-suspension formula for reversible generation

**Theorem (proved; exact classical inputs checked).** Let A be a connected pointed space with p-primary torsion reduced integral homology. Suppose that, for some integer r>=1, A kills every r-connected GEM whose homotopy groups are p-primary torsion. Then the aspherical class of the smallest reversible fundamental localizer containing A->* is exactly
\[
 \mathcal N_{A,r}=\{X\in\mathcal S_*:P_A(\Sigma^rX)\simeq *\}.
\]
Here P_A is ordinary, unstable, uncompleted A-nullification. No cardinality bound, bounded-exponent hypothesis, or p-completion assumption is present.

**Proof.** Write \(\mathcal C_A=\{Y:P_AY\simeq *\}\). Mapping into all A-null spaces proves that \(\mathcal C_A\) is invariant under weak equivalences, closed under retracts and pointed homotopy colimits, and closed under the two cofiber implications "first and second imply third" and "first and third imply second." In particular it is closed under suspension. The same closure properties hold for \(\mathcal N_{A,r}\), since suspension preserves pointed homotopy cofibers and pointed homotopy colimits.

First observe that every member of \(\mathcal C_A\) has p-primary torsion reduced integral homology. Indeed A->* is an \(H\mathbb Z[1/p]\)-equivalence, so the universal property of ordinary A-nullification implies that every A-acyclic space is \(H\mathbb Z[1/p]\)-acyclic. Flatness of \(\mathbb Z[1/p]\) says exactly that each reduced integral homology group is annihilated elementwise by powers of p. One may also prove this directly by testing against Eilenberg--Mac Lane spaces of injective cogenerators over \(\mathbb Z[1/p]\).

We prove desuspension closure. Suppose \(\Sigma X\in\mathcal N_{A,r}\), so \(\Sigma^{r+1}X\in\mathcal C_A\). Its homology implies that every \(\widetilde H_iX\) is p-primary torsion. Since \(\widetilde H_0X\) is free abelian, X is connected. Consequently \(\Sigma^rX\) is r-connected, and its infinite symmetric product is an r-connected GEM with p-primary torsion homotopy groups, by the Dold--Thom theorem. The hypothesis therefore gives
\[
 SP^\infty\Sigma^rX\in\mathcal C_A.
\]
Apply Bousfield's Theorem 3.3 to the connected space \(W=\Sigma^{r-1}X\), with k=2. In his nullity-class notation, where \(\langle U\rangle\ge\langle V\rangle\) means that U kills V, the exact identity is
\[
 \langle\Sigma^rX\rangle
 =\langle\Sigma^{r+1}X\vee SP^\infty\Sigma^rX\rangle.
\]
Both wedge summands are A-acyclic. This identity therefore gives \(\Sigma^rX\in\mathcal C_A\), or \(X\in\mathcal N_{A,r}\).

The remaining cofiber implication follows by rotation: if Y and C belong to \(\mathcal N_{A,r}\) in a cofiber sequence X->Y->C, the cofiber sequence Y->C->Sigma X puts Sigma X in that class; desuspension then puts X in it. Thus A.3 constructs a reversible fundamental localizer from \(\mathcal N_{A,r}\). It contains A->*, since every positive suspension of A is A-acyclic.

For minimality let W be any reversible fundamental localizer containing A->*. Its aspherical class contains \(\mathcal C_A\), because the ordinary localizer generated by A->* is contained in W. If \(X\in\mathcal N_{A,r}\), then \(\Sigma^rX\in\mathcal C_A\), and r successive uses of reversible desuspension give X W-aspherical. Hence \(\mathcal N_{A,r}\) is the smallest possible class. The reversible cofiber criterion identifies the localizers themselves. QED.

### Eilenberg--Mac Lane generators

For every n>=1 and A=K(F_p,n), the theorem applies with
\[
 r=\max\{1,n-1\}.
\]
For n>=2, Bousfield 1997, Lemma 8.13 states that K(G,n), for any nonzero p-torsion abelian group G, kills every (n-1)-connected generalized polyGEM with p-torsion homotopy groups, subject to one additional condition only when G/pG=0. Taking G=F_p removes that condition. A GEM is a generalized polyGEM, so this covers arbitrary abelian p-primary groups, including unbounded-exponent and divisible ones. It is unnecessary to replace them by finite groups or exchange nullification with an inverse limit.

For n=1, K(F_p,1) kills K(F_p,2): use the simplicial bar construction of the abelian topological group K(F_p,1), whose levels are its finite powers. Ordinary nullification preserves finite products (Bousfield 1996, the proof of Theorem 1.7), and its acyclic class is closed under geometric realization, so every level and hence the bar construction is acyclic. Lemma 8.13 with n=2 then shows that K(F_p,1) kills every simply connected p-torsion GEM. The bound r=1 follows. The argument does **not** prove that ordinary K(F_p,1)-acyclic spaces are themselves desuspension-closed: the initial suspension in Theorem 3.3 is essential.

All these generators have p-primary reduced integral homology. This follows from the usual nilpotent localization criterion applied away from p, or inductively from the bar construction starting with the finite cyclic group. Thus no p-local or p-complete variant has silently entered the formula.

In particular the candidate with zero finite stable part now has the exact unstable description
\[
 \mathcal A_{\minrev(K(F_p,2))}
 =\{X:P_{K(F_p,2)}(\Sigma X)\simeq *\}.
\]
Its non-RT-fixedness is therefore equivalent to finding a pointed space X with
\[
 \Sigma^\infty X\in\operatorname{loc}(\Sigma^\infty K(F_p,2)),
 \qquad P_{K(F_p,2)}(\Sigma X)\not\simeq *.
\]
No such X is asserted here.

### Generators containing B(Z/p)

If A is connected with p-primary reduced integral homology and B(Z/p) is A-acyclic, then A kills all simply connected p-torsion GEMs by the preceding bar argument and Lemma 8.13. The formula therefore holds with r=1. In particular, for every connected space V with p-primary reduced integral homology,
\[
 \mathcal A_{\minrev(B\mathbb Z/p\vee V)}
 =\{X:P_{B\mathbb Z/p\vee V}(\Sigma X)\simeq *\}.
\]
For V=*, this gives an ordinary-nullification characterization of the minimum of the interval in D.3, without identifying it with an ordinary nullification localizer on arrows.

## Two sharpened candidate pairs

### Divisible Eilenberg--Mac Lane source

For A=K(C_{p^infinity},2), the same proved formula holds with r=2: every 2-connected p-torsion GEM satisfies the extra condition \(\pi_2/p\pi_2=0\) in Lemma 8.13. Thus, with B=K(F_p,2),
\[
 B\in\mathcal A_{\minrev(A)}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P_A\Sigma^2B\simeq *.
\]
The elementary equality P_A B=B does not decide this question. Both suspension spectra annihilate the sphere: for A, express C_{p^infinity} as a filtered union of finite cyclic p-groups, commute K(-,2) and suspension spectra with that filtered homotopy colimit, and apply Lee to each finite stage.

**A specific difficulty with the stable-class comparison.** The equality
\(\operatorname{loc}(\Sigma^\infty A)=\operatorname{loc}(\Sigma^\infty B)\)
would imply that every homology theory killing A also kills B. This is a substantial restriction, not a consequence of equal Morava heights. Bousfield 1997, Lemma 10.4 and the paragraph immediately after it (printed p.1346), explicitly consider the exceptional possibility of a spectrum E with \(\operatorname{tran}_p(E)=n\) but \(E/p_*K(\mathbb Z,n+1)=0\), equivalently \(\widetilde E_*K(C_{p^infinity},n)=0\). By definition of the transitional dimension, \(\widetilde E_*K(F_p,n)\ne0\). The paper says no such E is known. I found no later primary resolution in the searches performed. This is recorded as a warning about the proposed proof, not as a claim that the 1997 problem remains open today.

### Suspending before applying Q

Let B=K(F_p,2) and A=Q(\Sigma B). Snaith splitting gives
\[
 \operatorname{loc}(\Sigma^\infty A)
 =\operatorname{loc}(\Sigma^\infty B),
\]
since the linear summand is \(\Sigma\Sigma^\infty B\) and every higher summand lies in its tensor-ideal localizing closure. Thus the stable part of minrev(A) is nonzero and has zero finite part.

The finite-suspension formula applies with r=2. Indeed A has p-primary reduced integral homology and first integral homology \(H_3(A)=\mathbb F_p\). Bousfield 1996, Corollary 3.2(ii), says that A kills its infinite symmetric product; the factor \(K(\mathbb F_p,3)\) is a homotopy retract of that product. Lemma 8.13 therefore gives all 2-connected p-torsion GEMs. Consequently the exact remaining test for B is
\[
 B\notin\mathcal A_{\minrev(Q\Sigma B)}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 P_{Q\Sigma B}(\Sigma^2B)\not\simeq *.
\]
No value of this nullification has been established. The usual space retraction B->QB->B does not answer it: the first map is generally not an infinite-loop map, so it cannot simply be delooped to obtain a retraction from QSigma B.

## Verified further structure of the stable candidate

Carmeli's primary preprint `arXiv:2309.12734`, *On the Non-Existence of J-Homomorphisms of Higher Height*, Section 5, supplies an exact description on the subcategory generated by chromatic telescopes. Write
\[
 \mathcal I_n=\operatorname{loc}(\Sigma^\infty K(F_p,n)),\qquad
 \mathcal T=\operatorname{loc}\{T(0),T(1),T(2),\ldots,HF_p\},
\]
where T(j) is a v_j telescope and all categories are taken p-locally. Propositions 5.2--5.3 and Proposition 4.5 give
\[
 \mathcal I_n\subseteq\operatorname{loc}(F(n)),\qquad
 \mathcal I_n\cap\mathcal T
 =\operatorname{loc}\{T(n),T(n+1),\ldots,HF_p\}.
\]
Here F(n) is a finite type-n spectrum. In particular \(\mathcal I_2\) contains every telescope of height at least two and contains HF_p, while D.2.6 proves that it contains no nonzero finite spectrum. Equal chromatic support is consequently insufficient to identify these localizing subcategories. These are structural facts about the stable candidate; they give no unstable separator.

The primary preprint was opened at the exact propositions and their proofs. It is published under the title *The chromatic colocalization of the spherical units is of height 1*, Geometry & Topology 29(8) (2025), 4449--4467, DOI `10.2140/gt.2025.29.4449`. The publication metadata and indexed PDF were checked, but direct opening of the publisher's PDF returned an unreadable server page; no assertion about changed published numbering is made. Primary opened text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.12734 (also https://www.sas.rochester.edu/mth/sites/doug-ravenel/otherpapers/Carmeli-J.pdf).

## A connective obstruction that rules out one infinite-loop strategy

**Lemma (proved; independently reviewed by the parent and integrated as D.2.9).** Let C be an n-connective spectrum whose integral homology is p-primary torsion and whose degree-n mod-p homology is nonzero. If E is n-connective and belongs to loc(C), then E belongs to the connective nullity class of C. Here n-connective means that pi_i=0 for i<n, and the connective nullity class is the kernel of the spectral nullification P_C, generated by nonnegative suspensions of C under colimits and extensions.

**Proof.** Put C_i=Sigma^{-i}C and Y_i=P_{C_i}E, for i>=0. Bousfield 1996, Theorem 2.6, gives a fiber sequence

    Sigma^{n-i-1} H G_i -> Y_i -> Y_{i+1}

with G_i p-primary torsion. Therefore the fiber of Y_i->Y_j, j>i, is (n-i-1)-coconnective. Exactness and t-exactness of filtered colimits in spectra imply the same bound for the fiber of Y_i->Y_infinity, where Y_infinity=colim_i Y_i.

For each fixed i, compare Y_{i+1} with Y_infinity. Its fiber is (n-i-2)-coconnective, whereas C_i is (n-i)-connective. Both that fiber and its suspension receive no maps from any nonnegative suspension of C_i. Thus Map(C_i,Y_{i+1})->Map(C_i,Y_infinity) is an equivalence. The first mapping space is contractible because Y_{i+1} is C_{i+1}-null and C_i=Sigma C_{i+1}. It follows that Y_infinity is null with respect to all positive and negative shifts of C. (The use of Y_{i+1}, rather than Y_i, is essential to control pi_0.)

Every cofiber of E->Y_i belongs to loc(C), by the ordinary spectral nullification construction. Hence the cofiber of E->Y_infinity belongs to loc(C). Thus E->Y_infinity is the stable localization away from loc(C). Since E already belongs to loc(C), Y_infinity=0. The preceding fiber bound with i=0 now gives Y_0 in Sp_{<=n-1}. On the other hand spectral nullification by n-connective C preserves n-connectivity, as its cone-attachment construction stays in Sp_{>=n}. Hence Y_0=0. QED.

This proof does not assume C compact and does not interchange Map(C,-) with an arbitrary filtered colimit. The coconnective fiber bounds replace that invalid interchange.

Applied to C=Sigma^infinity K(F_p,2), it shows that every E in I_2 intersect Sp_{>=2} has Omega^infinity E ordinarily K(F_p,2)-acyclic, by Bousfield 1996, Theorem 2.10 or Proposition 3.1. In particular sufficiently connective covers of the telescopes in I_2 cannot supply a separating infinite-loop space for the minimal K(F_p,2)-generated reversible class.

For A_r=Q Sigma^r K(F_p,2), r>=0, its suspension spectrum is (r+2)-connective, has first homology F_p, and has stable localizing class I_2 by Snaith splitting. The same lemma shows that every (r+2)-connective E in I_2 gives an ordinarily A_r-acyclic infinite-loop space. It does **not** show that the suspension Sigma^{r+1}K(F_p,2) is A_r-acyclic: that suspension is not an infinite-loop space supplied by the argument. The descending chain Rev(A_{r+1}) subseteq Rev(A_r) and the exact ordinary-nullification test remain candidates, without an established strict inclusion or collapse.

The parent independently reviewed this lemma, and it is integrated as D.2.9. The adjacent calculation is also proved there: for r>=1,

    P_{A_r} A_{r-1} = K(F_p,r+1),
    W_{r-1} = W_r join minRev(K(F_p,r+1)).

Indeed E=Sigma^{r-1}Sigma^infinity B has lower truncation Sigma^{r+1}HF_p, and HF_p belongs to I_2 by smashing the generator with HF_p and splitting the resulting nonzero module. Thus the higher cover belongs to I_2 and is killed by the connective lemma. The lower section is Sigma^infinity A_r-null by connectivity; Bousfield Corollary 2.3 and Theorem 2.10 compute the ordinary nullification. Its unit is a W_r-equivalence, which gives the join equality. This isolates one EM space for each potential strict step, but does not establish the strictness.

## 2026 periodic-homotopy source: scope checked

Barkan--Heuts--Shi, *On periodic homotopy and homology equivalences of spaces*, arXiv:2604.10867 (April 2026), was opened through Sections 1--5 at https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10867. Theorem 1.6 compares T(n)-equivalence of maps between infinite-loop spaces Omega^infinity E and Omega^infinity F, under L^f_{n-1}E=L^f_{n-1}F=0, with the n-truncation and v_n-periodic tests. Corollary 1.8 gives a pullback formula for localization of such a space at T(0) direct-sum ... direct-sum T(n) **homology**; this is not ordinary nullification at a finite type-(n+1) complex. Proposition 2.9 gives the usual EM acyclicity thresholds. These results do not identify the arbitrary stable localizing ideals generated by the divisible and finite EM candidates.

Theorem 4.1 does give a precise useful finite-generator comparison: f is a T(i)-homology equivalence for all i<=n if and only if P_{Sigma V}(Sigma^k f_(p)) is an equivalence for k sufficiently large, for any finite type-(n+1) pointed complex V. Section 5.4, especially Proposition 5.11, keeps an explicit nilcompletion-injectivity hypothesis in its further extension beyond infinite-loop spaces. No zero-finite-part separator is claimed from this paper.
