# A mod-four cup obstruction through the fourteen-skeleton

Status: proposed exact strengthening of the finite part of D.2.43;
the cone argument is complete.  The Stiefel-manifold product input
has been independently supplied by `simple_higher` and is recorded
here explicitly for audit.  No conclusion about the full target is
drawn.

Work two-locally.  Let `M_q=M(Z/q,7)` and

`K=(M_4 vee S8_r) union_(i eta7+2r) e9`.

The Moore comparison `m0:M_2 -> K` has bottom degree two and top
degree one.  Let `l4:S14 -> M_2` be the element fixed in D.2.41,
so `t=m0 l4=partial_K(2c)` up to sign.  D.2.43 proves that t has
exact order four in K.

## 1. The exact product in the cone of t

Choose `a in H7(K;Z/4)` whose value on the bottom cellular
generator is one.  Let `b=red_4 beta_4(a) in H8(K;Z/4)`, where
`beta_4:H7(-;Z/4)->H8(-;Z)` is the integral coefficient Bockstein.
On the Moore subcomplex, b evaluates as one on the eight-cell.
The other eight-cell r does not enter this choice: b is fixed
functorially by a, rather than by an arbitrary splitting of H8.

The cone `C_t=K union_t e15` has unique extensions of a and b.
Its degree-fifteen mod-four cohomology is generated by the top cell.
The actual cone map

`C(l4) -> C(t)`

is m0 on the old Moore complex and has degree one on the top cell.
Morisugi--Mukai, *Lifting to mod 2 Moore spaces*, printed page 523,
immediately before Lemma 2.7, identifies

`C(l4)=V_(9,2)`.

Primary PDF, directly reopened in this audit:
<https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jmath1948/52/3/52_3_515/_pdf>.

This Stiefel manifold is the unit tangent sphere bundle of S8,
an oriented closed fifteen-manifold.  Its mod-two cohomology in
degrees seven and eight has generators a2,b2 with

`<a2 cup b2,[V_(9,2)]_2>=1`.

This also follows directly from Poincare duality, since each of
those two groups has dimension one.  Under the cone map, a pulls
back to the coefficient inclusion of a2 in Z/4, and b reduces
modulo two to b2.  The coefficient inclusion `i:F2->Z/4` satisfies
`i(x) cup y=i(x cup red_2(y))`.  Hence

`<a cup b,[C_t]_15>=2 in Z/4`.                         (1)

The orientation or the sign of l4 does not change the value two.
This argument uses actual mapping cones.  It requires neither an
unproved converse from a Hopf invariant nor a choice of an unstable
addition formula for l4.

## 2. Survival in every target of dimension at most fourteen

Let `j:K -> P` be a relative CW inclusion with all new cells of
dimension at least ten and with `dim P<=14`.  The same statement
holds for a homotopy-equivalent finite model and its specified
map from K.  Restriction induces isomorphisms in degrees seven
and eight with Z/4 coefficients, so a and b have unique extensions
`a_P,b_P`, and naturality gives `b_P=red_4 beta_4(a_P)`.

If `j t` were null, its actual nullhomotopy would extend j to

`C_t -> P`.

But `H15(P;Z/4)=0`, so `a_P cup b_P=0`.  Pullback would imply
`a cup b=0` on C_t, contradicting (1).  Thus

`j t != 0 in pi14(P)`.                               (2)

In particular the actual class of D.2.43 survives nontrivially
through all twelve-, thirteen-, and fourteen-cell attachments of
the target.  Its order there may decrease from four to two; the
argument proves nonvanishing, not preservation of exact order.

The free nine-sphere and the ten-/eleven-cells in the chosen target
model cause no issue: they also leave H7 and H8 unchanged.  Thus
the same conclusion applies to the actual fourteen-skeleton
receiving K in the counit construction of D.2.43.

## 3. The exact remaining scope

The actual fifteen-skeleton can carry a nonzero degree-fifteen
product `a cup red_4 beta_4(a)`.  Consequently the preceding proof
does not decide whether the class dies there or in the full target.
Together with the established fifteen-skeleton comparison, it
isolates fifteen-cell attaching maps as the only possible source
of complete nullity of this particular class.

The individual twelve-cell composites can still affect its double
and its exact order.  They need not be evaluated to prove (2).
No claim about arbitrary fourth-suspension extensions follows.
