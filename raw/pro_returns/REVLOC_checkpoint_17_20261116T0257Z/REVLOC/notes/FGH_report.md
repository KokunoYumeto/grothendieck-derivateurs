## Adversarial check of the root accessibility proof

The proof in sections/B.tex was read in full after it was written. No fatal defect was found in B.1--B.4. The model-independence argument uses actual cellular homotopy inverses and closes countably often under maps and homotopy tracks; compact subsets of a CW complex lie in finite subcomplexes. This produces small homotopy-equivalent subcomplexes at the countable union, not merely weak equivalences of unrelated fragments.

The important cofiber compatibility is exact. For X a CW subcomplex of Y and C=Y/X, a C-subcomplex is lifted by its unique noncollapsed cells. Any newly required boundary cell outside X maps back into the original C-subcomplex, because that subcomplex is closed under attaching boundaries. Boundary cells inside X collapse. Thus a chosen small C' can be lifted to a small Y' with X'=Y' intersect X and Y'/X'=C', not merely a comparison to C'. Alternating enlargements in the two designated A-vertices and restoring these equalities gives a small union triple; cofinal sequences of A-subcomplexes give membership at the two vertices, and cofiber two-out-of-three gives the third.

The cellular bar construction also admits the necessary small coherent subdiagram: select the subcategory generated by the fewer-than-kappa arrows present in the chosen cells, then alternate closing under structure maps and enlarging each object into a small A-subcomplex. The regular uncountable bound absorbs the object set, all finite strings, and countably many rounds. This supplies a subcomplex of the bar realization. Applying realization of Sing to strictify cellularity is safe only because model independence has already been proved; Sing of a countable CW complex need not be countable.

An empty-source edge case was sent to the root: if the cofiber Y_+ for an empty-source map is in the bounded class and Y is nonempty, that class contains S0 as a retract and therefore all pointed spaces by pointed-homotopy-colimit closure. Its localizer is total, generated by the empty-to-point map. The remaining non-total case has nonempty source and permits the fixed source zero-cell used in B.2.

The generator set must consist of all small arrows with A-cofibers. Replacing it by only maps A to a point gives an aspherical-generation/nullification construction and does not establish the claimed localizer. This was emphasized to the root before the proof was finalized.

## Completed spheres and full stable part (G.5)

The smallest reversible closure of `BZ/2 -> *` and `BZ[1/2] -> *` is accessible by B.4, non-total, and has the entire stable homotopy category as its stable part. This was appended as G.5 and independently reviewed by the root.

The crucial upper bound is the completed-sphere null class. The integral James--Hopf map has a fibre F receiving a map from S^n which becomes an equivalence at 2, by the already checked EHP theorem. For n>=2 all spaces in question are simply connected with finitely generated homotopy groups. May--Ponto Theorem 11.2.6, including its simply connected-base clause, and Proposition 11.2.4 therefore give the actual completed EHP fibre sequence. This verifies desuspension closure of completed-sphere nullity without assuming that completion preserves arbitrary fibres. Both generators are null: Miller for BZ/2, and mod-2 acyclicity plus mod-2 locality of completed spheres for BZ[1/2]. The nonzero group [RP2,S2hat]=Z/2 excludes RP2, so the resulting localizer is non-total. Its stable part contains S/2 by Kahn--Priddy and S[1/2] by the circle telescope. The cofiber of S->S[1/2] is a filtered homotopy colimit of S/2^r, so these two spectra generate S and then all spectra.

The word “proper” is deliberately not used for non-totality in this claim: properness elsewhere in the manuscript is a separate technical property.

## Finite target locality, including unlooping (C.8--C.9)

C.8 was added to the root's C.tex with explicit authorization. It proves that every A_rev-space has contractible mapping space into the 2-localization and the 2-completion of every simply connected finite complex K; the loop of either target is then local by cofiber rotation. The written C.8 statement highlights the completed target. The proof applies Strom's published Theorem 10 to the strong resolving class of targets null for a fixed X, not to the ordinary resolving class of all local objects. Finite wedge sums of double suspensions of K have finite cellular cone length after localization, and Strom Proposition 13 removes the double suspension. Rational acyclicity and arithmetic fracture transfer nullity to Khat. A separate James-word retraction settles completed connected finite H-spaces directly.

Exact Strom numbering matters. In the published Fundamenta Mathematicae 178 (2003), 97--108 version, Theorem 8 distinguishes the ordinary and strong resolving classes; Corollary 9 uses the strong class (the overbar is lost in plain-text extraction); Theorem 10 and Proposition 13 are the two inputs just used. The arXiv version's numbering is different. The printed page 103 was visually inspected to verify the overbars. The primary published PDF is https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/88494.

C.9 now removes the unlooped gap for every simply connected finite K, proving locality of both K_(2) and Khat. Its proof has three stages.

1. For Z=Khat and Y a finite wedge of suspended smash powers of Z, form the corresponding finite complex F from K. The map F->Y is a mod-2 homology equivalence, so Yhat=Fhat. The space Y is simply connected and 2-local. May--Ponto Theorem 13.1.4 gives the arithmetic square for Y without any finite-generation hypothesis. Since A_rev-spaces are rationally acyclic and null for Fhat by C.8, they are null for Y. Therefore Omega Y is local. The same assertion for Z=K_(2) follows directly from localized smash, wedge and suspension comparisons.

2. James splitting writes Sigma (OmegaSigma)^r Z as an infinite wedge of Sigma Z^smash(m), with finite multiplicity at each weight m. The multiplicity recurrence is written out in C.9. The one suspension can be moved among smash factors, so a splitting after one suspension suffices for the induction. Each summand has connectivity at least 2m. Thus the infinite wedge is the homotopy inverse limit of its finite weight truncations, and every positive OmegaSigma iterate of Z is a homotopy limit of the looped targets from stage 1. All these iterates are local. No compatibility of the chosen James splittings with cosimplicial structure maps is required, because locality is a property of the individual objects.

3. The ordinary derived OmegaSigma resolution converges on every simply connected space Z, including already completed Z. Its terms are (OmegaSigma)^(q+1)Z. Since every term is local and local objects are closed under homotopy limits, Z is local. This uses neither commutation of suspension with completion nor commutation of completion with totalization.

The exact primary references for stage 3 are Bousfield, “Cosimplicial resolutions and homotopy spectral sequences in model categories”, Geometry & Topology 7 (2003), 1001--1053, Sections 7.9 (p.1033) and 9.9 (p.1039), https://www.sas.rochester.edu/mth/sites/doug-ravenel/otherpapers/AKB-Cosimp.pdf; and Blomquist--Harper, arXiv:1612.08622v3, Theorem 1.8(b), https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.08622. Both were opened at the exact statement. James splitting and equivalence were checked in Hatcher Proposition 4I.2 (p.468) and Theorem 4J.1 (p.471). May--Ponto https://math.uchicago.edu/~may/PAPERS/116.pdf explicitly states at printed pp.243--244 that the fracture theorem for nilpotent spaces requires no finite-generation assumptions.

## Rational targets and the full sphere localizer (G.6)

G.6 proves W_S=What_2 intersect W_Q and Nul=Nhat_2 intersect rational acyclic spaces. The rational implication was not assumed from the start.

For each odd n>=3, take the inverse tower of Omega S^n_(2) under its H-space squaring map. This map induces exactly multiplication by two on every homotopy group. The only non-torsion homotopy group is Z_(2) in degree n-1; all others are finite 2-groups. The latter towers have zero limit and lim1. The former has limit zero and lim1=Z_2/Z_(2), with the explicit proof by the 2-adic sum map included in G.6. Hence the tower's homotopy limit is K(Z_2/Z_(2),n-2). There is no extra component because pi1 of every looped sphere is zero. This target is local. The quotient is a nonzero rational vector space, so K(Q,n-2) is a retract; arbitrary odd n and repeated looping give K(Q,d) for every d>=0. Thus W_S maps are rational cohomology equivalences, hence rational homology equivalences by linear duality over Q, with no finite-dimensionality assumption. The arithmetic square of each sphere then gives the stated equalities of null classes and localizers.

The finite-generation and odd-sphere rational facts were verified in May--Ponto Theorem 4.5.2 and Corollary 6.7.3. The arithmetic square again uses Theorem 13.1.4. All these source dependencies are included in FGH.bib and FGH.csv.

## Reflection audit and compact models (G.7 proved and audited)

An earlier attempted direct application of Przezdziecki's “Homotopical localizations at a space” Theorem 19 does not apply to 2-local spheres: the theorem assumes a target is a homotopy retract of a compactly topologized object. Such an object's homotopy groups are compact Hausdorff groups; in abelian degrees they are algebraically compact, as is every algebraic direct summand. Z_(2) is not algebraically compact: choose a 2-adic number a outside Z_(2), with binary digits a_i, and consider x_i=2x_(i+1)+a_i. Every finite subsystem has an integral solution, but a global solution in Z_(2) would force x_0=a. Thus the hypothesis cannot be silently inserted for a 2-local sphere. The opened paper also contains, after Definition 10, an assertion identifying the homotopy category of arrows with arrows in the homotopy category; taken literally in its usual meaning that is false. For f=(S0->*) and g=(*->S1), the derived arrow mapping space has pi0=Z but the ordinary arrow category has a unique commuting square. No result in F12/G/H14 relies on that assertion.

The new route uses the completed spheres instead, together with G.6. I independently checked the root's proposed compactness lemma and supplied a direct compact simplicial Kan model of completed spheres, avoiding reliance on that arrow-category assertion.

For a finite simplicial sphere K, let R be the reduced free F2-vector-space functor and consider the standard BK cosimplicial resolution X^q=R^(q+1)K. Each simplicial level of every X^q is finite. Every codegeneracy is linear, and every coface d^i with i>=1 is linear. A matching tuple (a_0,...,a_(q-1)) has a preimage constructed successively by c <- c+d^(j+1)(a_j-sigma_j c). The cosimplicial identities show that this corrects coordinate j without disturbing earlier coordinates. Thus each matching map is a degreewise split surjection of simplicial F2-vector spaces, hence a Kan fibration. The resolution is Reedy fibrant. Tot is right Quillen for the Reedy structure (as recalled immediately before Bousfield Proposition 8.1). Its m-simplices form a closed subset of the countable product over q of finite sets Hom(Delta[q]xDelta[m],X^q). Therefore Tot is a compact metrizable simplicial Kan model of Khat, with continuous operators. The loop targets Hom_*(S^m,Tot) retain compact metrizability in every degree.

For completeness, the compactness audit is as follows. Given a countable pointed simplicial subset K of X and a compact metrizable simplicial target Z, Hom_*(K,Z) is compact metrizable. The image C_K of Hom_*(X,Z) is closed and is the intersection of the images from K plus finite sets of simplices. This follows from compactness of the product of all target coordinates. For each member of a countable basis whose closure misses C_K, finitely many additional simplex constraints already exclude that closure. A countable enlargement K' therefore ensures that every map K'->Z restricts to a globally extendible map on K. Do this simultaneously for all loop targets Omega^m Z_n, a countable family, and iterate countably often. For a map from the union K_infty to one target, the closed sets of global extensions of its restrictions to K_i are nested and nonempty, so compactness gives a global extension of the entire map. When X is null, every such map is nullhomotopic. The cases m=0 and m>0 detect pi0 and all higher homotopy groups of the mapping space, proving K_infty null. This gives countable null subcomplexes. To translate to the CW form of B.2, use B.1 model independence after passing to |Sing X|; do not assert that Sing of a countable CW complex is countable.
