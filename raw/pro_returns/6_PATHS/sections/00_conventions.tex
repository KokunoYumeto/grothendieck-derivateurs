We work relative to Grothendieck universes $\mathbb U\in\mathbb V$. Unless explicitly enlarged, $\Cat$ denotes $\mathbb U$-small categories, $BX=|NX|$, and $W_\infty$ denotes the functors whose nerves are weak homotopy equivalences. We write $\Hot=\Cat[W_\infty^{-1}]$. The category $\Cat\,\mathrm{hot}$ has the same objects as $\Cat$ and functors modulo finite zigzags of natural transformations. An isomorphism in this category is stronger than a weak homotopy equivalence of nerves. No finiteness bound on the length of a zigzag may depend on an object of its source.

For a functor $u:A\to B$, the category $A/b$ consists of pairs $(a,u(a)\to b)$ and $b\backslash A$ of pairs $(a,b\to u(a))$. We call $u$ final if $b\backslash A$ is nonempty and connected for every $b$. We call it homotopy final if these categories are weakly contractible. A sieve is closed under sources; a cosieve is closed under targets. Coefficients in the manuscript are contravariant; when we write $M^A$ we explicitly use covariant functors from $A$.

A fundamental localizer is a class $W$ of functors satisfying weak saturation (identities, two-out-of-three and retracts), the terminal-object axiom $X\to e\in W$ when $X$ has a terminal object, and the relative Theorem A axiom: for functors $A\xrightarrow u B\xrightarrow v C$, if $A/c\to B/c$ lies in $W$ for every $c\in C$, then $u\in W$. A category is $W$-aspherical when its map to $e$ belongs to $W$. This convention is used only where a localizer occurs; a relative category $(M,\Sigma)$ initially requires just a wide subcategory $\Sigma\subset M$.

We distinguish equality, isomorphism in $\Cat$, equivalence of categories, isomorphism in $\Cat\,\mathrm{hot}$, isomorphism in $\Hot$, and a weak equivalence of a specified functor. A mapping space means a space in the $\infty$-categorical localization $L(M,\Sigma)$, with specified objects.
