# PATHS: Grothendieck's path categories against the simplicial localization, and which relative categories carry a homotopy theory of diagrams

Package 6 of 7 on Alexander Grothendieck, *Les Dérivateurs* (1990–91). File prefix for your deliverables: `PATHS`.

## What is to be settled

Chapters V, VII and IX build, for a category `M` with a wide subcategory `Σ`, categories of paths. A *type* is a finite oriented interval `I_τ`, `τ` a word in `+` and `−`; the types form a strict monoidal category `Ty` (maps preserve endpoints, the positional order and the direction of edges; VII ms. 27–57). A path of type `τ` in `M` is a functor `I_τ → M` whose backward edges lie in `Σ`. The total category `Ch_Σ(M; a, b)` has as objects the paths from `a` to `b` of all types, and as morphisms a map of types together with a natural transformation whose components at the two endpoints are identities; the internal components are **arbitrary** arrows of `M` (VII ms. 58–70). He proves `π0 Ch_Σ(M; a, b) = Hom_{M[Σ^{-1}]}(a, b)` (ms. 68–70), with composition induced by concatenation, and wants the homotopy type of `Ch_Σ(M; a, b)` to be the "external Hom", a mapping space with associative composition, pointed and linear refinements, and a comparison with Quillen's constructions (VII §§10–13; IX ms. 1–25). For `Σ` = all arrows the companion proves that the endpoint fibres of `Ch(X) → X × X` are the path spaces of `BX`, compatibly with composition (VII-c blocks at ms. 166–169). For general `Σ` nothing is proved, and the audit rows IX-037, IX-040, IX-041, IX-044, IX-049, IX-052, IX-072, IX-080, IX-088, VIIc-A046, V-I066 are left conditional on a comparison "with the modern mapping space" that nobody has written.

Second, in IX ms. 22–23 he proposes tests (a), (b), (c) for a pair `(M, W)` to "se prêter à une théorie homotopique", the crucial one being that `Y ↦ Hom_{M^Y[W_Y^{-1}]}(a_Y, b_Y)` send weak equivalences of `Y` to bijections. The companion shows the tests are too weak (a finite example, IX block at ms. 23) and that the naive prederivator `Y ↦ M^Y[W_Y^{-1}]` can fail to be homotopy invariant in `Y` (VII-c block at ms. 134). The correct criterion is not in the manuscript as audited so far, and as far as we know not in the literature in intrinsic form.

## Leads (unverified; prove or refute each)

- **Lead 1.** The Dwyer–Kan hammock localization is computed by the same types with vertical arrows **in `Σ`**. Write `Ch_Σ^Σ(M; a, b) ⊂ Ch_Σ(M; a, b)` for the wide subcategory with vertical components in `Σ`. The comparison to study is the inclusion `J`. When `Σ` has two-out-of-three, components adjacent to an endpoint through a backward edge are forced into `Σ`, but components further inside are not; so `J` is not an isomorphism and its being a weak equivalence is a real question.
- **Lead 2.** For `Σ` = isomorphisms, `Ch_Σ(M; a, b)` is homotopy discrete with `π0 = Hom_M(a, b)` (factorization categories of a fixed arrow have an initial object). For `Σ` = all arrows `J` is the identity.
- **Lead 3.** Call `(M, W)` *rectifiable at `A`* when the canonical functor `M^A[W_A^{-1}] → Ho Fun(N(A), L(M, W))` is an equivalence, `L` the ∞-categorical (or simplicial) localization. Model categories are rectifiable at every `A` that is small relative to the model category (the unbounded form is corrected in the section after the second review, below); categories with cofibrations or fibrations at every finite direct `A` at least. Grothendieck's test (b) is a consequence of rectifiability and the finite counterexample fails rectifiability.
