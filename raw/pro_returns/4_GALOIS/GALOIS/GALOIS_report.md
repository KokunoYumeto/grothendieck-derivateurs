## 2. Conventions that control the conclusions

The paper uses **presheaf variance**. An enhanced coefficient theory has the convention \(\mathbb D(A)=h\operatorname{Fun}(A^{\mathrm{op}},\mathcal C)\). All-small-shapes derivators satisfy Der1–4 in the stated universes; coherent-arrow strongness, sometimes called Der5, is not silently assumed. Sizes of coefficient categories, diagram shapes, mapping spaces, generators, and the groups in the cardinal arguments are specified. Categories of presentable coefficient categories require larger universes than their individual objects.

Five classes are kept separate: constant-coefficient equivalences, cohomological equivalences, homological equivalences, 1-complete maps, and 2-complete maps. Here 1-completeness means full faithfulness of the specified restriction on locally constant diagrams, and 2-completeness means its equivalence. An abstract equivalence between two coefficient categories is a different statement. In particular, an abstract Morita equivalence does not establish equivalence of the monads connected by a given map of spaces.

Grothendieck’s weak, absolute Loc(3) is distinguished from relative Loc(3 bis), and literal Loc(6a), (6b), and (6c) are tested separately. A full subcategory of the arrow category being accessible, the corresponding localizer being generated by a set, and its being generated by its aspherical objects are three different conditions. Likewise, structural Grothendieck fibrations, universally equivalence-preserving maps, model fibrations, and sharp maps are not identified without a proof.

For enhanced coefficients, cotensors recover the mapping-space information needed to test a **specified restriction functor** from its homotopy-category full faithfulness (C.1). This is compatible with leaving reconstruction from an unenhanced ordinary category and ordinary fiber functors open (F.8). The latter data do not themselves supply the coherent cotensors, adjunction, and higher transformations used in the former argument.

## 4. Corrections to the proposed leads and source material

The first correction is structural: the 1-complete class need not satisfy two-out-of-three, already for sets. It therefore need not be a fundamental localizer. Its relative Theorem A nevertheless holds for every derivator in the paper’s scope (B.3). Failure of two-out-of-three must not be attributed to a nonexistent general descent obstruction.

For a map to a point, constant-coefficient equivalence and 1-completeness agree directly from the definitions, without enhancement. Thus an integrally acyclic group gives \(BA\to *\) in the 1-complete class. That projection cannot be the claimed separator in the prompt’s CATHOT afterword. The reverse map \(*\to BA\) separates constant equivalence from 1-completeness when the coefficient theory detects the nontrivial group (C.8). The supplied CATHOT B.6 mitosis construction itself was checked and retained; its validity is separate from this directional error.

The blanket fiber-detection claim in Lead 3 is false. The finite rational composition example and the finite coherent 2-complete example both survive verification. Strengthening individual comparison maps to 2-complete maps does not make a weak-fibration definition tested only over aspherical bases composable. A total locally constant equivalence between genuine HOT-fibrations need not induce such an equivalence on every fiber, even over a completely Set-aspherical base. A separate section into \(\operatorname{Groth}(BC_2\to *)\) over \([1]\) refutes total-to-fiber detection for arbitrary universally 1-complete-preserving maps (D.2–D.6).

The positive replacement uses the Thomason model fibrations of an accessible fundamental localizer. A weak equivalence between these fibrations is a weak equivalence between fibrant objects in a slice, and pullback to a point is right Quillen. This proves fiber detection without assuming right properness of the ambient model category. It does not identify all model fibrations with sharp maps. The repaired weak-fibration argument also retains its asymmetric conclusion: a 1-complete input initially gives a constant-coefficient equivalence upstairs. Full local-coefficient direct-image control gives the composable cohomological class; its identification with 1-completeness uses the enhanced criterion of C.2.

Reverse Mayer–Vietoris requires separate correction. Both Loc(6b) and Loc(6c) fail for the locally constant class of every nontrivial derivator. They hold for the terminal theory. For the intermediate 1-complete class, Loc(6b) fails for every nontrivial theory, whereas Loc(6c) holds exactly in the thin case, where the coefficient category is a complete preorder and the class is surjectivity on components. Constant spectrum-homology classes instead satisfy all three implications by exact triangles (B.6).

The Part D audit also corrects individual manuscript and companion assertions. Bundle classification does not supply unique isomorphisms. Taking the discrete regular object \(E=G\) in the quotient construction makes the displayed fiber inclusion the identity. The antipodal-sphere/reflected-circle quotient retains a rational class in degree three. The six-object orbit compositions multiply endpoint signs. A proper cosieve can have an empty-to-point comma transition. Compatible internal Homs do not upgrade a constant equivalence to full faithfulness: the claimed implication needs cohomological equivalence and the actual monoidal unit.

The transcription corrections are kept local. On I ms. 93, the intended nonisomorphism must target full cohomology, rather than the incorrectly repeated invariant part. On XVI ms. 78, the printed inclusions \(X_i\subset Y_i\) and \(X_j\subset Y_j\) must read \(X_i\subset X_j\) and \(Y_i\subset Y_j\). “Cofibrations” in the Loc(7F) paragraph is a typo in a statement about fibrant objects. The qualification “que je sache!” at I ms. 122 is treated as a question, not a proof. Toën’s prose immediately after Definition 3.1.1 reverses an implication: Lemma 3.3.2 and its proof give P-equivalence implying O-equivalence, not the converse.

Two recent-source qualifications matter. Di Vittorio’s checked version proves HDer1–4 for the stated construction but leaves HDer5 as Conjecture 3.2.23; the relevant shapes include enriched shapes, not merely ordinary categories. Kaledin’s shape category includes posets of finite chain dimension, which need not be finite posets. Neither source is used to manufacture an unspecified functor from arbitrary derivators to higher linear coefficient categories.

## 5. General descent, accessibility, and the cardinal argument

**Relative Theorem A is settled for arbitrary Der1–4 derivators (B.3).** The proof constructs partial-local projectors as derivator morphisms. In the universal simplicial-presheaf model, diagrams inverting any specified set of arrows are closed under pointwise homotopy colimits. Cisinski’s universal property therefore gives the needed left projector; dualization gives the right projector. For an opfibration \(T\to S\), left Kan extension from a fiber preserves vertical local constancy. In presheaf variance its pointwise formula is the coproduct over arrows from the image of the tested object to the chosen base object, evaluated on cocartesian transports. Passing to right adjoints makes the right projector commute with fiber restriction.

For maps of opfibrations that preserve cocartesian arrows, the comma-category transport adjunction supplies the required right-Kan base change. The unit and counit are isomorphisms on every fiber and hence globally by Der2. The unit alone gives the 1-complete version. The Grothendieck replacement of a natural transformation satisfies the transport hypothesis. This closes the earlier general-descent gap without a model presentation, stable compact generation, or strongness assumption on the target derivator. It also settles the general gluing assertion D.11 and the direct excision and filtered-colimit consequences in B.5.

**Accessibility is settled for all presentable infinity-category coefficients at the constant and locally constant levels (B.4).** Choose a suitable uncountable regular cardinal \(\kappa\). The functor \(X\mapsto\operatorname{Fun}(X,\mathcal C)\), with left Kan extensions as morphisms, is the copower functor into the appropriate presentable category \(\operatorname{Pr}^{L}_{\kappa}\). Inverse images of the equivalence-arrow subcategory are accessible. Pulling this description back along classifying space gives the ordinary full arrow subcategory of small categories; the proof checks the passage from infinity-categorical to ordinary filtered indexing. A small generating family and filtered closure then give localizer set generation. The constant class is handled using a small family of colimit generators of \(\mathcal C\). This proof does not establish accessibility of full-faithfulness arrows for every presentable coefficient category.

The literal Loc(7) analysis is also more precise than a general model-structure appeal. Each of Loc(7a), (7b), and (7c), for a nontrivial fundamental localizer, forces the minimal localizer, without an accessibility hypothesis (Cisinski, 2006, Theorem 6.4.22). For an accessible fundamental localizer, Loc(7F) is equivalent to properness. The implication from the manuscript’s universal factorization class to generation by aspherical objects is proved using a pullback lemma and graph factorization. For nonzero rings, the locally constant class satisfies Loc(7F) exactly when no rational prime is a unit, precisely the case where it equals ordinary weak equivalence. Nonzero constant Eilenberg–Mac Lane homology localizers are nonproper. Intermediate 1-complete assertions are recorded separately; their lack of two-out-of-three prevents an automatic Brown-category conclusion.

**B.10–B.12 resolve the cardinal and ind-representability questions.** Every small group embeds in an acyclic simple group of a controlled larger infinite cardinality. The HNN construction checks that the relevant cyclically reduced words have infinite order even when an original generator has finite order. The acyclic-envelope proof uses conjugate axis and diagonal maps, field Künneth, and integral universal coefficients. Alternating the two constructions preserves embeddings and the cardinal bound.

The resulting \(\mathrm{KT}_{\mu}\) construction associates to a connected small CW complex \(Y\) an acyclic map \(BG\to Y\), surjective on fundamental groups with perfect kernel, such that every homomorphism from \(G\) to a group of cardinality at most \(\mu\) kills that kernel. The simplex choices are made simultaneously for all subcomplexes. The induction proves both asphericity and injectivity of subcomplex fundamental groups; the latter is indispensable for the graph-of-spaces attachments. The relative homology comparison works with every local system on the target, since its restriction to a simplex is constant. The kernel and cardinal bounds are computed explicitly. Independent review checked the infinite-stage argument and the additional subcomplex-injectivity step.

B.12 applies this to the CATHOT E17 question. A small filtered ind-object of small categories whose associated functor is invariant under ordinary weak equivalence sees **at most one-types**: the specified unit to its stagewise fundamental-groupoid reflector is an isomorphism in the stated ind-category, with the original index retained. The proof uses localization by left Kan extension, a uniform small-target bound, actual isomorphisms of functor categories including noninvertible transformations, and the ind-Hom formula. It does not exchange components with an infinite product or assume stagewise homotopy invariance. The checked CATHOT C.1–C.4 reflector theorem is cited rather than reproved.

## 6. Coefficient calculations, towers, and reconstruction

Part C computes the five requested classes for sets, groupoids, truncated spaces, spaces, unbounded derived modules over every associative unital ring, spectra, and localized spectra. For derived modules, the three distinct tests are ordinary coefficient homology; coefficient-acyclic homotopy fibers; and exact components and fundamental groups together with coefficient-homology equivalence of universal covers. Noncommutative rings are treated by integral chain models and scalar extension, without assuming a symmetric monoidal module category. The zero ring is the terminal case. For general localized spectra the exact test uses based-loop generalized homology; no unjustified replacement by universal-cover homology is made. The Morava K-theory examples establish strictness by explicit sphere degree maps. The rational class is the known fundamental-group-sensitive rational equivalence, and its implication to Toën schematization is strict (C.7–C.14).

E.2 constructs an ordinary derivator of presentable R-linear stable infinity-categories and proves that its constant class is the locally constant class for R-modules. Its actual next locally constant class is computed in E.8: components must match and the induced based-loop maps must be locally constant equivalences for R-modules. For rational coefficients this fixes the first two homotopy groups exactly and all higher groups rationally. The maps from \(K(\mathbf Z/p,2)\) and \(K(\mathbf Z,2)\) to a point are detected at this next stage by scalar actions; \(K(\mathbf Z/p,3)\) is not. The proof checks free linear cocompletion, the specified monad comparison, and all components, including nontrivial fundamental-group action.

E.10 constructs the **entire ordinary rational coefficient tower**. Its stage \(r\geq1\) fixes homotopy groups through degree \(r\) exactly and higher groups rationally. Each step is strict, witnessed by \(K(\mathbf Z/p,r+1)\), and the intersection is ordinary weak equivalence. The construction iterates presentable categories of localized spaces. For a product-stable localization with class contained in component equivalences, the connected evaluation monad is \(M\mapsto L(\Omega X\times M)\). Its value at the point detects the loop comparison; product stability gives sufficiency. Distinct initial and terminal objects force bijectivity on components. B.3, B.4, and E.7 justify iteration. This settles existence of an ordinary coefficient tower while leaving its identification with specified higher rational linear categories open.

The reconstruction results distinguish data precisely. All set-valued fibers recover the fundamental groupoid; finite-set Galois data give the appropriate profinite object. Enhanced linear local systems with enhanced evaluation recover the based-loop E1 algebra through its free-evaluation monad. The infinity-topos \(\operatorname{Fun}(X,\mathcal S)\) recovers \(X\) by shape, and its homotopy category together with the specified constant-diagram functor recovers its homotopy type by Yoneda. Hoyois’s pro-groupoid reconstruction retains its local-connectedness, split-local-system, and covering hypotheses. Kaledin’s theorem concerns an enhanced shape-indexed system. None of these assertions proves reconstruction from arbitrary bare ordinary local-system data (F.1–F.8).

Part G records the allowable functoriality: equivalences preserve the classes, products give intersections, and retractions give the stated comparisons. An arbitrary morphism of derivators need not induce a localizer inclusion. Nonterminal coefficients detect components at the locally constant level; the thin and nonthin cases differ at the other levels. Thus realization functorial in inclusions of accessible localizers does not automatically become functorial on all derivator morphisms.

## 8. Exact remaining open items

1. **B.13 — Accessibility beyond the established scope.** For arbitrary bare Der1–4 derivators, set generation and literal arrow-category accessibility of the locally constant class, and the corresponding accessibility questions for the constant class, remain unresolved here. B.4 answers them for all presentable enhanced coefficients. Accessibility of the 1-complete class also remains unproved for general presentable coefficients outside the explicit concrete families of Part C: the equivalence-arrow argument does not automatically apply to full-faithfulness arrows. The missing ingredient is a uniform small testing family or bound; no counterexample is asserted.

2. **B.14 — Remaining intermediate fibration and properness questions.** For a bare derivator, literal Loc(7a) for the 1-complete class and the universal-pullback-preserving factorization in Loc(7F) remain unproved. The available argument produces cohomological control, and the upgrade uses the enhanced equality of C.2. Literal Loc(7c) is already proved without enhancement. For general localized spectra, properness of the locally constant class is reduced to equality with its aspherically generated class, but is not evaluated for every spectrum; the first explicitly unevaluated test is K(1) at a fixed prime.

3. **E.11 — Arbitrary functorial raising and higher linear realizations.** Realization is proved for accessible locally constant classes, hence for all presentable enhanced coefficients, and the ordinary rational tower is constructed. A functorial raising operation on arbitrary bare derivators and arbitrary morphisms is not established. Identification of the third and subsequent rational stages with specified rational linear 2-categories and higher coefficient categories remains unconstructed. The category of presentable linear categories cannot simply be iterated within the original universe.

4. **F.8 — Reconstruction from bare ordinary data.** The precise untreated linear test asks whether pointed simply connected finite CW complexes with equivalent ordinary rational local-system categories, compatible with their ordinary evaluations, must have equivalent rational based-loop E1 algebras. No proof or counterexample is supplied for that implication. The space-valued ordinary category without its specified constant-diagram functor is likewise not covered by the proved shape/Yoneda reconstruction.

5. **G.6 — A coherent functorial higher coefficient assignment.** An iteratable higher coefficient or 2-derivator construction for arbitrary bare derivators still requires specified objects, morphisms, higher transformations, and diagram shapes. This overlaps E.11 and does not reintroduce a descent gap: relative Theorem A, general gluing, presentable accessibility at the stated levels, and the ordinary tower are proved. “Open” here records precisely the absence of that additional coherent construction in this package.
