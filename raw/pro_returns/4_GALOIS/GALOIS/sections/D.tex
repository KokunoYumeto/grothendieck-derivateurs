\begin{claim}{D.1}{Proved}{The two pullback tests are different}
Fix a class $W$ of functors between small categories. A \emph{universal $W$-preserving map} is a functor $p:E\to B$ such that, for every functor $v:T'\to T$ over $B$ belonging to $W$, the induced functor
\[
 p^*v:E\times_B T'\longrightarrow E\times_B T
\]
belongs to $W$. All pullbacks in this definition are strict pullbacks in $\Cat$. This class of maps is closed under composition and arbitrary strict base change. It differs in general from the class obtained by testing only $T',T$ with weakly contractible nerves.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
For composable $E\xrightarrow{p}B\xrightarrow{q}S$, the pullback of $v:T'\to T$ over $S$ first lies in $W$ after pullback by $q$, and then lies in $W$ after pullback by $p$. The canonical isomorphism of the iterated strict pullbacks with the pullbacks along $qp$ proves composition. A pullback of $p$ along $B'\to B$ acts on $\Cat/B'$ by the same strict pullback as $p$ after forgetting to $B$; this proves base-change stability. These arguments do not require two-out-of-three for $W$. The difference from the restricted test is demonstrated in D.4 below.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{D.2}{Proved}{What the local-coefficient repair actually proves}
Let $\D$ satisfy the derivator axioms of Part A on all small categories, with the manuscript's presheaf variance. Suppose $p:E\to B$ has the following property after every strict base change: for each $M\in\D(e)$, $p_*M_E$ is locally constant and its evaluation at $b$ is canonically $H^*_{\D}(E_b,M)$. If $v:T'\to T$ is a $1$-complete equivalence over $B$, then
\[
 w:E\times_BT'\longrightarrow E\times_BT
\]
is a constant-coefficient $\D$-equivalence. The analogous statement with left Kan extensions holds. This does not assert that $w$ is $1$-complete or $2$-complete.

If the direct-image hypothesis instead holds for every locally constant coefficient on every base-changed total category, then $v\in W^{\mathrm{coh}}_{\D}$ implies $w\in W^{\mathrm{coh}}_{\D}$. Such cohomological fibrations are closed under composition and base change; the dual assertion holds for homological fibrations. Under the enhancement hypotheses of C.2 these are universal $W^1_{\D}$-preserving fibrations.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Put $E_T=E\times_BT$, $E_{T'}=E\times_BT'$, and denote their projections by $p_T,p_{T'}$. Universality and pointwise detection identify the canonical mate
\[
 v^*(p_T)_*M_{E_T}\longrightarrow(p_{T'})_*M_{E_{T'}}
\]
with an isomorphism: at $t'\in T'$ both sides are the cohomology of the same strict fibre $E_{v(t')}$. The object $K=(p_T)_*M_{E_T}$ is locally constant. Full faithfulness of $v^*$ on local systems, applied to $\Hom(N_T,K)$ for every $N\in\D(e)$, and the two adjunctions to the point identify the canonical comparison
$H^*_{\D}(T,K)\to H^*_{\D}(T',v^*K)$ as an isomorphism by Yoneda. Composition of right Kan extensions identifies this map with the constant-coefficient cohomology comparison induced by $w$. Testing every $M$ proves the first assertion. The left-Kan proof uses $\Hom(K,N_T)$ instead.

For the strengthened assertion fix $L\in\LC_{\D}(E_T)$. The same mate identifies $v^*(p_T)_*L$ with $(p_{T'})_*w^*L$, and $(p_T)_*L$ is locally constant. The defining property of $W^{\mathrm{coh}}_{\D}$ applied to this coefficient proves the comparison for $L$, as required. For $E\xrightarrow{p}B\xrightarrow{q}S$, first $p_*L$, then $q_*p_*L$ is locally constant. The canonical composition isomorphism identifies the latter with $(qp)_*L$. Pasting the two universally invertible base-change mates gives the base-change mate for $qp$. This proves composition with all quantifiers on coefficients retained. A preliminary pullback proves base-change stability. Finally C.2 identifies $W^{\mathrm{coh}}_{\D}$ and $W^1_{\D}$ in the stated enhanced setting.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{D.3}{Refuted}{Composition for the manuscript's weak rational fibrations}
For $\D=D(\Q)$ there are finite categories and functors $E\xrightarrow{p}B\xrightarrow{q}[1]$ such that $p$ and $q$ are the universal weak fibrations of I~ms.~117, while $qp$ is not one. Thus I-b-B081-04 is false, as is the unqualified composition claim at I~ms.~118.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Write $G=C_2$ additively. The category $B$ has objects $a,b$, one identity at $a$, automorphism group $G$ at $b$, two arrows $a\to b$ labelled by $G$, and no arrows $b\to a$. Postcomposition adds labels. The functor $q$ sends $a$ to $0$, $b$ to $1$, and is the Grothendieck opfibration of $e\to BG$. Its strict fibres are $e$ and $BG$.

An opfibration has a left adjoint from its over-comma category to its strict fibre, given by cocartesian transport. For presheaf coefficients this computes the right Kan extension on the fibre. Its constant direct image is locally constant precisely when the transport functors are constant-coefficient equivalences, and the same computation applies after arbitrary base change. Here the only nonidentity transport is $e\to BG$. For an arbitrary unbounded complex $M$ of rational vector spaces, the trivial $\Q[G]$-module $\Q$ is projective, since the averaging idempotent $(1+\sigma)/2$ splits its augmentation. Hence both its derived invariants and derived coinvariants with trivial coefficient $M$ are canonically $M$. Therefore $q$ has the required universal weak property.

Define $L:B\to\Set$ by $L(a)=L(b)=G$ and let the arrow with label $g$ act by $z\mapsto g+z$. Let $E=\int_BL$ and $p:E\to B$ be the projection. There is a unique lift of a base arrow with any prescribed source, and also with any prescribed target. Thus $p$ is a covering, its transports are isomorphisms of discrete categories, and it satisfies the universal weak property even for spaces. The composite $qp$ has fibre $G_{\mathrm{disc}}$ at $0$ and the regular action groupoid $G\ltimes G$ at $1$. The latter has one arrow between every ordered pair of objects and is contractible. The transition induces
\[
 \Q\longrightarrow\Q^2,\qquad r\longmapsto(r,r)
\]
on degree-zero cohomology. It is not an isomorphism, so $(qp)_*\Q_E$ is not locally constant. The finite-category laws, all associative triples, and the component counts $2$ and $1$ are independently checked in \texttt{code/D\_verify\_finite.py} and its output.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{D.4}{Refuted}{The restricted $2$-complete fibration condition still fails composition}
Let $\D(A)=\Fun(A^{\op},\Set)$. Call $p:E\to B$ a restricted $2$-complete fibration if, for every $v:T'\to T$ over $B$ with $BT'$ and $BT$ weakly contractible, $E\times_BT'\to E\times_BT$ belongs to $W^{\mathrm{lc}}_{\D}=W_1$. There are finite $E\xrightarrow{p}B\xrightarrow{q}[1]$ for which both factors have this property and the composite does not. The first factor is a $\Hot$-fibration; the second is even a universal $W_1$-equivalence. Consequently this is a counterexample to the replacement ``$2$-complete throughout'' in Lead~3, including its composition assertion.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
We use distinct symbols $K,H,B,E$ for the four categories in the construction. Let $K$ be the poset of nonempty proper subsets of $\{0,1,2,3\}$. Its nerve is the barycentric subdivision of a tetrahedral boundary. Its objects, arrows and compositions are respectively those subsets, inclusions and composition of inclusions. Fix the maximal chain
\[
 \sigma=(\{0\}<\{0,1\}<\{0,1,2\}).
\]
For composable inclusions $u:x\to y$, $v:y\to z$, let $c(u,v)\in C_2$ equal $1$ precisely on $\sigma$ and $0$ otherwise. It is normalized: a pair containing an identity has value zero. It is a cocycle, since there is no chain of three strict inclusions, while the cocycle identity for a triple containing an identity cancels directly. Its evaluation on the mod-$2$ fundamental cycle is $1$.

Let $H$ have the objects of $K$ and arrows $(u,m)$ with $u\in K(x,y)$ and $m\in C_2$. Define
\[
 (v,n)(u,m)=(vu,m+n+c(u,v)),\qquad 1_x=(1_x,0).
\]
The normalized cocycle identity proves both unit laws and associativity. The projection $H\to K$ is a fibration and an opfibration: once a lift of an arrow is fixed, any required factorization is obtained by solving uniquely for one $C_2$-label. Its fibre is $BC_2$ and each transport acts identically on $C_2$.

We calculate $\Pi_1H$. Put $t_x=(1_x,1)$ and $h_{xy}=(x\leq y,0)$. In the groupoid completion the defining relations are
\[
 t_x^2=1,\quad t_yh_{xy}=h_{xy}t_x,\quad
 h_{yz}h_{xy}=h_{xz}t_x^{c(x,y,z)}.
\]
Collapse a spanning tree of the one-skeleton. All $t_x$ become a central element $t$ of order dividing two. Delete the interior of the triangle $\sigma$ from the triangulated sphere. The remaining disk has the same one-skeleton; its untwisted triangle relations kill the edge generators. The omitted triangle relation is then $t=1$. Thus $H$ is connected and simply connected. This calculation uses the full presentation, and not merely vanishing of its first homology.

Put $x_0=\{0\}$ and let $B$ be the Grothendieck construction of $e\xrightarrow{x_0}K$ over $[1]$. It has the objects of $K$ and a new object $a$, with $B(a,x)=K(x_0,x)$, no arrows $x\to a$, and the evident compositions. The projection $q:B\to[1]$ sends $a$ to $0$ and $K$ to $1$. There is a retraction $r:B\to K$, $r(a)=x_0$, and the arrow $a\to x_0$ defines $1_B\Rightarrow ir$, where $i:K\hookrightarrow B$. Set $E=B\times_KH$ and $p:E\to B$. The retraction and natural transformation lift to $E\to H$: the lift over $a\to x_0$ has label zero, and normalization of $c$ proves naturality. Thus $BE\simeq BH$, so $E$ is connected and simply connected. Its strict fibre over $0\in[1]$ is $BC_2$.

We verify the universal restricted tests. For a weakly contractible category $T\to B$, the pullback cocycle is a coboundary because $H^2(BT;C_2)=0$. Choose a normalized one-cochain $d$ with $\delta d=c|_T$. Relabelling an arrow by $m\mapsto m+d(u)$ gives an isomorphism between the pullback of $p$ and $T\times BC_2$. This isomorphism preserves the vertical $C_2$-loops. A map between two weakly contractible test bases therefore induces a bijection on components and an isomorphism on fundamental groups of the total categories, because it preserves their common vertical generator. This proves the exact proposed property for $p$. Moreover opfibration transport is an equivalence of groupoids; the fibre-to-comma adjunction and Quillen's Theorem~B, after every base change, show that $p$ is a $\Hot$-fibration \citep[\S1, Theorem B and its corollary]{Quillen1973}.

For any category $T\to[1]$, with no contractibility assumption, the projection $q_T:B\times_{[1]}T\to T$ induces an equivalence of fundamental groupoids. Its fibres are $e$ or $K$, and it has a section choosing $a$ or $x_0$. Each fibre is connected and simply connected. Consequently its fundamental groupoid has a unique path from the section object to any given fibre object. These paths are natural: factor an arrow of the Grothendieck construction into its cocartesian lift followed by a vertical arrow, and use uniqueness in the target fibre. They give a natural isomorphism from section followed by projection to the identity in the groupoid completion. Thus $q_T\in W_1$ for every $T$. Two-out-of-three now proves the restricted test for $q$.

Apply the test $\{0\}\to[1]$ to the composite. Its upstairs comparison is $BC_2\to E$. The target is simply connected, whereas the source has fundamental group $C_2$. Restriction sends a set to the trivial $C_2$-action. The two-point transitive action is absent from its essential image. Thus this comparison is $1$-complete but not $2$-complete, proving failure of composition. The checker verifies all $1{,}552$ associative triples in $H$ and $2{,}064$ in $E$; an independent finite presentation calculation eliminates all $73$ and $87$ generators, respectively. Its fibre presentation remains $\langle t\mid t^2=1\rangle$.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{D.5}{Refuted}{Total equivalence need not detect fibres after either blanket substitution}
The following two assertions are false. First, a $2$-complete equivalence between $\Hot$-fibrations over a completely $\D$-aspherical base must be $2$-complete on every strict fibre. Second, a $1$-complete equivalence between universal $W^1_{\D}$-preserving fibrations must be $1$-complete on every fibre. Both failures occur for set-valued coefficients. In contrast, under the enhancement hypotheses of C.2, a $1$-complete equivalence between $\Hot$-fibrations over the same base is $1$-complete on every fibre.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
For the first assertion use $H\to K$ from D.4 as a map over $K$ to $1_K:K\to K$. Both structural maps are $\Hot$-fibrations. The categories $H$ and $K$ are connected and simply connected, so the total map and the map $K\to e$ belong to $W_1=W^{\mathrm{lc}}_{\Set}$. Its fibre map is $BC_2\to e$, which does not belong to $W_1$. This also refutes the qualification in I-b-B084-07 after replacing ordinary asphericity by complete asphericity and ordinary equivalence by $2$-complete equivalence.

For the second assertion let $R$ have two objects $b,a$, automorphism group $C_2$ at $b$, only the identity at $a$, one arrow $b\to a$, no arrows $a\to b$, and the relation that precomposing the arrow $b\to a$ by either automorphism leaves it fixed. Thus $a$ is terminal. The opfibration $q:R\to[1]$ has transition $BC_2\to e$. The section $s:[1]\to R$ chooses the unique object in each fibre. Its total map is a weak homotopy equivalence because both categories have terminal objects. At $0$ its fibre map is $e\to BC_2$, which is not $1$-complete: an underlying function from a singleton to a regular $C_2$-set need not be equivariant.

We verify the stronger structural hypothesis on $q$. After an arbitrary base change $T\to[1]$, it is the Grothendieck opfibration with fibres $BC_2$ over the objects of colour $0$ and $e$ over colour $1$. For a locally constant set-valued presheaf $L$ on the total category, transport over an arrow from colour $0$ to colour $1$ is invertible and its compatibility with vertical loops forces the $C_2$-action at its source to be trivial. Right Kan extension is calculated on the strict fibres by the opfibration adjunction. Its values are invariants at colour $0$ and the original set at colour $1$. Transport is invertible within each colour, and over a cross-colour arrow it is invertible because the source action is trivial. Thus the direct image is locally constant. The same fibre formula proves the base-change mate for every further pullback and every local system on that pullback. D.2 and the set case of C.2 show that $q$ universally preserves $W^1_{\Set}$. The identity of $[1]$ does too. This proves the second refutation, with all four arrows of $R$ checked by \texttt{D\_verify\_more.py}.

For the last assertion, write $f:E\to F$ over $S$. The strict fibres of both structural $\Hot$-fibrations compute their homotopy fibres. The map $BE_s\to BF_s$ is therefore the homotopy pullback of $Bf$ along $BF_s\to BF$. C.2 characterizes $W^1_{\D}$ by a property of all homotopy fibres; that property is preserved by homotopy pullback. Hence every $f_s$ is $1$-complete. No analogous argument applies to $W^{\mathrm{lc}}_{\D}$, as the first example proves.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{D.6}{Known}{A fibration class with the requested stability and fibre detection}
Assume $W=W^{\mathrm{lc}}_{\D}$ is an accessible fundamental localizer. Equip $\Cat$ with the Thomason model structure associated to $W$, whose existence is \citet[Theorem 5.2.15]{Cisinski2006}. Its fibrations are closed under arbitrary strict base change and composition. If $p:E\to S$ and $q:F\to S$ are such fibrations and $f:E\to F$ is a functor over $S$ belonging to $W$, then $f_s:E_s\to F_s$ belongs to $W$ for every object $s\in S$. Right properness of the model structure is not needed for this assertion.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
The cited theorem gives a cofibrantly generated, left proper model structure with weak equivalences exactly $W$ and the usual Thomason cofibrations. Fibrations have the right lifting property against its trivial cofibrations; this directly implies stability under base change and composition. For $s:e\to S$, the pullback
\[
 s^*:\Cat/S\longrightarrow\Cat/e
\]
is right Quillen: its left adjoint sends $T\to e$ to the composite $T\to e\to S$ and preserves cofibrations and trivial cofibrations because their underlying functors do not change. The objects $p,q$ are fibrant in $\Cat/S$, since a terminal object there is $1_S$. A right Quillen functor preserves weak equivalences between fibrant objects, so $s^*f=f_s\in W$. For completeness this last model-category fact follows by factoring a map between fibrant objects through a trivial cofibration and a fibration, then using a path-object factorization to express its image as a retract, in the homotopy category, of the images of trivial fibrations; a right Quillen functor preserves these trivial fibrations. Equivalently it is the dual form of Ken Brown's lemma.

These are the model fibrations of the specified localized Thomason structure. We do not identify them with either the restricted fibrations of D.4 or every universal $W$-preserving functor of D.1. In particular, absent right properness, a model fibration is not asserted to preserve an arbitrary $W$-equivalence between arbitrary objects of a slice. This distinction is why the preceding counterexamples do not contradict the theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{D.7}{Proved}{Universal constant equivalences control arbitrary coefficients}
Let $\D$ be a derivator on all small categories with both pointwise homotopy Kan extensions, pointwise detection, and the disjoint-union axiom. If every strict pullback of $u:A\to B$ is a constant-coefficient $\D$-equivalence, then $u^*:\D(B)\to\D(A)$ is fully faithful. In particular this holds if every strict pullback of $u$ is $1$-complete or $2$-complete. Thus I-b-B084-01 is affirmative under these hypotheses, without an enhancement assumption.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Universal equivalence implies that each strict fibre $A_b$ is $\D$-aspherical. Pulling back over $B/b$ shows that $A/b$ is $\D$-aspherical as well. Their inclusion and every transition between the comma categories are constant-coefficient equivalences by two-out-of-three. The same reasoning applies after every base change. Hence $u$ is universally a weak cohomological fibration.

We prove the needed fibre formula for arbitrary inverse-image coefficients. Fix $b\in B$, put $H=A/b$, $S=A_b$, and let $v:H\to B$ remember $u(a)$, with $h:H\to e$, $t:S\to e$. The two functors
\[
 h_*v^*,\quad t_*t^*b^*:\D(B)\longrightarrow\D(e)
\]
are the right adjoints in the desired comparison
$(u_*u^*F)_b\to H^*_{\D}(A_b,F_b)$.
Their left adjoints are $v_!h^*$ and $b_!t_!t^*$, respectively. We check that the canonical left mate $b_!t_!t^*\to v_!h^*$ is invertible.

For $z\in B$ and $\gamma:z\to b$, let $J_\gamma$ have factorizations $z\xrightarrow{\beta}c\xrightarrow{\alpha}b$ with $\alpha\beta=\gamma$, and morphisms $k:c\to c'$ satisfying $k\beta=\beta'$ and $\alpha'k=\alpha$. It has initial object $(z,1_z,\gamma)$ and terminal object $(b,\gamma,1_b)$. Put $K_\gamma=A\times_BJ_\gamma$ using the middle-object functor. The universal weak fibration property over $J_\gamma$ implies $A_b\to K_\gamma$ is a constant-coefficient equivalence. The comma category $z\backslash v$ is the disjoint union of the $K_\gamma$ indexed by the set $B(z,b)$. Pointwise left Kan extension therefore identifies the mate, evaluated at $M\in\D(e)$ and $z$, with
\[
 \coprod_{\gamma\in B(z,b)}H_*^{\D}(A_b,M)
 \longrightarrow
 \coprod_{\gamma\in B(z,b)}H_*^{\D}(K_\gamma,M).
\]
Each summand is invertible. Pointwise detection proves that the mate is an isomorphism, so the right-adjoint comparison is an isomorphism as well. Every indexing category used is small, including an empty hom-set or an infinite hom-set; the disjoint-union axiom covers both cases.

Now evaluate the unit $F\to u_*u^*F$ at $b$ and follow it by the fibre comparison. Its composite is the constant unit $F_b\to H^*_{\D}(A_b,F_b)$, which is invertible because $A_b$ is $\D$-aspherical. Therefore the unit is pointwise invertible and hence invertible. This is exactly full faithfulness of $u^*$. The second adjunction then also has invertible counit $u_!u^*F\to F$. All maps are canonical mates; no abstract identification of endpoints is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{D.8}{Known}{The HOT conclusions and the weak-contractible-base equivalence}
For the homotopy derivator of spaces, all five equivalence classes of Part A equal $W_\infty$. The universal weak cohomological fibration condition, its homological counterpart, the test on every map between weakly contractible bases, and universal preservation of $W_\infty$ are equivalent. This settles I-b-B096-04 for the usual derivator axioms and settles Ib-C021, Ib-C026, Ib-C048, Ib-C069, Ib-C092 and Ib-C096 in the modern HOT setting. For a general derivator, the weak-contractible-base test is equivalent to the two universal weak conditions under Der~3~$a'$; the latter axiom follows from \citet[Corollary 3.8]{Cisinski2009LocallyConstant}.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
For spaces, a constant-coefficient equivalence $u:A\to B$ induces bijections $[BB,Z]\to[BA,Z]$ for every small space $Z$, by choosing the source coefficient to be a point. Yoneda in the homotopy category implies that the specified map $Bu$ is a weak equivalence. Conversely Cisinski's theorem gives equivalence of local-system categories under a weak equivalence of nerves; full faithfulness then gives the local homology and cohomology comparisons by adjunction. The chain of Part A proves equality of the five classes. Thus in the HOT case changing any one of these five adjectives changes none of the assertions.

For the general weak-condition equivalence, suppose first that right direct images of constants are locally constant and satisfy the universal strict-fibre formula. For $v:T'\to T$ over the base, both $BT'$ and $BT$ weakly contractible, the canonical base-change map on such a direct image is invertible by pointwise detection, since it compares the same strict fibres. Der~3~$a'$ makes that direct image constant on $T$. Its cohomology is its constant value, and the same holds on $T'$. The comparison of total cohomology is therefore invertible. The left-Kan proof is dual. Conversely the restricted test includes every inclusion of a chosen point into a category with a terminal object. Apply these tests over the slices $B/b$: their pullbacks are the fibre inclusions $E_b\to E/b$. Selecting any object $(b,u:b\to b')$ of $B/b'$ and using two-out-of-three gives all transition comparisons $E/b\to E/b'$. Repeating after every base change yields the universal weak cohomological condition. Initial objects and undercategories give the dual condition. The point is arbitrary, not necessarily the distinguished terminal or initial object.

For HOT, these fibre and comma comparisons are weak equivalences. Quillen's Theorem~B identifies the strict fibres with homotopy fibres, after every base change. Consequently each strict base-change square is homotopy Cartesian: compare its homotopy fibres over every component of the new base, using the identical strict fibre on both sides. It follows that pullback preserves weak equivalences. The reverse implication follows by applying universal preservation to the contractible test bases. Equivalently, these are the sharp maps of the right proper Thomason model structure; \citet[Proposition 2.7]{Rezk1998Sharp} gives precisely this homotopy-Cartesian characterization. This is the intrinsic class appearing in Chapter XI, and is not asserted to coincide with its smaller class of model fibrations.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{D.9}{Refuted}{Errors not removed by changing the equivalence class}
The uniqueness assertion I-b-B087-05, the two implications I-b-B093-04 and I-b-B093-05, the orbit relations I-b-B095-01, the properness assertion Ib-C003, and the internal-Hom assertion Ib-C082 are false as stated. Their corrections are independent of a wholesale replacement by $W^{\mathrm{lc}}_{\D}$.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
A trivial two-point bundle over a circle has prescribed identity monodromy but admits the nonidentity automorphism exchanging its two sheets. Hence there is no unique isomorphism realizing its classification. This can be modeled by the product of the four-object circle poset with a two-point discrete category. Every arrow in the product preserves the sheet label, and exchanging labels defines a nonidentity automorphism over the base. The classification is of an isomorphism class, with a framing if one wishes to prescribe rather than conjugate monodromy. No change of weak-equivalence class removes these automorphisms.

For I-b-B093-04 take $G=C_2$, $E=G$ as a discrete regular $G$-set, and $Z$ the four-object circle poset with an orientation-reversing involution. All are finite posets, $E/G=e$, and for a field of characteristic different from two, $H^*(E;k)^G=k$. The involution acts as minus one on $H^1(Z;k)$, so $H^*(Z;k)^G\to H^*(Z;k)$ is not an isomorphism. Yet
\[
 (G\times Z)/G\longrightarrow Z,\qquad [g,z]\longmapsto g^{-1}z
\]
is an isomorphism of categories. The inclusion of the fibre over the unique base object is the identity of $Z$. It is therefore an equivalence for every one of the classes under discussion. Conditions (a)--(c) in the manuscript do not force the asserted failure on a fibre. The same example also has $H^*(Z;\Q)^{C_2}=\Q$ but total category $Z$, so it refutes the proposed implication to asphericity even when the base is completely aspherical. In condition (b) the transcription reproduces a printed map from the invariants to themselves; the only interpretation consistent with the preceding page is the inclusion into $H^*(Z;k)$.

For I-b-B093-05 use $E=S^2$ with the antipodal action and $Z=S^1$ with reflection, over $k=\Q$. The actions on their reduced cohomology in degrees $2$ and $1$ are both multiplication by $-1$. Since the diagonal action is free and finite, averaging identifies rational cohomology of its quotient with invariants of the rational cohomology of $S^2\times S^1$. The tensor of the two negative eigenspaces is a positive eigenspace in degree $3$. Thus
\[
 H^*((S^2\times S^1)/C_2;\Q)
   \cong \Q[0]\oplus\Q[3]
\]
as graded vector spaces. It is not rationally acyclic, despite $H^*(S^1;\Q)^{C_2}=\Q$. The omitted cross term is the error. The program \texttt{D\_verify\_more.py} constructs the quotient of the product of the six-object sphere poset and the reflected four-object circle poset. Its normalized rational chain dimensions are $(12,60,96,48)$, its boundary ranks are $(11,49,47)$, and its Betti numbers are $(1,0,0,1)$. The categories are given there by all objects, arrows and the orbit composition rule. This independently checks the top-degree class in a finite categorical model. As failure of constant equivalence implies failure of $1$- and $2$-complete equivalence, strengthening the conclusion does not repair this implication under its original cohomological hypotheses.

For I-b-B095-01 write the six-object sphere poset as pairs $(i,\epsilon)$ with $i=0,1,2$ and $\epsilon\in\{\pm1\}$, ordered by increasing $i$. The antipodal involution changes both endpoint signs. Label an arrow orbit by the product of its endpoint signs. Composition then multiplies labels. Therefore the correct relations are
\[
 p_1^-p_0^-=p_1^+p_0^+,\qquad
 p_1^-p_0^+=p_1^+p_0^-.
\]
The displayed relations in the manuscript equate arrows of different signs and are false. This is a categorical composition calculation, unaffected by the choice of coefficients. The program checks all four sign products. The count of the later poset model should not be inferred from these incorrect relations.

For Ib-C003 take the inclusion $\{1\}\hookrightarrow[1]$. It is a Grothendieck opfibration: the only arrow issuing from its image object is an identity. The strict-fibre-to-over-comma comparisons are identities, after all base changes. Nevertheless its comma categories at $0$ and $1$ are empty and terminal, so the transition is $\varnothing\to e$. It belongs to none of $W_\infty$, $W^1_{\Set}$ or $W^{\mathrm{lc}}_{\Set}$. Properness supplies a fibre formula, but does not supply local constancy of the fibres. An additional transport hypothesis is required.

For Ib-C082 take $e\to BC_2$ with $\D=D(\Q)$. Averaging proves the constant-equivalence assertion exactly as in D.3. The trivial representation and the sign representation, both in degree zero, have no nonzero equivariant map between them, whereas their restrictions have a one-dimensional space of linear maps. Thus restriction is not full. Derived rational local systems have compatible tensor products and internal Homs, so the stated hypothesis is present. The missing adjective is ``cohomological'': with locally constant internal Homs preserved by pullback, cohomological invariance applied to $\underline{\Hom}(L,M)$ compares $\Hom(L,M)$ and proves $1$-completeness. In this argument the unit must be the actual monoidal unit. For a stable theory it cannot be replaced by the terminal zero object. Replacing ``equivalence'' in the conclusion by ``$2$-complete'' only strengthens an already false conclusion; replacing the hypothesis by $2$-completeness makes full faithfulness tautological and does not validate the manuscript's inference.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{D.10}{Proved}{The doubled-arrow test and the scope of the repair}
For the space derivator, the doubled-arrow construction of I-b-A069 cannot produce the proposed failure of weak cohomological fibration. The same gluing step is valid for $W^{\mathrm{lc}}_{\D}$ and $W^1_{\D}$ when $\D$ has the complete enhanced coefficient category of C.1. This statement concerns that specified base change only.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
A functor $X\to[1]$ decomposes $X$ into a sieve $X_0$ and a cosieve $X_1$. Pullback along the fork $0\to1\leftarrow0'$ is
$P=X\amalg_{X_1}X$. Every composable chain in this category lies in a single copy of $X$, because once a chain enters the common cosieve it cannot leave it. Hence
\[
 NP=NX\amalg_{NX_1}NX.
\]
The two inclusions of $NX_1$ are monomorphisms of simplicial sets. Its realization is therefore the homotopy pushout of $BX\leftarrow BX_1\to BX$. If $X\to[1]$ is a weak cohomological HOT-fibration, both inclusions $X_i\to X$ are weak equivalences. Thus either copy of $X$ maps by a weak equivalence into $P$, and $X_1\to P$ is one too. The other two comma categories for the fork are copies of $X_0$, so their fibre and transport comparisons are also weak equivalences.

Under the enhanced hypotheses, apply $\Fun(-,\mathcal C)$ to this homotopy pushout. It becomes the pullback of infinity categories
\[
 \Fun(BP,\mathcal C)\simeq
 \Fun(BX,\mathcal C)
 \mathop{\times}_{\Fun(BX_1,\mathcal C)}
 \Fun(BX,\mathcal C).
\]
If restriction to $BX_1$ is an equivalence, its pullback is an equivalence. If restriction is only fully faithful, the diagonal into this self-pullback is still an equivalence: full faithfulness identifies a specified equivalence between the two restricted objects with a unique equivalence between the two original objects, together with its contractible space of choices. Thus the gluing step retains the required full faithfulness, and the remaining comparisons follow by composition. C.1 transfers the exact fully faithful or equivalence condition to the homotopy categories used in the question.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{D.11}{Proved}{The doubled-arrow generalization for every derivator}
Let $\D$ satisfy Der~1--4 on all small categories. If a cosieve inclusion $i:X_1\to X$ belongs to $W^{\mathrm{lc}}_{\D}$, then both copy inclusions $X\to X\amalg_{X_1}X$ belong to $W^{\mathrm{lc}}_{\D}$. Consequently the strengthened $2$-complete gluing step in the specified doubled-arrow construction is valid without an enhancement. The corresponding $1$-complete gluing step is also valid, by B.5.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
B.3 proves that $W^{\mathrm{lc}}_{\D}$ is a fundamental localizer, including relative Theorem A. Let $V$ be the fundamental localizer generated by the single functor $i$. It is accessible and is contained in $W^{\mathrm{lc}}_{\D}$. The nerve correspondence and its associated simplicial model structure, whose cofibrations are all monomorphisms, are \citet[Theorems~1.4.3 and~4.2.15]{Cisinski2006}. In that model structure $Ni$ is a trivial cofibration. As in D.10, the nerve of this categorical pushout is the simplicial pushout, since a composable chain cannot leave the common cosieve after entering it. Each inclusion $NX\to NX\amalg_{NX_1}NX$ is therefore a pushout of the trivial cofibration $Ni$, and is a $V$-equivalence. Passing back to categories proves the stated $W^{\mathrm{lc}}_{\D}$ comparison. This argument does not assume that the whole localizer $W^{\mathrm{lc}}_{\D}$ is accessible.

For $W^1_{\D}$ apply Loc~(6a), as proved in B.5 from the fully faithful relative-Theorem-A assertion of B.3, to the map of cosieve Mayer--Vietoris squares
\[
 (X_1,X_1,X,X)\longrightarrow
 (X_1,X,X,X\amalg_{X_1}X).
\]
The first three maps are $\id_{X_1}$, $i$, and $\id_X$, so the fourth is $1$-complete. Interchanging the two copies gives the other inclusion. The remaining fibre and transport comparisons for this specified fork are the ones already identified in D.10. Thus the previous general descent gap is resolved by B.3; no conclusion about all possible base changes of the original weak condition is added.
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}{D.12}{Refuted}{The original claim already fails for $0$-type coefficients}
For $\D(A)=\Fun(A^{\op},\Set)$, a universal weak cohomological and homological fibration need not preserve constant-coefficient equivalences. Thus the truncated-type qualification I-b-B079-07, as well as I-b-B079-03 and I-b-B079-04, is false in degree zero.
\end{claim}
\begin{proof}
Let $G=C_2$. The category $EG=G\ltimes G$ has object set $G$, a unique arrow between any ordered pair of objects, and the unique possible composition. The functor $EG\to BG$ sends the arrow $x\to y$ to $yx^{-1}$. It is a covering: an arrow of $BG$ has exactly one lift from any prescribed object and exactly one lift ending at any prescribed object. A presheaf on a strict fibre is just a family indexed by a discrete two-point set. Right and left Kan extensions along the covering are respectively fibrewise products and coproducts, and transport permutes their factors. Hence constants have locally constant direct images, the strict fibre formulas hold, and all these assertions persist under arbitrary base change.

The functor $e\to BG$ is a constant-coefficient equivalence for sets since both indexing categories are connected, and constants have the same Hom sets over every connected indexing category. Its pullback comparison is $G_{\mathrm{disc}}\to EG$. These categories have two and one connected components. For a two-element set $M$, the restriction of sections of the constant presheaf is the nonbijective diagonal $M\to M^2$. This proves failure of the constant-equivalence conclusion. The input map $e\to BG$ is neither $1$-complete nor $2$-complete; consequently it does not contradict the weaker repaired statement D.2. The groupoid and its two-point fibre occur explicitly in the finite checker.
\end{proof}
