# Supplementary audit: arbitrary-derivator relative Theorem A

The original gap was closed. `sections/B_general.tex` supplies claim B.3, Proved, for every Der1--4 derivator on all small categories, in the paper's presheaf variance. It proves relative Theorem A for both W_D^lc and W_D^1, as well as the fiberwise Grothendieck-opfibration descent versions. The W_D^1 conclusion uses only the unit of the partial-local adjunction; it does not require essential surjectivity. Thus W_D^1 fails to be a fundamental localizer because of two-out-of-three, not because of relative Theorem A. None of these results assumes strongness, a model presentation, compact generation, stability, or an infinity-categorical enhancement.

The decisive source is Hörmann's explicitly checked October 20, 2015 author version of *Fibered Multiderivators and (co)homological Descent*, Main Theorem 2.5.5(2), proof pp. 52--54, and Lemmas 2.5.12--2.5.13, pp. 46--48. Its proof establishes the needed Grothendieck-opfibration descent using partial Cartesian projectors. In Hörmann's general nontrivially fibered setting, stable compact generation is used to produce those projectors.

For an ordinary arbitrary derivator those projectors follow from Cisinski (2009), Theorem 3.6: that theorem already allows an arbitrary subcategory of arrows to be inverted. Lemma 3.9 and the proofs of Proposition 3.10 and Theorem 3.13 extend verbatim to this arbitrary subcategory, because pointwise homotopy colimits of simplicial presheaves that invert a specified set of arrows again invert those arrows. This gives both partial-local adjoints as morphisms of derivators. No stronger derivator axiom is hidden here.

The written proof verifies fiberwise base change directly. For an opfibration T→S and fiber inclusion i_s, left Kan extension i_s! preserves vertically locally constant objects: at x over t it is the coproduct, indexed by arrows t→s, of the values at the cocartesian transports of x. Taking right adjoints shows that the right partial-local projector commutes with fiber restriction. For a map of opfibrations, right Kan extension commutes with fiber restriction by the comma formula: the fiber comma inclusion has a left adjoint given by cocartesian transport. Unit and counit are therefore fiberwise isomorphisms, hence isomorphisms by Der2. The resulting equivalence of vertically local diagrams reflects full local constancy using the transport comparisons in the fiber categories. Finally the Grothendieck construction of the slice diagram A/s has a forgetful functor to A with adjoint section, inducing an equivalence on locally constant diagrams.

Cisinski's own 2009 §1 only asserts a weak basic localizer. The arbitrary-derivator conclusion here is not justified by silently replacing that phrase with basic localizer; the additional partial-projector argument is included in full. A web search located Cisinski's 2019 MathOverflow discussion as context, but the paper's proof and references rely on the primary papers and the supplied complete argument.

Suggested result-register entry: B.3; Relative Theorem A for LC_D for every derivator satisfying Der1--4 on all small categories; Proved; sections/B_general.tex; A.4, Cisinski2009 Theorem 3.6 and internal-Hom argument; Part B and relative descent used in Part D; Ordinary-category statement, not merely an enhanced statement.

The coefficients agent independently reviewed the adjunction/mate proof. An initial objection used covariant rather than the paper's presheaf convention; under the stated presheaf convention the coproduct formula is correct, as the example of the terminal-vertex representable on [1] confirms. The substantive condition that the map between opfibrations preserve cocartesian arrows is included explicitly in the proof; the Grothendieck replacement of a natural transformation preserves the chosen transports strictly. The construction of the right partial-local projector is explicitly by dualization, not by a claim that a further right adjoint of the model-level inclusion is cocontinuous.

The no-Der5 hypothesis was also traced through the cited foundational paper, opened directly at `https://www.math.univ-toulouse.fr/~dcisinsk/puhot.pdf`: Cisinski (2008), Definitions 0.1--0.2, pp. 608--609, define a derivator as a weak left and weak right derivator with Der1--4 only. Proposition 3.12, p. 621, makes every such two-sided derivator a left derivator in that paper's stronger one-sided terminology; applying its dual enables Corollary 3.26, p. 627, for every small shape. Thus the universal property used in B.3 does apply without the coherent-arrow strongness axiom. Later sources use different terminological conventions, so their stronger definition must not be substituted silently.

The theorem also strengthens E.7: accessibility of W_D^lc is now the only additional hypothesis needed there to construct a realizing derivator of localized spaces. Product stability follows from the fundamental-localizer axioms, and Cisinski (2006), Theorem 4.2.15 for the test category Delta supplies the accessible space localization. E.7 records functoriality for specified inclusions of accessible fundamental localizers; no unrestricted functor on all derivator morphisms is claimed.
