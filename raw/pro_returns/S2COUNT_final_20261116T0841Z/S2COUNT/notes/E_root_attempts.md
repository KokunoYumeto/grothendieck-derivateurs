# Root investigation of the remaining E2 step

## Countable compactness versus nonsingularity

The essential-ultrapower construction in E.12 gives a countable finitely solvable unsolvable system over every residual simple group with pointwise finite normal-generation norm. Its arbitrary multiplication-table relations can have dependent exponent rows. Introducing variables y_i=r_i makes rows independent only by removing the original constraints; adding y_i=1 restores the dependencies. A relative homology equivalence of actual two-dimensional models needs an integral boundary isomorphism, not simply a larger presentation.

A possible refinement is an essential countable subgroup P' of an ultrapower with relative H_1 and H_2 zero. Its existence has not been proved. The normal closure of the diagonal perfect group is perfect, but conjugators witnessing essentiality in the whole ultrapower need not lie in that normal closure. This is a precise obstruction to combining the two closures inside one countable subgroup.

## Nested commutator ranges

For a nonabelian simple E and a≠1, put S_a={v[a,u]v^-1:u,v in E}. This is a conjugation-invariant generating subset containing 1. For h,a≠1 the word [h,[a,t]b] is nonconstant: otherwise all [a,t] would centralize a conjugate of h, contrary to their normal generation. Thus the first nested substitution can preserve nonconstancy.

The needed induction is stronger: after arbitrarily many translated substitutions, the remaining root word must still be nonconstant and its image must omit the next enumerated element. The property that AA^-1 generates E is insufficient, because a generating set may lie in a transporter of a small subgroup into a proper subgroup. A bound on N_E(c) controls expressions for c, not lengths of conjugators modulo C_E(c). The conjugation orbit map can have unbounded inverse distortion. No general nested-range lemma is claimed.

## Metrizable topology

A29 gives a nondiscrete Hausdorff metrizable group topology. A countable such group is not complete, so a completion supplies points outside the group. Translating this into a countable acyclic equation system is unresolved. Linear recurrences x_i=a_i x_(i+1) retain a nonzero relative homology class. Replacing child exponents by zero using commutators gives an identity exponent matrix, but arbitrary conjugators destroy the uniform small-neighborhood control needed for the metric diagonal. A nondiscrete invariant metric is already excluded by A30; it must not be inferred from ordinary metrizability.

## Literature distinctions

Ershov's 1979 algebraic compactness paper uses a purity condition defined through maps into finite groups. Its profinite-retract theorem is not a theorem about arbitrary finitely solvable group equation systems and is not used. Berrick's displayed sequential construction does not by itself prove the asserted two-dimensional geometric dimension for arbitrary uncountable perfect locally free groups; the countable grope construction and its free products are safe. Recent simple-envelope constructions that preserve geometric dimension add uncontrolled H_2 and therefore do not give the required rank-one example.

These are research records, not claims of nonexistence or independence. The smallest remaining case is exactly E2 with H_2=Z and bound ℵ₀.

## Further work after checkpoint 01

### Bounded normal length and ultrapowers

In the E.12 essential-ultrapower construction one can choose the new point to be represented by distinct conjugates of a fixed nonidentity a. These have uniformly bounded N_E, and so do their differences from any fixed coefficient. There is a useful bounded-length refinement of normal generation: fix d=[u,v]≠1. Expressing u as a uniformly bounded product of conjugates of b and expanding its commutator with v expresses d as a bounded product of conjugates of commutators [b,t]. Expressing a fixed c through conjugates of d then gives such an expression for c, with total exponent of b zero. If b ranges through a bounded N_E-ball, one can also arrange expressions with total exponent of b one and uniformly bounded length.

This does not yet produce an independent-row unsolvable system. The essentiality equations for all possible old values still constrain the same new root variable. Giving each equation a new copy of the root makes its exponent row independent only until equality of the copies is imposed; those equalities restore the dependencies. Conjugators chosen in the ultrapower may have unbounded N_E even when the represented elements have bounded N_E.

The latter obstruction has an elementary precise form. If P is normally generated by a coefficient subgroup H, q:H→E is fixed, and p=∏ z_i h_i^{ε_i}z_i^{-1}, then every extension f:P→E satisfies N_E(f(p))≤ΣN_E(q(h_i)). Thus a normally generated block cannot give a fixed new variable arbitrary images whose N_E values are unbounded. This is a restriction on a proposed construction, not a proof of the desired countable quotient theorem.

### Nested identities really can occur

The monoid agent found a depth-three obstruction in the simple group Homeo_c(R). With [a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}, take a positive bump d supported in (2,3) and a nonidentity h supported in (0,1). For every t, the commutator q=[d,t]=d(d^t)^{-1} moves x≤1 to a point≤x, so q(0,1) lies below 1. Consequently [h,q] is supported below 1 and [d,[h,[d,t]]]=1. This group was already excluded by the uniformly simple case, so the example does not settle the residual class. It does refute the proposed unrestricted shortcut from simplicity and torsion-freeness to nonconstancy of every nested commutator word.

Finite computations of normal-subset commutators were used only to test the attempted lemma. A5 and A6 pass the tested class pairs. In S4, a transposition against the double-transposition class gives a nonnormal subgroup of order two, so the corresponding assertion for arbitrary groups is false. Neither computation is offered as evidence of a general simple-group theorem.

### Geometry of homology-killing blocks

The Bartholdi–Mikhailov construction was reread through its actual nonpositively curved block and surface gluing. Its finite three-dimensional block has a two-dimensional collapse and the cut surface dies in its H_2. A finite gluing preserves the old fundamental group and generates no new π_2 once the old π_2 is zero. However, the block contributes new homology; killing its H_1 through Higman amalgams need not remove its H_2. Iterating replacements changes the old complexes instead of giving the needed compatible inclusions. A mapping telescope restores compatibility in dimension three. No two-dimensional infinite-union conclusion follows from the checked finite statement.

A two-dimensional acyclic envelope with the required coefficient quotient extension property would help, but none has been established. Binate envelopes have the wrong dimension. A surface handlebody analogue fails the needed normal-closure property: killing meridians can also kill coefficient longitudes. Small-cancellation quotients after a perfect stage add independent H_2 classes. These are the exact failures of the geometric routes tried.

## Refinements through 07:35 UTC

The independent perfect-normal-subgroup theorem is now Statement E.16, with the disjoint-lift normal-closure estimate E.16a. A countable homology core need not be normal. If an ambient group and its core are simple, the normal core of a proper core subgroup is trivial, while its normalizer has quotient of order at most two in rank one. This allows a chain of almost self-normalizing countable simple cores; the available lemmas do not exclude that configuration. Passing to a maximal simple quotient of the original group would lose the actual two-dimensional model and is not a valid reduction.

The elementary-subgroup/equation-system route has another precise limitation. Unrolling a countable multiplication table into a tree of separate variable occurrences gives finite prefixes that can be eliminated to free frontier variables, but repeated occurrences no longer have to take the same value. Adding the equalities restores the exponent dependencies. Linear chains that enforce equality of successive copies retain a relative H₁ class. Neither modification supplies the required acyclic aspherical block.

Statement E.17 proves the bounded-transport branch directly: on a fixed canonical-normal-length ball, a fixed finite product of conjugated commutators has enough bounded preimages to carry the countable diagonal. The literature condition is Assumption 4.1 of Gismatullin–Majcher–Ziegler, arXiv:2010.03394v2, not an imported conclusion about simplicity of ultraproducts. In fact, the printed Theorem 4.3(2) lacks an upper bound on the norm of its element g, and the following example shows why that matters. Let G_k=A₅×Z with norm ||(a,z)||=1_(a≠1)+k|z|. The bounded-norm part of its metric ultraproduct is A₅ and is simple; the conjugator condition holds with bound 2. Yet the central element (1,1), of norm k, has conjugate products only in the Z factor and cannot cover a nontrivial A₅ element with errors of norm less than 1. E.17 uses no part of the disputed implication.

For the unbounded-transport branch, one attempted root equation is x_o=t d t⁻¹. On the conjugacy class of d, the function δ_d(a,b)=inf{N(t):tat⁻¹=b} measures transporter length. Failure of a transport bound can make this orbit metric unbounded. Substituting t by a bounded product of commutators gives a controlled change in that orbit metric and can exclude an individual candidate. What is missing is an invariant range for further substitutions: the remaining root range can become bounded or constant because of mixed identities. No infinite diagonal is inferred from one exclusion.

A weaker sufficient condition than bounded individual transport is the fixed-ball word-coverage statement itself, W_a(B_C^m)⊇B_(2C), uniformly for a≠1. This is the active route for transformation groups with local displacement. An arbitrary countable subgroup of a larger group does not inherit that statement merely by embedding into a countable elementary subgroup: the forbidden-system coefficients must still lift through the original epimorphism. Only an elementary subgroup, or a subgroup with its own proved coverage property, supports that transfer.

The minimum D=min_{g≠1}N(g) exists when N is pointwise finite. If D=1, every nonidentity element is conjugate to d or d⁻¹ and N is globally 1, an already excluded case. Thus a remaining group has D≥2, and E.17 forces the stated transport failure already on the one ball of radius 2D. This does not say that one fixed conjugacy class has unbounded transporter length: the conjugated element can vary.

The arbitrary-arity version of the tree argument was also checked. At each stage it is enough to avoid a universal fixed root: if the specialized root map is constant at a different value, that candidate is already excluded. The obstruction is therefore the quantifier swap between “for every frontier tuple there exists a root” and “there exists a root for every frontier tuple.” Finite equational avoidance does not by itself make that swap valid. A bad finite prefix remains bad under every subsequent substitution, so a Baire argument on the later coefficient sequence alone cannot repair it.

## Third-hour quotient and equation investigations

Brown--Leary, *Groups of type FP via graphical small cancellation*, Groups Geom. Dyn. 19 (2025), 1--35, DOI 10.4171/GGD/796, was checked at Theorems 1.2 and 2.7, Proposition 3.2, and Corollary 1.4 from the publisher PDF https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/49996. Their graphical groups H(S) have actual two-dimensional classifying spaces, and H(S)→H(T), S⊊T, has a nontrivial acyclic kernel. Thus a quotient can preserve homology and actual geometric dimension in this carefully arranged setting; one must not assume all useful constructions are increasing unions. However, these H(S) are finitely generated and have infinitely generated second homology. The amalgamated groups G(S) improve homological finiteness, but the stated geometric dimension two conclusion is for G(∅); the conclusion for arbitrary S is cohomological dimension two. No passage from the latter to an actual two-dimensional model is made here.

The literal graphical H(S) presentations have generators the directed edges of a finite complex K. Abelianized relators are multiples of graph cycles. Consequently the graph boundary map onto the augmentation-zero vertex group survives as a quotient of H₁(H(S)); replacing K by an uncountable graph does not automatically give small H₁. An extra balancing construction would be needed. Adding relators to an already aspherical two-complex cannot erase its H₂ by an inclusion, since there are no three-boundaries. Replacing the old presentation by a quotient presentation avoids that elementary obstruction, but requires a fresh proof of asphericity at the limit.

The finite-normal-length ultrapower has a useful normal subgroup B, the elements represented by sequences with uniformly bounded N. It is the normal closure of the diagonal E in the full ultrapower P, is the unique minimal nontrivial P-normal subgroup, is perfect, and inherits one-variable unimodular solvability (details in notes/A_E_attempts.md §8). These statements do not make B simple as an abstract group. The conjugators witnessing essentiality may lie in P outside B. Passing to a countable subgroup of B therefore does not yet give the essential, relatively acyclic extension needed by the diagonal.

One attempted improvement uses fixed coefficient conjugators, whose N-values can be arbitrarily large without enlarging the norm of the conjugated element. For a fixed pair (a,g), a≠1, bounded-factor commutator expressions can absorb some arbitrary conjugators into coefficients. But an old node of the diagonal tree must accommodate infinitely many future specializations a=e_n e_j⁻¹. No finite list of coefficient conjugators is known to work for all of them, or even for an entire prescribed N-ball with fixed a. Making the list a countable product introduces tail variables with nonzero exponent. An unanchored linear spine has nonzero relative H₁. A root anchor can make its integral boundary invertible, but then either the spine solves recursively for every root or the extra nonlinear constraints require a new asphericity argument. No solution is inferred from the matrix alone.

## Countable-image obstruction and the surviving homology cost

The old-coefficient root h=[a,y₀] and tail y_n=[a c_n⁻¹,y_(n+1)] block a prescribed map q:P→E, with q(h)≠1 and the q(c_n) enumerating E. Each stage is an injective cyclic HNN extension: the endpoints are a⁻¹ and a⁻¹h at the root, then (a c_n⁻¹)⁻¹ and (a c_n⁻¹)⁻¹y_n. Nonzero exponent in a or the latest stable letter proves infinite order. If an extension sends a to q(c_n), the nth tail forces y_n=1 and hence y₀=1, contradicting q(h)≠1. The root row is zero in relative cellular degree one, so for perfect P it contributes one independent H₂ class.

The first endcap used a fresh Higman group and did not preserve normal generation by P. The corrected version, now E.21, uses t₀ a t₀⁻¹=h and t_n=[h,t_(n+1)]. Both endpoints in every additional HNN extension have infinite order. The first-homology generator moves from a to t₀ to t₁ and dies in the countable union; H₂ remains H₂(P)⊕Z. Killing P forces h=1, then a=1, then all y_n and t_n=1. Thus the whole extension is normally generated by P. This repair removes the normal-generation gap, but not the homology cost.

If P instead has H₁(P)=Z generated by h and H₂(P)=0, the root can kill that old first-homology generator while introducing the new generator a. This permits a finite or countable moving reservoir. At a countable limit all earlier H₁ generators have been killed, leaving a perfect group; the next root then adds H₂. A new free first-homology generator is not forced by the old nontrivial image, and a shared finite-arity root has only countably many descendants. Adding many root constraints gives independent zero-boundary rows. None of these modifications keeps rank-one H₂ through the required uncountable diagonal.

Later two-dimensional attachments along actual subcomplex inclusions cannot remove a cellular two-cycle because there are no three-cells. Quotient replacement may escape that elementary argument, but requires a fresh actual-dimensional asphericity proof at the limit. A mapping telescope of the known replacement moves has dimension three. No deduction that the E2 group is impossible is made.
