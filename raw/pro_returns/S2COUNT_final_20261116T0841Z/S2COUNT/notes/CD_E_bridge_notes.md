# Further bridge checks, 16 November 2026

These are scope checks and unsuccessful approaches, not a resolution of E2.

## Finite interpolation does not supply uniform control

Let E be a nonabelian simple group. For distinct p_1,...,p_k in E,
every prescribed tuple (b_1,...,b_k) is realized by a one-variable word
v(x) over E having exponent sum zero: v(p_i)=b_i.

Here is an elementary proof. Let K be the kernel of the augmentation
E*<x> -> <x>. It is generated by the conjugates x^j E x^(-j), j in Z.
Evaluation at p_1,...,p_k gives a subgroup D of E^k containing the
diagonal E. Every coordinate projection is onto. A proper subdirect
subgroup of a finite product of nonabelian simple groups has some
two-coordinate projection which is the graph of an isomorphism; this
follows inductively from Goursat's lemma. In the present situation the
diagonal E forces that isomorphism to be the identity. Evaluating x g
x^(-1), for all g in E, then forces p_i g p_i^(-1)=p_j g p_j^(-1), so
p_j^(-1)p_i is central. The center of E is trivial, contradicting
p_i != p_j. Hence D=E^k.

Multiplication by x also gives exponent-one interpolation on every finite
set. Thus finite interpolation, and even arbitrary finite prescribed
collisions of a unimodular word map, are compatible with the elementary
algebra of an infinite simple group. They do not furnish a single word
that controls a whole countably infinite norm ball. No assertion of
countable interpolation is made.

## Fixed versus variable conjugators

In the canonical conjugation-invariant norm N, a fixed coefficient t may
occur as a conjugator without increasing N(t^(-1) g t). Consequently a
finite list of coefficient conjugators can be chosen separately for any
specified finite family of witness values. In contrast, a newly adjoined
variable which must belong to the normal closure of the old coefficient
group has a fixed finite norm bound under every extending map.

The unresolved uniformity is therefore precise: a finite defining word
contains only finitely many fixed conjugators, and those choices must
continue to work against infinitely many later candidate root values.
Neither finite interpolation nor the finite avoidance lemma supplies this
uniform finite list. Allowing unrestricted variable conjugators abandons
the bounded normal-generation condition needed by the construction.

## Singular-root recycling

The new root construction uses h=[a,y_0] together with the chain
y_n=[a c_n^(-1),y_(n+1)]. If a target map has a in the image of the
coefficient group, the enumeration of its image collapses some y_n and
then y_0, forcing h=1. The OLD-h version is valid: with commutator
[u,v]=uvu^(-1)v^(-1), the first relation is an HNN extension conjugating
a^(-1) to a^(-1)h. Each later stage conjugates k_n^(-1) to k_n^(-1)y_n,
where k_n=a c_n^(-1). The latter endpoint contains the last stable letter
once, so it has infinite order by Britton normal form. The full proof is
in sections/CD_E_singular_root_hnn.tex and was sent to the parent/E agent.

The terminal H1 can be removed without a fresh Higman retract. Adjoin
t_0 with t_0 a t_0^(-1)=h, and then t_n=[h,t_(n+1)] for every n.
These are cyclic HNN extensions: their endpoints are respectively a,h,
and h^(-1),h^(-1)t_n. The latter has last stable letter exponent one.
Each edge homology map kills the current primitive H1 generator, so
H2 is unchanged and the direct limit H1 is zero. Killing P kills h,
then a, every y_n, and every t_n. Thus the strengthened block is normally
generated by P, blocks the prescribed epimorphism q, and has exactly
one additional H2 class. Parent and E agent independently audited this
strengthening and incorporated it in E.21.

Reusing a fixed h by appending relations h=[a_i,y_(i,0)] does not preserve
the relative second homology: each displayed row has exponent sum zero
in every new variable. In a genuinely two-dimensional attachment, its
relative cellular cycle is not the boundary of a three-cell. Cancelling
new first homology with additional rows does not remove this cycle.
Thus a direct repetition spends one independent relative H2 class per
such root relation. This observation does not rule out a substantially
different replacement construction.

  their dimension-preserving simple embedding has infinite-dimensional
  rational H2. The introduction identifies Camm amalgams, product-of-trees

## Notes-only extension of the tree exclusion

The proof in sections/CD_E_Higman_tree.tex also applies to a cellular
action by semisimple isometries on a complete locally finite CAT(0)
polyhedral complex, after canonical barycentric subdivision. An isometry
conjugate to its square has zero translation length, and semisimplicity
makes it elliptic. Closed convex fixed sets admit unique nearest-point
projections, so the same nested-fixed-set argument gives a common fixed
point for each adjacent Higman pair. That pair stabilizes the cell whose
relative interior contains the point, hence fixes its barycenter in the
subdivision. Finite combinatorial balls about this fixed vertex give the
same minimal-orbit-prime contradiction. Cellular isometries are determined
by their action on vertices after subdivision, so the whole action is
trivial. No assertion is made for actions with parabolic elements or
without local finiteness.

Bridson's Theorem A in “On the semisimplicity of polyhedral isometries”,
Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 127(7) (1999), 2143--2146,
DOI 10.1090/S0002-9939-99-05187-4, supplies semisimplicity for connected
M_kappa-polyhedral complexes with finitely many cell isometry types.
The exact Theorem A text was recovered in the search-indexed original
first page; direct PDF retrieval was blocked, so no full-paper audit is
claimed. This corollary remains outside the main paper at the parent's
request. Bibliographic primary record:
https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A6f67eb6f-e005-4394-9d9e-09946f288a38

## Research state and exact next step (08:25 UTC)

The improved singular-root block is a genuine new tool: any specified
nontrivial epimorphism from a perfect actual-gd2 group to a countable group
can be obstructed inside a normally generated actual-gd2 extension. Its
precise unavoidable cost in this construction is one free H2 generator.
This improves the local obstruction problem but does not decide E2--E4.

The remaining constructive step is to obstruct such a map while inducing
an isomorphism on integral homology, or to provide a compatible replacement
which reuses the existing H2 generator and proves actual geometric
dimension two at every limit. Ordinary two-dimensional subcomplex
attachments cannot remove old H2 cycles. A three-dimensional telescope
or a mere cohomological-dimension-two argument does not meet the target.

The H1-reservoir variation explains why rotating a primitive generator
helps only for finitely many stages: h=[a,y_0] kills a primitive old H1
class and leaves a new class a, so it adds no H2. Along an infinite chain
the old H1 classes map to zero; at a countable limit the reservoir has
vanished. A fresh primitive generator at that limit need not have a
nontrivial image whenever the old perfect core does, and cannot normally
generate the old core in the reverse direction while retaining its
nonzero abelianization. Restarting with a null-homologous old h restores
the extra H2 cost. No reset bypassing this issue was established.

On the target side, the unresolved alternative is still a countable simple
group with a nontrivial Higman image, one-variable unimodular solvability,
a mixed identity, pointwise finite unbounded canonical norm, and failure
of the bounded-transport condition of E.17. E.22 excludes locally finite
tree automorphism targets, including the colored-tree examples used to
show that mixed identities plus pointwise finite norm alone do not imply
uniform simplicity. Further family exclusions would not by themselves
settle the remaining homology-preserving obstruction step.
