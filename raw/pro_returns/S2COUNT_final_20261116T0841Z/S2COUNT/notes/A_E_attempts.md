# E: normal-generation and nested-image attempts

This file records exploratory work after completion of Part A and the B audit.
It does **not** settle E2. No finite calculation below is used as an all-group
theorem.

## 1. The exact residual class

The construction audited in E leaves a possible countable nonabelian simple
quotient E for which

    N(g) = sup_{b != 1} rho_b(g)

is finite at every g and unbounded as g varies. Here rho_b is conjugacy word
length with letters conjugate to b or b^-1. Every one-variable equation of
exponent sum +1 or -1 is solvable in E. A remaining E has a mixed identity,
is neither torsion nor locally linear, and admits a nondiscrete Hausdorff
metrizable group topology. That topology is not known to be invariant under
both left and right translations.

## 2. Why the maximal norm does not immediately extend A.31

Write S_b={v[b,u]v^-1 : u,v in E}. Its elements have N-norm at most 2N(b).
After the first A.31-style root substitution, the whole root image lies in
one N-ball of finite radius. Later substitutions increase the number of
variable occurrences. Consequently global unboundedness of N does not give
arbitrarily large differences inside this fixed root image. The hypothesis
used by A.31 is unboundedness of rho_b(d) as b varies for one fixed d, which
the residual case explicitly lacks.

For every K and every b!=1, the N-ball {g:N(g)<=K} is contained in S_b^K:
choose one nonidentity [b,u]; normal-generation length of g in this
commutator is at most N(g), and its inverse also belongs to S_b. This is a
uniform image statement, but it gives no bound on the N-norms of the
conjugating variables used in a preimage. Those variables are precisely the
ones that must themselves be supplied by subsequent tree levels.

If w(x) has exponent sum epsilon in {+1,-1}, collecting the powers of x
writes w(x) as a product of conjugates of its coefficient occurrences,
followed by x^epsilon. Hence

    |N(w(x))-N(x)| <= sum N(coefficient occurrences).

Solutions of w(x)=g are therefore N-bounded by g and the coefficients.
This estimate concerns exponent-one equations; conjugacy and commutator
preimage equations have exponent zero, so the estimate does not fill the
missing preimage bound.

## 3. Nested commutator images

A possible replacement for uniform simplicity is to study repeated maps

    T_a(u,v)=v[a,u]v^-1.

The full image S_a is a symmetric normal generating subset whenever a!=1.
If all child variables remain unrestricted, this is sufficient. Once all
children are constrained by subsequent tree levels, the image need no
longer be conjugation-invariant. The condition that the subgroup generated
by pairwise image differences is E does not by itself imply preservation
under T_a. The precise unresolved strengthening is a bounded transporter
or another invariant of nested images strong enough to survive restriction.

For a fixed c!=1, the map E/C_E(c) -> c^E identifies the conjugacy class
set-theoretically with a coset space. The class c^E has N-diameter at most
2N(c). This does **not** show that conjugators modulo C_E(c) have bounded
S_a-length. An inverse bound for this orbit map is the extra assertion
needed by the proposed Schreier-graph route.

## 4. Finite audit and its exact scope

`code/A_E_normal_subset.py` constructs multiplication tables directly from
permutations. For each nonidentity conjugacy class C and each conjugacy
class representative a it computes the subgroup generated by [a,C].
Simultaneous conjugacy makes this exhaustive over a.

* A5: all 16 nonidentity class pairs give the whole group.
* A6: all 36 nonidentity class pairs give the whole group.
* S4: the proposed normal-subset lemma is false without further
  hypotheses. With a a transposition and C the class of double

## 5. A bounded centralizer-separation lemma

The following statement is proved, but does not settle E2.

**Lemma.** Let E be a centerless simple group. Suppose every strictly
descending chain of centralizer subgroups has at most d proper inclusions.
Let S be a symmetric generating subset containing 1. For c!=1 put

    H_n = C_E({c^w : w in S^n}),  n>=0.

Then H_d=1. In particular, for any a,c!=1 there is w in S^d such that
[a,c^w]!=1.

**Proof.** Because 1 is in S, the H_n form a descending chain of
centralizer subgroups. Since S is symmetric,

    H_(n+1) = intersection_{s in S} H_n^s.

If H_(n+1)=H_n, the inclusions H_n<=H_n^s and H_n<=H_n^(s^-1)
give H_n^s=H_n for every s in S. Thus H_n is normal in E. It is contained
in C_E(c), which is proper since E is centerless and c!=1. Simplicity
therefore gives H_n=1. If H_d were nontrivial, none of the d transitions
from H_0 through H_d could be equality. There is in addition the proper
inclusion E > H_0, giving d+1 proper inclusions between centralizer
subgroups, contrary to the hypothesis. Hence H_d=1. The final assertion
is exactly the definition of that centralizer. QED.

The limitation is that this controls separation by conjugates under a set
which **generates E**. A restricted T-image can have trivial centralizer
without being known to generate E; trivial centralizer alone does not
justify applying the lemma at the next tree level.

## 5a. A concrete obstruction to the pure nested-word shortcut

Pure nested commutators need not stay nonconstant, even in groups of
homeomorphisms rather than finitary permutation groups. Use the convention
[a,b]=aba^-1b^-1. Let d be an orientation-preserving homeomorphism of R
which is the identity outside (2,3) and satisfies d(x)>=x everywhere,
with strict inequality at some point. Let h!=1 be supported in (0,1).
For every orientation-preserving t, put q=[d,t]. The conjugate tdt^-1
also has nonnegative displacement. Thus, for x<2,

    q(x)=d((tdt^-1)^-1(x))=(tdt^-1)^-1(x)<=x.

It follows that q((0,1)) lies in (-infinity,1). The support of [h,q]
is contained in (0,1) union q((0,1)), and is therefore disjoint from the
support of d. Hence

    [d,[h,[d,t]]] = 1

for every t. The coefficient elements d and h are nonidentity. Taking
positive bumps of infinite order on the two disjoint intervals makes
their coefficient subgroup Z^2, and expansion in Z^2 * <t> shows that
the displayed mixed word is nonidentity there.

This observation does not construct a residual quotient from section 1:
the full compact-support homeomorphism group falls in the bounded normal
generation setting already handled by A.27. It does show that failure of
the nested-word condition cannot simply be identified with torsion or
local linearity.

## 7. Two additional target exclusions from the Higman core

These are proved improvements to the list of excluded targets, but do not
decide E2. Let G be any group normally generated by images of finitely many
copies of the four-generator Higman group H used in E.4 and A.28.

**LEF targets.** Every homomorphism G -> L to a locally embeddable into
finite groups (LEF) group is trivial. Recall that LEF means: for each finite
subset F of L there is an injective map F -> Q to a finite group preserving
every product xy=z for which x,y,z all belong to F.

It suffices to kill a map f:H -> L. Take F to contain 1, the images of the
four generators and their inverses, and the images of every prefix of the
four fixed relator words. If f is nontrivial, at least one generator image
is not 1; include it (it is already among the chosen images). A LEF model
preserves the identity, inverses, and all steps in evaluation of each
relator, so the four chosen elements of Q satisfy the Higman relations.
They therefore define a homomorphism H -> Q. Injectivity on F keeps the
specified generator image nonidentity, contradicting the verified fact
that every finite image of H is trivial. Hence f is trivial. Killing every
Higman image kills its normal closure, which is G.

More generally this argument works for any finitely presented group with
no nontrivial finite image, not just H. No assertion that arbitrary
quotients of LEF groups are LEF is used.

**Locally indicable targets.** Every homomorphism G -> L to a locally
indicable group is trivial. A nontrivial image of one Higman copy would be
a nontrivial finitely generated perfect subgroup of L. Local indicability
would give an epimorphism of that subgroup onto Z, contradicting
perfectness. Again normal generation transfers the conclusion to G.

Thus any residual countable simple quotient of the specified A28 group
must fail both LEF and local indicability, in addition to the exclusions
already listed. This does not prove that no such group exists.

## 8. The finite-norm part of an ultrapower

This section is proved. It sharpens the structure of the residual case,
but does not establish bounded transport or settle E2.

Let E be a nonabelian simple group and assume that its canonical norm

    N(g) = sup_{b != 1} rho_b(g)

is finite at every element. Fix a nonprincipal ultrafilter U on the
natural numbers and let P=E^N/U be the ordinary group ultrapower. Write

    B = {[(g_i)] : N(g_i) is bounded on a U-large set}.

Here U-large means belonging to U; values away from such a set may always
be changed without changing the ultrapower element. Then:

1. B is the normal closure of the diagonal E in P.
2. Every nontrivial normal subgroup of P contains B. Thus B is the unique
   minimal nontrivial normal subgroup of P.
3. B is perfect.
4. Fix any nontrivial commutator d=[u,v] in E and put
   C0=max(N(u),N(v)). Every g in E with N(g)<=R is a product of at most
   R commutators [u_j,v_j], with N(u_j),N(v_j)<=C0. Thus the factor-radius
   may be chosen independently of R.
5. If E solves every one-variable unimodular equation, so does B.

**Proof of 1.** B is a normal subgroup by the triangle inequality and
conjugation invariance of N, and contains the diagonal E. Conversely fix
d!=1 in E. If N(g_i)<=R, then rho_d(g_i)<=R. Pad these expressions if
necessary with an empty suffix. There are only finitely many choices of
length <=R and signs. One choice occurs on a U-large set, so [(g_i)] is
a product of at most R conjugates in P of the fixed diagonal elements
d and d^-1. Hence B is the normal closure of d, equivalently of E, in P.

**Proof of 2.** Let x=[(x_i)]!=1 and let d!=1 be any fixed element of E.
The set of i with x_i!=1 is U-large. On it,

    rho_{x_i}(d) <= N(d).

The same finite-choice argument expresses the diagonal d as a product
of at most N(d) conjugates in P of x and x^-1. Every nontrivial normal
subgroup of P therefore contains E and then, by 1, contains B. Taking
any nontrivial normal subgroup of P contained in B proves minimality.

**Proof of 3.** E is perfect. Each of its conjugates in P is a perfect
subgroup contained in B, so each is contained in [B,B]. Their generated
subgroup is B by 1. Equivalently, [B,B] is a nontrivial normal subgroup
of P contained in B, so 2 gives [B,B]=B.

**Proof of 4 (sharpened by the E agent).** Since rho_d(g)<=N(g)<=R,
write g as a product of at most R conjugates of d and d^-1. A conjugate
of d is [u^t,v^t], while a conjugate of d^-1 is [v^t,u^t]. Conjugation
preserves the N-norms of these two factors, giving the assertion. The
conjugators t themselves need not have bounded norm. An alternative
ultrapower contradiction using 3 gives the weaker conclusion that some
factor-radius and some number of factors exist for each R, but the
present direct proof gives the stronger uniform factor-radius C0.

**Proof of 5.** Suppose w(x)=a_1 x^{n_1} ... a_s x^{n_s}=1 with all a_j
in B and sum n_j=epsilon in {+1,-1}. Collecting the powers of x rewrites
w(x) as a product of conjugates of the coefficient occurrences followed
by x^epsilon. Thus every solution, in any group carrying this invariant
norm, satisfies

    N(x) <= sum_{j=1}^s N(a_j).

Choose coordinate representatives for the coefficients with a common
finite bound on the right side. Coordinatewise solvability in E gives
solutions x_i, and the displayed estimate puts [(x_i)] in B. Their
ultrapower class solves w=1. QED.

The missing conclusion is that B be simple as an abstract group, or
even that the normal closure **inside B** of a fixed diagonal a!=1 be
all of B. Statements 1 and 2 use arbitrary conjugators from P, whose
norms may be unbounded. They do not replace those conjugators by
elements of B. The bounded-transport condition in E.17 would provide
precisely this replacement on the required bounded balls.

One elementary consequence of unimodular closure is worth retaining:
for any coefficient word u(y), the map

    y -> u(y) y u(y)^-1

is surjective on every individual conjugacy class of E. Indeed the
equation u(y) y u(y)^-1=a has exponent sum 1, and every preimage y is
conjugate to a. Hence its preimages have N(y)=N(a). This does not yet
give bounded transport between a prescribed pair of conjugate elements.
For example the apparently helpful equation

    y c y^-1 d^-1 y = 1

has N(y)<=N(c)+N(d), but gives y c y^-1=y^-1 d, with a residual factor
y^-1. It is not the desired equation y c y^-1=d.

## 10. Finite equational avoidance in a fixed commutator set

This is a proved strengthening of the finite-avoidance input in A.29.
It does not use the canonical norm until its final consequence.

**Lemma.** Let E be a nonabelian simple group that solves every
one-variable unimodular equation. Fix d!=1 and define

    C_d = {[d,t] : t in E}.

Given finitely many coefficient words w_1(x),...,w_k(x) in E*<x>
such that w_j(1)!=1, there exists x in C_d, x!=1, for which every
w_j(x)!=1. Equivalently, 1 belongs to the closure of C_d\{1} in the
group Zariski topology. If the canonical N is finite everywhere, the
chosen element always satisfies N(x)<=2N(d).

**Proof.** First recall that for p,q!=1 there is c in E such that
[p,cqc^-1]!=1. Otherwise p centralizes every conjugate of q, hence its
normal closure E, contradicting the trivial center of E.

Construct a zero-exponent coefficient word V(x) such that V(1)!=1
and V(x)=1 whenever any w_j(x)=1. For the first word choose c_1 with
[w_1(1),c_1 d c_1^-1]!=1 and set

    V_1(x)=[w_1(x),c_1 d c_1^-1].

Inductively, once V_(j-1)(1)!=1, choose c_j with
[V_(j-1)(1),c_j w_j(1)c_j^-1]!=1 and set

    V_j(x)=[V_(j-1)(x),c_j w_j(x)c_j^-1].

Every V_j has exponent sum zero in x. It has nonidentity value at1
and is the identity whenever at least one of w_1,...,w_j is the
identity. Put V=V_k. Choose a constant c such that
[cV(1)c^-1,d]!=1 and put

    v(x)=[cV(x)c^-1,d].

The equation x v(x)=1 is unimodular, so it has a solution x in E.
It has no solution x=1. At any solution,

    x=v(x)^-1=[d,cV(x)c^-1],

so x belongs to C_d\{1}. If any w_j(x)=1, then V(x)=1, hence
v(x)=1 and x=1, a contradiction. Thus this solution avoids every
specified equation. The norm bound follows from conjugation
invariance and N([d,t])<=2N(d). QED.

For k=0 one may take any nonidentity commutator [d,t]; such a t exists
because E is centerless. Adding the equations xg^-1=1 for finitely
many g!=1 shows that C_d is infinite, and the lemma supplies a choice
different from all previously chosen nonidentity elements.

Consequently the recursive A.29 topology construction can choose all
its shrinking nonidentity elements from this single C_d and hence
from B_(2N(d)). This does not make the resulting topology invariant
under all conjugations. Indeed pointwise finite N still forbids a
positive nondiscrete conjugation-invariant norm. Nor does finite
avoidance justify avoidance of countably many universal fixed-point
laws in the variable-arity tree: those are a different quantifier
condition.

## 11. Further candidate exclusions: finite PL and compact flow PL

### 11.1. A direct obstruction on the circle

**Lemma (proved here).** If a,b are orientation-preserving piecewise
linear homeomorphisms of the circle, each with finitely many
breakpoints, and aba^-1=b^2, then b=1.

**Proof.** Conjugacy invariance and the power rule for rotation number
give rot(b)=2rot(b) in R/Z, hence rot(b)=0. Therefore b has a fixed
point. If b is nontrivial, its support is a nonempty finite union of
proper open intervals. Since supp(b)=supp(b^2), conjugation by a
permutes these intervals and their oriented endpoints. Choose an
endpoint p and the side pointing into its support interval. Some
positive power a^k fixes p, preserving that side. The corresponding
one-sided slope lambda of b at p is positive and differs from 1:
otherwise b is the identity on a small interval inside its support.
Taking the one-sided derivative of

    a^k b a^-k = b^(2^k)

at p gives lambda=lambda^(2^k), because the slopes of a^k and a^-k
cancel at a fixed point. This contradicts lambda!=1. QED.

Consequently every homomorphism from the four-generator Higman group
H to this PL circle group is trivial: each of its generators is the
b-term in one of the relations aba^-1=b^2. Any group normally generated
by an image of H consequently has no nontrivial homomorphism into this
PL circle group or any of its subgroups.

On the real line one must retain a fixed-point hypothesis in this
two-element lemma. The translation b(t)=t+1 and dilation a(t)=2t
give a nontrivial counterexample to its unrestricted line version.
For a map from the perfect Higman group to the finite-breakpoint PL
line group, however, the two eventual-affine germ homomorphisms have
solvable targets, so their images are trivial. Every image element
then has compact support, and the endpoint argument applies.

**Literature consequences.** Hyde--Lodha, arXiv:2302.04805v3,
Definition 1.2 and Proposition 1.4(3), place their finitely presented
simple groups Q_n inside the finite-breakpoint PL circle group.
Hyde--Lodha--Rivas, arXiv:2102.02419, Corollary 1.0.8, does the same
for the simple fast-ring derived groups H_n (and also embeds them in
Thompson's T). Both families are therefore excluded as quotients of
the Higman-normally-generated A.28/A.31 construction, without deciding
their canonical norms or equation closure.

Primary sources:

* https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.04805
* https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.02419

### 11.2. Compact minimal-flow version

**General form.** The proof below works for a group acting faithfully
and orbitwise in the positive direction on any compact space with a
minimal free R-flow, provided every group element has a continuous
flow-displacement function, its maps on individual leaves are locally
finite PL maps, and its set of one-sided leafwise slopes, over all
leaves, is finite. Under these assumptions aba^-1=b^2 forces b=1.
No zero-dimensional transversal is needed in this general statement.

**Lemma (proved here).** Let (X,phi) be an infinite minimal Stone
system, and let PL(phi) be the group of piecewise linear
homeomorphisms of its suspension used by Matte Bon--Triestino. If
a,b belong to PL(phi) and aba^-1=b^2, then b=1. In particular every
homomorphism H -> PL(phi), and hence H -> T(phi), is trivial.

**The geometric input.** The suspension Y is compact and every flow
orbit is a dense copy of R. In particular Y is connected. Each g in
PL(phi) preserves flow orbits and has a unique continuous displacement
function tau_g:Y->R with g(y)=Phi_(tau_g(y))(y). Its leafwise local
form is (x,t)->(x,f(t)) with f piecewise linear. A finite chart cover
shows that g has only finitely many possible one-sided leafwise
slopes, all positive. These are exactly the properties of the
definition that the proof uses.

**Proof.** Suppose b!=1. If b has a fixed point p, its restriction to
the dense leaf through p is nontrivial: otherwise continuity would
make b trivial on all of Y. On that leaf choose an endpoint q of a
nonempty support component and the side inside that component. The
one-sided leafwise slope lambda at q is not 1, since b is locally
piecewise linear. Conjugation by a preserves the support and the side.
Writing lambda_j for the corresponding slope at a^j(q), the relation
aba^-1=b^2 gives

    lambda_j = lambda_(j+1)^2,
    lambda_j = lambda^(1/2^j)  (j>=0).

These are infinitely many distinct slopes, contradicting the finite
slope set of b. Thus b has no fixed point anywhere in Y.

The displacement tau_b is then nowhere zero, since the flow has no
periodic orbit. Connectedness and compactness give a constant sign
and epsilon>0 such that |tau_b(y)|>=epsilon for every y. Let
A=||tau_a||_infinity and B=||tau_b||_infinity. The cocycle rule for
displacement implies that a^k b a^-k has displacement bounded in
absolute value by 2kA+B. On the other hand, the constant sign of
tau_b gives

    |tau_(b^(2^k))(y)| >= 2^k epsilon

at every y. This contradicts a^k b a^-k=b^(2^k) for large k. Hence
b=1. QED.

The circle and compact-minimal-flow arguments were independently
checked by the E agent; the local PL and continuous-displacement
properties were then verified directly in the primary source. The
proof uses no assertion
about mixed identities, uniform simplicity, or unimodular closure.

For the source definition see Matte Bon--Triestino, *Groups of
piecewise linear homeomorphisms of flows*, arXiv:1811.12256v5,
Definition 3.1 (p.8) and the displacement description in Appendix A
(p.23). The original paper numbers pages starting at 1; the PDF
viewer has zero-based pages 7 and 22.

* https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.12256

**The other Hyde--Lodha--Rivas family.** The general form also applies
to G_lambda=<T-tilde,T-tilde_lambda>, lambda>1 irrational, from
arXiv:2102.02419, Theorem 1.0.1. In the proof of Corollary 1.0.2
(p.11), the authors give the following action on R^2, commuting with
Z^2, for g in the period-one lift of Thompson's T:

    eta_1(g)(x,y)=(x+(g(y)-y)/lambda, g(y)),
    eta_2(g)(x,y)=(g(x), y+lambda*(g(x)-x)).

The induced action on the torus preserves each leaf of the free
minimal irrational flow

    Phi_t(x,y)=(x+t/lambda,y+t) mod Z^2.

For eta_1 the displacement is g(y)-y, and for eta_2 it is
lambda*(g(x)-x). These are continuous functions on the torus.
The leafwise slopes of either generator are the slopes of g, of
which there are finitely many. Products and inverses preserve
continuous displacement, local finite piecewise linearity on leaves,
and finiteness of the possible slope values. The original G_lambda
action is its restriction to the leaf through the origin; this leaf
is dense, so the torus realization is faithful. The general lemma
therefore proves that H -> G_lambda is always trivial as well.

This conclusion is a deduction here from the explicit torus action,
not a quoted assertion of the HLR paper.

### 11.4. A stronger PL-line exclusion

The following argument subsumes the PL-line candidate exclusions
above and does not require a compact flow space.

Let BPL_+(R) denote the group of orientation-preserving, locally
finite piecewise linear homeomorphisms of R which are bi-Lipschitz.
The bi-Lipschitz constant can depend on the element. Locally finite
means that every compact interval contains only finitely many
breakpoints. This is a group, since products and inverses retain
these properties.

**Proposition (proved here).** Every homomorphism from the Higman
group H to BPL_+(R) is trivial. More precisely, if a,b,c belong to
BPL_+(R) and satisfy

    aba^-1=b^2,     cac^-1=a^2,

then b=1.

**Proof.** First suppose uvu^-1=v^2 in BPL_+(R), where v!=1
has a fixed point. Some component of its support has a finite
endpoint p. On the side inside that component, local finite piecewise
linearity gives a positive slope lambda!=1. The homeomorphism u
preserves the fixed set of v, the support, and the orientation of
its components. The same derivative calculation as above, now at
u^-k(p), gives

    v'_(side)(u^-k(p))=lambda^(2^k),  k>=0.

If lambda>1, these slopes are unbounded. If lambda<1, they tend to
zero. Either case contradicts the existence of positive lower and
finite upper slope bounds for the bi-Lipschitz homeomorphism v.
Thus a nontrivial v in such a relation must be fixed-point-free.

Return to the displayed relations and suppose b!=1. Then b is
fixed-point-free, so an orientation-preserving topological change of
coordinates takes it to t->t+1 or t->t-1. In these coordinates the
first relation gives in either case

    a(t+1)=a(t)+2.

Consequently a(t+n)-(t+n)=a(t)-t+n for every integer n. This has
both positive and negative values, so continuity gives a fixed point
of a. The second relation, together with the preceding endpoint
argument, implies a=1. The first relation then gives b=b^2, hence
b=1, a contradiction. QED.

Every Higman generator is the b-term of one such pair of successive
relations, proving the assertion for H. Therefore a group normally
generated by an image of H has no nontrivial map to BPL_+(R) or any
of its subgroups.

For the applications above, a locally finite PL map with a finite
global set of positive slopes is bi-Lipschitz. This is immediate for
the period-one and period-lambda lifts of T and is preserved by
products. It is also supplied by the compact finite-chart definition
for restrictions of PL(phi) to a dense leaf. Thus the new argument
simultaneously covers G_lambda and the minimal-flow groups T(phi).
The finitely presented Q_n have faithful periodic PL line actions,
and the fast-ring H_n admit lifts of their finite-breakpoint PL circle
actions. The original G_rho have bounded sets of slopes in their
locally finite PL actions (their finite generating maps have finitely
many slopes), or alternatively are already excluded by their MIF
property.

The proof was independently checked by the E agent and sent to the
root agent.
The local finite and bi-Lipschitz hypotheses are substantive:
arbitrary orientation-preserving line homeomorphisms admit faithful
Higman actions, while even finite-breakpoint PL line maps can realize
one nontrivial relation aba^-1=b^2 through dilation and translation.
The proposition uses two consecutive relations, not just one.

### 11.5. Limits of the one-dimensional exclusions

Darbinyan--Steenbock, *Embeddings into left-orderable simple groups*,
JLMS 105 (2022), 2011--2045, Theorem 1, embeds every countable
left-orderable group into a finitely generated simple left-orderable
group. In particular the faithful line action of Higman's group gives
such a simple overgroup containing Higman. Their enlarged flow groups
T(G,phi) allow arbitrary local homeomorphisms, with countably many
breakpoints in their chart convention. They do not satisfy the local
finite PL hypothesis used in Section 11.4. Thus that section cannot
be promoted to an exclusion of all simple left-orderable groups.
The source supplies no assertion of unimodular closure or pointwise
finiteness of the canonical norm for these overgroups.

## 12. A countable simple MI boundary example with finite, unbounded N

This section proves that mixed identities and pointwise finite
canonical N, even in a countable non-torsion simple group, do **not**
imply uniform simplicity. It does not supply unimodular closure.

### 12.1. A concrete tree group

Let Q be the finite multigraph with vertex set {0,1,2}, with two
edges between 0 and 1, three between 0 and 2, and four between 1 and
2. Let T be its universal covering tree, and color vertices by their
image in Q. Thus the color code is

    a = [[0,2,3], [2,0,4], [3,4,0]].

The three vertex degrees are 5,6,7. Let A be the full group of
color-preserving automorphisms of T, and G=A^+ the subgroup generated
by the pointwise edge stabilizers. The coloring is normal (A is
transitive on each color), and A has Tits' independence property.
All nonzero code entries are at least 2, so A preserves neither a
proper nonempty subtree nor an end. G is nontrivial and simple,
and its action has the same minimality and absence of invariant end.
These facts are the Tits theorem and its normal-subgroup lemma,
recalled and used in Gismatullin, arXiv:0905.0913v6, Section 2,
especially Proposition 2.8 and the paragraph immediately preceding
Proposition 2.9 (PDF pp.6--8).

One can also see nontrivial half-tree pointwise stabilizers directly:
at a vertex, interchange two same-color branches away from a chosen
incident edge, using the rooted color-preserving isomorphism between
the branches. Extend by the identity elsewhere. The resulting
nonidentity element fixes one half-tree pointwise and belongs to an
edge stabilizer, hence to G.

The action is of general type: a minimal tree action without an
invariant end or line has two hyperbolic elements with disjoint pairs
of fixed ends. The tree is not a line, and an invariant line would
contradict minimality. Fix two such elements for later use.

For g!=1, let rho_g(f) be the word length in the conjugacy classes
of g and g^-1, and put N_G(f)=sup_(g!=1) rho_g(f). Define ell(f)
to be the least number of factors from pointwise half-tree
stabilizers whose product is f. This is finite: each edge stabilizer
is a product of the two complementary half-tree stabilizers, by
independence, and G is generated by edge stabilizers.

Gismatullin's Proposition 2.8 proves that, for every nonidentity
g in G, every element of every pointwise half-tree stabilizer has
rho_g-length at most 4. Indeed, if g is hyperbolic the bound is 2;
if g is elliptic, a product of two conjugates of g is hyperbolic,
after which the former bound gives 4. Consequently

    N_G(f) <= 4 ell(f) < infinity   for every f in G.

However G is not uniformly simple. Theorem 3.11 of the same source
(PDF p.15) would otherwise make T a uniform subdivision of a
biregular tree. T has no vertices of degree 2 and has three distinct
vertex degrees 5,6,7, making this impossible. Hence N_G is globally
unbounded. For every integer m>=1 choose f_m,g_m in G, with g_m!=1,
such that rho_(g_m)^G(f_m)>m.

### 12.2. Extraction of a countable example

The automorphism group of the countable tree T is second countable
for the topology of pointwise convergence on vertices; so is G,
as a subspace. Choose a countable dense subset D of G. Start with
the countable subgroup E_0 generated by D, all the pairs f_m,g_m,
the two transverse hyperbolic elements above, and one nonidentity
pointwise half-tree stabilizer element a.

Given a countable E_n, for each pair f,g in E_n with g!=1 choose
in G an expression of f as a product of at most 4 ell(f) conjugates
of g or g^-1. Let E_(n+1) be generated by E_n and all conjugators
in these countably many finite expressions. Let E be the union of
the ascending sequence E_n. This is a countable group. Each pair
f,g in E appears together in one E_n, so

    rho_g^E(f) <= 4 ell(f),     N_E(f) <= 4 ell(f) < infinity.

In particular every nonidentity element normally generates E, and
E is simple. Also rho_(g_m)^E(f_m) >= rho_(g_m)^G(f_m)>m, since an
expression using conjugators in E is an expression in G. Therefore
N_E is unbounded. The seeded hyperbolic elements have infinite
order, so E is not a torsion group.

The E-action on T is faithful and minimal. For minimality, density
gives exactly the same vertex orbits as G: if u,v are vertices and
g(u)=v, the nonempty open set {h in G:h(u)=v} meets E. Every
E-invariant subtree consequently has G-invariant vertex set.
The two seeded hyperbolic elements make the action of general type.
The element a pointwise fixes a half-tree, so the boundary action
is not topologically free.

Fima--Le Maitre--Moon--Stalder, *High transitivity for more groups
acting on trees*, Theorem B, states that for a faithful minimal
general-type action of a countable group on a tree, mixed-identity
freeness is equivalent to topological freeness on the boundary.
It follows that E satisfies a nontrivial mixed identity.

This proves the announced example. Notice that the witness
construction did not assume that a dense subgroup automatically
inherits normal-generation bounds: it inserted every required
conjugator explicitly. Nor was non-uniform simplicity merely
inherited from the ambient group: it was forced by the seeded bad
pairs and monotonicity of conjugacy word length under subgroup
inclusion.

### 12.3. A variant containing any prescribed countable group

Replace the code by

    a = [[0,2,3], [2,0,4], [aleph0,aleph0,0]].

This code again defines a normally colored countable tree: the code
existence criterion only requires a(i,j)=0 iff a(j,i)=0 and
connectedness of the color-adjacency graph; symmetry of the
nonzero cardinal entries is not required. Its vertex degrees are
5,6,aleph0. Every nonzero code entry is at least 2. All preceding
simplicity, finite-N, unbounded-N and mixed-identity arguments apply.
In particular it is not a uniform subdivision of a biregular tree.

At a color-2 vertex v, there are countably infinitely many
isomorphic rooted branches whose first vertex has color 0. Choose
identifications of these rooted branches with one reference branch.
Each permutation of this countable family defines a coherent tree
automorphism, fixing v and every branch whose first vertex has color
1. This gives an embedded copy of Sym(N) inside a pointwise edge
stabilizer, hence inside G. Every countable group K embeds in
Sym(N), by its regular action (and harmless extra fixed points when
K is finite). Include this copy of K in E_0 in the extraction above.

We have therefore proved: for every countable K, there is a
countable simple non-torsion group E containing K, satisfying a
mixed identity and with N_E finite at every point but unbounded
globally. In particular K may be Higman's group. This shows that
the Higman-image restriction does not salvage the implication
``MI + pointwise finite N => uniform simplicity''.

The locally finite version cannot contain a nontrivial Higman image:
the CD agent subsequently proved the elementary adjacent-fixed-set
and minimal-prime argument in sections/CD_E_Higman_tree.tex, audited
independently by the root and E agents. This is a locally finite
tree statement, not property FA: Higman does act nontrivially on its
non-locally-finite amalgam tree. The infinite-valence variant here
is consistent with that obstruction.

### 12.4. An explicit unimodular equation missing in these examples

Both versions can be chosen to fail unimodular closure explicitly.
At a vertex v with at least three same-color neighboring branches,
choose coherent swaps a=(12), b=(13) of three rooted branches,
fixing every other branch. Both a and b lie in G, since each fixes
pointwise an incident edge. Include them in E_0. They are involutions.

Let c=ba. It permutes those three branches cyclically, fixes v, and
fixes every other branch pointwise. Hence Fix(c) has exactly one
boundary vertex, namely v: v is the unique fixed vertex adjacent
to a nonfixed vertex. Suppose z^3=c in Aut(T). Then z commutes with
c, preserves Fix(c), and therefore fixes v. Its permutation sigma
of the neighbors of v would satisfy sigma^3=the single 3-cycle
induced by c. This is impossible even on an infinite neighbor set:
sigma commutes with its cube, so preserves the three-element
support of that cube; no permutation of three elements has cube
equal to a 3-cycle. Therefore c has no cube root in Aut(T).

Consider the one-variable equation

    x a x a^-1 x^-1 = b.

Its exponent sum in x is 1. Its left side is (xa)x(xa)^-1, so any
solution x would be conjugate to b and satisfy x^2=1. Since a^2=1,
the displayed equation then gives

    (xa)^3 = ba = c,

contradicting the preceding root obstruction. Thus this equation
has no solution in the entire tree automorphism group, and in
particular no solution in G or E. The root and E agents independently
checked this proof.

### 12.5. Exact scope and remaining gap

More generally, one-variable unimodular closure forces an odd-root
condition on every product of two involutions. If a^2=b^2=1 and
n=2k+1, solve

    (xa)^k x (xa)^(-k) = b.

The left side is conjugate to x and has exponent sum one, so a
solution has x^2=1 and satisfies (xa)^(2k+1)=ba. Thus ba has an
n-th root z=xa inverted by a, and when N is defined,
N(z)<=N(a)+N(b), since x is conjugate to b. This is a useful
necessary condition, but says nothing when the target is torsion-free.

These are rigorous boundary examples, not solutions of E2. They
show that the unimodular-closure hypothesis is doing substantive
work in the proposed residual classification, even after imposing
countability, simplicity, a mixed identity, pointwise finite and
globally unbounded N, non-torsion, and a faithful Higman subgroup.

The obstruction in Section 12.4 excludes every subgroup containing
the two coherent swaps a,b. It does not exclude every subgroup of
these tree automorphism groups: an arbitrary torsion-free subgroup
need not contain either involution. No claim here proves or refutes
existence of a countable simple unimodularly closed subgroup with
the other residual properties.
