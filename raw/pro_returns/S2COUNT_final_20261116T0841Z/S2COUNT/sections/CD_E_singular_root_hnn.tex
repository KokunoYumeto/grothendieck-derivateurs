\paragraph{A prescribed-map obstruction using one additional homology class.}
Let $P$ be perfect and admit an actual two-dimensional classifying space.
Let $q:P\twoheadrightarrow E$ be an epimorphism to a countable group,
and choose $h\in P$ with $q(h)\ne1$. Enumerate $E$ as $(e_n)_{n\ge0}$
and choose $c_n\in P$ with $q(c_n)=e_n$. There is an inclusion
$P\hookrightarrow G$ into a perfect group with an actual two-dimensional
classifying space such that
\[
 H_2(G;\mathbb Z)\cong H_2(P;\mathbb Z)\oplus\mathbb Z
\]
and $q$ does not extend to a homomorphism $G\to E$.
Moreover, $P$ normally generates $G$.
Here and below $[u,v]=uvu^{-1}v^{-1}$.

\begin{proof}
Start with $P*\langle a\rangle$ and adjoin $y_0$ with
\[
 h=[a,y_0],\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
 y_0a^{-1}y_0^{-1}=a^{-1}h.
\]
Both $a^{-1}$ and $a^{-1}h$ have infinite order in the free product,
as detected by the exponent sum of $a$. This is an HNN extension
identifying two infinite cyclic subgroups, so its graph-of-spaces
classifying model has dimension two and contains the old model.
Successively adjoin $y_{n+1}$ with
\[
 y_n=[a c_n^{-1},y_{n+1}].
\]
Writing $k_n=a c_n^{-1}$, this relation is
\[
 y_{n+1}k_n^{-1}y_{n+1}^{-1}=k_n^{-1}y_n.
\]
The element $k_n^{-1}$ is nontrivial and has infinite order in
$P*\langle a\rangle$. The word $k_n^{-1}y_n$ has the latest stable
letter $y_n$ exactly once, and every nonzero power is reduced as a
word in the preceding HNN extension. Britton's normal form therefore
makes it infinite order as well. Thus this is again an HNN extension
along infinite cyclic subgroups. All the old groups inject.

Let $L$ be the union of these stages. The compatible graph-of-spaces
models form an increasing union of two-dimensional CW complexes.
Every sphere and every nullhomotopy relevant to its homotopy groups
is contained in a finite subcomplex and hence in a finite stage.
Consequently the union is an actual two-dimensional $K(L,1)$.

The relative cellular complex over $BP$ has new one-cells
$a,y_0,y_1,\ldots$. The root relation has zero exponent vector on
these cells, and the relation with left side $y_n$ has boundary
exactly $y_n$. Hence
\[
 H_2(BL,BP;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z,\qquad
 H_1(BL,BP;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\langle a\rangle.
\]
Since $P$ is perfect, the exact sequence of this pair gives
$H_1(L;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\langle a\rangle$ and a split exact
sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow H_2(P;\mathbb Z)
 \longrightarrow H_2(L;\mathbb Z)
 \longrightarrow\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0.
\]
Adjoin a stable letter $t_0$ with
\[
 t_0 a t_0^{-1}=h.
\]
Both endpoints have infinite order, since $a$ is in the initial free
product and $h\ne1$ belongs to the torsion-free group $P$.
This HNN extension kills the primitive class $a$ in first homology
and adds the primitive class $t_0$. The Mayer--Vietoris map from the
first homology of the edge circle to $H_1(L)$ is an isomorphism;
thus second homology is unchanged. Now successively adjoin $t_{n+1}$
with
\[
 t_n=[h,t_{n+1}],\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
 t_{n+1}h^{-1}t_{n+1}^{-1}=h^{-1}t_n.
\]
The left endpoint has infinite order in $P$ and the right endpoint
has exponent one in the latest stable letter $t_n$. Each step is
therefore a cyclic HNN extension. Its edge homology map kills the
primitive class $t_n$ and is injective, so $H_2$ is unchanged and the
new $H_1$ is generated by $t_{n+1}$. Let $G$ be the union. The maps
on first homology are zero, and hence $H_1(G)=0$, while
$H_2(G)=H_2(P)\oplus\mathbb Z$. The preceding argument with the
increasing union of actual two-dimensional classifying spaces applies
again.

If $f:G\to E$ extended $q$, then $f(a)=e_n$ for some $n$. Thus
$f(k_n)=1$ and the $n$th chain relation forces $f(y_n)=1$.
The earlier relations force $f(y_{n-1})=\cdots=f(y_0)=1$.
Finally $q(h)=f([a,y_0])=1$, a contradiction.

In the quotient by the normal closure of $P$, the element $h$ is
trivial. The first $t_0$ relation makes $a$ trivial; then all the
$y_n$ relations make the $y_n$ trivial. The $t_n$ relations make
every $t_n$ trivial as well. Thus this quotient is trivial, proving
normal generation by $P$.
\end{proof}

Each repetition over a perfect old group adds the displayed independent
relative second-homology class. Thus it does not by itself give a
transfinite obstruction construction with second homology of rank one.
