## Rational unipotent resolution

H5.9 proves that the precise finite-dimensional rational unipotent localizer is nonaccessible when the universe has no measurable cardinal, whereas Vopenka for the universe makes it accessible. Thus the requested uniform affirmative accessibility lead fails; the answer has the same kind of set-theoretic dependence as H.2/H.3. The counterexample uses the Heisenberg group over R_k=Q^k/(functions supported on fewer than k coordinates). Every allowed representation kills the center: a nonzero central image would produce a finite-rank form f(rs), hence a finite-dimensional algebra quotient, hence a Q-valued algebra homomorphism and a nonprincipal countably complete ultrafilter. The least-partition proof locates the resulting measurable cardinal inside the same universe. For any proposed generating set, take regular k above its sizes: the Heisenberg group is a k-filtered colimit of products of the allowed test H(Q), making it local for those generators, although its center-killing W-equivalence is not inverted by mapping into itself. Primary sources Milne2022 Propositions14.32/14.36, Bardyla--Supina--Zdomskyy2026 Theorem1.1, and Cisinski2006 Scholium4.2.17 were opened. The key ring and ultrafilter steps are fully rederived.

## General factorability resolved by a bounded test

E.8 replaces the unbounded existence problem by a catalogue depending only on the base and lambda=max(input simplex cardinalities,aleph0). Its members are all lambda-sized Kan maps whose ordinary fiber homology modules are exhausted by the explicit upper central filtration at lambda+. There are at most 2^lambda members. Given a desired factorization, a countable closure under finite horn fillers and finite integral chains bounding cycles, both in the total and in every vertex fiber, produces such a bounded factorization. Total homology stays equal to that of X; fiber homology injects into the original hypercentral modules.

For each catalogue member V→B and each equivalence LX≃LV over LB, the test asks for a coherent section of a family whose fiber is hofib[hofib(V→LV)→hofib(B→LB)] at the specified point and path. The theorem gives this formula precisely, identifies the section space with a homotopy limit, and explains the skeletal obstruction test. It retains all coherence; individual nonempty fibers or solvability at every finite stage alone do not suffice. A section gives a map X→V whose localization is the chosen equivalence and therefore is an HZ-equivalence. Conversely every factorization supplies a section.

Strict Cat realization is established using cSd²Ex² and the natural counit QX→X, then an ordinary Thomason cofibration factorization, a left-proper pushout, and a final ordinary fibration replacement. This uses actual slice diagrams and Cisinski Theorem5.2.12, which has been read. The original Thomason Theorem4.9 and Corollary5.5 were also opened. No identification of Ho(Arr) with Arr(Ho) is used.

E.8.1 proves: every map to connected Y factors iff H1(Y;Z)=0; equivalently the point map does. For H1Y=0 an explicit plus construction makes the localization unit acyclic and E=Y×^h_LY LX gives the factor. With both endpoints HZ-local, factorability is exactly the hypercentral homotopy-fiber criterion. General factorability forces componentwise H1-surjectivity, but S1∨S1→S1 is a counterexample to its sufficiency.

Root's proposed identical-product candidate also collapses. If Z is connected with finite pi_n in every degree, then Z^I is the filtered homotopy colimit of its finite powers indexed by finite partitions of I. This follows on homotopy groups because every function to a finite group has finite image. Both Sigma BZ/2 and Omega Sigma BZ/2 are ordinarily BZ/2-acyclic, the latter by the James construction, so all their identical powers are ordinarily acyclic. Varying factors with unbounded exponents in a fixed homotopy degree need not remain in the primary endpoint; for example product_i Sigma BZ/p^i has non-torsion H2.

These results close specific proposed families. They do not prove the full primary interval has only two elements, nor settle the general fixed-stable-part set-size question.
