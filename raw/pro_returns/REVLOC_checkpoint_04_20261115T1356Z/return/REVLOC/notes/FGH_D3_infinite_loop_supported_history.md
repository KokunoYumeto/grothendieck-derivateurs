# Earlier supported-only statement and complete proof

Preserved before the divisible-bottom extension. The earlier open limitation below is superseded by the current D.3.8, not silently discarded.

```latex
\begin{claim}{D.3.8}{Proved}{A fixed stable part and a lowest degree determine the supported infinite-loop case}
Fix a prime $p$ and an integer $n\geq1$.  Let $\{X_i\}_{i\in I}$
be a set or class of connected infinite-loop spaces such that every
$X_i$ is $(n-1)$-connected and has $p$-primary torsion homotopy
groups.  Assume that
\[
                  H_n(X_j;\mathbb F_p)\ne0
                  \quad\text{for at least one }j\in I.
 \tag{D.3.8a}
\]
Put
\[
 \mathcal L=\loc\{\Sigma^\infty X_i:i\in I\},\qquad
 W=\min\mathrm{Rev}\{X_i\to *:i\in I\}.
\]
Then $T(W)=\mathcal L$, and
\[
 W=\min\mathrm{Rev}
   \{\Omega^\infty E\to *:
                         E\in\mathcal L\cap\Ho(\Sp)_{\geq n}\}.
 \tag{D.3.8b}
\]
Consequently, for fixed $p$ and $\mathcal L$, there are at most
countably many reversible localizers admitting such a presentation,
as $n$ varies.  If $\mathcal L$ is generated by a small set of
spectra, each such localizer is accessible even when the original
family $I$ is a class.  The hypothesis (D.3.8a) excludes a family
whose first nonzero integral homology is entirely $p$-divisible;
no conclusion for that remaining case is asserted here.
\end{claim}

\begin{proof}
Choose for each $i$ a connective spectrum $E_i$ giving its indicated
infinite-loop structure, with $X_i\simeq\Omega^\infty E_i$.
Since $X_i$ is connected and $(n-1)$-connected, its connective
choice has $E_i\in\Ho(\Sp)_{\geq n}$.  The homotopy groups are
$p$-primary, so $E_i[1/p]=0$.  The corresponding reduced homology
of $X_i$ is $p$-primary as well, either by the nilpotent localization
criterion or by its Postnikov tower degree by degree.

The exact space--spectrum interface is
\citet[Theorem~2.10 and Proposition~3.1]{Bousfield1996ULP}:
for a space $A$ and a connective spectrum $E$,
\[
 P_A\Omega^\infty E\simeq
          \Omega^\infty P_{\Sigma^\infty A}^{\Sp}E,
 \tag{D.3.8c}
\]
and $A$ kills $\Omega^\infty E$ in ordinary nullification if and
only if $\Sigma^\infty A$ kills $E$ in spectral nullification.
In particular, since $X_i$ kills itself, $E_i$ belongs to the
spectral nullification kernel generated by $\Sigma^\infty X_i$,
and hence to $\mathcal L$.  Each $X_i$ is therefore among the
generators on the right of (D.3.8b).  This proves one inclusion.

First suppose $I$ is small and let $A=\bigvee_{i\in I}X_i$.
Then $C=\Sigma^\infty A$ is $n$-connective, has $p$-primary
integral homology, and has $H_n(C;\mathbb F_p)\ne0$ by
(D.3.8a).  Also $\loc(C)=\mathcal L$.
The spectral conclusion of D.2.9 gives
$P_C^{\Sp}E=0$ for every
$E\in\mathcal L\cap\Ho(\Sp)_{\geq n}$.
Equation (D.3.8c) then makes $\Omega^\infty E$ ordinarily
$A$-acyclic, and consequently $W$-aspherical.  This proves the
opposite inclusion.  The use of ordinary nullification here is
justified by (D.3.8c); it is not an inference from stable
asphericity alone.

For a class $I$, every individual member of
$\loc\{\Sigma^\infty X_i:i\in I\}$ lies in the localizing
subcategory generated by a small subfamily.  Indeed the union
of those subcategories over all small subfamilies is itself
closed under shifts, cofibers and small coproducts: a small
collection of small subfamilies is contained in their small
union.  This union contains all the generators and therefore
is $\mathcal L$.  Given an individual
$E\in\mathcal L\cap\Ho(\Sp)_{\geq n}$, choose such a small
subfamily and adjoin the one witness $X_j$ from (D.3.8a).
The preceding argument applied to its wedge makes
$\Omega^\infty E$ ordinarily acyclic for that wedge, hence
$W$-aspherical.  This proves (D.3.8b) in the class case also.

The equality $T(W)=\mathcal L$ follows directly from minimality:
$R(\mathcal L)$ contains every specified aspherical generator,
so $W\subseteq R(\mathcal L)$ and $T(W)\subseteq\mathcal L$;
the suspension spectra of the original generators give the
reverse inclusion.  Formula (D.3.8b) depends only on
$(\mathcal L,n)$, proving the countable bound.

Finally suppose $\mathcal L=\loc\{F_a:a\in J\}$ for a small
set $J$.  Apply the small-subfamily observation to each $F_a$
and take the union of the resulting source subfamilies, together
with the witness $X_j$.  This is a small subfamily with the
same stable part and the same support bound.  Its reversible
closure equals $W$ by (D.3.8b), and B.4 makes it accessible.
\end{proof}

This fixes the possible number of supported infinite-loop
presentations of a given stable part.  It neither determines
which of the countably many displayed localizers coincide nor
decides the adjacent strictness test in D.2.9.  In particular,
it does not replace the nonlinear suspension-nullification
test needed for the zero-finite-part separator.

```
