\subsection{A restriction on monotone maps to support-lex cuts}

\begin{lemma}[Proved: monotone cuts follow a common initial flag]
\label{G:monotone-lex-cuts}
Let $E\le F,D$ be finite strong objects of $\Cdelta$, fix an
embedding of $E$ in $M_0$, and let $\Omega_F,\Omega_D$ be the
sets of strong embeddings extending it. Give these sets the
signed lexicographic orders determined by flags
\[
 E=A_0\subsetneq A_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq A_d=F,
 \quad A_i=\operatorname{cl}_F(A_{i-1}\cup\{a_i\}),
 \quad \epsilon_i\in\{+1,-1\},
\]
and
\[
 E=B_0\subsetneq B_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq B_e=D,
 \quad B_i=\operatorname{cl}_D(B_{i-1}\cup\{b_i\}),
 \quad \eta_i\in\{+1,-1\}.
\]
Suppose $f\mapsto C_f$ is a $G_E$-equivariant map from
$\Omega_F$ to initial segments of $\Omega_D$, monotone for
inclusion. Then it is constantly empty, constantly full, or
has one of the following finite boundary descriptions.

There is a common initial flag of length $j$: strong
isomorphisms $h_i:B_i\to A_i$, fixing $E$, compatible under
restriction, with $h_i(b_i)=a_i$ and
$\eta_i=\epsilon_i$ for $1\le i\le j$. The boundary of
$C_f$ follows the target prefix $f h_j$, and then either:
\begin{enumerate}
\item it is immediately before or immediately after the
entire fiber of extensions of $f h_j$; here $j\ge1$, and
when $j=e$ the fiber is a single point; or
\item $j<d,e$, $\eta_{j+1}=\epsilon_{j+1}$, and its next
threshold is $f(a_{j+1})$ in the $b_{j+1}$-coordinate.
In this case $B_{j+1}$ and $A_{j+1}$ are not isomorphic
over the identified preceding stage and distinguished new
pivot. Consequently no actual source and target embeddings
with that preceding prefix can have equal next pivots.
\end{enumerate}
In the second case the source and target next pivots lie in
the same raw-order gap of the common preceding stage.
The descriptions include all target points lexicographically
before the specified prefix, with inequalities reversed at
the negative signs. The choice of before/after and all the
identifications are fixed, independent of $f$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix one source embedding and identify its image with $F$.
Its initial segment is invariant under $G_F$. We first give
a boundary normal form, using the smooth-cut lemma
\ref{G:smooth-lex-cuts}.

At the first target pivot, an invariant cut cannot split a
raw gap of $F$: every $G_F$-orbit in that gap is cofinal and
coinitial in its convex pivot cell. A nontrivial boundary is
therefore at a coordinate $c\in F$. If the target fiber with
pivot $c$ is empty, this is the boundary. If it is nonempty,
strong closure puts its entire first flag stage inside $F$,
and the cut either ends just before or after that fiber, or
continues inside it. In the latter case repeat the argument
at the next pivot, with the previous strong stage fixed.
After finitely many steps one reaches a whole-prefix-fiber
endpoint, a threshold coordinate at which the next fiber is
empty, or a principal cut at a full target embedding in $F$.
The empty and full cases are separate. Thresholds lying in
the preceding strong stage give endpoints of the permitted
next-pivot gap and are absorbed into a whole-prefix-fiber
endpoint. Thus an essential further threshold lies outside
that stage, in its permitted raw-order gap.

This normal form uses only one realized target flag prefix
inside $F$, followed if necessary by one coordinate of $F$.
Equivariance transports the same fixed code along every
source embedding. There is no choice varying with $f$:
all order relations among the finitely many parameter
coordinates are preserved by the source embeddings.

We now use monotonicity to determine this code. Its first
coordinate outside $E$ must equal $a_1$. Otherwise freely
amalgamate two copies of $F$ over $E$, placing corresponding
coordinates in disjoint small raw-order boxes, so that the
$a_1$-coordinates increase in sign $\epsilon_1$ while the
first boundary coordinates decrease in sign $\eta_1$.
These two independent prescriptions are possible because the
coordinates are distinct. The resulting source embeddings
increase, whereas their target cuts strictly decrease.
Strictness can be witnessed by a free target extension over
$E$ whose first pivot lies between the two threshold values;
both values are in its permitted gap. This contradicts
monotonicity. The same argument with the single varying
coordinate rules out opposite signs.

If this is a realized first target stage, unique strong
closure gives
\[
 h_1(B_1)=\operatorname{cl}(E\cup\{a_1\})=A_1.
\]
Restrict the argument to source copies agreeing on this
strong set. The first remaining boundary coordinate must
then be exactly $a_2$, with matching sign, by the same
independent perturbation in an ordered free amalgam over
$A_1$. Continuing proves that every realized target prefix
is the asserted common signed initial flag. The proof also
applies when the final moving boundary coordinate is only
a threshold: it must be the next source pivot with the
same sign.

If that threshold has the same pointed strong closure type
as the target next stage, the unique isomorphism realizes
that target stage inside $F$. Its fiber in $\Omega_D$ is
nonempty by strong extension, so the normal form continues
or stops at its endpoint. Thus the terminal empty-fiber
case occurs precisely with incompatible next closure types.
Equal actual next pivots would force the two closures to be
equal, which is impossible. This proves the alternatives.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope.}
For two chain components whose union is a chain, the set of
target predecessors of a source point is such a monotone
equivariant cut. The lemma therefore constrains their
interleaving. It does not construct a simultaneous ordered
template for a finite tree of components. In particular it
does not supply functorial values at unrealizable closure
ties, an exact indexing category, or compatibility with left
$K$-translations. Those remain separate gates.
