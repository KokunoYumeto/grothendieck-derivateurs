# Affine stabilizers and quotient homology (09:20 UTC)

This is exploratory work. The splitting lemma is being written independently by dynamics_totality as pending G28; no new theorem is asserted here before audit.

Let S be a smooth locally free Cdelta monoid, K=S^gp, T its G26 finite-parameter tree, and H=K semidirect G. A cut stabilizer H_c maps onto the open subgroup G_[c] stabilizing its K-orbit, with locally cyclic kernel K_c. The action on K_c is trivial: K_c injects K_ab and its rank-one subgroup is preserved by G_[c]; a nonzero vector has open stabilizer, so the action kernel is open normal and hence the whole open group. Thus H_c is a locally split central extension of G_[c] by K_c.

A local splitting over G_D is unique because open strong stabilizers have no nontrivial continuous characters into a discrete group. Unique splittings on all sufficiently large supports are compatible with restriction/conjugation. The lifted embedding category on the cofinal support cosieve has A-torsors of arrows, where A=K_c. Its base nerve is contractible; the resulting central categorical extension has pi1=A. Directed transport paths identify this group with H_c modulo the normal closure of local sections. Hence the kernel A injects in that quotient, yielding a unique global continuous splitting. This was reached independently by free_monoids and dynamics_totality and is being checked in full typed form.

The stronger claim that this section is K-conjugate to the standard G_[c] copy is not automatic. General continuous nonabelian H1 already fails for the interval monoid on a moving ordered orbit Ω: the signed-interval cocycle g↦[x0,gx0] is nontrivial by augmentation in Z[Ω]. It has no fixed bounded cut.

Potential bounded-cut counterexample: let D consist of an infinite moving antichain L below an infinite moving chain Ω, both smooth singleton orbits. Its interval monoid I(D) is locally free (finite Ω charts are finite directed stars merging into a chain). The affine interval-group action associated with a base object has no fixed group vertex, again because K_ab=aug Z[D] and no finite invariant vector has augmentation1. Nevertheless the join of any two distinct L vertices is the same nonprincipal ramification cut and is fixed under the affine action. To establish this inside T one must explicitly check the embedded D and its common upper sets in finite free charts. This is currently a candidate, not a registered theorem.

Regardless of conjugacy, the affine G_F section of H_c maps trivially in Γ=K_G: its composite into Γ is a homomorphism with open kernel (locally the section equals the original open G_c). No proper open normal subgroup of G_F exists. Consequently the image of H_c in Γ is the image of K_c, hence locally cyclic. This may be the right input to a quotient homology model.

Current homology obstacle: the interval tree is not simplicial. New cut stabilizers can be nontrivial cyclic groups, original vertices need not be dense in completion intervals, and an affine section need not give a standard fixed-group replacement. A correct proof must account for interval chains after H or its normal subgroup generated by G acts. No positive derived-colimit vanishing for arbitrary S has yet followed.
