# Canonical prefix-poset and finite-cut route (08:29 UTC)

This is an active proof-development note, not a theorem asserting a tree resolution.

Let S be a locally free monoid and K=S^gp. The natural map S→K is injective: a finite positive equality in K is witnessed in a later free chart and hence in S. The group K is locally free: for any fixed finite positive generating tuple, its image subgroups in later free-group charts are free of bounded rank; after the rank is minimal the surjections between those image groups are isomorphisms by Hopficity. Every finite subgroup-generating tuple in K is contained in one generated by positive elements.

Define x≤y in K iff x^{-1}y∈S. This is a left-invariant partial order. Its intervals are linearly ordered by Levi's lemma. It has finite (2,2) interpolation: if x1,x2≤y1,y2, put representatives of all four group vertices, all four positive increments, and the finitely many group equalities in a common free chart. In the directed Cayley tree of that free chart, the four positive paths have an interpolating vertex k. Its image satisfies xi≤k≤yj. Iteration gives finite interpolation.

The nerve of this poset is the universal-cover model of BS obtained from the action of S on K. It is contractible because BS→BK is a weak equivalence for a filtered union of free charts. The K action on original vertices is free, and any automorphism of S acts canonically. A loop consisting of finitely many positive or negative intervals has a finite cancellation certificate: lift its finite endpoint and increment data into one free chart, subdivide along that chart's Cayley tree, and cancel opposite identical intervals. Subdivision of an original interval is legitimate because it is linearly ordered, and its pieces cover it.

## Candidate completion -- not yet established as a tree

Inside the Dedekind–MacNeille completion, retain the original K vertices and close under bounded binary meets and joins, taking only finite terms. Each retained element has finitely many original parameters and lies between original lower and upper bounds. Consequently its stabilizer for a smooth external automorphism action is open. Intervals between original bounds should be the corresponding linear Dedekind completions; this must be proved carefully for the retained substructure. The hoped-for conclusion is a canonical median/order tree whose original positive intervals are preserved. The precise pretree axioms and gluing argument have not yet been completed.

## Stabilizer argument conditional on that tree realization

At a nonprincipal meet branch cut c, there cannot be two distinct incoming germs and two distinct outgoing germs. Choose original lower vertices x1,x2 in the incoming germs and original upper vertices y1,y2 in the outgoing germs. Interpolation gives an original k with xi≤k≤yj; in a tree this k must be c, contradicting nonprincipality. Thus a nonprincipal meet cut has a unique incoming germ, and a join cut has a unique outgoing germ. A stabilizer preserves the distinguished germ.

For an element g of its K-stabilizer, choose an original vertex x far enough along that germ that x and gx belong to one linearly ordered segment. Then x^{-1}gx or its inverse is in S. If g is in the kernel of K→K_ab=S_ab, positive nonvanishing forces g=1. Thus the stabilizer embeds in K_ab and is abelian. Local freeness of K then makes it locally cyclic. This conditional argument was independently audited by dynamics_totality; the completion hypothesis is essential and remains explicit.

## Homology gate after completion

Even a canonical smooth tree with locally cyclic cut stabilizers does not by itself prove the universal T1 assertion. Dense order-tree intervals have no canonical discrete edge basis. One still needs a functorial acyclic interval resolution compatible with products, or an explicit quotient homology model to which the bounded-rank coefficient theorem applies. Treating an abstract dense tree as a simplicial graph would be invalid.

## Literature checked

Opened Horak–Stein, arXiv:math/0503407v1, Definitions12–15 and22, Theorem5 statement, and the order-tree definitions in Section2. Their construction needs a rectifiable simply-connected formal extension of a partial order. Their “acyclic” partial-order condition is stronger than the raw K-prefix order: an ordinary directed free-group Cayley tree already has mixed incoming/outgoing branching. Thus their theorem cannot be applied without a separately constructed extension or blow-up. It also does not automatically give the required smooth equivariance or interval homology resolution.
