\begin{proposition}[Proved: a free-factor nerve-colimit obstruction]\label{C:freefactor-nerve}
There is a diagram of finitely generated free groups, with every
transition a free-factor inclusion and with free group colimit, whose
ordinary simplicial-set colimit of nerves has second integral homology
$\mathbb Z$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the category consisting of a parallel pair $f,g:0\rightrightarrows1$
and the diagram
\[
 G_0=F(a,c),\qquad G_1=F(a,c,b),\qquad
 f=(a,c),\quad g=(ab,cb^2).
\]
The automorphism $a\mapsto ab$, $c\mapsto cb^2$, $b\mapsto b$
has inverse $a\mapsto ab^{-1}$, $c\mapsto cb^{-2}$, $b\mapsto b$.
Thus both maps have free-factor image.

We claim that their image subgroups intersect only in the identity.
For a nonempty reduced word $w$ in $a,c$, consider $g(w)$ in the free
product $F(a,c)*F(b)$. A negative first letter gives a nonzero initial
$b$-block, and a positive last letter gives a nonzero terminal $b$-block.
In the remaining case, $w$ starts positively and ends negatively, so it
has a positive-to-negative transition. At such a transition the two
underlying generators differ, since $w$ is reduced. Its $b$-exponents
are therefore $1-2$ or $2-1$, both nonzero. These are the only places
where $b$-blocks from distinct letters can merge. No intervening
$a,c$ block reduces to the identity: it is a subword of the original
reduced word. Hence at least one nonzero $b$-block survives, and
$g(w)\notin F(a,c)$. This proves the claim for arbitrary word length.

Consequently $BG_0\vee BG_0\to BG_1$, induced by $f,g$, is a
simplicial monomorphism. Indeed its two images intersect in each nerve
degree only in the all-identity simplex. The ordinary coequalizer $Y$
of $Bf,Bg$ is the pushout of this monomorphism along the fold
$BG_0\vee BG_0\to BG_0$. This is a homotopy pushout: realizations of
simplicial monomorphisms are inclusions of CW subcomplexes, or one may
factor the fold through its mapping cylinder before pushing out.

Free groups have classifying spaces homotopy equivalent to graphs.
The Mayer--Vietoris sequence therefore gives
\[
 H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong
 \ker(f_{\mathrm{ab}}-g_{\mathrm{ab}}:\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z^3)
 =\mathbb Z(2a-c),
\]
since $(f_{\mathrm{ab}}-g_{\mathrm{ab}})(a)=-b$ and its value on $c$
is $-2b$. On the other hand,
\[
 \colim G=\langle a,c,b\mid a=ab,\ c=cb^2\rangle\cong F(a,c).
\]
The first relation kills $b$, and the second is redundant. Thus the
comparison $Y\to B(\colim G)$ cannot be a weak equivalence.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope of the obstruction.}
The parallel-pair indexing category is not quasi-filtered and is not
total. This example does not refute totality-to-exactness or establish
anything about the totality of AKR. It rules out deriving asphericity
of an ordinary nerve colimit solely from free values, free-factor
transitions and a free group colimit.

The program \texttt{finite\_freefactor\_nerve.py} checks the Nielsen
inverses, exact word identities certifying the group quotient, and the
integer Mayer--Vietoris matrix. The latter has a unit $3\times3$ minor,
all $4\times4$ minors zero, and primitive kernel generator
$(2,-1,-2,1)$. Its additional check of all $39{,}364$ reduced nonempty
words of length at most nine is supplementary; the proof above is
unbounded.
