\begin{lemma}[Proved: exact fixed subsets of the repaired copy maps]
\label{C:beta-fixed-subsets}
Consider a repaired attachment for injections $f,g:S\rightrightarrows T$
between finite sets. Assume that the partial relation
$f(x)\mapsto g(x)$ has no nontrivial directed cycles.
Let $F=\{f(x):f(x)=g(x)\}$ and write the nonconstant directed paths of
the partial relation $f(x)\mapsto g(x)$ as
\[
 p_{\nu,0}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow p_{\nu,l_\nu-1}.
\]
Here $l_\nu$ is the number of vertices of the path, not its number of
edges. Put $L=\max_\nu l_\nu$, or $L=1$ when there are no such paths.
For $s=4k+1$ and $k\geq n\geq L$, let
$\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_{4k}:T\to Z$ be the repaired copy maps.
For $0\leq i<j\leq4k$, their set of fixed source vertices is exactly:
\begin{enumerate}
\item if $(i,j)=(r,4k-r)$ with $0\leq r\leq L-2$, it is
\[
 F\ \cup\ \{p_{\nu,t}:0\leq t\leq l_\nu-r-2\};
\]
\item if $(i,j)=(2k-r,2k+1+r)$ with $0\leq r\leq L-2$, it is
\[
 F\ \cup\ \{p_{\nu,t}:r+1\leq t\leq l_\nu-1\};
\]
\item for all other pairs it is $F$.
\end{enumerate}
An empty index range contributes no points. Thus at most $2L-1$
distinct fixed subsets have to be tested, independently of $s$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fixed points in $F$ are shared by every copy; private points are shared
by no two distinct copies. On a path of length $l$, write $t$ for its
zero-based position. Its repaired coordinate is
\[
 \beta_i(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 t+i,&i\leq2k,\ t+i\leq2k,\\
 t+i+2k-l+1,&i\leq2k,\ t+i>2k,\\
 t-i+6k-l+2,&i>2k,\ t-i+6k-l+2>2k,\\
 t-i+4k,&i>2k,\ t-i+6k-l+2\leq2k.
 \end{cases}
\]
For fixed $t$, this expression is strictly increasing on the first
arm and strictly decreasing on the second arm: within each branch its
step is respectively $1$ or $-1$, and at a branch transition the jump
has the same sign because $l\leq k$. Thus an equality between distinct
copies must use $i\leq2k<j$.

If both values lie in the lower branch, equality says $i+j=4k$.
Writing $i=r$, the branch inequalities reduce exactly to
$t\leq l-r-2$. If both lie in the upper branch, equality says
$i+j=4k+1$. Writing $i=2k-r$, the inequalities reduce to $t\geq r+1$.
For the first-lower/second-upper case, equality would give
$i+j=6k-l+2$; the lower inequality forces
$j\geq t+4k-l+2$, contrary to the upper inequality. For the
first-upper/second-lower case, equality would give
$i+j=2k+l-1$, whereas the lower inequality implies
$j\geq t+4k-l+2$, impossible since $l\leq k$.
These calculations give precisely the two displayed families, path by
path. Different paths have different tags in the repaired target.
Only $l\leq k$ was used, so the calculation applies to every admissible
common bound $n$ and every actual path size $l\leq n\leq k$; no
restriction to the tested size-three examples is involved.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Proved: the small-overlap case is harmless]
\label{C:beta-small-overlap}
Assume that $f$ and $g$ induce injections on binary graph components
and that their underlying point images meet in at most one point.
Then every equalizer subgraph of a pair of copy maps
$\alpha_i,\alpha_j:T\to Z$ is connected in each component of $G_T$.
Thus the fixed-path criterion applies to a subsequent attachment for
any such copy pair, even when $K(T)/F(T)$ is nonzero.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
An internal vertex of a nonconstant point path belongs to
$f(S)\cap g(S)$. Hence every path has length at most three, at most
one has length three, and a fixed point cannot coexist with a
length-three path. In Lemma~\ref{C:beta-fixed-subsets}, the first
prefix and suffix are respectively $f(S)$ and $g(S)$, including the
fixed points. All remaining subsets have at most one point.
The image of each source graph component is connected, and injection
on components prevents two distinct source components from lying
in one ambient component. This proves the claim for the two full
images; the singleton and empty cases are immediate. The equalizer
graph is induced on its fixed vertices by the established AKR
two-point routing property, as in
Proposition~\ref{C:fixed-path-kernel}.
\end{proof}
