# Exact fixed-path kernel for a repaired AKR attachment

New proposition `C:fixed-path-kernel` is written separately in
`sections/akr_attachment_fixed_paths.tex` for audit before inclusion.

## Statement

For a single repaired quasi-filter attachment for `f,g:S→T`, the entire
kernel on `colim K` is generated by classes `[(g-f)σ]`, where `σ` is a
graph path in `G_S` between vertices on which `f` and `g` agree. The
class is independent of the selected path modulo old colimit relations.
Its only relevant endpoint data are the components of the fixed
subgraph inside a common source component.

This proves that injectivity of `π0 Fix(f,g)→π0 G_S` suffices to preserve
the old second homology, without any assumption that `H(f)` or `H(g)`
is injective. Conversely, disconnected fixed components are only a
potential obstruction: their explicit path-difference classes must be
computed in the old colimit.

## Proof mechanism

The cell fibers over a repaired target are paths or circles. For
vertices this follows from (10), (11), and the fact that every point of
the prescribed targets is reached by a V-arrow. For W-edges, apply the
proved AKR routing invariant to every copy intersection, then again to
each adjacent overlap. Circular fibers are precisely fixed cells; the
neighbor word has net displacement one, and injectivity cancels the
two halves explicitly.

There is consequently a degreewise exact sequence of graph chain
complexes:

`0 → C(Fix) → C(G_S)^s → C(G_T)^s → C(G_Z) → 0`.

The first map is diagonal and the middle map is neighboring incidence.
The two associated homology sequences reduce its extra cycle relations
to `ker(H0 Fix→H0 G_S)`. A path lifts an endpoint difference; forgetting
copy indices sums the neighboring legs to `g-f`, by the signed count
one. Existing source-cycle relations already vanish in the old
colimit. The earlier exact attachment criterion then proves equality,
not merely one inclusion, for the claimed kernel.

## Scope

The proposition applies to the prescribed repaired path targets,
including all admissible numerical parameters and all relabellings.
Its point-coverage induction is explicit. It makes no unproved claim
about enlarged targets with arbitrary unused points, or arbitrary
injection diagrams satisfying only numerical counting inequalities.

The all-stage C.1 assertion is still unresolved. No finite test is
used as an induction. The next concrete task is the fixed-subgraph test
on the point-pair K2 branch, which has a nonzero quotient `M=K/F` and
therefore reaches cases absent from the earlier selected substages.

## Further all-stage local lemmas

`sections/akr_protected_intersections.tex` proves two additional claims.

1. Root's strengthened protected routing: two nonadjacent marked
   vertices in an arrow image force the entire protected arrow to
   factor. The complete induction through neighboring copies is
   written out. Distinct protected-cell boundaries therefore meet
   only in an empty set, a vertex, or one edge.
2. Two-image saturation: a protected-cell chain supported in the union
   of two arrow image graphs uses only protected cells factoring
   through one of the two arrows. For the remaining cells the weighted
   bound outside the first image, minus at most two points per cell
   from the second, gives more than half their marked incidences
   outside the union; a private outside boundary edge contradicts the
   proposed chain support. This works over every abelian coefficient
   group.

Neither is a statement about equality only after taking colim K. That
is the exact unresolved distinction in trying to apply these local
lemmas to the fixed-path kernel.
