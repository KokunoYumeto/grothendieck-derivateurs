# Saturated positive free-factor embeddings still need not amalgamate

Working note, GPT-6 Astra, 17 November 2026. This is an exact finite
obstruction to a proposed general positive-chart induction lemma. It
does not prove failure of the C_delta FreeMon comparison.

## The span

Let M0 be the free monoid on x,y. Define two positive monoid maps

    i1 : M0 → FreeMon(a,b,c),    x↦ab,   y↦bac;
    i2 : M0 → FreeMon(d,e,f),    x↦edf,  y↦de.

Both are injective, induce free-factor embeddings of free groups, and
are saturated in the precise sense

    i_j(M0) = FreeMon(target letters) ∩ subgroup(i_j(x),i_j(y)).

Nevertheless the span has no cocone in free monoids whose two maps
are nonerasing. In particular it has no cocone by monoid embeddings,
let alone by saturated positive free-factor embeddings.

## Free-factor and saturation checks

For i1, the triple (ab,bac,b) is a basis of F(a,b,c). Write its
members as x,y,z. The inverse basis formulas are

    a=x z^{-1},    b=z,    c=z x^{-1} z^{-1} y.

Substitution checks both composites equal the identity. Thus the
subgroup generated by x,y is a free factor, and the monoid map is
injective. The second map is obtained by renaming a,b,c as d,e,f
and interchanging x and y, so it has the same properties.

For saturation, use the labelled graph consisting of the directed
cycle ab and the directed cycle bac, meeting only at their common
base vertex. The first has one interior vertex and the second two.
At every vertex both outgoing positive labels and incoming positive
labels are distinct. Adding inverse edges therefore gives a folded
inverse automaton, or equivalently a graph immersion into the rose
with labels a,b,c. Its fundamental-group image is precisely
H=<ab,bac>; the graph has free rank 2 with the two displayed cycles
as a basis. Any positive word in H labels a closed path in this
graph: reduce a loop representing that element, using uniqueness of
labelled path lifting in the folded graph.

A positive path starting at the base vertex chooses either a or b.
The first choice is then forced to complete ab; the second is forced
to complete bac. Every intermediate vertex has just one outgoing
positive edge. Therefore the positive root-loop language is exactly
{ab,bac}*. This proves H∩FreeMon(a,b,c)=FreeMon(ab,bac), which is
the required saturation. Renaming and swapping proves saturation
for i2.

The two induced positive cones on the common subgroup agree exactly
with its prescribed free monoid M0; the defect is not a mismatch of
those induced cones.

## The obstruction

Suppose j1 and j2 formed a cocone in a free monoid P, with the images
of c and f both nonempty. Use word length in P; this is available
even when P has infinitely many free generators. From the first
span leg,

    |y|=|b|+|a|+|c|=|x|+|c|>|x|.

From the second,

    |x|=|e|+|d|+|f|=|y|+|f|>|y|.

These inequalities contradict each other. Equivalently, adding the
two equations gives |c|+|f|=0. Any embedding of free monoids is
nonerasing on generators, so the claimed amalgam cannot exist.

The group amalgam itself is free: the common rank-two subgroup is a
free factor of both rank-three groups, so its amalgam is free of
rank four. This failure is specifically an incompatibility of the
positive words with a common ambient free-monoid length. It already
occurs before any issue of strict coherence for a larger category.

## Consequence and limitation for C_delta

An induction choosing arbitrary saturated positive free-factor
charts cannot be justified by a general amalgamation theorem with
only those hypotheses. Stronger restrictions on compatible length
cones, on support configurations, or on already prescribed smaller
charts would be necessary.

This span has not been realized as a coherently prescribed piece of
the R17 C_delta free-group diagram. The result therefore does not
refute the desired special chart construction and does not settle
the remaining comparison problem. In particular it does not turn
the positive abelian stabilization into a strict FreeMon diagram.

## Independent finite verification

`code/D_saturated_amalgam_check.py` checks both inverse free-basis
identities by exact free reduction, verifies the folded inverse
automaton and its forced first-return paths, checks its free rank,
and adds the exact integer rows giving the length contradiction.
Its output is `code/outputs/D_saturated_amalgam_check.json`.

The argument for all possible target free monoids is the word-length
proof above. The finite program is an independent consistency check,
not a bounded search substituted for that proof.
