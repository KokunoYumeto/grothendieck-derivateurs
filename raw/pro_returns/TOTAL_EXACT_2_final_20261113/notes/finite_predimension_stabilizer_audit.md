The audited hypotheses are: finite simple 3-uniform hypergraphs with

predimension strictly greater than `delta(A)`; increasing induced closed

## 1. The actual quotient and its point fibres

There are `s=2N` factors, namely one E and `s-1` interior D-copies.
The two endpoint D-copies are subobjects of that *same* E factor, not
additional factors. This distinction is essential to the three-index
bound and is respected by both versions of the proof.

An interior label outside F belongs to at most one of B and C. Because
the consecutive overlap types alternate, it can pass across at most
one interior overlap. At E, the only extra possibility is an element
in `d0(B) intersection d1(C)`. It links the first and last interior
copies through E and therefore has precisely a subset of the three
cyclic positions `{s-1,0,1}`. Its adjacent interior labels cannot
continue through their other overlaps.

No such fibre can join an F fibre. If, for example, an endpoint image
`d0(b)` equals `d1(f)` with f in F, agreement on F makes this equal to
`d0(f)`, and injectivity forces b=f. The corresponding argument works
at the other endpoint. Full-circle fibres are therefore exactly the
individual F fibres, with no merging of their distinct labels.

This proves both injectivity of each factor map and exactness of the
stated consecutive intersections. The only nonconsecutive intersections
outside F occur between the first and last interior factors through E.
They are not omitted by the proof; they are exactly the three-index
case.

## 2. Edges and inducedness

For `s>=8`, every proper point-visibility interval has at most three
indices and hence has index diameter at most two, strictly less than
half the circle. If two factors contain all vertices of an edge, any
proper one of these vertex intervals contains the unique shortest
index path joining those factors. All the other intervals containing
both endpoints contain that same path: a competing path is too long
to fit in a three-index interval. Full-circle intervals impose no
restriction.

At each consecutive step, the three points lie in the indicated
overlap. That overlap is induced in both neighboring factors, so the
copied edge belongs to both, with the corresponding labels. Thus the
edge propagates along the whole short path. When all three vertices
belong to F, the same argument propagates it around the circle.

Consequently no factor acquires a new induced edge from a distant
factor. The visibility of an edge is exactly the intersection of its
three vertex visibilities, and edge identifications are generated by
adjacent overlaps. In particular the argument does not replace a
strict hypergraph quotient by a homotopy-colimit model.

## 3. The cut estimate and all subsets

Cutting *any* one overlap produces a linear sequence of free amalgams.
This remains true if E is an interior factor of that sequence: its
incoming and outgoing overlap images may intersect inside E, but the
next newly adjoined factor has no identifications with the preceding
union except its prescribed incoming overlap. Both overlap images in
E are closed by transitivity from the endpoint D-embeddings. Thus all
factors are closed in the linear amalgam L.

Restoring the deleted overlap imposes at most
`m=max(|B|,|C|)` pair identifications on vertices. For a subset U of
the quotient, take its *full* inverse image U~ in L. Every induced
edge in U has a copied lift in U~. Hence, with the actual numbers of
lost vertex and edge copies denoted by dv and de,

    delta_L(U~)-delta_Z(U)=2dv-3de,    0<=dv<=m, de>=0.

The lower bound `delta_Z(U)>=delta_L(U~)-2m` is therefore correctly
oriented. No assumption that edge multiplicities are preserved is
needed.

If the proper visibility intervals of U miss a factor index r,
cutting `(r-1,r)` cuts none of those fibres. A full F fibre becomes
one connected path. Thus each selected vertex has a unique lift.
The edge argument above, or simplicity after these vertex lifts,
gives a unique edge lift as well. The induced structures on U and
U~ are isomorphic. Positivity follows from L, and whenever U properly
contains a factor, U~ properly contains that factor's copy in L.
This gives the required strict relative inequality.

If the proper intervals cover every factor index, a lifted non-F
vertex has a linear visibility interval of length at most three.
Cutting an original proper interval can split it into two lifts,
but each still has this bound. Let J contain the first-appearance
indices of selected non-F vertices. The windows of length three
starting at J cover all s indices, so `|J|>=ceil(s/3)`.

At a noninitial index in J, the selected subset U_j has a point
outside the incoming closed overlap A_j. Submodularity gives

    delta(U_j)-delta(U_j intersection A_j)
      >= delta(U_j union A_j)-delta(A_j) >= 1.

The last inequality uses strict closure and integrality. Other
increments are nonnegative; an initial nonempty selected subset has
predimension at least one. Therefore

    delta_Z(U) >= ceil(s/3)-2m > delta(E) >= delta(D)>0

under `s>3(delta(E)+2m)`. The endpoint closed embedding justifies
`delta(E)>=delta(D)`. This proves positivity for *every* nonempty U
and closedness of E and every D factor, rather than checking only
unions of whole factors.

## 4. Orderability

After embedding E in the real line, both endpoint coordinate maps
on D are strictly increasing and agree on F. Their convex linear
interpolation has a uniform positive lower bound Delta on adjacent
gaps, obtained by taking the smaller endpoint gap for each adjacent
pair and then the minimum over the finite set of pairs.

In each two-step move, updating all coordinates outside B changes
each coordinate by at most `M0/N`; hence every adjacent gap stays
at least `Delta-2M0/N>0`. The second update affects only B outside
F and therefore leaves C fixed. These are exactly the B-then-C
overlap equations, including the first overlap with d0 and the last
overlap with d1. The endpoint equality on F is used precisely where
a coordinate belongs to both B and C.

The real-valued maps form an actual cocone on the set quotient. It
is not necessary that distinct quotient points receive distinct real
values. Sorting by value and then breaking ties is legitimate because
no two points in any one factor have equal values. Thus every factor
order remains strict, including E's prescribed order. There is no
implicit orientation or directed-cycle assumption.

## 5. Degenerate bases and the restricted category

The proof also handles empty B or C, B=C, B=D, C=D, or identical
endpoint embeddings. When B=C, all points of that base have full
visibility and there are no one-sided base labels. If B=D, an entire
neighboring D-copy may coincide with its predecessor, but its image
is still injective and induced; the other alternating overlap still
stops every non-F label after at most one edge. None of the counts
requires factor images to be pairwise distinct. For one-point D the
gap condition is vacuous and the stated arbitrary positive Delta is
harmless.

If the original objects have every raw point and hyperedge closed,
each point and edge of Z occurs in a closed factor. Transitivity
therefore gives the same property in Z. Both the full category and
the point-and-edge-closed subcategory are covered.

## 6. Stabilizer generation and minimal supports

The strong-limit hypotheses used are the usual explicit ones: every
finite subset lies in a finite closed subobject; a finite closed
extension embeds over its base; and every isomorphism between finite
closed subobjects extends to an automorphism. Countability, the
closed-subobject restriction property, joint embedding, and the
proved amalgamation property provide these by the standard rich
sequence and back-and-forth construction. The subgroup argument
needs no stronger saturation property.

For g fixing F, choose closed D containing B union C and then closed
E containing D union gD. The finite bridge applies to id_D and g|D.
Embed its Z over E. Homogeneity extends its successive D-embeddings
to automorphisms g_i, with the chosen endpoints `g_0=1`, `g_s=g`.
Agreement on B or C gives `g_i^{-1}g_(i+1)` in the respective
pointwise stabilizer. Their finite product is exactly g. Thus

    <G_B,G_C>=G_(B intersection C)

holds algebraically, not only after taking topological closure.

Finally, an open U contains G_B for some finite closed B. Choose
one of least cardinality. For g in U, both G_B and G_(gB) lie in U,
so their generated group G_(B intersection gB) does too. The
intersection is closed. Minimality forces `B=gB`, and the finite
linear order makes g fix B pointwise. This proves `U=G_B`. Repeating
the intersection argument with any other closed C satisfying
`G_C<=U` proves `B subset C`, as asserted.

## Remaining mathematical scope

These results supply the finite support theorem needed by the
proposed all-action argument. They do not by themselves identify
arbitrary A-sets with smooth automorphism-group sets, nor prove that
the relevant categories of elements retain their homotopy types
under saturation. Those steps must be audited independently before
concluding totality or a counterexample to the main implication.
