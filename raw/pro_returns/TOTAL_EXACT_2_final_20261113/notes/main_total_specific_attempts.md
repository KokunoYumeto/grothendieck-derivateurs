# Further total-specific attempts: restricted limits, positivity, and variance

AI-produced by GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI), 13 November 2026.
Status: the main implication and the totality of K* remain unresolved.
This note records new precise reductions and the exact obstruction in
the attempted total-specific arguments. It does not promote a failed
strictification argument to a proof.

## 1. Exactness is equivalent to a restricted compact-torsor limit property

Let T=R/Z, with its usual compact group topology. For every objectwise
monomorphism i:M->N in Ab^A and every character

    chi : colim_A M -> T,

define the contravariant diagram

    X_i,chi(a) = {psi in Hom_Z(N(a),T): psi i_a = chi_a}.

The character group here has the topology inherited from the product
T^{N(a)}. The additivity equations define a closed subgroup of that
product. Restriction to i_a(M(a)) is continuous, so the displayed fibre
is closed. It is nonempty because the divisible group T is injective.
It is a torsor under the compact group
Hom_Z(N(a)/i_a(M(a)),T). For f:a->b, precomposition with N(f) maps
X_i,chi(b) continuously into X_i,chi(a), since chi is compatible.

**Proposition.** For every small A, including empty and disconnected A,
the following are equivalent:

1. Abelian colimits over A are exact.
2. Every diagram X_i,chi just defined has a nonempty strict inverse limit.

**Proof.** If colimits are exact, colim i is injective. Injectivity of T
extends chi along this monomorphism to a character on colim N. Its
restrictions to N(a) give the required compatible family.

Conversely, suppose some colim i has a nonzero kernel element x.
Choose a character of the cyclic group generated by x that does not
vanish on x: for finite order n>1 use the value 1/n in T; for infinite
order use, for instance, 1/2. Extend this character to colim M by
injectivity of T. A compatible point of X_i,chi would extend it to
colim N, which is impossible because x dies there. Thus (2) forces
preservation of every monomorphism. Colimit is already right exact,
so it is exact. QED.

Only these character-extension torsors are asserted here. One can
formulate a broader equivalence for all compact abelian torsors using
Pontryagin duality, but that additional statement is not needed and is
not being invoked without its topological hypotheses.

### The finite-family obstruction gives explicit circle-potential torsors

For the column map D:P_q->P_p^k of a finite family, write J=im D and
L=ker D. The previously proved calculation gives colim J=Z/epsilon(L),
and its map to colim P_p^k=Z^k is zero. If epsilon(L)=dZ is proper,
choose a nonzero theta in T annihilated by d (for d=0 choose 1/2).
The character of colim J sending 1 to theta defines the preceding
extension-torsor diagram for J->P_p^k. At an object r its points are
precisely the families of potentials lambda_i:A(p,r)->T such that

    sum_i [lambda_i(ga_i)-lambda_i(ga_0)] = theta
                                                 for every g:q->r.

Each fibre is nonempty by injectivity of T and is a compact affine
subgroup coset of a product of circles. A compatible family would have
lambda_i(h)=lambda_i(1_p) for every h:p->r, contradicting the nonzero
right side. For finite hom-sets these are finite-dimensional compact
group torsors, possibly disconnected.

Under common successors, epsilon(L) proper is exactly failure of an
affine equalizer for the specified family. Thus proving inverse-limit
existence from totality for this restricted class would settle the
corresponding triple or finite-family question. The construction is a
reformulation, not an extra consequence of totality.

## 2. The precise compact-convex condition is a joint Reiter condition

For a nonempty small A with common successors, define the following
condition R(A). For every finite list of pairs of ordinary arrows

    b_j,c_j : p_j -> q       (j=1,...,m)

and every epsilon>0, there exist r and finitely supported nonnegative
real coefficients t_g on A(q,r), with sum_g t_g=1, such that

    sum_j || t b_j - t c_j ||_1 < epsilon.

The norms are in the free real l1 spaces on A(p_j,r). Different source
objects p_j are allowed. Call this the joint Reiter condition only as
an explicit definition; no literature-priority assertion is made.

**Proposition.** R(A) is equivalent to nonempty strict inverse limits
for all contravariant diagrams of nonempty compact convex subsets of
Hausdorff locally convex real vector spaces, with continuous affine
transition maps.

### Compact-convex limits imply R(A)

If R(A) fails for a finite list and epsilon>0, put

    D_r[g] = ([g b_j]-[g c_j])_j

in the l1 direct sum on the disjoint union of the hom-sets A(p_j,r).
The convex hull of these columns misses the open l1 ball of radius
epsilon, whenever A(q,r) is nonempty. Hahn--Banach separation in this
normed space gives a bounded functional lambda of linfinity norm one
with lambda(D_r[g])>=epsilon for all g. This also works for infinite
hom-sets: only finite-support convex combinations are used, and one
separates a convex set from a disjoint open convex set. No compactness
of the column hull is assumed.

Let C(r) be all tuples lambda_j:A(p_j,r)->[-1,1] satisfying

    sum_j (lambda_j(g b_j)-lambda_j(g c_j)) >= epsilon

for every g:q->r. It is a nonempty closed convex subset of a product
of compact intervals. If there are no such g, it is the whole cube.
Every inequality involves finitely many coordinates, hence is closed
in the product topology. Restricting along postcomposition makes C a
contravariant diagram with continuous affine maps.

Any compatible point would satisfy lambda_j(h)=lambda_j(1_pj) for
every h:p_j->r. Its inequality at r=q and g=1_q would become
0>=epsilon. Thus the inverse limit is empty, a contradiction.

### R(A) implies compact-convex limits

Let X:A^op->CompConv be such a diagram, with X(a) embedded in a
Hausdorff locally convex real vector space E_a. Compatibility in the
compact product product_a X(a) is equivalent to the equations

    ell(x_a-X(f)x_b)=0

for every arrow f:a->b and every continuous linear functional ell on
E_a. These functionals separate points. It suffices to satisfy each
finite collection of these equations to arbitrary accuracy, then use
the finite intersection property for their closed epsilon strips.

Fix a finite collection. Choose a common successor q of all its
objects and arrows j_a:a->q. For each arrow f:a->b in the collection,
use the pair j_a,j_b f:a->q. Choose a bound B>=1 for the absolute
values of every selected ell on its compact set X(a). Apply R(A)
to the pairs with a tolerance smaller than the desired accuracy/B.
Obtain r and a probability combination t of q->r arrows. Choose
z in X(r) and form the genuine convex combination

    x = sum_g t_g X(g)(z) in X(q),       x_a=X(j_a)(x).

Affinity allows the combination to pass through every structure map.
For each chosen f and ell, grouping terms by the composite arrow
h:a->r gives

    |ell(x_a-X(f)x_b)|
    <= B ||t j_a - t(j_b f)||_1.

Thus the finite collection is satisfied to the required accuracy.
Compactness supplies an exactly compatible family. QED.

This result distinguishes the two conditions being compared:

- Exactness supplies *signed integral* combinations with defect exactly
  zero, for arbitrary finite ordinary families (and hence finite lists
  of pairs after the usual common-target reductions).
- R(A) supplies *nonnegative real* mass-one combinations with arbitrarily
  small defect.

The present work proves neither exact=>R(A) nor R(A)=>exact. In
particular one must not replace signed exact balancing by approximate
positive balancing merely because both use affine combinations.
The compact Hausdorff Ramsey condition implies the compact-convex
condition by forgetting convex structure, but the converse is not
asserted.

The proved AKR positive gap in `compact_convex.tex` shows that K* fails
R(A), already on the initial triple. Consequently quasi-filteredness
does not supply this condition. Total=>R(A) remains a separate
unproved possible route to ruling out the AKR candidate.

## 3. Why the simplicial/iterated-elements strictification attempts stop

### Objectwise convex contractibility is not the missing input

Every nonempty convex space is contractible. Consequently the homotopy
limit of a diagram of such spaces is weakly contractible, irrespective
of A: the objectwise weak equivalence to the terminal diagram induces
an equivalence on derived limits. The compact-convex AKR diagram has
an empty strict limit despite this property. Replacing its fibres by
their singular complexes, face-poset models, or contractible nerves
therefore does not by itself turn the homotopy-coherent point into a
strict point. A strictification theorem would be an additional claim,
not a consequence of fibre contractibility.

### Iterated covariant element categories do not reverse variance

For H:A->Set and G:El_A(H)->Set, the iterated category of elements
is the category of elements of the single covariant A-set

    a |-> coproduct_{h in H(a)} G(a,h).

Thus totality applies to every iteration, but these projections remain
discrete opfibrations over A. A contravariant potential/torsor diagram
instead gives a discrete fibration when regarded as sets. There is no
canonical reversal of its structure maps: they are generally neither
injective nor surjective, and a choice of inverse images is not a
functor. The dual-functions construction changes the variance, but
returns to the multiplicative-coefficient/pinning issue already
exhibited by the free Boolean algebra example.

If every contravariant transition is a bijection, inversion does give
a covariant local system. In that special case a simply connected BA
already forces compatibility after choosing a point in one fibre:
the functor factors through the fundamental groupoid. This uses no
homology above degree one and therefore does not address the new
content of totality beyond quasi-filteredness.

### Constant-group extensions detect only the already-known covering step

For an objectwise exact sequence of covariant abelian diagrams

    0 -> constant G -> E -> M -> 0,

the map El E -> El M is a covering of categories: on each fibre over
an element of M, every structure map is an equivariant map of G-torsors
and hence a bijection. Simple connectivity of components of El M
therefore splits the covering componentwise as a set-valued covering.
This argument is already available under quasi-filteredness. It
preserves distinct constant translates in the *set* colimit of E,
but does not prove injectivity of constant G in the *abelian* colimit.
The latter can identify sums of different set components. Making the
chosen set sections additive would require compatibility on products,
precisely the point not supplied by the covering argument.

## 4. Status of the total-specific route

No construction here uses the vanishing of H2 or higher homology of
every El_A(H) to produce the required compatible potentials. That is
the missing substantive step. The restricted character-torsor property
is equivalent to exactness; the positive Reiter property is equivalent
to compact-convex inverse-limit existence. Neither has been derived
from totality, and the identities above are recorded as reductions,
not solutions.
