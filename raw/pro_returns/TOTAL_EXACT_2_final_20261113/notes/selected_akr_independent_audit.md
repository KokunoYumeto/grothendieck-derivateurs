# Independent audit of the selected large-target AKR theorem

Status: the selected construction proof is complete as audited here.
It proves existence of a quasi-filtered category which is not total.
It does not prove that every admissible repaired C.1 construction has
the same persistence property, and it does not decide total => exact.

## 1. The two orders have distinct jobs

Every object carries an auxiliary order on its actual points and a
separate auxiliary order on H(X)=pi_0(G_X). No compatibility between
these two orders, and no monotonicity of the quotient X->H(X), is used.

Actual-point monotonicity makes every f-to-g partial transition an
increasing partial bijection; hence its only cycles are fixed points.
It also gives the corresponding boundedness for closed chains of
morphisms and their partial inverses. Component monotonicity gives the
same no-cycle property for H(f),H(g). These are separate finite path
decompositions, and the chosen n bounds both.

The large-k ordering lemma applies separately to these two pairs. Its
threshold is eventually permanent, so the two thresholds, the graph
diameter inequality, and the source expansion inequality can all be met
simultaneously. The additional condition 3n divides 4k+1 causes no
problem: for odd n, the permitted k form an unbounded arithmetic
progression. All old orders are retained.

## 2. Component injectivity is proved before using convexity

The actual graph G_Z is the strict graph colimit of the necklace:
the established cell-fibre calculation identifies its vertices and edges
with exactly the equivalence classes generated by adjacent overlaps.
Taking graph components preserves this colimit, since components are
the set coequalizer of the two edge-endpoint maps and colimits of sets
commute with colimits.

Thus the actual H(Z) is the strict Set necklace colimit of H(S),H(T).
Because n also bounds the component-path sizes, the repaired
component-level beta target represents this same colimit. Its canonical
copies are injections, and the ordering lemma orders that target.
Transporting the order to H(Z) proves the required increasing
component injections. This step does not identify physical point paths
with component paths and does not depend on the graph convexity lemma.

## 3. The linear-amalgam lemma is valid for strong geodesic convexity

If two graphs are amalgamated along component-injective embeddings of
a graph with convex component images, the incidence graph of connected
components is a matching. Each nontrivial new component therefore
glues just one component from each side along one connected convex
subgraph. No two components of either old graph become connected.

A shortest path with endpoints in one piece cannot make an excursion
through the other. The excursion's endpoints lie in the common convex
subgraph. Either the excursion is shortest within the other piece, in
which case all its vertices lie in that subgraph, or it can be shortened,
contrary to global shortestness. This proves that every shortest path
stays in the original component, not merely that one internal shortest
path exists.

Inductively this applies to a linear necklace: the next gluing map is
still convex and component-injective into the existing amalgam by
composition. Hence each original copy is componentwise convex and
component-injective in the whole linear amalgam.

## 4. The cut and lift argument checks out

Fix endpoints in a single old T-component and an ambient shortest
path P in G_Z. There is an old path of length at most D, so |P|<=D.
This upper bound never assumes an intrinsic finite distance between
different old components.

At most D+1 vertices occur on P. Every proper visibility interval has
size at most 2n-2; fixed-point fibres have full visibility and are
excluded from the union. Consequently

    4k+1 > (2n-2)(D+1)

gives an index r missed by all proper visibility intervals of P's
vertices. Delete the overlap between copies r-1 and r.

For a used vertex, a proper fibre avoids r and is unchanged by this
cut, while a full circular fibre becomes a connected path. Used edge
fibres are also connected after the cut. If one endpoint has proper
visibility, the edge visibility is contained in it. If both endpoints
have full visibility, the two-point routing invariant factors the edge
through every copy, so its fibre is a full circle before the cut.

In the cut graph L, the preimages of an old cell are precisely the
connected components of that cell's cut incidence fibre. Hence every
vertex and edge of P has exactly one preimage. The unique preimages
respect endpoints and give a path P_tilde in L of the same length,
with both endpoints in the originally selected copy of T.

Convexity of that copy in L gives

    d_T = d_L <= |P_tilde| = |P| <= d_T.

Thus P_tilde is a shortest path in L and must remain inside the copy.
Its image P remains inside the corresponding copy of G_Z. This proves
the asserted strong componentwise convexity. The convexity lemma
correctly does not claim, on its own, to prevent distinct components
from becoming joined by much longer paths.

## 5. The fixed-set argument now applies at every selected stage

For fixed vertices u,v in one source component, convex isometric
embeddings give

    f(I_S(u,v)) = I_T(fu,fv) = g(I_S(u,v)),

where I denotes all vertices on all shortest paths. The partial
bijection g^(-1)f therefore restricts to a permutation of the finite
interval I_S(u,v). It is increasing for the auxiliary point order,
so the permutation is the identity. Thus the fixed subgraph meets
each source component in a connected subgraph or not at all.

The exact fixed-path kernel formula then kills no old H2 class at a
quasi-filter attachment. For a disjoint-sum attachment the cycle group
is the direct sum of the two old cycle groups, so the attachment map
from their direct sum is injective and again kills no old class.
Processing the finitely many new targets of one full stage one at a
time is legitimate: no arrows between distinct new targets are
introduced, and the other targets do not alter the source or target
graphs of a remaining attachment.

## 6. The initial class is present for arbitrary selected first choices

In the first complete stage, only the two quasi-filters for the ordered
pairs (v1,v2) and (v2,v1) have a binary cycle. For either pair the
actual two-point beta target has one undirected cycle of length 4k+1,
and the third point of W is private to each copy. A cycle orientation
has augmentation plus or minus one; choose its sign to make the
augmentation one. The two junction edges cancel in the signed count,
leaving 2k-(2k-1)=1.

A pair involving v3 and just one of v1,v2 gives only edges with a
private binary endpoint, so its binary graph has no cycle. Repeated
point maps give stars or disjoint edges and isolated vertices.
Identity quasi-filters and weak sums give forests as well.

The old cycle groups at V and W vanish, and there are no arrows between
different new targets at this stage. Therefore colim K is Z^2, its
augmentation is (1,1), and the binary second homology is generated by
the difference of the two augmentation-one cycle generators. This
argument allows the two selected target sizes to be different.

The selected induction proves that this nonzero class survives every
stage. Every finite nerve chain in the union belongs to a stage;
filtered unions of chain complexes commute with homology. The class
is therefore nonzero in H2 of the final binary category of elements.

## 7. Audit conclusion and wording

No substantive mathematical gap was found in the combined selected
construction proof. One wording change was sent to the author:
the theorem's item saying that an attachment "preserves second
integral homology" should say that it "induces an injection on second
integral homology," because no isomorphism claim is proved or needed.

The resulting theorem establishes the strict inclusion

    total categories  <  quasi-filtered categories

in the componentwise sense used in the manuscript. The opposite
strictness question involving exactness, the main total-to-exact
implication, and universal parameter-independent C.1 persistence
remain separate.
