# Alternating strong amalgams and stabilizer intersections

Status: **proved and independently audited**. The complete audit is `notes/finite_predimension_stabilizer_audit.md`; the root branch also checked the finite bridge independently. The original proposal is retained below as the construction record. The separate all-action criterion in `sections/predimension_totality_reduction.tex` has also passed an independent audit, so its combination with this support theorem now proves totality.

Use the full ordered positive-predimension category A from `predimension_category.md`, or its point-and-edge-closed full subcategory. Empty sets are allowed as formal closed amalgamation bases. Write δ=2|V|−3|E|.

## Finite bridging lemma

Let B,C≤dD be closed induced subobjects and put F=B∩C. Let d_0,d_1:D→E be increasing d-closed embeddings with d_0|F=d_1|F. Then there is an increasing d-closed extension E→Z and a finite sequence of increasing d-closed embeddings

    a_0=d_0, a_1,...,a_(2N)=d_1 : D→Z

such that a_(2j)|B=a_(2j+1)|B and a_(2j+1)|C=a_(2j+2)|C. Here the endpoints mean their composites with E→Z.

### 1. The strict target and its point fibres

Choose N≥4, to be increased below. Take one E-copy and the 2N−1 interior copies D_i for 1≤i≤2N−1. Regard the E-copy as carrying D_0 via d_0 and D_(2N) via d_1. Form the strict set quotient by the following adjacent identifications:

    D_i and D_(i+1) are identified on the same labelled B, for even i;
    D_i and D_(i+1) are identified on the same labelled C, for odd i.

The first and last identifications use the displayed embeddings into E. Thus the index graph is a circle with s=2N indices: E,D_1,...,D_(2N−1).

For a point of F, all copies carry the same point and the two endpoint maps agree, so its fibre is the full index circle. Every other point of an interior D-copy belongs to at most one of B,C, so it can cross at most one consecutive overlap. Away from E its fibre has at most two indices. An E-point may be in both d_0(B) and d_1(C), joining the two adjacent interior copies through E; this produces at most the three cyclically consecutive indices {s−1,0,1}. No other propagation is possible: a point in B\C cannot cross the next C-overlap, and conversely. A point of E or a copy outside all relevant overlaps is private. Therefore every non-F point has a proper cyclic interval of at most THREE indices. In particular all factor maps are injective, and consecutive overlaps are exactly the prescribed B- or C-images.

Give the quotient Z exactly the union of the copied hyperedges. Because s≥8, each proper visibility interval has length at most three, strictly less than half the circle. An edge whose vertices are visible in two factors propagates along the unique short index path, using inducedness of B,C in D and of their endpoint images in E. If all three vertices are full-visibility, the same propagation runs around the whole circle. Thus every factor embedding is induced, an edge's visibility is the intersection of its vertex visibility sets, and all edge identifications are generated by the adjacent overlap relations. This is an actual hypergraph quotient, not a homotopy colimit.

### 2. Predimension and closure

Let m=max(|B|,|C|), and choose N also so that

    s=2N > 3(δ(E)+2m).                                      (S)

Delete any one overlap to form a linear amalgam L. All its factors E,D_i are d-closed in L: each gluing base B or C is d-closed in both adjacent factors, including its image in E by transitivity. The quotient q:L→Z restoring the cut identifies at most m vertex pairs. For U⊆Z, put U~=q^{-1}U. As edges are only identified, not added beyond their copied preimages,

    δ_Z(U) ≥ δ_L(U~)−2m.

Exactly, the difference δ_L(U~)−δ_Z(U) is 2d_v−3d_e with d_v≤m and d_e≥0.

If the proper point-visibility intervals of U miss an index r, cut the overlap (r−1,r). Each point of U then has one lift; induced edges have one lift as well. Hence U and U~ are isomorphic. Positivity follows from L. If U properly contains any E- or D-factor, so does its lift, and strict closure in L gives the required strict inequality.

Otherwise those intervals cover all s indices. In the linear chain, let J be the indices at which at least one non-F lifted vertex first appears. All vertices first appearing at a given j are visible only among j,j+1,j+2. Therefore 3|J|≥s. At each such stage, the selected subset has a point outside the incoming closed overlap. Submodularity and strict closure make its integral predimension increment at least one. Other stages have increment zero or at least one; the initial nonempty factor subset also has predimension at least one. Thus

    δ_L(U~) ≥ ceil(s/3),
    δ_Z(U) ≥ ceil(s/3)−2m > δ(E) ≥ δ(D)>0.

The last weak inequality uses the endpoint d-closed embedding D→E. This proves that Z is positive-predimension and every E- and D-factor is d-closed in Z.

### 3. Ordering by two-step coordinate interpolation

Embed E increasingly in R, and regard d_0,d_1 as strictly increasing coordinate maps D→R. For 0≤t≤1, let

    p_t(x)=(1−t)d_0(x)+t d_1(x).

All p_t are strictly increasing and agree on F, whose coordinates are constant. Let Δ>0 be a lower bound for all their adjacent-coordinate gaps; the minimum of the corresponding two endpoint gaps suffices. If |D|≤1 take any positive Δ. Let M=max_x |d_1(x)−d_0(x)|. Increase N until 2M/N<Δ.

Set a_(2j)=p_(j/N). Obtain a_(2j+1) by keeping the B-coordinates at p_(j/N) and changing every coordinate outside B to p_((j+1)/N). Every coordinate differs from p_((j+1)/N) by at most M/N, so every adjacent gap is at least Δ−2M/N>0. Hence this intermediate map is increasing and agrees with a_(2j) on B.

Now set a_(2j+2)=p_((j+1)/N). The intermediate map already has the new coordinates on C\B, and B∩C=F has constant coordinates. Therefore it agrees with this next full map on C. These are exactly the alternating overlap identities. The endpoints are the given maps into E. Together with the chosen coordinate map on E, they form an actual cocone on the strict set quotient. Order Z by its resulting R-values, breaking equal values arbitrarily. Every factor remains strictly increasing, because no two points of any one factor have equal coordinates.

All lower bounds on N can be met simultaneously. This proves the finite bridging lemma. If the factors are point-and-edge-closed, Z is too: every atom belongs to an old d-closed factor, and transitivity applies.

## Consequence for the strong Fraïssé limit

Let M be a countable strong Fraïssé limit of A, built as a rich union of finite d-closed subobjects, and let G=Aut(M), preserving the order. Every finite subset is contained in a finite d-closed subobject; every isomorphism between finite d-closed subobjects extends to an automorphism. These are the standard strong-limit properties, obtainable by the usual countable back-and-forth from the proved amalgamation property.

For a finite d-closed subset B, write G_B for its pointwise stabilizer. The finite lemma gives the algebraic equality

    <G_B,G_C> = G_(B∩C).                                    (G)

The forward inclusion is immediate. For the reverse inclusion, fix g∈G_(B∩C). Choose a finite closed D containing B∪C, and a finite closed E containing D∪gD. Apply the finite lemma to id_D and g|D, obtaining a closed finite extension E≤dZ and the alternating D-copies. Richness embeds Z in M over E. Extend each D-copy to an automorphism g_i of M, with g_0=id and g_(2N)=g; these endpoint choices are allowed because the given endpoint embeddings are exactly id_D and g|D. Consecutive restrictions agree on B or C. Hence

    g_i^{-1} g_(i+1) ∈ G_B or G_C,

respectively. Multiplying these differences proves g∈<G_B,G_C>. Alternatively any extension at the last endpoint differs from g by G_D⊆G_B∩G_C. This is actual generation, not merely topological density.

## Why this could finish the support route

Assuming (G), any open subgroup U≤G contains some G_B for finite closed B. Choose B of least cardinality with G_B≤U. For g∈U, both G_B and G_(gB) lie in U, so (G) puts G_(B∩gB) in U. Minimality forces gB=B. Since the order makes a finite set rigid, g fixes B pointwise. Thus U=G_B. The same intersection argument shows B lies in every finite closed support C with G_C≤U.

This is a support theorem for all open subgroups, rather than only definable imaginaries. The next step was to justify the comparison between arbitrary injective A-sets and the corresponding smooth G-sets, and then use the independently checked contraction of nonempty cosieves in B/A. That comparison is proved separately in `sections/predimension_totality_reduction.tex` and has now passed independent audit. Together the results prove totality and the main counterexample.
