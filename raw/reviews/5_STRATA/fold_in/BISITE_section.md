At the time of writing the author of this prompt has read them, the proofs of Parts C, D, E in outline, and has checked none: treat them like leads and verify what you use. Its conventions: coefficients on `S` are presheaves, `HOT(S) = Ho Fun(S^op, 𝒮)`; a sieve is open; for a poset `P` the geometric model is `|NP|` stratified by the least vertex with positive coordinate, with downsets open, and its exit-path category is `P^op`.

- **For Parts A and B: the tests for smooth and proper functors, with the converse.** STRATA D.3: for `f: X → Y` and an arrow `α: f(x) → y`, the *outgoing lifting category* has objects the arrows `v: x → x'` with `f(v) = α` and morphisms the arrows of the fibre `X_y` under `x`; `f` is `W∞`-proper iff all of them are weakly contractible (the fibre inclusion `X_y → X/y` is coaspherical), and `W∞`-smooth iff the dual incoming categories are. D.4: for every derivator (Der 1–4) the right base-change map `g^* f_* → f'_* g'^*` of a strict pullback square is invertible when `f` is proper or `g` smooth, and `f'_! g'^* → g^* f_!` when `f` is smooth or `g` proper; conversely, properness of `f` is equivalent to right base change for all strict pullbacks with space-valued coefficients (tested on fibre squares and the terminal coefficient), and dually. So for `W∞` the classes `Prop` and `Liss` are exactly the classes of universal base change of the two Kan extensions: state Part A's transporter Galois connection (II §6) against this. D.7: a full inclusion of posets is proper iff it is a cosieve and smooth iff it is a sieve; product projections are both; the image of a proper map is upward closed, of a smooth map downward closed. D.9: two corrections to the transcription to check against the pages (I ms. 54: the target of the fibre inclusion defining smoothness is `y_0\X`; XII ms. 59: with downsets open, `x ≤ y` gives `y` in the closure of `x`).
- **For Part B (`Liss ∩ Prop ⊂ Fib_W`, ms. 225–226) and Part E.** E.1: a functor that is a Grothendieck fibration and an opfibration is proper and smooth, and its transition functors are then automatically homotopy equivalences (they are adjoint). D.6: the converse fails for a specified functor: the four-element poset `x < a, b < c` over `[1]` (`x ↦ 0`) is proper, smooth and a fibration, not an opfibration, and its realization is not a fibre bundle. D.5: properness alone gives the strict-fibre formula for `f_*` and nothing about homotopy fibres: `{1} → [1]` is proper, with empty strict fibre over `0` and comma transition `∅ → e`. E.2 (Known; Cisinski 2006, Théorème 5.3.14, Proposition 5.3.13, Corollaires 6.4.8 and 6.4.16, opened by that answer): every functor factors functorially as a `W∞`-equivalence followed by a proper and smooth functor, which is locally constant and whose strict fibres are homotopy fibres. E.3: the alternating replacement `Φ, ΨΦ, ΦΨΦ, …` of VI ms. 192–195 has a colimit that is proper and smooth, with unit a total weak equivalence, fibrewise on locally constant weak fibrations; E.4: that colimit need not be a fibration (lifting-category conditions pass to filtered colimits, universal arrows do not: chains `0 < … < n < t` over `[1]`). E.8–E.10: every locally constant object of `HOT(S)` is represented by a strict bifibration when its values are 1-types, or `BS` is a 1-type, or every arrow of `S` is a monomorphism or every arrow an epimorphism (all posets); E.11 leaves the general case open, with the five-element monoid `{1, e_{ij}}`, `e_{ij}e_{kl} = e_{il}`, as the first undecided base. So between "bifibration", "`Liss ∩ Prop`" and "`Fib_W`" there are three notions for `W∞`: say in Part B which inclusions hold, with D.6 and D.5 as the separating examples, and in Part E which of them your axioms on `(M, W, Liss, Prop)` can express.
- **For Part B, general `W`.** G.5 reproves, under the relative Theorem A axiom and without accessibility, that weak equivalences are stable under base change along `W∞`-perfect functors only for `W∞` and the class of all functors (POLES E.5). G.4 (Known; Cisinski 2006, §9.2.1, Corollaire 9.2.15, Proposition 6.2.3): for the truncation localizer `W_n` the functors `p: T → B` along which strict pullback preserves `W_n` are those for which `τ_{≤n} B(T ×_B X) → τ_{≤n}BT ×_{τ_{≤n}BB} τ_{≤n}BX` (a pullback of `n`-types) is an equivalence for all `X → B`, with an elementary description for `n = 0`. G.3: a strict bifibration need not preserve `W_0` under base change (the two-object contractible groupoid over the group `C_2`). So for `W ≠ W∞` the class `Fib_W` is not contained in, and does not contain, the bifibrations: use these as the test cases for the complementarity question over a general `W`.
- **For Part B (the two fine localizations and the duality).** G.1: the two fine theories over `S` are `Fun(S^op, 𝒮)` and `Fun(S, 𝒮)`, exchanged by `S ↦ S^op`, with the same locally constant part `Fun(BS, 𝒮)`; they are not equivalent over a fixed base in general (the fork `a ← b → c`: by B.6 the presheaf ∞-category determines the idempotent completion of the base, and the fork is not equivalent to its opposite). Opposition of the base supplies no dualizing object: a duality between the two theories over a fixed `S` is additional structure (Lead 4, Part G).
- **For Part D (topological spaces; Lead 2).** The poset case is now a set of exact statements to build on and to test Lead 2 against. A.1 (Known; Barwick–Glasman–Haine, Proposition 3.12.11, Example 3.12.15): for every poset `P`, `Fun(P^op, 𝒮)` is the ∞-topos of *hypercomplete* sheaves on `P` with downsets open; A.2 (Refuted; Aoki, Example A.13): without hypercompletion this fails for `P` the opposite of `N × {0, 1}` ordered by the first coordinate, although every interval is finite; it holds when every principal downset is finite. A.4: for finite `P` the least-vertex stratification of `|NP|` is conical, with contractible strata and exit-path category `P^op`, so constructible sheaves on `|NP|` are `Fun(P^op, 𝒮)`; A.5: also with coefficients in a compactly assembled or a stable presentable ∞-category; for general presentable coefficients only a fully faithful functor is proved (A.6 open). A.12: the exit-path computation holds for every poset. B.3: the same dictionary for finite categories with no non-identity endomorphisms or isomorphisms (parallel arrows allowed); B.7: for a category with a non-invertible endomorphism (already the monoid `{1, e}`) no stratified space over a poset has `Fun(S^op, 𝒮)` as its constructible sheaves. D.5 and D.8: under this dictionary every map `|Nf|` of finite posets is a proper map of spaces, while `f` is proper in his sense only under the lifting test; restriction is the topological inverse image, but the two Kan extensions are in general *not* the topological direct images: for `{0} → [1]` the right Kan extension of a two-point set is constant and the topological direct image is a skyscraper, which is not constructible. The identification is proved for closed inclusions (cosieves), for open ones in the form `BX ∖ BB` deformation retracting to `BA` (C.2), and for product projections. So "proper" in Lead 2 cannot mean "proper map of realizations", and "smooth" does not mean a locally trivial map (D.6). In Part D decide which class of maps of spaces contains the realizations of his proper functors and makes the sheaf-theoretic direct image agree with `f_*`, and the dual class for cosheaves and `f_!`.
- **For Part E (the abstract theorem; the reflective locally constant theory).** A.9 (Known; Cisinski 2009, Theorem 3.13): the reflection `ρ` of VI ms. 65–69 is left Kan extension along `S → BS` and there is also a coreflection. C.6: for a sieve `A ⊂ X` with complementary cosieve `B`, and presentable coefficients, `Fun(X^op, 𝒞)` is the lax pullback of `Fun(A^op, 𝒞)` and `Fun(B^op, 𝒞)` along `i^*j_* ≃ t_*s^*`, `s, t` the source and target functors of the category of crossing arrows; C.7 (Refuted): the same recipe applied to the homotopy categories gives a wrong category already for `X = [1]` (`Z` classes of maps against one). C.8: the locally constant parts form a pullback of ∞-categories. So an abstract theorem stated for the 1-categories `Ho_W^𝓛(S)` alone cannot contain a gluing axiom of this kind: say which of your axioms are about the categories and which need an enhancement or a derivator in the `S`-variable.
