- **Gluing a topos from an open part, a closed part and a link (Parts B and C).** STRATA C.1, C.6: a category `X` with a sieve `A` (open) and the complementary cosieve `B` (closed) is the same thing as `A`, `B` and the bimodule `M(a, b) = Hom_X(a, b)` of crossing arrows; its presheaf topos is glued from those of `A` and `B` along the functor `i^*j_*`, as in SGA 4 IV 9.5.4 (the manuscript of *Les Dérivateurs* reproduces this at XII ms. 62–63), and `i^*j_* ≃ t_*s^*`, where `s, t` are the source and target functors of the category `E` of crossing arrows and commuting squares. C.2 (Known there; Douteau–Waas, arXiv:2112.02394v2, Theorem 6.1): `BE` is the homotopy link of the closed part, that is the homotopy type of a punctured neighbourhood of `BB` in `BX`, with its two maps to `BB` and to the open part. C.3: `BX` is the homotopy pushout of `BA ← BE → BB`. C.8: for locally constant coefficients in any complete and cocomplete ∞-category, in particular for sets, `Fun(BX, 𝒞)` is the pullback of `Fun(BA, 𝒞) → Fun(BE, 𝒞) ← Fun(BB, 𝒞)`; for sets this is a van Kampen theorem for the fundamental groupoid of the glued topos, with the link as the third term. C.4 (Refuted): the link cannot be replaced by the space of all paths between the two parts. So for presheaf topoi there are three different objects built from an open part and local data at the closed part: the open part alone; the glued topos (constructible objects); and the locally constant objects on the glued topos (the pushout of groupoids). Part C's van Kampen theorem and the 2-cartesian squares of scan 166 should be stated so that these are instances, or the difference said.
- **The example closest to §14.** STRATA F.1–F.2: for `X = P¹(C)` and `S = {0, 1, ∞}`, the exit-path category of `(X, S)` has one object `u` with automorphism group `G = π_1(X − S)`, free on `a, b`, three objects `p_0, p_1, p_∞` with trivial automorphism group, and `Hom(p_i, u) = G/⟨m_i⟩` for the three peripheral elements `m_i`; constructible sheaves of sets on `(X, S)` are the functors on it (F.8, with Jansen, arXiv:2308.09551, Theorem 6.7): a `G`-set `Y`, three sets `Y_i`, and maps from `Y_i` to the `m_i`-invariants of `Y`. The link of `p_i` is the groupoid of the infinite cyclic group `⟨m_i⟩`.
