Notation of that answer: `Ch(X)` is the category of all paths in `X` of all types (finite words of forward and backward arrows; a morphism lies over a map of types); `𝒫(X)` is the category of functors from the integer fence (arrows `2i → 2i ± 1`) to `X` that are constant, with identity arrows, outside a finite interval; both have an endpoint functor to `X × X`. Its "intrinsic" cofibrations and fibrations are the classes `Cof_W` and `Fib_W`: the maps along which cobase change, respectively base change, preserves `W`. Its central question (whether the path categories with arbitrary vertical arrows have the type of the mapping spaces of the localization, B.8 there) is left open by it; nothing below depends on that.

- **For Part B (`Liss ∩ Prop ⊂ Fib_W`, ms. 225–226): a candidate counterexample for every `W` larger than `W∞`.** PATHS E.9: let `X` be the one-object category of the group `C_2`, `x` its object, and `q: 𝒫(X; x, −) → X` the functor "end of a path starting at `x`". It is `W∞`-smooth and `W∞`-proper (a base change of the endpoint functor followed by a projection; E.4, E.5), its total category is weakly contractible and its strict fibre has two contractible components. The functor `e → X` is a rational homology equivalence, and its strict pullback along `q` is the inclusion of the fibre in the total category, which is not one (`Q² → Q` on `H_0`). So `q` is not in `Fib_W` for `W` the localizer of rational homology equivalences. An inference of the author of this prompt, unverified: every fundamental localizer contains `W∞` (Cisinski 2004), the lifting categories of STRATA D.3 for `q` are weakly contractible, hence `W`-aspherical for every `W`; if `W`-smoothness and `W`-properness are the conditions that these lifting categories (equivalently the fibre-to-comma inclusions, after every base change) are `W`-aspherical (Maltsiniotis 2005; Cisinski 2006, §5.3: state the exact results), then `q` is `W`-smooth and `W`-proper for every `W`, and the same holds for every discrete bifibration. Then `Liss ∩ Prop ⊂ Fib_W`, with all three classes taken for the same `W`, fails for rational homology by `q`, for `W_0` by the two-object contractible groupoid over `C_2` (STRATA G.3; the same example with a smaller total category), and for every homology localizer by the three-sheeted covering of POLES C.10: that would answer the question put in "After POLES" above. - **For Parts B and E: the path functors between "bifibration", `Liss ∩ Prop` and `Fib_W`.** E.3 (Refuted there): the endpoint functor `Ch(X) → X × X` of the all-type path category is `W∞`-smooth and, already for `X = [1]`, is not proper and is neither a Grothendieck fibration nor an opfibration; the deduction of properness by reversing paths (VII ms. 161–166) fails because reversing a path does not reverse the category of types. E.4: `𝒫(X) → X × X` is smooth and proper for every `X` and, already for `X = [1]`, neither a fibration nor an opfibration: its lifting categories are filtered unions of categories with a terminal or an initial object, and the union keeps contractibility and loses the universal object (the mechanism of STRATA E.4). E.6: over the five-element monoid of STRATA E.11 neither path functor is a bifibration; E.11 leaves the bifibration question for that base open. E.5: for every functor `f: A → X` the category `E(f) = A ×_{X,σ} 𝒫(X)` gives `f = p_f i_f` with `i_f` in `W∞` and `p_f` smooth and proper, with strict fibres the homotopy fibres of `Bf`, natural in strictly commuting squares; the functors are explicit and no model structure is used (compare STRATA E.2, which takes such a factorization from Cisinski 2006, and Evrard's cocylinder, attached). E.8: the cotensor `Fun([1], X) → X × X` has the right total type and the wrong strict fibres (empty over `(1, 0)` for `X = [1]`). In Part B these are further maps in `Liss ∩ Prop` that are not bifibrations, beside STRATA D.6. - **For Part E: what "enough maps in `Cof_W`" or "enough maps in `Fib_W`" gives with no further choice.** D.8: if `M` has an initial object and pushouts, `W` contains the isomorphisms and has two-out-of-three, and every map factors as a map in `Cof_W` followed by a map in `W`, then `(M, Cof_W, W)` satisfies the finite precofibration axioms CF1–CF4 of Rădulescu-Banu (dual to Cisinski's catégories dérivables). The one step that is not formal is that a map in `Cof_W ∩ W` stays in `W` after every cobase change; it is proved by factoring the map along which one pushes out. Consequently (Cisinski, *Higher categories and homotopical algebra*, Theorem 7.6.17, as cited there) for every finite direct category `A` the localization of the strict diagrams `M^A` at the pointwise class is the ∞-category of `A`-diagrams in the localization of `M`. The dual statement (terminal object, pullbacks, `Fib_W`) is used in D.17 and D.18. D.9: these hypotheses do not make every object cofibrant (the diamond `0 < a, b < 1` with `W` the identities and the two maps out of `0`) and give nothing for infinite coproducts or sequential colimits (finite sets with isomorphisms). D.17: Grothendieck's M0, M2, M′, M″ of XIII ms. 7–10 supply both factorizations with intrinsic classes, so a "quillénisable" pair (XIII ms. 50–51) has the finite theory on both sides; his "structure de Quillen faible exacte" asks `W = TF ∘ TC`, which is not `TC = C ∩ W` and `TF = F ∩ W`; XIII ms. 53 considers examples without small filtered colimits; all small shapes follow with the transfinite axiom Cof 4 of XIX ms. 33. D.18 (Known; Cisinski 2006, Théorèmes 5.2.15 and 6.1.11): for an accessible fundamental localizer, `(Cat, W)` always has enough intrinsic cofibrations, while enough intrinsic fibrations is right properness (POLES E.2); the proof of D.18 contains a short argument, without accessibility, that enough intrinsic fibrations force `W` to be generated by the maps `A → e` with `A` `W`-aspherical. D.23: a second proof that no homology localizer `W_E`, `E` a non-zero spectrum, connective or not, has enough intrinsic fibrations. For Part E this is a floor: the axioms "terminal object, pullbacks, two-out-of-three, enough maps in `Fib_W`" on `(M, W)` already give finite homotopy limits and the fibre theory of XII ms. 240–261 (POLES G.13), with no chosen class of fibrations.
