# BISITE: the two fine localizations, smooth and proper maps, and the six-operations shape of a derivator

Package 7 of 7 on Alexander Grothendieck, *Les Dérivateurs* (1990–91). File prefix for your deliverables: `BISITE`.

## What is to be settled

In VI ms. 81–84 Grothendieck stops to say what he does not understand. Over an object `S` of a category of models `(M, W)` he can form the coarse localization `(M/S)[W_S^{-1}]`, which gives the locally constant relative homotopy types; but the fine theories `HOT_W(S)` and `HOT_W(S^op)`, which he has for `M = Cat` by testing a map `X → Y` over `S` on the comma categories `s\X → s\Y`, respectively `X/s → Y/s`, he cannot define for a general `(M, W)`. He writes that he has perhaps always treated `Cat` "non comme une catégorie nue, mais comme un site, ou plutôt … comme un bi-site", with a Zariski and a co-Zariski topology, a smooth and a proper one; that topological spaces, simplicial sets and presheaves on a test category have such notions of localization and ∞-groupoids do not; and that the adjunction formulas for the two topologies go in opposite directions for a reason he has not understood.

He returns to this in XII §6 (ms. 207–227): a class `𝓛` of "isomorphismes locaux" stable under base change and composition defines `W_S^𝓛` (maps over `S` whose base changes along all of `𝓛` are in `W`); the maximal such class is asked for (ms. 222; the companion identifies it with the `W`-smooth maps); and he is struck (ms. 224–225) by the "complémentarité, voire … dualité" of smooth and proper maps, conjecturing "une dualité parfaite" in simplicial sets, perhaps in every presheaf category, and for topological spaces "deux versions complémentaires, ou duales" of relative homotopy type. He proposes axioms (ms. 225–227) and leaves them. Chapter II §§5–6 ("Dualité formelle"; "Transporteurs, noyaux, stabiliseurs relatifs à `W`") sets up the Galois connection between classes of weak equivalences and classes of base-change maps that underlies all this; XII §7 and XIII §§4–6 continue it. None of those three is audited yet.

Since then: the covariant and contravariant model structures on simplicial sets over a base and the theory of smooth and proper maps of simplicial sets (Joyal; Lurie, *Higher Topos Theory*, §§2.1 and 4.1.2; Cisinski, *Higher categories and homotopical algebra*, Chapter 4), asphericity structures (Maltsiniotis 2005), smooth and proper maps relative to a fibration (Anel–Weinberger), and abstract six-functor formalisms (Liu–Zheng, Gaitsgory–Rozenblyum, Mann, Scholze; for derivators Hörmann). This package settles how much of his proposal these contain, proves the rest or shows it false, and states what a derivator is from the six-operations point of view.

## Leads (unverified; prove or refute each)

- **Lead 1.** For `M` = simplicial sets and `W` the weak homotopy equivalences, the two fine localizations of `M/S` defined by his recipe (tests on `S_{s/} ×_S X`, respectively `S_{/s} ×_S X`) are the covariant and contravariant model structures, and through the nerve they restrict to his `HOT_W(S)`, `HOT_W(S^op)` on `Cat/S`. His "perfect duality" in simplicial sets is the duality between smooth and proper maps of Lurie and Cisinski, exchanged by `K ↦ K^op`.
- **Lead 2.** For topological spaces over `Y`, the two versions are sheaves and cosheaves of spaces on `Y` (maps `X → Y` tested on the open sets of `Y`, respectively the dual notion), with proper maps and locally aspherical ("shape-submersive") maps playing the two roles; for stable coefficients on locally compact Hausdorff spaces they are exchanged by Verdier duality in Lurie's form.
- **Lead 3.** A derivator **D** on `Cat` carries two half-formalisms: with `f_!` (left Kan extension), `f^*`, and `f^! = f^*`, base change holds against all maps for `f` smooth, and the projection formula holds when **D** is monoidal; dually with `f_*` for `f` proper. Neither half alone is a six-functor formalism on all of `Cat` in the sense of Mann, because base change fails for general pullback squares (it holds for comma squares). The two halves are exchanged by `X ↦ X^op`, which is why his adjunction formulas for the Zariski and co-Zariski topologies go in opposite directions.
- **Lead 4.** For a stable derivator and a homotopy finite category `S`, limits over `S` are colimits twisted by a canonical dualizing object (Groth–Shulman, generalized stability; Groth–Šťovíček), and this is the Verdier duality of the bi-site: it identifies the two fine theories on homotopy finite bases.
