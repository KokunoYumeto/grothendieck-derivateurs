## 2. Disagreements and conventions

No two returns contradict each other on a statement, as far as the reviews and my reading of BISITE go. The points where a reader can be misled:

1. **Proper and smooth.** Grothendieck-proper = Cisinski-proper (2006 and 2019) = Lurie-smooth (HTT 4.1.2.9); Grothendieck-smooth is the opposite class (BISITE B.5). Opfibrations are proper, fibrations smooth. STRATA and PATHS use Grothendieck's names with lifting categories (STRATA D.3); BISITE F.11, F.12, F.19 use "cohomologically smooth, étale, proper" in Mann's sense, which is a third vocabulary: there every map of the allowed class has `f^! = f^*`, and "cohomologically proper" is finite discrete bifibration.
2. **Which class is called `W`-smooth.** The stabilizer of the aspherical functors (II ms. 66, 89), not the transporter into `W` (BISITE A.9). `Fib_W` is the transporter of `W` itself. The three differ, and `Liss_W ∩ Prop_W ⊂ Fib_W` is a statement about all three for the same `W`.
3. **Which localizer axioms.** All seven returns work with weak saturation, the terminal-object axiom and relative Theorem A (Loc (3 bis)). Grothendieck's Définition 1.1 (XVI ms. 2) has moderate saturation and absolute Theorem A (Loc (3)). Since BISITE A.32 these are different notions. Statements of the returns about "every fundamental localizer" are about the relative notion.
4. **`W_n`.** In CATHOT to PATHS and in BISITE Part B, `W_n` is the localizer of `n`-truncation. BISITE Part C writes `V^{(n)}` for the Street-nerve equivalences of strict `n`-categories, to keep them apart.
5. **Rational coefficients.** Three localizers occur: rational homology equivalences (PATHS E.9, D.23; POLES); `W_{D(Q)}^{lc}`, equivalences on locally constant rational complexes, which is "isomorphism on `π_1` and rational isomorphism on higher homotopy" (GALOIS Part C; BISITE B.12); and `W_1`. A covering is an intrinsic fibration for the last two and for no homology localizer (BISITE B.13). "Fails for rational homology" in the PATHS review and the handoff is about the first.
6. **Variance of coefficients.** Presheaf variance `D(X) = Fun(X^op, ·)` throughout; with it the tests `s\X` give `Fun(NS^op, Spaces)` and the proper maps, the tests `X/s` give `Fun(NS, Spaces)` and the smooth maps (BISITE B.1, B.3, B.6). Grothendieck's `W^g` (ms. 222) is the second. BISITE F.15 takes the entrance category of `M̄_{0,n}` (the opposite of Jansen's category); STRATA Part F speaks of the exit-path category. To align when integrating.
7. **Row labels.** The bifibration question is row VIc-C022 in the current companion ("VIc-C008" in STRATA and in the prompts is the first numbering).
8. **The handoff's sentence** that Kan extensions are topological direct images "only for closed inclusions and product projections" is too strong: STRATA D.8 is a positive statement for those two classes; BISITE D.11 claims all proper maps of finite posets.

## 3. What becomes decidable or sharper from the combination

**(a) One rigidity statement in three forms.** The localizer `W∞` is the only non-trivial fundamental localizer for which: the fibration axiom Loc (7) in its strong form holds (XVI 6.1 with Cisinski; POLES); `Liss ∩ Prop ⊂ Fib` holds (BISITE B.9); strict pullbacks along smooth and proper functors are homotopy pullbacks. Against that, the reversible cofibration axiom Loc (6 b) forces `W ⊃ W_Z` (POLES). So XVI Théorème 5.4 cannot hold as stated, and his "peut-être" at XII ms. 225 is true only for `W∞`. For the truncations `W_n` and the coefficient localizers `W_D^{lc}` there is a fibre theory (enough intrinsic fibrations; BISITE E.8) and no complementarity; for homology localizers there is neither (POLES C.4–C.5, PATHS D.23).

**(b) Intrinsic fibrations for `W_n`: a criterion (mine).** Let `p: E → B` be `W∞`-smooth and proper, so that its strict fibres `F_b` are homotopy fibres. Then `p ∈ Fib_{W_n}` if and only if the boundary maps `π_{n+1}(B, b) → π_n(F_b, x)` are zero for all `b` and `x` (for `n = 0`: `π_1(B, b)` acts trivially on `π_0(F_b)`). Sketch: by BISITE B.17 membership means `τ_{≤n}(E ×_B X) ≃ τ_{≤n}E ×_{τ_{≤n}B} τ_{≤n}X` for all `X → B`; compare the fibres of both sides over `τ_{≤n}X`: they agree below degree `n`, and in degree `n` they are `π_n(F)/∂π_{n+1}(X)` and `π_n(F)/∂π_{n+1}(B)`; `X = e` gives necessity. It accounts for every example of the returns: coverings and `W_1` (BISITE B.13: the fibre is discrete), the central extension over `S²` (B.12: `π_2(S²) → π_1(BC_2)` is onto), the path functor over `S^{n+1}` (B.10), `EC_2 → BC_2` for `W_0`, and GALOIS D.4. Not written out; it is follow-up D5 and, for `n = 1`, the form in which it enters LM5.

**(c) The bifibration question: one family of candidates is excluded (mine).** Let `M` be the five-element monoid `{1, e_ij}` (`BM ≃ S²`). A bifibration `E → BM` with fibre `C` is a pseudo-action of `M` on `C` by functors `T_m` that have right adjoints. If every `T_m` is an equivalence of categories, then `T_e ≅ T_e T_e` gives `T_e ≅ 1`, the pseudo-action is given by a normalized 2-cocycle `c` on `M` with values in the abelian group `A = Aut(1_C)`, and `E` is the quotient of `C × M_c` by the free action of the one-object groupoid of `A`, where `M_c` is the central extension of `M` by `A` (the functor `(f, (m, α)) ↦ (m, fα)`). If `BE` were contractible, `BC × BM_c` would be a `K(A, 1)`, so `BC` would be a 1-type. The fibre must be `ΩS²`, with `π_2 = Z`. So **no bifibration with invertible transitions represents the path local system over `BM`**. More generally a strict bifibration with invertible transition functors only sees the action through the 2-group of autoequivalences of `C`, a 2-type. What remains is the case of idempotent left adjoints `T_e` that are weak equivalences and not equivalences; PATHS E.4 (filtered unions lose the universal arrow) and STRATA E.4 show where such functors come from. This is follow-up D1.

**(d) B062 after E.12 and E.11.** False as asked (no factorization assumed); true with a coaugmented replacement functor into the intrinsic fibrations. Between the two: with "every map is a `W`-map followed by an intrinsic fibration" and no functoriality. PATHS D.8 and BISITE E.7 show that this hypothesis already gives a category of fibrant objects (when every object is fibrant over the terminal object); for such a category the inclusion of the fibrant objects of a slice into any intermediate full subcategory induces an equivalence of localizations by the standard argument with a non-functorial replacement, provided the replacement of an object of the intermediate subcategory stays reachable inside it, which is automatic: the replacement lies in `Fib_W S`. So (c) should hold under "enough intrinsic fibrations" alone **(mine, not written out)**: the functors `R`, `η` of E.11 are needed only up to the localization. Follow-up D3 asks for the proof or a counterexample; the example of E.12 fails exactly this hypothesis.

**(e) Loc (3) against Loc (3 bis) after A.32.** `W†` works because its aspherical objects are the contractible categories, so that absolute Theorem A is Quillen's, while the class is not determined locally. Any class containing `W∞` with two-out-of-three, the split-retraction clause and the same aspherical objects is a weak fundamental localizer. With strong saturation the question is whether a functor `Φ` on `Hot` exists that inverts `X → e` only for contractible `X` and whose class of inverted maps is not local over a base; the strong saturation of `W†` itself is `W_0` (every connected `X → e` is a retract of a `W†`-map), which is local. Follow-up D2.

**(f) Which statements need more than the pair `(M, W)`.** Collected from PATHS D.8–D.20, STRATA C.7, B.8, BISITE E.3, E.7, E.13, A.26: "enough intrinsic fibrations" gives finite homotopy limits, the fibre theory of XII ms. 240–261 and rectification of finite inverse diagrams; it does not give the fine theories over a base, all small Kan extensions, or gluing. The fine theories need a presentation (BISITE E.3) or a derivator; no return derives them from axioms on `(M, W, 𝓛)` short of that. So his question at ms. 226 (can the hypotheses of the adjunction formulas be simplified) has the answer: the formulas are those of a presheaf ∞-category on the base (E.3, E.5), and the hypotheses reduce to the lifting-category conditions (F.2); an axiomatic derivation from `(M, W, Liss, Prop)` alone is not available and BISITE does not claim one.

**(g) Topological spaces.** STRATA (exit paths; hypercompletion), BISITE D.1–D.4 and D.11 together: open tests give cosheaves on finite Alexandrov bases; restriction to a closed subspace is not homotopical for open tests (D.4), which is the reason his hole-filling needs the "topos at the missing points" (the link of the stratum; STRATA Lead 3). The general statement (D.12) is undecided; its first case is the real line. Follow-up D4 and the insert for LM5.

## 4. What the seven establish about Grothendieck's text

Answers to questions he asks or leaves:
- `Cat hot → Hot` has no right adjoint; the representable types are the 1-types (CATHOT). V ms. 44–46 hold (GALOIS, PATHS).
- "Totally aspherical = filtered" (XII ms. 88; row II-C-003) is false in general (the injective ordinals), true for preorders, monoids, finitely many objects (TOTASPH). Row XIIb-a-B035 is false.
- `𝓛^ = Liss_W` (XII ms. 222: "J'ignore s'il est égal"): yes (BISITE B.6).
- `Liss ∩ Prop ⊂ Fib_W` (ms. 225, "peut-être"): only for `W∞` (BISITE B.9).
- "Dualité parfaite" in simplicial sets (ms. 225): yes, as the covariant and contravariant theories exchanged by `K ↦ K^op` (BISITE B.3, B.22); "dans toute catégorie `A^`": not by transport through categories of elements, and only with an involution exchanging the two classes (C.2–C.4); for topological spaces: cosheaves on finite Alexandrov bases, no unstable duality (D.2, D.3), stable duality by Lurie's Verdier duality under Aoki's criterion (G.9, G.12).
- Axioms a, b ⇒ c (ms. 217–218): no (E.12).
- The opposite directions of the adjunction formulas for the two topologies (VI ms. 84): opposition of the base exchanges smooth and proper; reversing adjunctions also takes opposite coefficient categories (BISITE F.8).
- The bifibration question (VI ms. 186; row VIc-C022): yes over posets, over bases with only monomorphisms or only epimorphisms, and for 1-truncated values (STRATA E.10); open over the five-element monoid, with invertible transitions excluded (section 3 c).
- Composition of path categories is associative in `Hot` for every admissible `Σ` (IX-037; PATHS C.P4); whether his path categories compute the mapping spaces of the localization is open (PATHS B.8).
- `W_D^{lc}` is a fundamental localizer for every derivator (I ms. 99–100; GALOIS B.3); `W_D^1` is not.
- Loc (6 b) forces `W ⊃ W_Z`; `W̃∞` does not exist inside `W∞` (POLES).
- The uniform theorem of XIII ms. 57–72 holds under its hypotheses, and its Alexandrov qualification adds no infinite case (BISITE A.25, A.27, A.28).

Assertions of the text that are false as written (each with a counterexample in a return and, where the companion has the row, its id):

- XII ms. 88, total asphericity implies filtered (TOTASPH; II-C-003); XIIb-a rows B035, C005, C035.

- XII ms. 274, Théorème 3° and Corollaire 1 without conservativity (POLES G.17–G.18; BISITE A.33).
- VII ms. 161–166, properness of the all-type endpoint functor by reversing paths (PATHS E.3; BISITE B.20).
