%%APPEND title="Degree two and the localizer correction"
\textbf{Strictness of $b_n\Rightarrow c_n$ for every $n\geq3$.}\ours\ The inclusion $i:S^2\vee S^{n-1}\to S^2\times S^{n-1}$ satisfies $c_n$ and not $b_n$, for every $n\geq3$.

\textit{Proof.} Both spaces are simply connected, so only constant coefficients $M$ occur. The product is the wedge with one cell of dimension $n+1$ attached. Hence $H^j(i;M)$ is an isomorphism for $j<n$ and injective for $j=n$. The relative group $H^{n+1}(S^2\times S^{n-1},S^2\vee S^{n-1};M)\cong M$ maps isomorphically to $H^{n+1}(S^2\times S^{n-1};M)$, so the connecting map out of $H^n(S^2\vee S^{n-1};M)$ vanishes and $H^n(i;M)$ is surjective. This is $c_n$. If $\pi_n(i)$ were injective, the attaching map of the top cell would be null-homotopic in the wedge and the identity of the wedge would extend to a retraction $r$ of the product. Then $r^*$ would be a ring homomorphism with $i^*r^*=\id$; since $i^*$ is an isomorphism in degrees $2$ and $n-1$, $r^*$ would send the generators $a,b$ of the cohomology of the wedge in these degrees, for which $ab=0$, to classes whose product generates $H^{n+1}(S^2\times S^{n-1};\mathbf Z)$. So $\pi_n(i)$ is not injective and $\tau_{\leq n}i$ is not an equivalence.\hfill$\square$

This extends the example $S^2\to K(\mathbf Z,2)$, which gives strictness at $n=3$ only; at $n=2$ the two conditions coincide, as shown above.
