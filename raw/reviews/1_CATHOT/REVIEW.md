## A reduction for E.17 (reviewer's argument; needs a second check)

E.17 asks: if `K = (K_i)_{i ∈ I}` is a small filtered diagram in `Cat hot` and `H_K(X) = colim_i π0 Fun(X, K_i)` inverts `W∞`, is `K` isomorphic in `Ind(Cat hot)` to `(Π_1 K_i)`? By the answer's criterion (E.17a) this holds iff `H_K` inverts every `η_X: X → Π_1 X`. I claim it follows from one group-theoretic statement.

**(KT_μ)** For every connected CW complex `Y` and every cardinal `μ` there are a group `G` and an acyclic map `t: BG → Y` (so `G → π_1 Y` is onto with perfect kernel `P`) such that every homomorphism from `G` to a group of cardinality `≤ μ` kills `P`.

Proof of E.17 from (KT_μ). By A.7, `H_K(X) = H'(BX)` with `H'(Y) = colim_i [Y, BK_i]`. Take `μ ≥ |Mor K_i|` for all `i`. Let `Y = ⊔_c Y_c` be a space with components `Y_c`, `π_c = π_1 Y_c`, and choose `t_c: BG_c → Y_c` as in (KT_μ). (a) Restriction along `q: ⊔ BG_c → ⊔ Bπ_c` is an isomorphism of categories `Fun(⊔ Bπ_c, K_i) → Fun(⊔ BG_c, K_i)` for every `i`: a functor is a family of objects `k_c` with homomorphisms `G_c → Aut(k_c)`, which kill `P_c`; transformations are the same on both sides. So `H'(τ_1 Y) → H'(⊔ BG_c)` is bijective. (b) This map is the composite of `a: H'(τ_1 Y) → H'(Y)` and `t^*: H'(Y) → H'(⊔ BG_c)`. (c) `t^*` is injective: an acyclic map `t_c` is an epimorphism in the unpointed homotopy category (the fold map `Y_c ∪^h_{BG_c} Y_c → Y_c` is an equivalence, being acyclic and a `π_1`-isomorphism), and filtered colimits preserve injections. Hence `a` is bijective; for `Y = BX` this is `H_K(η_X)`. All components are treated at once, so infinitely many components cause no trouble.

(KT_μ) should follow from Maunder's proof of the Kan–Thurston theorem (replace each simplex `σ` of dimension `≥ 2` by `K(C_σ, 1)` with `C_σ` an acyclic group containing `π_1` of the already constructed part over `∂σ`): the kernel of `G → π_1 Y` is the normal closure of the cone groups `C_σ`, and by B.7 each `C_σ` can be taken acyclic and **simple of cardinality `> μ`**, so that every homomorphism to a group of cardinality `≤ μ` kills each `C_σ` and hence `P`. I have not checked Maunder's induction (injectivity of `π_1` over subcomplexes) against the source. This is the statement to hand to the next prompt.

If E.17 holds, Grothendieck's sentence at V ms. 46 ("cela équivaut à la condition qu'il puisse se représenter par un système inductif sur N formé de catégories de Thomason-Kan") is true with "Thomason–Kan" read as local, and it says more than he meant: an ind-representable functor on all of `Cat hot` that factors through `Hot` factors through `Π_1`.

## Consequences the answer does not draw

1. **A weak equivalence between Thomason-fibrant categories need not be an isomorphism in `Cat hot`** (row V-I060). Let `R` be a fibrant poset of type `S¹`. `η: R → Π_1 R` is a weak equivalence of fibrant categories (groupoids are fibrant). It is not invertible in `Cat hot`: `π0 Fun(BZ, R) = π0 R` is a point, `π0 Fun(BZ, Π_1 R) = Z`. Same mechanism as A.6 (i).
2. **Test (b) cannot be asked on all shapes.** For a small `(M, Σ)` the functor `H(X) = Hom_{M^X[Σ_X^{-1}]}(a_X, b_X)` is ind-represented on all of `Cat hot` by the sequence of path categories (V ms. 44–45, row V-I047: it is the `π0` theorem applied to `M^X`). By D.8, if `H ≅ [B−, Z]` on all small `X` then `Z` is a 1-type; by E.17 (if it holds) `W∞`-invariance of `H` on all small `X` already forces `H` to factor through `Π_1`. So "sends weak equivalences of `Y` to bijections" (V ms. 46; VII ms. 129, row VIIc-B002; IX ms. 22, rows IX-079–081) is a reasonable test only for `Y` in a class bounded relative to `M` (finite categories, as he proposes himself at V ms. 51), and a model category is rectifiable only at shapes small relative to the universe in which it is complete. On finite `Y` the obstruction disappears: D.3, D.5.
3. **V ms. 48–49, the Question.** The answer is no for fibrant `K, K', K_n, K'_n` (E.4) and yes when `K` and `K'` are local (E.18). His expected mechanism (minimal lengths `l(n) → ∞`) is not what happens in E.4, where the lengths are 3 at every stage and the stagewise homotopies are incompatible. The unbounded-length mechanism does occur for the infinite fence (C.5), which is contractible, not fibrant, not local.

## What the literature has, what would be new

Already in the literature (and cited as such by the answer): Thomason's model structure and Raptis's bounded variants (A.5, D.9); embeddings into simple and into acyclic groups (B.5–B.7: Higman–Neumann–Neumann, Baumslag–Dyer–Heller; the alternating union is noted on MathOverflow); locality of simply connected spaces for homology (B.9, Bousfield); the shift on the finitary symmetric group (F.2: Matumoto–Minami–Sugawara, Arlin–Christensen); F.4, F.5, F.9; compact generation of spaces (F.7); McDuff's theorem (G.3); Fiedorowicz's monoid (G.9); Sweeney's semigroup (G.10); Barmak–Minian and Cianci–Ottina for posets (G.7 objects, G.8); `RP^n` as an acyclic category with `n+1` objects (G.11: Carcacía-Campos, four days before the answer); realization of finite simply connected complexes by finite monoids (G.5: Ben-Bassat, unrefereed).

Not found by the answer's searches and not known to me (elementary, but I know no reference): the non-existence of the right adjoint and the classification C.2–C.5; D.1 in its explicit form, D.3–D.5, D.8; E.4, E.5, E.18; E.20–E.25 as proofs for Grothendieck's exact path model (the companion has the comparison for all arrows; the direct proof of `T² = 1` and of associativity in `Hot` is new to it); F.3, F.10–F.13 (F.12 is the most substantial: no finite categorical height is faithful or full on homotopy classes); G.4, G.6 (a conjecture of October 2026 refuted), G.12, G.13; the arrow minima in G.7, G.15.
