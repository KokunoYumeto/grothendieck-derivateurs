## Mathematical conclusions and repairs

The variance convention is fixed before using any modern theorem. The manuscript treats presheaves, so its explicit 2-categorical source is Dia^coop. Its *dérivateur à gauche* has right Kan extensions, and its *dérivateur à droite* has left Kan extensions. Modern covariant statements are translated by opposites. The successive provisional and systematic axiom numberings are not conflated. A restricted diagram domain must contain the opposites and comma categories required by the assertion being applied; the elementary closure clauses alone do not automatically supply every shape used later.

The opening presheaf calculations are largely correct. The commentary supplies the pointwise Kan formulas, failures of second adjoints, the support adjunctions, and all four exceptional endpoints of the finite-ordinal chain. The chain has 2n+7 terms with the edition's restriction to elementary faces and degeneracies. Arbitrary monotone maps also have the indicated second adjoints under the relevant pointed finite-limit and finite-colimit hypotheses, by factorization. The stronger common-chain assertion is false, as the edition already says.

The distinction between conservative and faithful underlying diagrams is exhibited by a nonsplit extension in the category of arrows of abelian groups. Its nonzero derived extension class vanishes at both endpoints. The same example gives an explicit negative answer to the question on manuscript p. 18: for unrestricted morphisms of derivators, evaluation at the base category is not faithful. A represented derivator of the complete and cocomplete category [1] turns this class into a nonzero modification whose base component is zero. The construction is checked on both 1-cells and 2-cells. It already works for strict natural morphisms, but no conclusion is asserted for subcategories in which all morphisms must preserve designated Kan extensions.

HOTAB on these pages is the connective derived theory of abelian groups. It is pointed and additive but is not stable. Free simplicial abelianization preserves homotopy colimits and fails to preserve homotopy pullbacks. The two-by-two discrete-space example gives a comparison Z^4→Z^3 with an explicit nonzero kernel. The finite stable exactness theorem does answer the later “Perplexité” after exactness is restricted appropriately; it does not prove preservation of all Kan extensions. The diagonal array of copies of a field gives the distinct obstruction k^(N)→k^N for arbitrary products and coproducts. The exact positive finiteness statement is cited in its verified version.

The withdrawn cofinality axiom is genuinely false for arbitrary coefficients. The inclusion of the initial endpoint into [1] has contractible relevant slices but does not make restriction fully faithful. The coefficient arrow 0→Z is a direct counterexample. On manuscript pp. 47–48, the surviving slice calculation is valid; the invalid final step invokes the wrong cofinal orientation. The unstruck remark about the left Kan extension of the terminal space diagram also needs correction: it is equivalent to the indicated cohomological cofinality condition, not a stronger condition implied by either orientation. The commentary isolates these failures without declaring the valid preliminary calculations defective.

The proper and smooth base-change theorem is correct. The lift-category description gives complete proofs of pullback stability and composition; the latter uses Quillen's Theorem A after an explicit identification of the intermediate functor. Strictness matters: an equivalence of categories which misses an object can fail both properness and smoothness. The two-object indiscrete groupoid gives a small example. The conjectural converse on manuscript p. 58 is now a theorem: spaces alone detect universal homotopy exactness (Groth, Ponto, & Shulman, 2014, Theorem 3.16). The commentary supplies direct proper and smooth converse arguments using the relevant fibre tests, rather than only reporting the later theorem.

The last constant-coefficient definitions have their stated formal two-out-of-three and Yoneda properties. The passage from the slice condition to stability under every local isomorphism needs one step omitted in the printed argument, which mentions only open immersions. Local isomorphisms are discrete fibrations; unique lifting gives the required isomorphism X′/y′≅X/g(y′). Locally constant coefficients are handled by the canonical transformation from the slice projection to the constant terminal object and conservativity. Finally, the proof of Proposition 4 is sound in its Hom-set form under the one-sided left-Kan hypothesis. Its cohomology notation additionally requires the absolute right Kan extensions defining H*. The commentary states and proves both forms with the exact existence hypotheses.

The comparisons with higher categories concern the whole prederivator, not its base category alone. Arlin's strict and pseudonatural embeddings are distinguished, including their size and indexing-domain hypotheses. Renaudin's comparison is stated for left proper combinatorial model theories, their localization at Quillen equivalences, and adjunctions. Riehl–Verity supplies the pointwise Kan and comma-square passage from complete and cocomplete quasicategories. No claim that every base-category functor has a unique coherent lift is inferred from these results.

## The marked development

The single `development` block is the cone test at manuscript p. 60. For a small category K, adjoin an initial object and map the resulting category K^triangleleft to [1], sending the new apex to 0 and K to 1. The functor is always smooth and a weak equivalence on nerves. It is proper precisely when NK is weakly contractible. If K is nonempty and connected, every strict base change satisfies ordinary right Beck–Chevalley for every complete ordinary coefficient category. The proof treats arbitrary base changes by examining each comma category and constructs the corresponding ordinary cones directly.

For the four-object crown, K has the homotopy type of a circle. The resulting five-object source passes all those ordinary-coefficient tests but fails the space-valued test through S^1→Map(S^1,S^1), whose image has degree zero while the target has integer-indexed components. The proposition is marked `ours` for this explicit construction and organization; it claims no priority. The properness/finality criteria themselves are known and cited. We did not find this exact organization in the checked portions of Maltsiniotis (2005, §§1 and 4) and Groth–Ponto–Shulman (2014, §3). This is a bounded literature statement, not a claim to have searched every possible source. A separate mathematical review checked the arbitrary-base-change assertion, empty and nonempty cases, variance, and degree calculation.

The represented-coefficient trichotomy at manuscript p. 62 is an elementary worked consequence of naturality and is deliberately not marked `ours`. It includes thin and empty coefficient categories. The functor BG→e for a nontrivial group passes every represented-prederivator test but fails the HOT test, directly witnessed by the identity and constant maps in [BG,BG]. Both this calculation and the evaluation counterexample received an independent mathematical check.
