# DER XV-b: mathematical audit, commentary, and reader

## Scope, responsibility, and contents

This package audits the whole supplied excerpt of Alexander Grothendieck's *Les Dérivateurs*, Chapter XV, part 2: the opening continuation of manuscript page 56, manuscript pages 57–137, and the inserted pages 133 bis, 133 ter, 133 quater, and 133 quinquies. The authoritative attachment contains 42 PDF leaves. They correspond to leaves 36–77 of the editors' complete chapter file and printed pages 34–75. These three numbering systems are not interchangeable. The manuscript has 85 explicit page markers in the supplied transcription; the opening continuation precedes its first marker.

The mathematical audit and English commentary are AI-produced by **GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI, ChatGPT Pro mode)**. The French source remains Grothendieck's, in the electronic edition deciphered and transcribed by M. Künzer, J. Malgoire, and G. Maltsiniotis. This package does not attribute the commentary to a human and does not certify any subsequent assembly or editing with Claude or another system.

The six required files are complete. There are **40 commentary blocks**, approximately **17817 whitespace-delimited words** of commentary, **289 audit rows**, **48 correction or retained-reading records**, and **12 bibliographic entries**. The overview contains approximately 1761 words. Counts of words include TeX commands and mathematical tokens and are therefore reproducible measures of file size, not linguistic word counts. The built reader sets the English commentary into the French text; the overview and bibliography follow it. The archive also contains the corrected transcription, build support, and the code and reviewed component inputs needed to reproduce the assembly.

The supplied transcription was read in full, including its displays, diagrams, marginal page markers, editorial brackets, and footnotes. The overlapping assignments were the opening through the beginning of manuscript page 84; sections 7–8 from the heading on page 84 through page 115; and sections 9–10 on pages 116–137, including the supplementary pages. Every numbered proposition, theorem, lemma, corollary, displayed axiom, conjecture, and question is represented in the audit, together with substantive unnumbered steps and footnote claims. Compound statements are separated when their statuses or repairs differ. The register uses the source's proof status: for example, a theorem announced but not proved here is `stated`, even when an independently established modern theorem is identified in the repair column. The value `false` is reserved for an assertion or inference that is actually false as written. A missing step alone is not recorded as a counterexample. Purely typographic footnotes, organizational comments, and the repetition of a statement already audited are addressed in the corresponding entry or textual register rather than counted as new mathematical results.

The supplied earlier-commentary index ends at **DER XIIapp**. It contains no commentary for XIII, XIV, or XV-a. We therefore could not check continuity against the immediately preceding package's commentary. References to earlier blocks use the actual supplied index, especially the earlier discussions of converse excision, evaluation conservativity, constant coefficients, and finite products of localizations. The text of those earlier commentary packages was not supplied, and this report does not certify their proofs as independently read. For the opening assumptions, the editors' complete Chapter XV PDF was consulted at manuscript pages 44–56. ## Principal mathematical conclusions

### Accessibility and the abandoned generation theorem

The commentary translates the manuscript's cardinal convention carefully. A good bound π corresponds to modern π⁺-presentability, but accessibility of an embedded class here is the stronger bounded-presentation recognition condition bπ. It is not interchangeable with accessibility of the full subcategory in the modern sense. L-accessibility adds stability under all small filtered colimits. This distinction is necessary both for the positive results in section 5 and for evaluating the failed proof in section 6.

The proofs supplied include the missing equality adjustment in the opening zigzag; the intersection and continuity arguments for closed cofinal indexing sets; the filtered opfibration and interchange of colimits used in flattening; bounded-diagram regrouping; the endpoint of the transfinite intersection induction; its sharp cardinal bound; and the failure of arbitrary unions, illustrated by bounded-exponent groups and a Prüfer group. The passage to diagram categories is related explicitly to SGA 4 I.9.22–9.24.

Proposition 5.9 needs a precise qualification. Its displayed weakening to (a), (b), (c), and (d₀) at an arbitrary bound is not justified by its written subobject argument. We give a complete proof under the full standing assumptions at an arbitrary bound, and a second proof under (d₀) at a good presentability bound. We have not proved the simultaneous removal of both groups of supplementary hypotheses, and we have not found a counterexample to that remaining generality. The operative good-cardinal case is established. The register does not label the proposition false merely because the broader sketch remains incomplete.

For the cancelled proof of 6.1, the continuous diagram of inclusions S→B, indexed by subsets S of size at most π in a set B larger than π, gives a complete counterexample to the final inference: its colimit is the identity of B, while none of its stages is an isomorphism. This diagnoses the loss of the bound on the codomain. It does not by itself decide the theorem.

The theorem itself is false. Cox, Feigert, Kamsma, Mazari-Armida, and Rosický (2026, Theorem 5.1) classify the presheaf categories in which pure monomorphisms are cofibrantly generated. For the finite fork P = (1←0→2), the requisite linear-preorder condition fails. The development at manuscript page 84 proves that pure monomorphisms in Set^P satisfy the manuscript's stronger L-π recognition criterion for every infinite π, and proves closure under pushouts, composition, transfinite composition, and retracts. The small object argument then turns the conclusion proposed in 6.1 into cofibrant generation, contradicting the cited theorem. This is a counterexample inside a presheaf category satisfying the relevant filtration and exactness conditions, not merely an analogy with ordinary accessibility.

The external non-generation theorem belongs to the cited authors. The bounded-recognition comparison and its application to Grothendieck's assertion are supplied here and marked as such, without a priority claim. The consequence for 6.2 is limited to the unconditional small-set reduction. Non-cofibrant generation alone does not disprove the intrinsic equivalence in 6.3 or the existence of a factorization system generated by a proper class; those distinctions are preserved in the audit.

### The successful generation argument and the interval criterion

Section 7 recovers a valid argument because the class Mon∩W has cancellation and pushout properties unavailable to an arbitrary accessible class. Effective unions keep the attachment monic; pushout stability and two-out-of-three keep the residual inclusion in W. Well-poweredness bounds the strictly increasing chain of subobjects. The commentary retains the full original subobject clause in (d) and the well-poweredness explicitly needed by Lemma 7.3. It does not assert a counterexample to the abstract theorem with the latter condition omitted.

The model recognition argument is compared with Smith's theorem in Beke (2000, Theorem 1.7), keeping its closure and solution-set assumptions. It is not used to vindicate the unrestricted theorem of section 6. Worked group-action examples identify generators and the corresponding lifting maps. Two finite categorical examples show concretely why the exactness properties used in the presheaf argument do not hold automatically in Cat.

The right-properness condition printed in (7.4.3) places the fibration assumption on the pullback arrow rather than the original right vertical arrow. The endpoint inclusions in the simplicial interval give a counterexample to that printed formulation. This is an edition slip, not a transcription error.

Abstract Lemma 8.7 is false as stated. In groups, take weak equivalences detected by Hom(C₂,−), use the cylinder Z→1 with its unique endpoints, and form the prescribed mapping cylinder of 1→C₂. The right factor C₂*Z→C₂ identifies distinct involutions and is not a weak equivalence. The proof omitted stability of the relevant endpoint weak equivalence under pushout. That condition repairs the cylinder argument.

Theorem 8.4 survives its reduced hypotheses by a direct interval-lifting and retract argument. This proof is established literature, explicitly Cisinski (2006, Lemma 1.4.13), and is cited rather than marked as a new result. The commentary also preserves the extra force of the reverse-excision hypotheses: the two converses in (8.2.4) are not consequences of ordinary left properness. Cubical examples are stated only with the test-category scope supported by the checked convention; no unqualified strict-test or cartesian-product claim is made.

### Localizer axioms and the final derivator statement

Sections 9–10 distinguish weak Theorem A, Loc(3), from its relative form, Loc(3 bis). Modern results for relative fundamental localizers cannot simply be substituted for conclusions advertised under the weak list. The collapse argument in 9.12 is supplied in full with stability under product made explicit. The gap in obtaining that operation from the weak list alone is retained, as the edition itself observes on manuscript page 133 quinquies. The editorial bracket on page 133 bis reverses the roles of the two implications; the original implication called immediate is indeed immediate.

The commentary proves directly that retract closure and preservation of connected components imply reflection of weak equivalences by arbitrary coproduct summands. Under modern relative-localizer hypotheses, where coproduct closure is available, this defeats the proposed independence in the indicated direction. It does not silently settle the separately weaker axiom system. The two degenerate localizations, the terminal category and the walking arrow, and the component localization Set are computed through their universal properties.

The short proof of Corollary 9.3 also requires a replacement in the restricted perfect-functor class covered by Loc(6c′), whereas the page writes only Parf_W. Moreover, footnote 54 cites 9.3 for coproduct preservation, although 9.3 proves reflection and footnote 47 explicitly reserves the converse. These are recorded dependencies, not claimed counterexamples to those conclusions.

Theorem 10.1 is announced, but its promised next section is absent. The structural derivator conclusions are related to Cisinski's model structure for an accessible relative fundamental localizer and Groth's theorem on the derivator associated with a combinatorial model category. The pointwise Kan-extension formulas keep the contravariant indexing convention. Evaluation conservativity is proved through replacements in the diagram model structure, rather than inferred formally from strong saturation alone.

Part 10.1(3b) uses stale numbering: its prose calls for the fibration axiom, now Loc(6b), while its printed number is Loc(5b), now coproduct reflection. Reading the current number literally makes the claim false. The rational-homology localizer satisfies the displayed size and excision assumptions, as well as the indicated saturation, terminal-object, relative Theorem A, and summand conditions. Nevertheless BC₂→e is a rational homology equivalence and the regular action on two points cannot descend to a constant object. The component functor detects the nontrivial action after localization. The development proves the bounded mapping-cone filling argument needed for L-accessibility and the relative-chain excision needed for Loc(4), so the example is checked against the actual opening assumptions. It also exhibits the failure of the intended old-fibration axiom.

For the ordinary weak-homotopy localizer, Cisinski's published locally constant functor theorem gives the modern invariance result, including shifts through model-category coefficients. An explicit hom-set retract proves reflection by any nonempty shift. Empty shifts cannot detect equivalences. The commentary does not claim that a theorem about ordinary weak equivalences proves the full locally constant assertion for an arbitrary larger localizer under every reduced hypothesis list in 10.1.

## Reproduction and final reader verification

The supplied `build_reader.py` validates the six-file interface and inserts the blocks. The source uses exact unique word anchors where necessary. Its original treatment of the spaced supplementary page markers is retained: the final collapse block is attached with a unique substring under p133, which encompasses those supplementary pages in the supplied builder. No anchor-validation rule has been weakened. The companion style is retained except for any explicitly recorded layout adjustment needed to eliminate a demonstrated rendering defect.

On a normal TeX installation, run `python build_reader.py DER_XV-b`, then LuaLaTeX, BibTeX, and two further LuaLaTeX passes. This runtime initially lacked usable prebuilt formats and had broken system filename-database links. The build used a local cache and compatible missing TeX packages, without modifying the installed distribution. The runtime helper and environment-repair record are included as code; these repairs are not mathematical dependencies of the reader.

Final compilation, pagination, citation-resolution, and visual-inspection results are recorded in `BUILD_VERIFICATION.md`. The companion is complete as an audit of the supplied material. The specific mathematical limitations above remain visible rather than being hidden behind a blanket claim that every broad hypothesis reduction has been settled.
