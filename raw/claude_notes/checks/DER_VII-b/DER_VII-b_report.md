## Mathematical findings

The ordinary localization theorem is sound under the stated wide-subcategory hypothesis on the denominators. The commentary proves the evaluation map's invariance under both endpoint-fixed ladders and changes of type, including the telescoping naturality equations. No inverse of an internal vertical component is used. It also proves the functor-category form of the universal property, not only the factorization of objects, and verifies concatenation on total morphisms and its strict associativity with the standard numbered intervals. Worked span and parallel-arrow examples exhibit a relative localization and a surviving fundamental loop.

The category of increasing endpoint-preserving maps between two finite oriented intervals admits a categorical contraction whenever it is nonempty. The proof normalizes one target run at a time through idempotent retractions and natural transformations whose direction is determined by that run. It reaches Grothendieck's quickest map in at most the number of target runs. This proves the stronger assertion asked about on manuscript page 86. A four-object example separately shows why the quickest map need be neither initial nor terminal. The finite enumeration supplied with the archive checks 1,953 pairs of types, including 1,213 nonempty mapping categories, 5,503 objects, and 23,668 comparable pairs in the retraction checks. That enumeration is corroboration; the result rests on the general proof in the commentary.

The abstract comparison of a total category with the ind-object or sequential colimit of its fibres fails under the source's general assumptions. The constant terminal-fibre diagram over a nontrivial one-object group has point-valued sequential models and total homotopy type BG. The source recognizes this obstruction. Its related assertion that the preorder of a slice equals the corresponding lower ideal is false even for a walking parallel pair. The commentary gives the complete finite counterexample, the correct lower-ideal restriction for the ind-object, and the stronger categorical filtering hypothesis under which the slice argument works.

For universal preservation of the total homotopy type of every Grothendieck fibration, the exact condition is contractibility of the comma categories α/t. Cartesian lifts identify the relevant comma categories by an adjunction, and the dual form of Quillen's Theorem A proves sufficiency. Applying the proposed base change to the discrete fibration T/t proves necessity. This universal criterion is distinguished from hypotheses that suffice only for diagrams satisfying the source's homotopy condition. The manuscript's assertion that the projection from the category of types is neither 0-smooth nor 0-proper is verified by two explicit disconnected lifting categories.

The properness and smoothness sufficient conditions stated on manuscript page 112 are also proved under the source's homotopy hypothesis. The strict fibres of the projection to the preorder are contractible by the smooth/proper fibre criterion and the extra coasphericity/asphericity. Parallel-pullback homotopy makes the coefficient diagram locally constant on those fibres. Left homotopy Kan extension in the smooth case, and right homotopy Kan extension in the proper case, then descend the diagram to the preorder. The cofinal sequence there gives the required canonical comparison. The proof uses the exact pointwise extension and local-constancy results in Cisinski's published 2009 paper. A one-object monoid with absorbing zero shows why these sufficient conditions must not be misreported as the universal comma criterion for arbitrary coefficients.

The sequential total category maps by a weak equivalence to the ordinary sequential colimit, without a full-faithfulness requirement on the bonding functors. The nerve and filtered-colimit arguments are explained, with the precise variance of the Grothendieck construction. A separate interleaving proof establishes independence of the sequential weak homotopy type under the parallel-pullback homotopy hypothesis. Its conclusion is an isomorphism in Hot; the construction does not give a preferred isomorphism or a categorical homotopy equivalence in Cat hot.

Finite categories are valid finite-presentation probes for the asserted Cat hot formula, but they need not be compact probes in Hot. The category of the group of order two, together with successive skeletons of its nerve and their simplex categories, supplies a fully worked counterexample. The mod-two cohomology calculation is included. Likewise, the pairing into stage n+m is valid for a compatible standard chain, but not for an arbitrary cofinal chain. A slowly growing alternating sequence gives a concrete failure, including an empty target path fibre; the general repair uses a sufficiently large stage k(n,m).

The suspicion about strict loop multiplication on manuscript page 113 is explicitly withdrawn in footnote 24. Kan's loop-group theorem supplies the classical correction. More decisively for this construction, the same chapter treats the unrestricted path comparison positively in §13, manuscript pages 157–170. The elementary lemma left unwritten at page 170 is the constant-path equivalence X → Fun(Iτ,X), stated at page 160 and used at page 166. It is not a sequential-colimit lemma: the relevant sequential projections are recalled as already checked on page 159. The commentary supplies the missing elementary proof by retracting successive end vertices of the oriented interval. This later result is confined to unrestricted paths and does not remove the general base-category obstruction.

The final parameterized localization formula is proved with the missing parameter Y and the correct ambient ind-category made explicit. The modern hammock comparison is stated at the level of mapping spaces with an explicit marking convention: Grothendieck's fibres here allow arbitrary internal vertical transformations, while a relative hammock model marks those transformations as weak equivalences. Recognizing similar zigzag shapes does not supply a comparison for arbitrary relative markings.
