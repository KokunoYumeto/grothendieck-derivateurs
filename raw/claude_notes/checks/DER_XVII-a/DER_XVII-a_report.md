## Mathematical conclusions

### Diagram cofibrations and transfinite gluing

The decisive distinction is between the relative latching condition of section 1 and the stronger open-set condition of section 2. Writing `Q_f(K,J) = X_J amalg_{X_K} Y_K`, the strong condition tests its comparison to Y_J for every lower open K inside a principal lower set J. The latching condition tests only strict predecessors. Theorem 2.1(1) extends the strong condition to every inclusion of lower opens without artinianity. Well-founded induction then identifies it with the latching tests and proves the trivial-cofibration equality under the stated hypotheses.

The commentary supplies the continuous relative filtration, finite and well-founded inductions, pushout/excision identities, and exact two-out-of-three steps. It does not assume that all weak equivalences are stable under pushout or that arbitrary objectwise weak equivalences survive arbitrary colimits. The relative cofibration data make the arguments work. Footnote 5 removes cofibrancy too broadly for the transition-map assertions of Corollary 2; footnote 7 already supplies the restriction. A finite example makes this explicit. The page-23 question literally asks for a locally finite result already proved; its surrounding discussion seeks the stronger artinian assertion. Both readings are recorded.

Lemma 2.6 is correct. A universal-property calculation proves the pushout identity actually needed. If *totalement cocartésien* means that all six faces of the intermediate cube are pushouts, the description is too strong; a finite-set counterexample disproves that subsidiary reading. The needed bottom face is nevertheless cocartesian. The commentary qualifies the interpretation instead of declaring the lemma false because of terminology alone.

Lemma 2.7, as an isolated statement, omits continuity of the ordinal filtration and mixes the starting points of its relative and absolute versions. A one-object example disproves the version without continuity. The relative composite starts at `Q_f(K,J)`; an initial pushout of `f_K` belongs only to the absolute map `f_J`. The filtration actually constructed for Theorem 2.1 is continuous, so the repair preserves the theorem.

Under the ordinary choice principles used here, an artinian poset with noetherian lower Alexandrov topology is finite. Successively choosing minimal remaining elements would otherwise give an infinite increasing sequence of finite lower opens. This explains why finite colimits suffice in Theorem 3.1's first branch. Noetherianity alone does not imply finiteness.

### Replacement, homotopy calculus, and adjunction

The diagram factorization is completed by adjoining one minimal missing object at a time. It factors the relative latching comparison, preserves previous choices, and takes unions of compatible data at limits. This avoids applying set-theoretic Zorn literally to a possibly proper class of partial factorizations. The branching chain-complex example gives the differential, factorization maps, kernel contraction, and relative quotient explicitly.

The page-41 QB relation is read as printed: precomposition with a weak equivalence, followed by cylinder homotopy. It implies equality after localization. The later target-enlargement equality criterion is stated separately. The source still localizes its homotopy quotient by fractions; the difference between the two formulas is therefore not itself a counterexample to its fraction-calculus assertion. The cited Brown result is registered as an assertion, with the precise later theorem identified.

The square-exactness clause is made explicit as surjectivity of `Hom_H(P,T) → Hom_H(B,T) ×_{Hom_H(A,T)} Hom_H(C,T)` for a homotopy pushout completing `B ← A → C` to P. A full proof uses common trivial-cofibration target denominators, a cylinder on A, and the gluing lemma. The homotopy pushout `* ← S^0 → *` and test object `S^1` show why injectivity fails. Chapter XIV supplies the explicit dual Hom formulation, not an independently recovered numbered Dér 6 definition. This consequence is distinguished from the stronger quadrangulated axioms criticized in that chapter’s editorial postface.

Proposition 3.2's restriction statement is proved by ordinary finality; its left Kan statement follows by identifying colimits over target opens with colimits over inverse images. Corollary 3.3 needs only the automatic inclusion `(TC)_{I′} ⊆ C_{I′} ∩ W_objectwise`; the stronger equality at a nonartinian target is unnecessary. The complete mapping-cylinder factorization shows why preservation of trivial cofibrations between cofibrant objects implies preservation of every weak equivalence between them.

Remark 3.6 is developed through two walking-arrow calculations. One satisfies the stated replacement-subcategory hypotheses but produces a candidate which is not a left adjoint; the other produces the adjunction but loses full faithfulness of the localized right adjoint. Theorem 3.7 is then proved through the two pieces of the mapping-cone category. Its mixed hom-sets, comma-category initial objects, extension by the initial object, descent of both adjunctions, and identification of their composite are explicit.

### Derivator axioms and arbitrary target categories

The historical Dér 2/Dér 3 labels were checked in Chapter I rather than inferred from modern numbering. Dér 2 is conservativity of the underlying-diagram functor. After the renumbering on manuscript pages 67–68, the final-object form of Dér 3 says that constants are fully faithful on a shape with a final object. The constant/evaluation adjunction gives a direct proof after localization, respecting the source's contravariant diagram convention. Stronger later variants concerning locally constant objects are not attributed to XVII, Corollary 3.9.

The unrestricted-target claim needs existence of the relevant comma colimits. A finite source can have infinitely many arrows into a target object. The `FinSet` example for `e → Bℤ` shows that the finite-colimit branch can fail even to admit the desired left adjoint. The cocomplete/transfinite branch has the needed colimits. Its assumptions also imply the ABC coproduct and sequential-composition axioms, as proved in the commentary, so the cited later arbitrary-shape theorems apply to those actual assumptions.

The final variance check changes a provisional audit verdict. Section 3.11 explicitly returns to the historical presheaf convention `D_W(J)=Fun(J^op,M)[W^-1]`. Its printed comma category `i′\I = (i′ ↓ u)` is correct: the left Kan value is a colimit over that comma category's opposite. The preceding covariant construction `H(I)=Fun(I,M)[W^-1]` instead uses `(u ↓ i′)`. The commentary computes the walking-arrow example in both conventions. It does not present the covariant value as a counterexample to the presheaf formula, and the proposed comma-orientation edition slip has been removed.

This also qualifies the direct inference to Corollary 3.9's infinite domain. Since `H(I)=D_W(I^op)`, the well-founded covariant proof directly treats presheaf indices whose opposites are artinian. Finite posets are unaffected by this translation. The manuscript does not spell out the opposition at the transition. This is a qualification of what its preceding proof establishes, not a declaration that the infinite conclusion is false: the later ABC arbitrary-shape theorem, under the actual cocomplete/transfinite assumptions, supplies the broader result. With a parameter Z the presheaf coefficient category is `Fun(Z^op,M)`; its role is distinguished from the covariant latching direction.

## Developments and examples

Three results are marked `\ours`, with complete proofs and bounded literature statements. The first characterizes well-founded posets by a universal monomorphism property for relative latching maps of set diagrams. Its converse uses the upward closure of a descending chain and checks every latching category. The second proves `(C∩W)_I = C_I ∩ W_objectwise` whenever every principal lower set is a chain, without artinianity, using well-ordered cofinal subsets. The third gives the finite-set obstruction to arbitrary-target extension. These are formulations and proofs supplied for the commentary, not claims of priority.

Further worked examples include intersecting branches in sets, failure of ordinary pushout to preserve an inadequately cofibrant weak equivalence of diagrams, the reverse chain, the explicit chain-complex factorization, two walking-arrow localizations, and the boundary-face poset of a triangle. In the last, a cofibrant replacement of the constant point has points at vertices and intervals at edges. Its colimit is a circle; first homology detects the noninvertible derived counit despite ordinary full faithfulness of constants.

The general page-38 question for arbitrary posets under exactly the strong open-set hypotheses was **not settled by this audit**. The principal-chain subclass is proved. The inspected direct/Reedy and ABC sources were not found to state the unrestricted equality in that form. This is a bounded search result, not an assertion that the problem is known to remain open throughout the literature.
