## 1. The shape of the whole

### 1.1 What the returned half is

The returned half is one experiment repeated in several settings: take a construction that is correct for ordinary categories of presheaves (SGA 4), ask what must replace it when the coefficients are homotopy types or an arbitrary coefficient theory **D**, and find the replacement by trying the naive transfer and watching it fail. Chapter I does this for cofinality and base change, II for filtering and connectedness, III–V and VII, IX for Hom-sets and paths, VI and VIII for fibred categories over a base, XII for pushouts. The 225 false rows are mostly the recorded failures of the naive transfer; Grothendieck withdraws a good part of them himself.

### 1.2 Structures and moves that recur

1. **One object, the category of factorizations, read at different depths.** For `u: A → B` and an arrow `b0 → b1`, the category of factorizations `b0 → u(a) → b1` (or its one-sided versions, the comma and lift categories) controls: homotopy exactness of squares (I), full faithfulness of `u*` (II §4, I-b, VIII), properness and smoothness (I, VI), and the Hom-sets of a pushout (`Hom(u',t) = π0(u'\U/t)`, XII ms. 72). The ordinary theory uses its `π0`; the theory for 1-types uses its fundamental groupoid (VIII, the three-level criterion [C]); the homotopy theory uses its nerve ([E] Groth–Ponto–Shulman 2014, Groth–Šťovíček 2016, Maltsiniotis 2005, as cited in the companion). [I] Nearly every withdrawn statement in I, II and XII is a place where `π0` of such a category was used and the nerve was needed: ordinary pushouts, ordinary Kan extensions `f_!1`, "total asphericity", connected lift categories.

2. **Three theories over a base, never two.** Over `S` there are the coarse localization (total spaces; locally constant objects), and two fine ones (tests on `s\X` or on `X/s`; contravariant and covariant diagrams). This triple appears as constant / locally constant / arbitrary coefficients in I-b, as the three localizations of `Cat/S` in VI, as groupoid fibrations in VIII, and as `(M, W, 𝓛)` with a class `𝓛` of "local isomorphisms" in XII §6. The stable pattern is a reflection `ρ ⊣ i` of the fine theory onto the locally constant one. [E] In today's terms: `Ho(Spaces/BS) ⊂ Ho Fun(N(S)^op, Spaces)` with its two adjoints (Cisinski 2009).

3. **Two replacements with different jobs.** The comma replacement `Φ, Ψ` (free split fibration, opfibration) makes restriction literal and is fine-invertible; the path replacement `C = Ch_∞` makes fibres homotopy-invariant and is only coarse-invertible. VI-b ms. 120 and VI-c ms. 142, 151–156 are the same confusion between them, caught three times [C]. [I] These are the fibrant replacements of two different model structures on the slice (right fibrations versus Kan fibrations; Lurie HTT ch. 2), which is why no single one can do both jobs.

4. **Integrate instead of gluing.** Wherever a colimit in `Cat` is wanted, the total category of the diagram replaces it: `f_!` by composition (VI), the integrated span instead of the pushout (XII ms. 120–169), the collage of a sieve and its complement with the category of crossing arrows (XIIapp). [E] Thomason 1979.

5. **Truncate, then look one level higher on the base.** Sets (II §3), groupoids (VIII), `n`-types (XII §6.1) are treated separately, and each time coefficients of level `n` detect level `n+1` of the base: locally constant sets see `π1`; locally constant groupoid families over a 1-connected base still carry an `H²`-class (VIII ms. 9, the tetrahedron counterexample [C]); `a_n ⇒ b_n ⇒ c_n ⇒ a_{n-1}` (XII ms. 178–187). See 3.2.

6. **No chosen fibrations or cofibrations.** Fibrations are the maps along which base change preserves `W`; cofibrations those along which cobase change does; smooth and proper maps are defined by base change against a coefficient theory. [E] These are the sharp maps and `h`-cofibrations of the later literature (Batanin–Berger 2017, as cited in the companion); the standpoint is that of relative categories.

7. **Duality by the twisted arrow category.** `X ↔ X^op` is never an identification; it is always mediated by `X ← Tw(X) → X^op` with both legs aspherical (I-b, X, XII ms. 136–143), and every notion comes in two forms (Zariski/co-Zariski, smooth/proper, sieve/cosieve).

### 1.3 Directions he points to without taking them

- **Stable ("triangulated") derivators** (I ms. 29, "Perplexité"). [E] Carried out by Heller 1997, Franke 1996, Keller 1991, Maltsiniotis 2007, Groth 2013 (values of a stable derivator are canonically triangulated). The motivation he gives, traces and determinants in SGA 5 (Pursuing Stacks §69), is met by [E] Groth–Ponto–Shulman, additivity of traces in monoidal derivators (2014), and on the geometric side by Ayoub's algebraic derivators (2007).
- **Relative homotopy types as ∞-stacks.** VI ms. 5: what the 1-categories `HOT(S)` lose "va se retrouver ... dans la structure des Hom internes ... en termes de ∞-champs en groupoïdes sur S ... Mais ceci est une autre histoire". [E] `HOT_{W∞}(S) = Ho Fun(N(S)^op, Spaces)`; the other story is ∞-topos theory (Lurie 2009).
- **`Cat` as a "bi-site"** (VI ms. 82–84): two ways of localizing, by `X/x` and by `x\X`, plus a smooth and a proper topology; the question how to define fine weak equivalences on `M/S` for a general model category; the remark that ∞-groupoids are the one model with no such notion. [E] For simplicial sets this is the covariant and contravariant model structures and the proper/smooth calculus (Joyal; Lurie HTT §§2.1, 4.1.2; Cisinski 2019). For a general `(M, W)` I know no treatment. [H] The class `𝓛` of XII ms. 219–226 is close to a class of étale maps in the sense of Joyal–Moerdijk, or a local class of morphisms (HTT §6.1.3).
- **A perfect smooth/proper duality in `Δ^` and in topological spaces** (XII ms. 224–225), giving "deux versions complémentaires, ou duales" of relative homotopy type. [E] for simplicial sets (as above). [H] for spaces it is sheaves versus cosheaves; see 3.1.
- **A homotopy theory generated by a localizer alone** (X ms. 3). [E] Cisinski 2006 for accessible localizers [C].
- **Mapping spaces from path categories, with composition, pointed and linear versions, and tests (a), (b), (c) for when a bare `(M, W)` carries a homotopy theory** (V, VII §11, IX). [E] Dwyer–Kan 1980 gives the mapping spaces; when the naive `A ↦ M^A[W_A^{-1}]` is the right prederivator is known for model categories and (co)fibration categories (Cisinski 2003, 2010). [C] His tests are too weak (finite counterexample, IX ms. 23). An intrinsic criterion on `(M, W)` is, as far as I know, not in the literature.
- **A cofibre theory dual to the fibre theory** (XII ms. 227), and **finite categories as computable models** (VII ms. 128, XIIapp).

### 1.4 Relations

**Pursuing Stacks.** [E] Derivators first appear in PS §69; the localizer axioms L1–L5 are those of PS §64 (X ms. 4 says so); "aspherical" comes from PS. [I] Les Dérivateurs is PS with two changes: the ∞-groupoids and the test categories are set aside (`Cat` is the only modelizer, and the ∞-stacks are "une autre histoire"), and functoriality in the base, which PS has only as "integration", becomes the subject. The price is visible everywhere: what PS would state with a stack he must state with a family of 1-categories of fractions and then recover coherence by Kan extension (IV).

**La Longue Marche.** I did not have its text before me; this paragraph rests on what the Esquisse says of it. [I] The common move is to refuse a single invariant in favour of the system of all its relatives with the operations between them: the tower of Teichmüller groupoids with gluing and forgetting there, the family `D(I)` with `u*, u_!, u_*` here; and to refuse base points: fundamental groupoids on many base points there, the endpoint functor `Ch(X) → X × X` and relative types over `S` here (he never forms a loop space without first forming the path fibration, VII, XII ms. 188–191). [H] The closest mathematical contact is 3.2 (coefficient theories as generalized Galois theories of the base) and 3.1 (stratified bases).

**Later derivators.** [E] Heller 1988 is independent and close. Cisinski proved the main structural expectations: model categories give derivators (2003), `W∞` is the smallest fundamental localizer (2004), realization of accessible localizers and the smooth/proper calculus (2006, with Maltsiniotis 2005), the universal property of `HOT` (2008), locally constant coefficients (2009). Groth 2013 and Groth–Ponto–Shulman supply the pointed and stable theory and the exact-square calculus that Chapter I circles. [C] The audit confirms that the converse conjectures of I ms. 58–59 are theorems and that spaces alone detect exactness.

**∞-categories.** [E] Every complete and cocomplete ∞-category gives a derivator, and prederivators see quasi-categories faithfully in a 2-categorical sense (Arlin 2020; Riehl–Verity). [I] Three recurring failures in the manuscript are exactly the three things a derivator of 1-categories cannot hold: (i) tests on sets of homotopy classes of finite objects (the functor `Hot → Hot_0^`, asked about in V, VII ms. 121–127, 150, IX ms. 9–13; false [C]), whereas `Spaces = Ind(finite spaces)` holds for ∞-categories [E]; (ii) strict functor categories `Fun(Y, C)` as mapping objects (VII ms. 136; false [C]); (iii) a right adjoint to `Cat hot → Hot` (V ms. 37; see 2.3, question D).

## 2. The claims marked open, conjectured or false

### 2.1 Reading the status column

The column records the status at that place of the manuscript. Using the repair and notes columns: almost all "conjectured" rows and most "open" rows are settled, by the manuscript later, by the literature, or by a companion proof. Roughly twenty rows remain unsettled in the audit, and they reduce to the five questions of 2.3.

### 2.2 Families among the false claims

- **F1, the wrong orientation of cofinality.** Contractible commas on the wrong side, or constant-coefficient equivalence taken for full faithfulness of `u*` (I-a M030–M071, II-A-017/018, the withdrawn Der 5). One counterexample, `{0} → [1]` with `0 → Z`, kills about fifteen rows. Settled.
- **F2, total equivalence taken for fibrewise equivalence.** The thirteen-category theorem (VIa-039), the fibration claims of I-b (B079, B084), VIa-068, the false Corollary at VI ms. 120, the localizer `a_n` (XIIc A050). [E/C] The axiom that would license it (new L5, W(4b)) forces `W = W∞` among proper localizers (Cisinski 2006, cited in VI-c).
- **F3, reverse excision.** Theorem 2 of XII ms. 78, "`W∞` satisfies W(7)", is false [C], and with it the reflection halves of statements in XIIb-a, XIIb-b, XIIc-a (footnote 124) and XIIapp (G12, G14). It holds for homology localizers `W_R` [C].
- **F4, tests on unpointed homotopy classes of finite categories** (V-I071–073, VIIc-A020–A053, C017, C037, IX-R01). Settled negatively [C, E Arlin–Christensen, Freyd].
- **F5, strict 1-categorical constructions assumed homotopy invariant:** ordinary pushouts (XIIapp), `f_!` of unions (XIIb-a B045–B055), `Fun(Y, C)` (VIIc B035–B038), properness of `Ch(X) → X × X` (VIIc C042–C083).
- **F6, variance and indexing slips** (the outer opposite in VI-a, signs and lengths in VII). No mathematical content beyond the corrections.

[I] **F2 and F3 are one statement with two poles.** The fibre axiom says a map is in `W` when its homotopy fibres are `W`-aspherical and that this passes to base change; it is left exactness of `Hot → Hot_W`. The ∞-topos of spaces has only two left exact localizations, itself and the trivial one, which is why exactly `W∞` and the class of all functors satisfy it (the audit notes at VIa-188 that the class of all functors is the counterexample when the component condition is dropped). On the other side, W(7 bis) (XII ms. 76: in a square of two sieves covering `X`, `U∩U' → U'` is in `W` iff `U → X` is) is equivalent to: **a map is in `W` iff its homotopy cofibre is `W`-aspherical** (take `U` a cone, after replacing the other map by a sieve inclusion with the mapping cylinder of XII ms. 228–234; conversely the two maps in the axiom have the same cofibre). Consequences: (a) if `g` is an integral homology isomorphism, its cofibre `C` is acyclic, `ΣC` is contractible, so `C → pt` is in `W`, so `g ∈ W`: every localizer satisfying W(7 bis) contains the integral homology equivalences `W_Z`; (b) with [C] that `W_Z` satisfies full W(7), **`W_Z` is the smallest localizer satisfying W(7 bis)**, in the same way that `W∞` is the smallest localizer; (c) no proper localizer satisfies both the fibre axiom and W(7 bis). So the axiom list he is assembling through XII has no common model except the trivial one, and everything proved in XII §§5–6 under W(7) or W(7 bis) is a theorem about homological localizers. [H] Localizers satisfying W(7 bis) should correspond to localizing subcategories of spectra (`g ∈ W` iff `Σ^∞ cofib(g)` lies in the subcategory). This would also answer row XIIba-A-053 (whether W(7 bis) alone gives "intersection and union aspherical ⇒ both pieces aspherical"): yes exactly when the aspherical objects are closed under stable direct factors, which is the extra hypothesis the audit says Chapter XVI introduces.

### 2.3 What is still unsettled, as five questions

**A. Total asphericity** (rows II-C-003, XIIb-a-B035, XIIba-C005, C035, C045, C047, C048 = XIIbb-A001). `T(A)`: every ordinary final functor out of `A` has aspherical target. Filtered ⇒ `T(A)`; the converse is proved by the companion for preorders [C] and left open for categories, the remaining issue being equalization of parallel arrows. [I] A general test: for any `H: A → Set`, adjoin one object `x` with `Hom(x, a) = H(a)`. The inclusion of `A` is final iff the category of elements `El(H)` is connected, and by the open–closed gluing formula of XIIapp [C] the new category has the type of the suspension of `B El(H)` when `BA` is contractible. Hence `T(A)` implies: **`El(H)` is acyclic for every `H` with `El(H)` connected.** Two consequences. First, for parallel `a, b: p → q` and `H = Hom(p, −)/(ca ∼ cb)`, one computes `H_1(El H) = Z/g`, `g` the gcd of the total weights of integer combinations `Σ n_k c_k` with `Σ n_k (c_k a − c_k b) = 0`; so `T(A)` forces an "affine" equalization of every parallel pair, the condition of Isbell–Mitchell's theorem on exact colimits. Second, the smallest non-filtered candidate fails: for the monoid `M = {1, e, f}` with `xy = y` on `{e, f}` (arrows of `BM` composing as in `M`), `BM` is contractible and cofiltered but not filtered, and the left `M`-set `H = {h, i}` on which `e` and `f` are both constant at `i` gives `B El(H) ≃ S¹`, so a final image of `BM` is an `S²`. [H] `T(A)` ⇔ filtered for `W∞`, and for `W_Z` total asphericity ⇔ exactness of `colim_A` on abelian groups. If the first holds, a `W∞`-Dwyer sieve is exactly one whose inclusion has an ind-right adjoint (crossing weights ind-representable), which decides the composition and classification rows at once and ties II §2.15–2.18 to XII §5.

**B. The path model against mapping spaces for general `Σ`** (rows IX-037, 040, 041, 044, 049, 052, 072, 080, 088, VIIc-A046, V-I066). For `Σ` = all arrows the companion proves the comparison and the compatibility with composition [C]. For general `Σ` the path categories allow arbitrary vertical arrows where the hammock localization requires them in `Σ`. The precise question: for which `(M, Σ)` does `Ch_Σ(M; a, b)` have the type of the Dwyer–Kan mapping space, compatibly with concatenation. For `Σ` = isomorphisms it does [I, by an initial-object argument on factorization categories]. I do not know the general answer.

**C. Strict bifibrations** (VIc-C008): is every locally constant object over `S` represented by a functor that is both a fibration and an opfibration. [C] Yes over bases with an initial or terminal object. [H] Yes in general, by a model whose fibres are categories of contractible patches; untested.

**D. The Thomason–Kan envelope** (V ms. 37–43; the companion's status block "The unproved reflection must remain a hypothesis"; the editors also ask for a proof). The claim is a right adjoint `ψ` to `Cat hot → Hot`, where `Hom_{Cat hot}(X, K) = π0 Fun(X, K)`. [I] **No such adjoint exists.** Suppose a small category `K` represented `X ↦ [BX, K(Z/2, 2)]` on `Cat hot`. Taking `X` a point, `π0 K` is a point. Take `X = BG` with `G` the finitary alternating group on a set of cardinality larger than that of `K`: `G` is simple, so every functor `BG → K` is constant on arrows, and `π0 Fun(BG, K) = π0 K` is a point. But `[BG, K(Z/2, 2)] = H²(G; Z/2) = Hom(H_2 G, Z/2) = Z/2`. So `K(Z/2, 2)` has no envelope. Groupoids do represent their own types, so the local objects contain the 1-types; [H] they are exactly the 1-types. This explains his own observation (VII ms. 136) that the path categories "n'ont aucune tendance à être des catégories de Thomason-Kan", and it removes the hypothesis under which Propositions 1–2 of V §4 were to be applied. I have not searched the literature for this.

**E. Abstract fibre axioms** (XIIc B062: do axioms a, b imply c; C030, C032: smooth/proper complementarity in general models; C039). These wait for XII §7 and XIII–XIX.

### 2.4 Which to pursue

1. **A**, because one statement about monoids and parallel arrows decides seven rows, the test above makes it computable, and the `W_Z` variant connects to a known theorem.
2. **The two poles of 2.2**, because it is a re-reading of a whole chapter, costs a page to prove, and must be compared with XII §8 and XVI §3 when they return.
3. **D**, because it is a three-line argument that settles a point the editors flagged; it needs a literature check and a second reader.
4. **B**, because it is the one place where his construction might differ from the standard one in substance.

## 3. Intuitions a chunk-by-chunk reading would miss

**3.1 `Cat` is a category of stratified spaces, and a derivator is a theory of constructible coefficients.** [I] The vocabulary is literal: "ouvert" and "fermé" are the open and closed sets of the Alexandrov topology; `i^!`, `j^?` in I ms. 3–4 are the recollement; `HOT(S)` versus `HOT^lc(S)` is constructible sheaves versus local systems on `BS`; proper and smooth base change keep their names. [E] For a poset `P`, `Fun(P, Spaces)` is the ∞-category of sheaves on `|P|` constructible along its strata, and in general constructible sheaves are functors on the exit-path ∞-category (Lurie HA App. A; Treumann 2009; Barwick–Glasman–Haine; Haine: stratified homotopy types are ∞-categories with a conservative functor to a poset). [I] The closing conjecture of the appendix to XII, `BX ≃ hocolim(BA ← BE → BB)` with `E` the crossing arrows, is the categorical form of gluing a stratified space from an open stratum, a closed one and the homotopy link (Quinn 1988); the companion's remark that replacing crossing arrows by all paths gives the wrong bridge is the difference between exit paths and paths. [H] Read this way, the manuscript is a six-operations formalism on the "bi-site" `Cat`, uniform in the coefficients ([E] Hörmann 2018 for the fibred version), and its natural continuation is the tame topology of the Esquisse: the derivator supplies the coefficients, the stratified space supplies the base. The Deligne–Mumford stratification of the moduli spaces would be the test case that touches La Longue Marche.

**3.2 The dropped notion of I-b is a Galois theory relative to a coefficient theory.** I-b distinguishes constant-coefficient **D**-equivalences from "2-complete" ones (equivalence on locally constant coefficients). Chapter X builds the localizer `W_D` from constant coefficients only. [I] Define `W_D^lc` as the maps inducing an equivalence on locally constant **D**-coefficients. Then: for **D** = sets, `W_D = W_0` but `W_D^lc = W_1` (covering theory); for `(n−1)`-types it is `W_n`; for `D(Z)` it is `W∞` (Whitehead: `π1` and integral homology of universal covers); for `D(Q)` it is isomorphism on `π1` plus rational homology of universal covers, the equivalences of non-simply-connected rational homotopy theory, strictly between `W∞` and `W_{D(Q)}`. The Klein bottle and `e → BC_2` counterexamples of I-b ms. 87–92 are exactly maps in `W_{D(Q)}` outside `W^lc_{D(Q)}`, and the false fibration statements there (B079, B084) should become true for `W^lc`. [H] `W_D^lc` is a fundamental localizer, `D ↦ W_D^lc` is the functor "fundamental ∞-groupoid as seen by **D**", and "when is `W_D^lc = W∞`" is the anabelian question in this setting. II-b §6 ("Transporteurs, noyaux, stabilisateurs relatifs à W") may already contain part of this.

**3.3 The corrections have a direction.** [I] Across chapters the same repair is made: a statement true after `π0` is replaced by a statement about a nerve (1.2, item 1); a test on homotopy classes by a test that keeps a groupoid or a space of maps (F4; the positive result is Arlin–Christensen's Whitehead theorem in the homotopy 2-category [C]); a strict colimit by a total category. He is deriving, case by case, the rule that a derivator must be used 2-categorically. [H] A statement of the manuscript phrased with `Hom`-sets in some `D(I)` is safe when it can be rephrased with the categories `D(I × J)` for all `J`, and suspect otherwise; this could be run as a mechanical check over the "stated" rows.

**3.4 Finite categories as presentations.** [C] The companion's small examples (`S²` on four objects, `S³` on six, a wedge of 2-spheres on three objects and 21 arrows, a two-object category with first cohomology in degree 3) show non-posets are far more economical than posets. [H] Grothendieck's recollection at VII ms. 128 about finite computation points at a notion of complexity of a homotopy type (least number of arrows of a category modelling it) that seems unstudied beyond posets and monoids ([E] McDuff 1979: every connected type is `BM` for a monoid).

## 4. Where the missing chunks limit this

- **XI, XII §§1–4, XII §§7–8, XVI.** These contain his characterization of `W∞`, the variants of W(4), and an appendix "Retour sur les axiomes W(7), W(7 bis)". Section 2.2 may describe a tension he resolved himself; the statement that the list has no common model must be checked against XVI §3 (Loc 6) and §6.
- **II-b** (biaspherical functors, formal duality, transporters and stabilizers relative to `W`) bears on 1.2 item 7 and on 3.2.
- **XIII, XV, XVII, XIX** (Quillen pairs and triples, the transfinite factorization, categories with cofibrations) are his construction of homotopy structures from `W`. My list in 1.3 of directions "not taken" (cofibre theory, the theory generated by a localizer, criteria on `(M, W)`) describes the returned half only.
- **XIV** (the abandoned "quadrangulated" axioms) is where the stable direction of I ms. 29 is attempted; I cannot say how far.
- **XVIII** (paratopos, `M ⊠ N`, accessible categories, about 200 pages) has no counterpart in what I read. It suggests a theory of presentable coefficient categories and their tensor product, which would change the picture of 1.4 on the ∞-categorical side.
- I-a exists only as a raw return (`returns/DER_I-a`), not as a checked chunk.

Corrections to the sections above:

- **To 2.2, attribution.** The statement "under W(7 bis), a map is in `W` iff its mapping cone is `W`-aspherical" is Grothendieck's own: XII ms. 266–267, for `W`-cofibrations, with the mapping cone `Z(g)`. He calls the reverse half of W(7 bis) "inhabituelle" (ms. 270) and proves "intersection and union aspherical ⇒ pieces aspherical" exactly when `W` is "stable par facteurs directs" (ms. 265, 270–272), which is the answer I gave for row XIIba-A-053. The rigidity of the fibration axiom is also his: XII Théorème 1 (ms. 12) and XVI Théorème 6.1, Corollaire 6.3 (ms. 83–85): a fundamental localizer satisfying Loc (5) and Loc (7 D) is contained in `W∞`, and one failing Loc (5) is `Fl(Cat)`. With Cisinski's minimality this gives uniqueness; I had credited the whole to Cisinski 2006. What remains my inference, unverified: the reverse half forces `W ⊃ W_Z`, so `W∞` fails it and no proper localizer has both.
- **To 2.2, extent.** The false Théorème 2 of XII ms. 78 is not corrected later. XVI Théorème 5.4 (ms. 79) asserts that `W∞` satisfies "tous les axiomes vus jusqu'à présent, savoir Loc (1 ter, 2, 3 bis, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9)", and Loc (6) is fixed as Loc (6 a, b) in footnote 13 (ms. 17'). He notes that `W_0` fails (6 b) and presumes the `W_n` do (ms. 17', footnote 36), but does not test `W∞`. Against this, XVI ms. 119–121 observes that Loc (6 b), like Loc (4), (5), (7), plays no part in proving that `HOT_W` is a derivator: his "localiseurs spéciaux" are defined by Loc (6' a, 8, 9). So the error does not reach the construction of the derivators `HOT_W`.
- **New consequence [I].** XVI 6.4 b (ms. 87–92) conjectures a smallest localizer `W̃∞ ⊊ W∞` satisfying all stability axioms Loc (1 ter, 2, 3 bis, 4, 6, 7, 8), a candidate for "types d'homotopie simples" (he half-retracts it in footnotes 41–43; the editors note Cisinski's `W_ω = W∞`). Under his reading of Loc (6) it would contain `W_Z` and satisfy Loc (7), hence fail Loc (5) and equal `Fl(Cat)`.
- **To 1.3.** XVI §3 constructs a theory of `W`-homotopy cofibres from Loc (6 a) (Loc (6 A): `(Cat, W, Cof_W)` is a category with cofibrations in the sense of K. S. Brown; Lemma 3.2.1). "A cofibre theory dual to the fibre theory" is therefore a direction he took. XVI ms. 85–86 also announces a descent axiom Loc (10) meant to characterize `W∞` internally, which fits the reading of the fibration axiom as left exactness [H].
