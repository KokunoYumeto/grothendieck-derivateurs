# POLES: the fibration axiom and the cofibration axiom for fundamental localizers

Package 3 of 7 on Alexander Grothendieck, *Les Dérivateurs* (1990–91). File prefix for your deliverables: `POLES`.

## What is to be settled

Through Chapters VI, XII and XVI Grothendieck assembles a list of axioms for a class `W` of weak equivalences in `Cat`, and asserts (XVI Théorème 5.4, ms. 79) that `W∞` satisfies all of them: Loc (1 ter, 2, 3 bis, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9). Two of them pull in opposite directions.

- **The fibration axiom** Loc (7) and its weakest form Loc (7 D) (XVI §4 and §6; in XII it is W(4) with its variants, in VI the "new L5"): a functor in `W` which is a homotopy fibration (in `Parf`) is universally in `W`, so its fibres are `W`-aspherical. He proves (XII Théorème 1, ms. 12; XVI Théorème 6.1, ms. 83) that with the connectedness axiom Loc (5) this forces `W ⊂ W∞`.
- **The cofibration axiom** Loc (6) (XVI §3, ms. 16'–27; in XII it is W(7) and W(7 bis), ms. 75–78), which he reads as Loc (6 a, b). The half (b), which he himself calls "inhabituelle" (XII ms. 270), says that in a Mayer–Vietoris square a cobase change that lies in `W` comes from a map in `W`. He shows (XII ms. 266–267) that under W(7 bis) a `W`-cofibration `g` is in `W` if and only if its mapping cone `Z(g)` is `W`-aspherical.

The companion's audit found that Théorème 2 of XII ms. 78 ("`W∞` satisfait à W(7)") is false: a finite acyclic non-contractible complex and the two cones of its suspension give a counterexample, while homology localizers `W_R` do satisfy W(7). The question of this package is the exact structure behind that failure, in Grothendieck's own axiom system, to the end.

## Leads (unverified; prove or refute each)

- **Lead 1.** For a fundamental localizer `W` (which contains `W∞` by Cisinski's minimality theorem), the reverse half of the cocartesian-square axiom (W(7 bis) "⇐", Loc (6' b), Loc (6 C)) is equivalent to: for every functor `g`, `g ∈ W` if and only if the homotopy cofibre of `g` is `W`-aspherical.
- **Lead 2.** Hence every such `W` contains the class `W_Z` of functors inducing isomorphisms on integral homology of nerves. Sketch: if `g` is a homology isomorphism between non-empty categories, its cofibre `C` is acyclic; the cofibre of `C → e` is the suspension of `C`, simply connected and acyclic, so weakly contractible, so `W`-aspherical; apply Lead 1 twice.
- **Lead 3.** `W_Z` satisfies Loc (6 a, b, c) and is the smallest fundamental localizer satisfying Loc (6 b). Consequently `W∞` fails Loc (6 b), XVI Théorème 5.4 is false as stated, and no fundamental localizer other than `Fl(Cat)` satisfies both Loc (5, 7 D) and Loc (6 b).
- **Lead 4.** The localizer `W̃∞` of XVI 6.4 b (ms. 87: the smallest localizer satisfying all the stability axioms Loc (1 ter, 2, 3 bis, 4, 6, 7, 8), which he conjectures to be a proper subclass of `W∞` giving "types d'homotopie simples") is `Fl(Cat)` when Loc (6) includes (b), and is `W∞` when Loc (6) is read as (6 a).
- **Lead 5.** The fibration axiom is left exactness: for an accessible `W`, Loc (7 D) holds if and only if the localization functor from the ∞-category of spaces to its localization at `W` preserves finite limits (equivalently pullbacks along the relevant fibrations), and Théorème 6.1 is the statement that the ∞-topos of spaces has exactly two left exact accessible localizations.
- **Lead 6.** Fundamental localizers satisfying Loc (6 a, b) (with accessibility) correspond to localizing subcategories `L` of the stable homotopy category, by `L ↦ W_L = { g : Σ^∞ cofibre(g) ∈ L }`; `W_Z` corresponds to `L = 0` and the `E`-homology equivalences to the `E`-acyclic spectra.
