# An actual two-dimensional tower excluding specified countable quotients

Author: C/path collaborator, 2026-11-14.

This note assembles the diagonal blocks proved in
`research/D_countable_diagonal.md` (being promoted to A.27) and the
independently checked finite-tree block for uniformly simple targets.
The output is an actual CW construction with an explicit continuum
cardinality bound. It excludes the target classes stated below. It
does not assert that every nontrivial countable quotient belongs to
one of those classes.

## 1. The constructed object and its exact conclusions

Let `B` be a specified connected countable CW classifying space of
dimension at most two, with nontrivial perfect fundamental group `H`.
Let `c=2^(aleph_0)`. Define `C` to consist of the following nontrivial
countable groups:

1. mixed-identity-free groups;
2. torsion-free groups with exactly two conjugacy classes;
3. nonabelian uniformly simple groups: for some finite integer `N>=1`, every
   element is a product of at most `N` conjugates of `a` or `a^(-1)`
   for every nonidentity element `a`.

The construction below gives an actual CW inclusion

\[
 B\ \subset\ X,
 \qquad G=\pi_1(X),
 \tag{1}
\]

with all of the following properties.

* `X` is aspherical and has dimension at most two.
* `X` has at most `c` cells, so `|G|<=c`.
* The canonical map `H -> G` is injective and `B -> X` is an
  ordinary integral homology isomorphism. In particular `G` is
  perfect; when `B` is acyclic, `X` is acyclic.
* `G` is normally generated by the displayed copy of `H`.
* Every one-variable equation over `G` with total variable
  exponent `1` or `-1` has a solution in `G`; the same is
  true in every quotient of `G`.
* There is no epimorphism `G -> E` with `E in C`.

Here the last assertion concerns quotient groups in `C`. It does
not assert the vanishing of all homomorphisms into an arbitrary
overgroup of such a quotient.

## 2. Lifting a diagonal block along a surjection from a subgroup

Let `A` be the fundamental group of any actual aspherical
two-dimensional CW complex `Z` already constructed. Let
`K <= A` be a countable subgroup and `q:K -> E` an epimorphism,
where `E` belongs to either class 1 or class 2 above. Choose
`h != 1` in `E`, enumerate `E` without repetitions as
`e_1,e_2,...`, and take the elements `b_0,b_1,...` supplied by
the proved diagonal construction. Choose once and for all lifts
`h_hat,e_hat_n,b_hat_n` in `K`. Attach new loops and two-cells
to `Z` with words

\[
 x_0=[\widehat h,x_1]\widehat b_0,
 \qquad
 x_n=[x_0\widehat e_n^{-1},x_{n+1}]
                   \widehat b_n\quad(n\ge1).
 \tag{2}
\]

Every coefficient is represented by a chosen finite loop in `Z`.
The construction uses only this coefficient list. It does not
require a homomorphism from all of `A` to `E`.

The proof in `D_countable_diagonal.md` applies over the
torsion-free coefficient group `A`: the relative exponent
boundary is the identity on the displayed variables; every
finite prefix, after eliminating its finitely many non-root
variables, is a single unimodular adjunction over `A` with free
tail generators. Forester--Rourke therefore proves asphericity
of each prefix and injectivity of the coefficient map. The
actual union of those prefixes is aspherical, since a sphere
map uses finitely many cells. The same finite-support argument
proves injectivity of `A` into the full presented group.
Thus the resulting actual inclusion `Z subset Z'` is an
ordinary homology isomorphism between aspherical complexes of
dimension at most two. It adds countably many cells.

Any map `pi_1(Z') -> E` whose restriction to `K` is `q` would
send (2) to the specified unsolvable diagonal system in `E`.
Consequently no such map exists. This proves the precise
coefficient-lifting assertion needed below.

In the quotient killing `A`, the first relation in (2) gives
`x_0=1`; then every remaining relation gives `x_n=1`.
Therefore `pi_1(Z')` is normally generated by `A`.

The geometric proof uses torsion-freeness of `A`, which follows
from its actual finite-dimensional classifying model. It does
not require torsion-freeness of a mixed-identity-free target
`E`, nor injectivity of `q`.

## 3. The finite-tree block for uniformly simple targets

Here is the full algebraic step for class 3, with the same
coefficient-lifting and geometric conclusions as Section 2.
Let `E` be a nontrivial countable `N`-uniformly simple group.
It is nonabelian and centerless. Put `d=2N` and define

\[
 W_a(u_1,v_1,\ldots,u_N,v_N)
      =\prod_{i=1}^{N}v_i[a,u_i]v_i^{-1}.
 \tag{3}
\]

For every `a != 1`, the function `W_a:E^d -> E` is onto.
Indeed choose `u` with `c=[a,u] != 1`, using that `a` is
not central. Every desired element is a product of at most
`N` conjugates of `c` and `c^(-1)`. A conjugate of `c` is
already a factor in (3), and the identity

\[
 c^{-1}=u[a,u^{-1}]u^{-1}
 \tag{4}
\]

puts a conjugate of `c^(-1)` into the same form. Identity
factors pad the product to `N` factors. Also `W_1=1`.
Every slot of (3), and the symbol `a` itself, has exponent
sum zero.

Index variables `x_s` by the vertices of the rooted
`d`-ary tree, including its root `emptyset`. Choose
`h != 1`, choose the root constant `b_emptyset`, and
enumerate `E` without repetitions as `e_1,e_2,...`.
The relations are

\[
 x_{\varnothing}=W_h(x_1,\ldots,x_d)b_{\varnothing},
 \qquad
 x_s=W_{x_{\varnothing}e_n^{-1}}
                 (x_{s1},\ldots,x_{sd})b_s
       \quad(|s|=n\ge1).
 \tag{5}
\]

Choose the constants one level at a time. At level `n`,
substitute the possible root value `p=e_n` into the
coefficient of every already specified internal vertex.
At the root the coefficient is `h != 1`, and at depth
`j<n` it is `e_n e_j^(-1) != 1`. Hence each of these
vertex functions is onto. Their finite-tree composite,
from all depth-`n` variables to the root, is onto:
given a desired root value, choose its children using
surjectivity at the root, then their children using
surjectivity at the next level, and continue through
the finitely many levels.

Choose a value different from `e_n` and choose a tuple
of depth-`n` inputs realizing it under this composite.
Declare that tuple to be the constants `(b_s)_(|s|=n)`.
This finite choice uses only constants chosen on earlier
levels. In any putative solution with root `e_n`, all
depth-`n` coefficients in (5) become the identity, so
all those variables are forced to equal precisely the
chosen constants. Substitution up the tree then makes
the root different from `e_n`, a contradiction. Every
root value occurs in the enumeration, so (5) has no
solution in `E`.

For a surjection `q:K -> E` with `K <= A`, lift all the
constants to `K` and attach (5) over the whole coefficient
space `Z`. The relative exponent boundary is again the
identity: the right sides have exponent zero in every
new variable, including the root occurrences in the
coefficients of non-root equations.

For a finite-depth prefix, eliminate non-root internal
variables in descending depth. Only finitely many
defining pairs are eliminated. The remaining root
relator is unimodular in the root variable, over `A`
with its free tail generators: every new root occurrence
on the right is inside a word with zero exponent in
each substituted slot, so the right side still has
root exponent zero. Forester--Rourke and the actual
CW-union argument prove asphericity and coefficient
injection exactly as in Section 2. Killing `A` forces
the root to be `1` in the first equation; then each
other equation has coefficient `1` and forces its
variable to be `1`. Thus this block is normally
generated by `A` as well.

For a finite target `E`, enumerate its elements once
without repetition and continue the sequence periodically.
Choose the constants by the preceding rule for that first
finite set of levels, and choose all later constants
arbitrarily. Retain the entire infinite tree and every
equation. The first levels already exclude every possible
root value; retaining all later equations preserves the
identity exponent matrix and the normal-generation proof.
Alternatively all finite targets may be omitted when the
starting group has no nontrivial finite images, since
normal generation then already rules out finite quotients
of the tower.

## 4. One complete pass, with its cardinal bound

Suppose `Z_beta` is an actual classifying complex of
dimension at most two with at most `c` cells; write
`A_beta=pi_1(Z_beta)`. Choose representatives for all
groups in `C` with underlying set either `N` or a finite
initial segment of `N`. There are at most `c` such group
structures, because a group operation is a function on
a countable set.

List all triples

\[
 (K,E,q),\qquad
 K\le A_\beta\text{ countable},\quad
 E\in\mathcal C,\quad q:K\twoheadrightarrow E.
 \tag{6}
\]

The number of countable subgroups of `A_beta` is at most

\[
 |A_\beta|^{\aleph_0}\le
       \mathfrak c^{\aleph_0}=\mathfrak c.
 \tag{7}
\]

For each such subgroup and countable target there are at
most `c` functions, hence at most `c` epimorphisms. The
whole list (6) therefore has cardinality at most `c`.

Also include every one-variable word over `A_beta` with
variable exponent `1` or `-1`; there are at most `c`
finite words. Adjoin a fresh variable and its relator
for each such word by the exact Forester--Rourke
one-pair construction of A.18. The coefficient group
is torsion-free at each attachment. That theorem
gives actual asphericity and coefficient injection;
the relative boundary is multiplication by `1` or
`-1`. Killing the coefficient group leaves the
relation `x^(+1)=1` or `x^(-1)=1`, so normal generation
is also preserved. These pairs are interleaved with
the countable diagonal blocks in the same well-order.

Well-order that list. Starting with `Z_beta`, attach its
countable block successively for each triple. All
coefficients for that block lie in its chosen subgroup
`K` of the group at the beginning of the pass. The
already proved coefficient injections identify `K`
with a subgroup of every later group in this pass.
Section 2 or Section 3 consequently applies at the
moment of attachment. This argument never presupposes
that `q` extends over blocks attached earlier in the
pass.

At intermediate limit ordinals take the actual CW
union. All successor complexes are aspherical, and
all canonical group maps are injective. A sphere map
or a nullhomotopy in a union has compact image and
uses finitely many cells, hence lies in an earlier
stage. This verifies asphericity and coefficient
injection at limits. Cellular homology commutes with
these unions, so each composite inclusion remains
an ordinary homology isomorphism. Normal generation
is transitive along the construction and at unions,
as is also seen by killing the initial coefficient
group in every displayed block presentation.

At most `c` blocks, each with countably many cells,
have been added. Their union, denoted `Z_(beta+1)`,
therefore still has at most `c` cells. No homomorphism
from its group to a listed target `E` can restrict to
the listed epimorphism on its subgroup `K`.

## 5. The outer omega-one union and the quotient argument

Start with `Z_0=B`, perform the pass of Section 4 at
each successor stage `beta+1` for `beta<omega_1`,
and take actual CW unions at limit stages. Define

\[
 X=\bigcup_{\beta<\omega_1}Z_\beta,
 \qquad G=\pi_1(X).
 \tag{8}
\]

Since `aleph_1<=c`, the union has at most `c` cells.
The established successor and limit arguments prove
all the topological, homological, and normal-generation
assertions in Section 1. In particular the group at
every stage is torsion-free, so the geometric input
needed for the next block remains available.

Suppose that `q:G -> E` is an epimorphism onto a group
in `C`. Identify `E` with its selected representative.
For every element of `E`, choose a preimage in `G`.
These countably many preimages all occur in one
`A_beta`: each individual element belongs to an
earlier group, and the supremum of countably many
countable ordinals is below `omega_1`. Let `K` be
the subgroup they generate. It is countable,
lies in `A_beta`, and `q|K` is onto `E`.

The triple `(K,E,q|K)` appeared in pass `beta`, and
its block was attached inside `Z_(beta+1)`. Restricting
the assumed `q` to that block yields exactly the
forbidden solution with its chosen coefficient lifts.
This contradiction proves the last assertion of
Section 1.

Every one-variable unimodular word over the final
group has a finite coefficient list appearing in
one earlier `A_beta`; its listed one-pair attachment
in that pass supplies its solution. For an equation
over an epimorphic image of `G`, lift its coefficients,
solve the lifted equation in `G`, and map the solution
to the image. This proves the equation-solving
assertion and its permanence without asserting a
geometric construction for arbitrary finite
nonsingular systems.

## 6. Scope for a normally singly generated acyclic base

For a starting acyclic two-dimensional group normally
generated by one nonidentity element `h`, the same
element normally generates `G`. Every nontrivial
quotient of `G` then has a nontrivial simple quotient:
in that quotient, order the proper normal subgroups
by inclusion. A chain of proper normal subgroups has
proper union, since containing `h` would force the
whole group and membership of `h` in a union already
occurs at one chain member. Zorn's lemma gives a
maximal proper normal subgroup. The associated
quotient is simple and remains countable when the
original quotient was countable.

Therefore every countable nontrivial quotient of this
particular output would have a countable simple
quotient which is neither mixed-identity-free nor
uniformly simple nor a torsion-free two-conjugacy-class
group. This is an exact necessary condition on a
remaining quotient. The construction does not prove
that no countable simple group satisfying those
remaining conditions exists, or that none can occur
as such a quotient.
