# Discrete normal-Frattini covers: exact obstruction

Research for Part A3(iii), after completion of Part D. This note does not claim a dimension-two construction or a general obstruction to one.

## Definitions and the quotient-size implication

Call a surjective group homomorphism `p:E -> S`, with kernel `K`, **normal-Frattini** when every normal subgroup `N` of `E` satisfying `NK=E` equals `E`.

For a nonabelian simple group `S`, a normal-Frattini epimorphism has the following exact properties.

1. Every proper normal subgroup `N` of `E` is contained in `K`. Indeed, its image is a normal subgroup of `S`; the alternative `p(N)=S` gives `NK=E`, hence `N=E`.
2. Every nontrivial quotient `E/N` surjects onto `S`, by the map `eN -> p(e)` established by `N <= K`. Consequently every homomorphism from `E` to a group of cardinality strictly smaller than `|S|` is trivial.
3. `E` is perfect. The image of `[E,E]` is `[S,S]=S`, so the defining property gives `[E,E]=E`.
4. Every lift of a nonidentity element of `S` normally generates `E`. Its normal closure has nontrivial image, hence has image all of `S` and is all of `E`.

These arguments use actual discrete group homomorphisms. No passage to a profinite completion occurs.

## Lemma: a perfect locally free group cannot have finitely many normal generators

**Proved statement.** Let `E` be a nontrivial locally free group. If `E` is normally generated by a finite subset, its abelianization is nontrivial.

**Proof.** Suppose instead that `E` is perfect and normally generated by `x_1,...,x_r`. Delete trivial entries, so the list is nonempty and each entry is nonidentity. For each `i`, choose a finite expression of `x_i` as a product of commutators `[u,v]`. Since the `x_j` normally generate `E`, express each of the finitely many elements `u,v` occurring in these expressions as a finite product of conjugates of the `x_j` and their inverses. Let `F` be the subgroup generated by all the `x_j`, all these `u,v`, and all conjugators occurring in the chosen expressions. This is a finitely generated free group, by local freeness. All the selected equations hold in `F`.

Write `gamma_1 F=F` and `gamma_{n+1}F=[gamma_n F,F]`. The lower central series of a free group has trivial intersection, as proved in the following paragraph. Hence each `x_j` has a finite lower-central depth, and there is an integer `d>=1` such that every `x_j` lies in `gamma_d F` while at least one does not lie in `gamma_{d+1}F`. The group `gamma_d F` is normal in `F`. Therefore every selected `u,v`, being a product of conjugates of the `x_j`, lies in `gamma_d F`. Their commutators lie in `gamma_{2d}F`, and thus each `x_i` lies in `gamma_{2d}F <= gamma_{d+1}F`. This is a contradiction. Here `[gamma_d F,gamma_d F] <= gamma_{2d}F` is the usual commutator induction; alternatively the formal-series degree filtration below gives exactly the same contradiction without this identity. QED.

For completeness, a direct formal-series proof avoids importing residual nilpotence. For a free basis `t_1,...,t_s` of `F`, map `t_j` to `1+T_j` in the multiplicative group of formal noncommutative integral power series with constant term one. Inverses exist by the geometric series. This homomorphism is injective: write a nonempty reduced word in maximal syllables as `t_{i_1}^{e_1} ... t_{i_k}^{e_k}`, with each `e_j` nonzero and adjacent `i_j` distinct. In its expansion the coefficient of `T_{i_1} ... T_{i_k}` is exactly the nonzero integer `e_1 ... e_k`. To obtain this monomial, each of the `k` syllable factors must contribute positive degree (otherwise the product has fewer than `k` maximal variable blocks), and then each must contribute degree one. Thus the expansion differs from one. If `U=1+u` and `V=1+v` have first nonzero positive terms in degrees at least `a,b`, respectively, their commutator has no positive term below degree `a+b`, by reducing in the quotient by the ideal of terms of degree at least `a+b`. Conjugation preserves the lower bound on degree. Choose `d` to be the minimum first positive degree of the expansions of the `x_j`. Every selected `u,v` then has first positive degree at least `d`, and every commutator has first positive degree at least `2d`. The displayed expressions for the `x_j` contradict the definition of `d`. This supplies a self-contained proof of the lemma.

## Corollary: the locally free cover route is excluded

**Proved statement.** There is no normal-Frattini epimorphism from a locally free group onto a nonabelian simple group. More generally, there is none onto a nontrivial perfect group having a finite normal generating set.

**Proof.** For the more general assertion, the image of `[E,E]` is the whole perfect quotient, so normal-Frattini forces `E` to be perfect. Lift a finite normal generating set of the quotient and let `N` be its normal closure in `E`. Then `NK=E`, whence `N=E`. Thus `E` is both perfect and finitely normally generated, contrary to the lemma. QED.

In particular, taking a subgroup of a locally free cover cannot produce the required normal-Frattini cover: its source remains locally free. This is an exact obstruction to that proposed operation, not a statement about other acyclic groups of geometric dimension two.

## A second obstruction: arbitrary towers of layered unimodular equations

**Proved statement.** Let `H` be a nontrivial countable torsion-free group. Let `(G_alpha)` be a transfinite increasing tower starting at `G_0=H`, taking unions at limit ordinals, whose successor steps have the form

`G_(alpha+1) = <G_alpha,t_alpha | r_alpha(t_alpha)=1>`,

where `r_alpha` is a finite word with coefficients in `G_alpha` and its total exponent of `t_alpha` is `+1` or `-1`. Then the union `G` has a homomorphism to a countable group whose restriction to `H` is injective. In particular, such a tower cannot give a group with no nontrivial countable images, regardless of the cardinality or length of the tower.

**Proof.** We construct a fixed countable torsion-free overgroup `U` of `H` in which every unimodular equation in one variable, with coefficients in `U`, has a solution.

Set `U_0=H`. Given a countable torsion-free `U_n`, enumerate all finite words in one new variable with coefficients in `U_n` having variable exponent `+1` or `-1`. There are countably many. Adjoin a solution for each equation, in that enumeration order, by its one-generator/one-relator presentation. Theorem 1 and Proposition 1 of Forester–Rourke apply at every finite step: the coefficient group is torsion-free; the relative exponent matrix is the one-by-one matrix `[+1]` or `[-1]`; and the pair is layered. The coefficient group embeds in the extension, and the extension is torsion-free. The union of this countable succession is a countable torsion-free group `U_(n+1)` containing `U_n`, in which every equation listed for `U_n` has a specified solution. Define `U` to be the union over `n<omega`. Any equation with coefficients in `U` uses finitely many coefficients, all lying in some `U_n`, and hence has a solution in `U_(n+1)`.

Now define compatible homomorphisms `f_alpha:G_alpha -> U` by transfinite recursion. Start with the inclusion `H -> U`. At a successor stage, apply `f_alpha` to the coefficients of `r_alpha`; the resulting equation over `U` has the same variable exponent and therefore has a solution `u` in `U`. Sending `t_alpha` to `u` extends `f_alpha` by the universal property of the relative presentation. At a limit, the compatible homomorphisms give a homomorphism on the union. The resulting `G -> U` restricts to the original inclusion of `H`, and its image is therefore both nontrivial and countable. QED.

**Additional proved property of the constructed target.** Suppose `H` is supplied with a countable acyclic two-dimensional classifying CW complex. The group `U` just constructed has such a complex as well. At each equation step, attach exactly one one-cell and one two-cell to the preceding model. Forester–Rourke Theorem 1(b) identifies the new second homotopy group with extension of scalars of the old one, hence with zero. A simply connected two-dimensional universal cover with zero second homotopy group is contractible, by Hurewicz and Whitehead. Thus each new complex is a classifying complex. Its relative integral cellular complex is `Z --(+1 or -1)--> Z` in dimensions two and one, so its ordinary homology is that of the preceding complex. At each limit, homotopy groups of the universal-cover construction and ordinary homology have finite support in the cellular union; equivalently any sphere and its nullhomotopy factor through a stage once the relevant finitely many cells have been included. This proves asphericity and acyclicity of the union. The total number of added cells in the construction of `U` is countable, and its dimension remains two.

The statement does not cover a presentation in which finitely or infinitely many new generators have genuinely interacting relators that cannot be added in the specified order. No theorem about such presentations is being inferred.

**Verified primary source.** Max Forester and Colin Rourke, *The adjunction problem over torsion-free groups*, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 102 (2005), 12670–12671, DOI `10.1073/pnas.0502940102`. The complete author PDF was opened and read at <https://math.ou.edu/~forester/papers/multi.pdf>. ## Remaining exact task

The proposed cover must therefore use a source that is not locally free. No source satisfying all three requirements—acyclicity, an actual two-dimensional classifying CW model, and the normal-Frattini property over a simple group of cardinality greater than the prescribed bound—has been constructed or extracted from the checked sources. The smallest target of the surrounding Part A problem remains `W=S^2`, with all homomorphisms to countable groups required to be trivial.
