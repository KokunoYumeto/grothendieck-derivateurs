## Dimension approaches tried and the exact failures

* Reusing A.1's cone groups does not control dimension. Its mitosis contains successively larger direct products of the input, so starting with an infinite cyclic group gives subgroups Z^r for every r. A group with a finite-dimensional BG cannot contain all these subgroups, since pulling back its universal cover gives an equally dimensional BG model for each subgroup and H_r(Z^r;Z)≠0.
* The relative simple-envelope operation uses only new one- and two-cells, but its acyclic repair has no dimension bound. Within an actual two-dimensional CW inclusion, H₂ is injective, because there are no three-cell boundaries. New H₂ from HNN relations cannot be removed by further such inclusions.
* Free products of arbitrarily many Higman groups are acyclic of geometric dimension two, but projection to any single Higman factor is a nontrivial countable quotient. They fail at μ=ℵ₀.
* Obraztsov's relatively aspherical small-cancellation presentations supply large simple groups, but extra two-relators supply uncontrolled H₂; no acyclic envelope is furnished. The modern Fournier-Facio–Sun theorem makes this obstruction explicit in its own Remark 4.5.
* Universal central extensions of these perfect groups remove their Schur multiplier from the quotient, but their central kernel is the old H₂. When this contains free abelian groups of unbounded rank, the extension has infinite geometric dimension. This operation does not repair the dimensional gap.
* Berrick's locally free acyclic cover A→P has the useful dimension two, but a quotient of A may detect the kernel of A→P even when P has no small images. The cited theorem does not rule this out.
* Generalized Higman relations indexed by a graph suggest a cardinal pigeonhole argument. With relation x_u^{-1}x_vx_u=x_v² the integral cellular boundary sends the edge to −x_v. Multiple incoming edges then create H₂. Restricting to one incoming edge removes that particular kernel but destroys the high-chromatic graph mechanism; it does not yield the required large example.

## Continued attack on the fixed two-dimensional target (19:32 UTC)

The following checks concern the still undecided case; they do not assert a theorem deciding it.

**Surface-block replacement and its exact cost.** For a map b:Σ_q→M with b_*[Σ_q]=0, the image of b^*:H¹(M;Q)→H¹(Σ_q;Q) is isotropic: for α,β, the cup-product pairing is ⟨b^*α∪b^*β,[Σ_q]⟩=⟨α∪β,b_*[Σ_q]⟩=0. The surface pairing is symplectic of dimension 2q, so this image has dimension at most q. By duality of the linear map on H₁, ker(H₁Σ_q→H₁M) therefore has rank at least q. If the other vertex space has H₁=0, the Mayer–Vietoris sequence of the double mapping cylinder surjects its H₂ onto that kernel. Thus a single positive-genus block that kills an old H₂ basis class necessarily creates new H₂. This explains why A.15 iterates the operation.

For an aspherical presentation complex, a zero-exponent-sum relator can be written as a product of commutators. Attaching its surface block makes the old relator disk a free face of the new cylinder three-cell; removing that pair yields a new two-dimensional aspherical model with the old group embedded. This is the mechanism of BM §5.3. It does not by itself give a two-dimensional model of an infinite sequence of such replacements: the old models cease to be literal subcomplexes, and a mapping telescope has dimension three. Omitting every eventually replaced relator from the final presentation is invalid, because the finite proof that an old relator follows from its replacement can itself use relators that are later removed. No infinite collapse is claimed.

## Exact generalized-Higman obstruction (A.17; 19:44 UTC)

The one-predecessor presentation G_p has explicit countable retractions: close a countable set of indices under p, keep those generators, and send every other generator to 1. If i is outside the closure, its relator maps to r(x_p(i))⁻¹·1·r(x_p(i))=1. If i is inside, its predecessor is inside and the same relator remains. Inclusion followed by this retraction is the identity. Hence every nontrivial finite word survives in a countable quotient. This proves residual countability for all such presentations, without any asphericity assumption. It definitively rules out the proposed one-incoming-edge repair at μ≥ℵ₀.

## Reverse commutator towers (A.21; 20:00 UTC)

A genuinely different two-dimensional extension is `E(H;a,b)=<H,x_n | x_n=[x_(n+1),a_n]b_n>`. Every finite initial set of relations eliminates its first generators, so the full two-dimensional relative presentation is aspherical and embeds H. Its relative ordinary cellular boundary is the identity; thus it preserves ordinary homology, actual geometric dimension two, and acyclicity. Its quotient after killing H is trivial. This avoids the layered finite-equation hypothesis of A.18.

It nevertheless also fails to remove countable images, for an exact reason proved in A.21. Embed the initial nontrivial countable torsion-free H into a countable torsion-free Q with two conjugacy classes by repeatedly adjoining cyclic HNN conjugators for every pair of nonidentity elements. For nontrivial a, the map `y↦[y,a]b` has image `Q\{a⁻¹b}` and infinite fibres, each a coset of the centralizer of a. Choose successive preimages away from the next missing singleton to solve every infinite reverse system inside Q. Indices with a=1 are resets; they separate finite backward-solvable blocks and at most one final infinite block. Every transfinite iteration therefore maps to this same countable Q, injectively on the original H. The D collaborator independently checked the reset argument and recursive choices.

The next possible modification is a reverse tower with more general zero-exponent words, or an amalgam of incompatible homology-preserving extensions. The co-singleton image calculation is specific to the commutator word and has not been generalized without proof.

## Congruence extension obstruction (A.23)

The verified nonsingular-system theorem gives a stronger exact restriction on every actual two-dimensional homology extension. In a relative presentation with independent exponent rows, replace the coefficient group H by any group K along any map H→K. The relative exponent matrix is unchanged, so K embeds in the group pushout. With K=H/N this says `<<N>>_E ∩ H = N` for every normal N of H. This is the full congruence extension property, proved for arbitrary sets of relations by finite normal-closure witnesses.

For a homology-equivalence inclusion of connected two-dimensional complexes, compatible maximal-tree collapses give a relative chain isomorphism C₂→C₁, so the hypothesis applies. Hence an actual two-dimensional homology extension cannot force an old h into the normal closure of an old g unless that normal-generation relation already held. If the larger group is simple, every nontrivial homology core is itself simple. If the target is aspherical, π₁ injection and cellular two-cycle injection make the core aspherical. This explains precisely why homology-preserving two-dimensional inclusions cannot be inserted as a general simplicity-forcing operation.
