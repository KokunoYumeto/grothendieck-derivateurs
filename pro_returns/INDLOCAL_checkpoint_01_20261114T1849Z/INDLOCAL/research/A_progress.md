# Part A research checkpoint

## Scope and present state

The written proofs are in `sections/A.tex`, Claims A.1–A.11. A1, A2, A3(i), A3(ii), and A4 are proved. A3(iii) is still being attacked: A.10 proves the requested dimension bound for every finite μ, and A.11 gives the exact threshold for countable resolving models. The case of infinite μ with unrestricted cardinality of the resolving model has not yet been settled. This checkpoint is not a completed Part A return.

The first unresolved concrete instance is an acyclic map BG→S² with a two-dimensional BG such that every homomorphism from G to a countable group is trivial. A countable G cannot work. General R2 supplies an uncountable G, but the checked construction does not give a two-dimensional classifying model.

## Proofs checked and strengthened

1. **A.1 / GALOIS B.10.** Both envelope operations are checked from their presentations. For the simple envelope, free-product cyclic reduction makes both associated words infinite order, so the HNN maps are injective. For the acyclic envelope, the field-coefficient diagonal/Künneth calculation kills degree n after lower degrees have been killed. Integral vanishing follows from rational coefficients and the Tor term over every F_p. Alternating the operations yields a simple acyclic group of exactly the chosen infinite cardinality.

2. **A.2 / A4 / GALOIS B.9.** The actual restriction Map(W,Y)→Map(X,Y) has contractible nonempty homotopy fibres. Its image is exactly the union of components whose maps kill ker π₁t. Existence is proved by the pushout Y∪ᴴ_XW, its van Kampen quotient, local-coefficient excision, and the universal-cover Whitehead argument. Contractibility follows by applying Map(−,Y) to the codiagonal W∪ᴴ_XW→W. This proves all homotopy groups, and separately handles disconnected and empty Y.

3. **A.3 / R2 Proof 2 / GALOIS B.11.** The graph-of-spaces argument is checked at every stage, particularly at V=T(K^(n−1))∪_{T(L^(n−1))}T(L). Both edge maps are injective before the remaining simplex cones are attached. Relative Mayer–Vietoris over a simplex proves the local-coefficient comparison. Van Kampen identifies the specified quotient G/N→π₁K: two-simplices impose precisely the triangular relators; higher boundaries have trivial quotient by their lower cone groups. N is perfect because its generating conjugates are perfect inside N. The sharper bound is max(ℵ₀,μ⁺,ν), for ν simplices of K: each simplex boundary only requires cones of size max(ℵ₀,μ⁺), independently of ν.

4. **A.4 / R2 Proof 1 / OWN_RESULTS §3.** The remembered cardinal estimate is proved using Leary's Theorem A: finite inputs give finite outputs and the construction preserves unions of finite subcomplexes. Hatcher's skeletal simplicial replacement preserves dimension and the relevant infinite cardinal bound. At a cell of dimension at least three, the homotopy pullback E=S^m×ᴴ_W BG is connected, acyclic over S^m, and has at most λ homotopy classes in every degree; a λ-cell CW model and the size lemma give the needed H'. The graph embedding into G×A' is injective even when H'→G is not. The map to the attached cell uses the pullback homotopy; local coefficients are trivial in the A' direction. At limits, actual nested CW models give the homology colimit and the surjective quotient maps give unique compatible factorizations. No unverified size premise remains.

5. **A.5–A.6 / simple kernel.** A relative version of the cell construction retains an initial BH as a CW subcomplex and extends its specified map, whenever H→π₁W is onto. The simple-envelope HNN construction is performed only on elements of that kernel, and every new stable/free letter maps to 1. Consequently the normal-generation calculation takes place inside the kernel, not just inside the ambient group. Alternate this operation with the relative acyclic resolution. Cofinal acyclic stages prove acyclicity of the limit map; cofinal simple kernel stages prove simplicity of its kernel. All chosen maps extend literally, and |P|=|G|=λ for any infinite λ>μ bounding the target model.

6. **A.7 / prescribed kernel homology.** Pulling BG→W back along the universal cover produces the specified deck-equivariant acyclic map BP→W̃. It proves H_n(P;Z)≅H_n(W̃;Z) as Z[π₁W]-modules; the action on P is ambient conjugation. These are the exact possible graded modules for the fixed W, and A.6 realizes them with P simple. The same map preserves the cohomology ring.

7. **A.8 / functoriality.** Choosing one standard cone per dimension makes the ordered-simplex construction strictly functorial for maps injective and order-preserving on each simplex; barycentric subdivision covers arbitrary abstract complexes and simplexwise injective maps. For arbitrary simplicial maps, use U(K), the simplicial set of all vertex tuples spanning a simplex, then Δ_inj/U(K), retaining degenerate simplices, and the face poset of its nerve. Object degrees force the resulting maps to be simplexwise injective. The comparison to K is checked by contractible comma categories, the last-vertex map, and a simplexwise affine weak equivalence. It provides an actual functorial replacement with the same cardinal control.

8. **A.9 / precise limitations.** The original simple-cone models cannot themselves extend to all simplex maps: Δ²→Δ³ has a simplicial retraction, forcing an isomorphism C₃→C₂ by simplicity, while the injected boundary group has H₂=Z and C₃ has H₂=0. Nor can an acyclic-resolution functor have simple kernel on every simply connected input: S²→S²∨S² has a retraction, and simplicity would make the resolving retraction an isomorphism, contradicting its specified H₂ projection Z²→Z. Both finite cellular computations are checked by `code/a_finite_checks.py`.

9. **A.10–A.11 / dimension progress.** The BDH basic realization with Higman's acyclic group has a finite model over each simplex of dimension at most that simplex. Explicit compatible mapping-cylinder models extend this to arbitrary finite-dimensional CW inputs. Each cone group is normally generated by the inserted Higman groups; every homomorphism to a finite group kills them. Thus R2 holds for all finite μ with no dimension increase. A countable source with nontrivial P cannot handle μ≥ℵ₀, since the identity G→G is then among the tested homomorphisms. Dimension alone supplies no cardinal bound: graph identities work for every μ.

## Dimension approaches tried and the exact failures

* Reusing A.1's cone groups does not control dimension. Its mitosis contains successively larger direct products of the input, so starting with an infinite cyclic group gives subgroups Z^r for every r. A group with a finite-dimensional BG cannot contain all these subgroups, since pulling back its universal cover gives an equally dimensional BG model for each subgroup and H_r(Z^r;Z)≠0.
* The relative simple-envelope operation uses only new one- and two-cells, but its acyclic repair has no dimension bound. Within an actual two-dimensional CW inclusion, H₂ is injective, because there are no three-cell boundaries. New H₂ from HNN relations cannot be removed by further such inclusions.
* Free products of arbitrarily many Higman groups are acyclic of geometric dimension two, but projection to any single Higman factor is a nontrivial countable quotient. They fail at μ=ℵ₀.
* Obraztsov's relatively aspherical small-cancellation presentations supply large simple groups, but extra two-relators supply uncontrolled H₂; no acyclic envelope is furnished. The modern Fournier-Facio–Sun theorem makes this obstruction explicit in its own Remark 4.5.
* Universal central extensions of these perfect groups remove their Schur multiplier from the quotient, but their central kernel is the old H₂. When this contains free abelian groups of unbounded rank, the extension has infinite geometric dimension. This operation does not repair the dimensional gap.
* Berrick's locally free acyclic cover A→P has the useful dimension two, but a quotient of A may detect the kernel of A→P even when P has no small images. The cited theorem does not rule this out.
* Generalized Higman relations indexed by a graph suggest a cardinal pigeonhole argument. With relation x_u^{-1}x_vx_u=x_v² the integral cellular boundary sends the edge to −x_v. Multiple incoming edges then create H₂. Restricting to one incoming edge removes that particular kernel but destroys the high-chromatic graph mechanism; it does not yield the required large example.
