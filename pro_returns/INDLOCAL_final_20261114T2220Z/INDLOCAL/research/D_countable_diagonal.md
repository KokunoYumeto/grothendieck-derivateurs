# A countable simultaneous acyclic system with no solution

This note records the diagonal construction found by the root agent and independently checked by the D agent. It supplies an exact obstruction to a proposed fixed countable target. It does not yet decide whether every group occurring as a detecting target in A.19 has such an obstruction.

## The mixed-identity-free case

Let `E` be a nontrivial countable group with the following property: every nonidentity word in `E*<t>` has a nonidentity evaluation at some `t=b` in `E`. This is the one-variable mixed-identity-free property. Choose `h != 1` in `E`, choose `b_0 in E`, and enumerate the elements of `E` without repetitions as `e_1,e_2,...`.

There is an explicitly recursive sequence `b_1,b_2,...` in `E` for which the system

`x_0 = [h,x_1] b_0`,

`x_n = [x_0 e_n^(-1),x_(n+1)] b_n` for `n >= 1`

has no solution in `E`. Throughout, `[a,b]=a b a^(-1) b^(-1)`.

Suppose `b_1,...,b_(k-1)` have been chosen. Put `p=e_k` and define the following words in `E*<t>`:

`f_0(t)=[h,t]b_0`,

`f_j^p(t)=[p e_j^(-1),t]b_j` for `1 <= j < k`,

`F_k^p(t)=f_0(f_1^p(...f_(k-1)^p(t)...))`.

Each coefficient `p e_j^(-1)` in this expression is nonidentity, because the enumeration has no repetitions. The free factor `E` is malnormal in `E*<t>`. More explicitly, reduced free-product normal form gives

`E intersect w E w^(-1) = {1}` whenever `w` is outside `E`.

Consequently, if `a != 1` belongs to `E` and `w` is outside `E`, then `[a,w]` is outside `E`: otherwise `w a^(-1) w^(-1)=a^(-1)[a,w]` would be a nonidentity element of that intersection. Multiplication on the right by an element of `E` keeps a word outside `E`. Induction from the innermost variable `t` therefore proves `F_k^p(t) notin E`.

In particular `F_k^p(t)p^(-1)` is a nonidentity mixed word. Choose `b_k in E` whose evaluation is nonidentity. This gives the precise inequality

`F_k^(e_k)(b_k) != e_k`.

The recursion uses only words already specified at earlier steps. To make it completely deterministic, fix an enumeration of `E` and take its first element satisfying the inequality.

If a solution of the full system existed in `E`, write its value of `x_0` as `e_k`. Its `k`th equation would force `x_k=b_k`, because its commutator coefficient becomes `e_k e_k^(-1)=1`. Substituting successively through the equations with indices `k-1,...,1,0` would then give

`e_k=x_0=F_k^(e_k)(b_k)`,

contrary to the chosen inequality. This proves nonsolvability.

## Exact exponent matrix and normal-generation statement

Write each equation as a relator with its left side first. The exponent-sum column of the relator with index `n` is exactly the basis vector belonging to `x_n`. All occurrences of `x_0` and `x_(n+1)` inside commutators have total exponent zero. Thus the actual relative ordinary cellular boundary is the identity

`Z^({r_0,r_1,...}) -> Z^({x_0,x_1,...})`.

Let `P` be the presented overgroup of `E`. Its quotient by the normal closure of `E` is trivial. Indeed the first relation becomes `x_0=[1,x_1]=1`; with `x_0=1`, each remaining relation becomes `x_n=[1,x_(n+1)]=1`. This explicitly proves normal generation by the coefficient subgroup, without any claim that the resulting presentation is layered.

## The two-conjugacy-class case, without mixed-identity terminology

Let `E` be a countably infinite torsion-free group with exactly two conjugacy classes. The same construction works directly. For `a != 1`, the map

`f_(a,b):E -> E`, `y -> [a,y]b`

has image `E minus {ab}`. This follows from `[a,y]b=a (y a^(-1) y^(-1)) b`: the middle factor runs over every nonidentity element. Each nonempty fiber is a right coset of `C_E(a)`, and is infinite because that centralizer contains the infinite cyclic subgroup generated by `a`.

If a map has infinite nonempty fibers, restricting its domain to a cofinite subset does not change its image. Hence every composite `F_k^(e_k)` in the construction has exactly the image `E minus {h b_0}`. Its image is infinite, so the required choice of `b_k` always exists. This establishes the diagonal system for these groups independently of whether a particular such group has any other mixed identities.

## Actual classifying spaces for torsion-free coefficients

This paragraph applies to either preceding construction whenever `E` is torsion-free. Let `B` be any specified CW classifying space for `E`. Attach all countably many loops `x_0,x_1,...`, and let `Y_N` be the space obtained by attaching only the relators of indices `0,...,N`.

For `N >= 1`, eliminate the defining generator--relator pairs in the order `x_N,x_(N-1),...,x_1`, using the relators of indices `N,N-1,...,1`. Each such relator expresses its eliminated variable as a word that does not contain that variable. Substituting that expression into the other attaching words gives the standard presentation-complex homotopy equivalence, relative to `B` and the generators that remain. The resulting complex consists of

`B wedge (the circles x_(N+1),x_(N+2),...)`,

one new loop `x_0`, and one remaining two-cell. That final attaching word has total exponent `+1` in `x_0`: every occurrence introduced on the right side lies in the outer commutator with `h` and contributes exponent sum zero. The base group is the torsion-free free product of `E` with the free group on the tail loops.

Forester--Rourke's unimodular one-pair theorem, in the exact form used and proved applicable in A.18, therefore says that `Y_N` is aspherical and that its canonical coefficient map from `E` is injective. The case `N=0` is the same one-pair statement directly. The theorem also makes each prefix group torsion-free.

Take the actual nested CW union `Y=union_N Y_N`. Every map of a sphere of dimension at least two has image in a finite subcomplex and therefore lies in one `Y_N`, where it is nullhomotopic. Thus `Y` is aspherical. Any loop in `B` that becomes nullhomotopic in `Y` has a nullhomotopy supported in some finite prefix; coefficient injection there proves that the original element of `E` was trivial. Hence the canonical map `E -> pi_1(Y)=P` is injective. No assertion that the maps between the prefix groups are injective is needed.

The relative cellular boundary of `(Y,B)` is the identity computed above. Consequently this actual inclusion of classifying spaces is an ordinary integral homology isomorphism. Only one- and two-cells have been added, so

`dim Y <= max(dim B,2)`.

In particular a specified two-dimensional model for `E` yields an actual two-dimensional model for `P`. The absence of a solution in `E` says precisely that no homomorphism `P -> E` restricts to the identity on the displayed coefficient subgroup. It does not say that `P` has no other nontrivial countable quotient.

## Countable existentially closed targets and quotient permanence

Every group existentially closed among all groups is mixed-identity-free in the one-variable sense used above. For a nonidentity word `w in E*<t>`, the actual free-product embedding of `E` into `E*<t>` realizes `w(t) != 1`. Existential closedness realizes this finite inequation in `E`. Thus every nontrivial countable existentially closed group has the diagonal obstruction. The same argument applies to a group existentially closed among torsion-free groups when it is torsion-free, because that free product is torsion-free.

The property of solving every countable integral-boundary-isomorphism system passes to epimorphic images. Given `q:E -> Q` and a countable system over `Q`, choose a lift in `E` for each occurring coefficient. The lifted words have exactly the same exponent matrix. A solution in `E` maps to a solution in `Q`. Therefore a group with that solution property cannot have a nontrivial countable mixed-identity-free quotient, or a countably infinite torsion-free two-conjugacy-class quotient.

This last conclusion does not cover all countable overgroups of a specified perfect group without finite images. For example, a central extension or an overgroup with an abelian normal subgroup can satisfy mixed identities; no map to a mixed-identity-free quotient preserving the required coefficient subgroup has been constructed here.

## Source and scope

The construction and its group-theoretic nonsolvability proof are new arguments within this project. The sole external geometric input is the Forester--Rourke unimodular adjunction theorem already fully sourced in A.18 (`CpathsForesterRourke2005Adjunction`, Theorem 1 and Proposition 1; and `CpathsForesterRourkeDiagrams`, Sections 1--3). In particular the torsion-free geometric conclusion does not infer torsion-freeness from that newer theorem.

## Subsequent proved extensions

The complete manuscript statement is now A.27, `sections/A_diagonal_systems.tex`. It includes the finite-branching construction for every nonabelian uniformly simple countable target, and the stronger lifted statement for an epimorphism `q:K -> E` from any subgroup `K <= H` of a torsion-free source. No extension of `q` to `H` is assumed. This subgroup version is what permits the continuum-cardinality construction in A.28.

### Exponent bookkeeping for more general tree words

The tree geometry needs zero exponent in each child slot of a word `W_a`, but the coefficient slot `a` may have any integer exponent `k_s` at vertex `s`. Substituting `a=x_o e_n^(-1)` then gives relative boundary columns

`delta(r_o)=e_o`, `delta(r_s)=e_s-k_s e_o` for `s != o`.

This is an actual isomorphism of free abelian direct sums. Its inverse is specified on basis vectors by

`delta^(-1)(e_o)=r_o`, `delta^(-1)(e_s)=r_s+k_s r_o`.

Each inverse value has finite support, even when infinitely many `k_s` are nonzero. The finite-depth elimination still leaves root exponent one because the outer root word has exponent zero in every child variable. Thus finite products of conjugates of `a` and `a^(-1)` can be used directly in this geometry; equal numbers of positive and negative occurrences of `a` are not required. The existing A.27 version uses commutator words and has `k_s=0`, hence the literal identity matrix.

### Finite interpolation over a nonabelian simple group

Let `E` be nonabelian simple. Fix distinct tuples `p_1,...,p_m in E^r` and arbitrary values `q_1,...,q_m in E`. There is a word `w in E*F(t_1,...,t_r)` with exponent sum zero in every variable and with `w(p_i)=q_i` for every `i`.

For `m=1`, the constant word `q_1` suffices. For `m>=2`, fix an index `i`. Choose a first other index `j`. Because `p_i != p_j`, choose a coordinate `l` where they differ, and put `u=t_l (p_j)_l^(-1)`. Then `u(p_j)=1` and `u(p_i)!=1`. The center of `E` is trivial, so choose `c in E` not commuting with `u(p_i)`. The word `v=[u,c]` has zero exponent in every variable, vanishes at `p_j`, and is nonidentity at `p_i`.

For each further index `j != i`, choose in the same way a coordinate-difference word `u_j` with `u_j(p_j)=1` and `u_j(p_i)!=1`. The nonidentity conjugates of `u_j(p_i)` generate the nontrivial normal subgroup `E`. They cannot all commute with `v(p_i)!=1`, since that would make `v(p_i)` central. Choose `c_j in E` such that

`[v(p_i), c_j u_j(p_i) c_j^(-1)] != 1`,

and replace `v` by `[v,c_j u_j c_j^(-1)]`. This replacement keeps every previously obtained zero value, adds the zero value at `p_j`, keeps the value at `p_i` nonidentity, and keeps every variable exponent zero. After finitely many steps it gives a word `v_i` vanishing at every selected point other than `p_i`, with `a_i=v_i(p_i)!=1`.

Simplicity expresses `q_i` as a finite product of conjugates of `a_i` and `a_i^(-1)`. Use exactly the same coefficient conjugators and signs with `v_i` to obtain a word `z_i`. Then `z_i(p_i)=q_i`, all its other selected values are `1`, and its variable exponents are zero. The product `w=z_1...z_m` has all the required evaluations and exponent sums.

This proves arbitrary finite interpolation. It does not supply one finite word meeting countably many independently specified evaluations. In the attempted extension of A.27 to a general simple target, a word at depth `j` is fixed before the future root value `p=e_k` is known. Simplicity gives a finite normal-expression length for each particular pair of elements, while the proof has no single finite arity bound for all the future differences `p e_j^(-1)`. That is the exact remaining gap in this approach.
