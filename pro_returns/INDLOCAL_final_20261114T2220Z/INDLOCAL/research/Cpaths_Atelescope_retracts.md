# Explicit countable retracts in two classes of free-group telescopes

These are proved restrictions on specified constructions. They make no
residual-countability assertion for all locally free groups. The maps
below are constructed on the stated free bases and checked against
the actual transition homomorphisms.

## 1. Brunnian basis images

Let

\[
 F(B_0)\xrightarrow{\phi_0}F(B_1)
 \xrightarrow{\phi_1}F(B_2)\longrightarrow\cdots
\]

be a sequence of injective homomorphisms between free groups, with
arbitrary small basis sets `B_n`. For each `b in B_n`, let
`T_n(b)` be the finite support of the nonidentity reduced word
`phi_n(b)` in the basis `B_(n+1)`. Assume this word is Brunnian
on that full support: the homomorphism killing any one chosen
letter of `T_n(b)` and retaining all the other basis letters
sends `phi_n(b)` to the identity.

**Proved assertion.** Every countable subset of
`G=colim_n F(B_n)` is contained in a countable retract subgroup of
`G`. In particular every nonidentity element survives in a
countable quotient.

### Constructing the selected bases

Choose countably many stage representatives for the prescribed
countable subset and put their finite word supports into sets
`J_n^0 subset B_n`. There are only countably many selected letters
altogether. Enlarge this family for countably many rounds using
both rules:

* if `b` is selected at level `n`, select every letter of `T_n(b)`
  at level `n+1`;
* select at level `n` every `b` for which `T_n(b)` is already
  wholly contained in the selected set at level `n+1`.

The forward rule adds countably many letters, because every
support is finite. The backward rule also adds countably many.
Indeed, for a countable subset `J subset B_(n+1)`, the group
`F(J)` is countable. Injectivity of `phi_n` makes the words
`phi_n(b)` for distinct basis elements distinct and nonidentity.
Hence only countably many `b` can have `phi_n(b) in F(J)`.
This is exactly the set whose full supports are contained in
`J`.

Let `J_n` be the union of the selected sets at level `n` over
all rounds. It is countable, and it satisfies both closure rules.
For the backward rule at the union, use finiteness of `T_n(b)`:
if it is contained in the union then it was contained in one
earlier round. Thus

\[
 b\in J_n\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 T_n(b)\subseteq J_{n+1}.
 \tag{1}
\]

### Exact commuting retractions

Let `i_n:F(J_n)->F(B_n)` be the free subgroup inclusion, and
let `r_n:F(B_n)->F(J_n)` fix the selected basis letters and kill
every other basis letter. The forward closure makes
`psi_n=phi_n|F(J_n)` a homomorphism into `F(J_(n+1))`, and

\[
 \phi_n i_n=i_{n+1}\psi_n.
 \tag{2}
\]

The retractions also commute:

\[
 r_{n+1}\phi_n=\psi_n r_n.
 \tag{3}
\]

To verify (3), take a basis element `b`. For `b in J_n`, its
full image support lies in `J_(n+1)`, so both sides retain its
original image word. For `b outside J_n`, (1) gives a letter
of `T_n(b)` outside `J_(n+1)`. The projection `r_(n+1)` kills
that letter. It factors through the homomorphism killing just
that letter, so the Brunnian property gives
`r_(n+1)(phi_n(b))=1`. This is also `psi_n(r_n(b))`.

Take group colimits in (2) and (3). They give homomorphisms

\[
 i:L:=\mathop{\rm colim}_n F(J_n)\longrightarrow G,
 \qquad r:G\longrightarrow L,
 \qquad ri={\rm id}_L.
 \tag{4}
\]

Thus `i` is injective, `r` is surjective, and `L` is countable.
The chosen stage representatives show that the prescribed
countable subset of `G` is contained in `i(L)`. The composite
`ir` fixes it. This proves the assertion, including the exact
detecting quotient map.

### Included commutator constructions

A single commutator `[x,y]` is Brunnian on its two distinct
letters. An iterated commutator in which every support letter
is essential in this sense is covered by the assertion as
well. In particular, the injective telescope sending distinct
basis elements to distinct pair commutators is covered.

For completeness, all pair commutators `[y_i,y_j]`, for one
choice of ordering of each unordered pair, are freely
independent. In any finite subfamily, their images in
`gamma_2(F)/gamma_3(F)` are independent: this quotient is the
free abelian group on the ordered pair commutator classes.
The subgroup generated by that finite family is free by
Nielsen--Schreier. Its abelianization maps onto a free abelian
group of rank equal to the number of chosen generators, so
its free rank is at least that number, and it is at most that
number because those elements generate it. Thus the rank is
equal. The map from the free group on that many generators
onto it is an isomorphism, using Hopficity of a finite-rank
free group. Any hypothetical relation in the whole family
would lie in a finite subfamily, so no such relation exists.

The independence calculation can equivalently be read in the
degree-two part of the Magnus expansion, where `[y_i,y_j]`
has leading term `Y_i Y_j - Y_j Y_i`.

## 2. A common word followed by private commutator products

The kill-one-letter property is not needed for the following
different class. Let `phi_n:F(B_n)->F(B_(n+1))` be any sequence
of homomorphisms. For each `n`, suppose there is a single word
`u_n in F(B_(n+1))` and words `v_(n,b)`, for `b in B_n`, with

\[
 \phi_n(b)=u_n v_{n,b}.
 \tag{5}
\]

Let `T_n(b)` be the finite full support of `v_(n,b)`. Assume:

* assigning one common group element to all the letters of
  `T_n(b)` makes `v_(n,b)` equal to the identity;
* for each `c in B_(n+1)`, only countably many `b in B_n`
  satisfy `c in T_n(b)`.

The first assumption is exactly that the total exponent sum
of `v_(n,b)`, after all its variables are identified, is zero.
It holds for every product of commutators. The second is a
countable incidence requirement, and holds in particular for
pairwise disjoint private supports. Neither assertion follows
merely from injectivity of a transition map.

**Proved assertion.** Every countable subset of this colimit
also lies in a countable retract subgroup. Transition
injectivity is not required for this retract statement.

### Closing supports by incidence

Choose stage representatives of the prescribed subset as
before. In addition, select all letters in `supp(u_n)` at
level `n+1`, for every `n`. Close for countably many rounds
under:

* all private supports `T_n(b)` of selected parents;
* every parent whose private support meets the selected set
  at the next level.

The second rule adds countably many parents because of the
countable incidence assumption. Hence the resulting `J_n`
are countable. They have the two exact properties

\[
 \begin{split}
 b\in J_n&\Longrightarrow T_n(b)\subseteq J_{n+1},\\
 b\notin J_n&\Longrightarrow T_n(b)\cap J_{n+1}=\varnothing.
 \end{split}
 \tag{6}
\]

The words `u_n` are supported in `J_(n+1)`. Thus (5) restricts
to maps `psi_n:F(J_n)->F(J_(n+1))`. Put
`L=colim_n F(J_n)`, and denote the structure maps into `L`
by `ell_n`.

### Retractions with a specified stage shift

Define homomorphisms `r_n:F(B_n)->L` on basis elements by

\[
 r_n(b)=
 \begin{cases}
  \ell_n(b),&b\in J_n,\\
  \ell_{n+1}(u_n),&b\notin J_n.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{7}
\]

These are well-typed because `u_n` lies in `F(J_(n+1))`.
They satisfy the exact equality

\[
 r_{n+1}\phi_n=r_n.
 \tag{8}
\]

For a selected parent, its whole image is selected at the
next stage, so (8) is the defining colimit equality for
`ell_n`. For an outside parent, (6) puts every private
variable outside the selected set at the next stage.
Formula (7) sends every one of these variables to the
same element `ell_(n+2)(u_(n+1))`. The private word
therefore becomes one. Since the common word is selected,
the remaining value is `ell_(n+1)(u_n)`, precisely the
second line of (7).

The maps in (8) give a homomorphism `r:G->L`. The selected
free subgroup inclusions give `i:L->G`, and (7) proves
`ri=id_L` on all structure maps. Thus `i` is injective,
`r` is a countable quotient, and `ir` fixes the prescribed
subset exactly. This proves the second assertion.

### A genuine non-Brunnian example class

Take a common word `u_n=[a_n,b_n]` at the next stage and
let `v_(n,b)` be a product of commutators in finitely many
private variables, with pairwise disjoint private supports.
The word in (5) usually survives killing one private
variable, because the common commutator remains. It is
therefore outside the first criterion. Nevertheless (7)
gives the countable retract under the stated incidence
condition. The same proof allows any one common word per
stage, not only a commutator.

The hypothesis that the common word is the same for all
outside parents at a given stage is used in (7) and (8).
An arbitrary family of different common words, or
uncountably many private supports through one selected
letter, is not covered by this argument.

## 3. Uniform templates with common letters and private inputs

Here is a further countable-quotient construction. Let the transition
maps `phi_n:F(B_n)->F(B_(n+1))` be injective. At level `n`, fix
a countable common alphabet `C_(n+1) subset B_(n+1)` and a finite
word template

\[
 w_n\in F(C_{n+1}\amalg\{t_1,\ldots,t_{k_n}\}).
\]

For each parent `b in B_n`, specify private input letters
`t_(n,b,1),...,t_(n,b,k_n)` in `B_(n+1)` such that

\[
 \phi_n(b)=w_n(C_{n+1};t_{n,b,1},\ldots,t_{n,b,k_n}).
 \tag{9}
\]

Assume each next-stage basis letter appears among the private
inputs of at most countably many parents. Repetitions among the
inputs of one parent are allowed. Common letters are designated
as such in the template; their use as common letters carries
no incidence restriction.

**Proved assertion.** Every countable subset of the colimit lies
in a subgroup on which a homomorphism to a countable group is
injective. In particular every nonidentity element survives
in a countable quotient.

Choose countable sets `J_n` containing stage representatives
of the prescribed subset, all common letters, and closed
forward under every selected parent's private inputs and
backward under every parent with a selected private input.
The incidence assumption gives countability exactly as in
Section 2. The two consequences are:

* a selected parent's entire image word is selected at the
  next stage;
* all private inputs of an outside parent lie outside the
  selected set at the next stage.

Add one extra basis symbol `z_n` at each level and define

\[
 K_n=F(J_n\amalg\{z_n\}),\qquad
 p_n:F(B_n)\longrightarrow K_n,
\]

where `p_n` fixes `J_n` and sends every outside basis letter
to `z_n`. Define the transition map `eta_n:K_n->K_(n+1)`
by retaining `phi_n(b)` on each selected parent and putting

\[
 \eta_n(z_n)=w_n(C_{n+1};z_{n+1},\ldots,z_{n+1}).
 \tag{10}
\]

The previous two support consequences prove on every basis
element that

\[
 p_{n+1}\phi_n=\eta_n p_n.
 \tag{11}
\]

Thus there is an actual homomorphism
`p:G->K=colim_n K_n`, where `K` is countable. The subgroup
`F(J_n)` at every stage maps into the next selected free
subgroup by precisely the original transition map. These
restrictions are injective. Hence a nonidentity selected
word remains nonidentity in every later `K_m`. The group
colimit criterion for equality proves that the induced
map from `L=colim_n F(J_n)` to `K` is injective. The same
is true for its map into `G`, and (11) identifies the
restriction of `p` with this specified injection. Since
the prescribed subset is contained in that copy of `L`,
the assertion follows. If an extra symbol `z_n` has no
preimage because the outside basis at that stage is empty,
no surjectivity is inferred for `p`; restricting its
codomain to `p(G)` supplies the claimed countable quotient.

For example, the templates

\[
 \phi_n(b)=[a_{n+1},b_{n+1}]
              [a_{n+1},c_{n,b}]
\]

have `a_(n+1),b_(n+1)` as common letters and `c_(n,b)` as
private inputs. When the private incidence is countable
and the given maps are injective, (10)--(11) give the
countable detecting quotient. The common letter
`a_(n+1)` may occur in uncountably many parent words.
Thus this construction permits a specified shared input
that the private-incidence argument in Section 2 would
not permit if that input were treated as private.

The proof uses one common template per stage. A family of
varying templates with unconstrained private-input types
does not automatically satisfy (10); no quotient map for
that family is asserted.

## 4. Actual two-dimensional models and audit status

For each sequence above, choose the rose with basis `B_n` as a
one-dimensional CW model of its free group and realize the
transition words by based cellular maps. Their ordinary
mapping telescope is a two-dimensional CW complex with
fundamental group the specified group colimit. It is
aspherical: a map from a sphere of dimension at least two
has bounded image under the telescope height function,
hence lies in a finite subtelescope; that subtelescope
deformation retracts onto its final graph, where the map
is nullhomotopic. Thus it is an actual classifying model.

When every basis image has zero exponent sum separately
in each next-stage variable, the induced maps on first
homology are zero. The telescope's ordinary homology is
the colimit of the graph homologies, so its positive
homology groups all vanish. In this acyclic case the
countable retracts in Sections 1--2 are themselves
acyclic: their homology is a retract of zero in every
positive degree. These are consequently explicit
countable detecting quotients of the large acyclic
two-dimensional groups in those construction classes.
For Section 3, the proved conclusion is the specified
countable detecting quotient. No acyclicity or retraction
assertion about its extra-generator target `K` is used.

The D agent independently checked the first closure and
commutation argument, including backward countability.
It independently checked (5)--(8), emphasizing that the
countable private-incidence requirement is essential and
that transition injectivity is not needed for that second
retract. Its final reading of those complete proofs, the
pair-commutator independence argument, and the telescope
argument found no correction needed. Its separate audit
of (9)--(11) also passed, including the use of the selected
free factors to preserve nontrivial elements even when
the larger quotient transition has kernel elsewhere.

No dimensional claim about an arbitrary uncountable
locally free group is used here. The dimension assertions
come from the displayed countable sequence of actual
graph mapping cylinders. In particular, no arbitrary
filtered-colimit dimension estimate is substituted for
that construction.
