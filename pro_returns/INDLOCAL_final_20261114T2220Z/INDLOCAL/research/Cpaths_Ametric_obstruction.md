# A quantitative Higman calculation for finite metric groups

Author: C/path collaborator. Written 2026-11-14.

Status: complete proof below. This is a research supplement; A.26–A.28 remain unchanged.

## 1. Exact hypotheses and estimate

Let `D` be a finite group with a function `ell:D -> [0,1]` that is positive on every nonidentity element, invariant under inversion and conjugation, and subadditive under multiplication. Assume also

    ell([g,h]) <= 4 ell(g) ell(h),       [g,h]=g h g^{-1} h^{-1}.

Put `d(g,h)=ell(g^{-1}h)`. Fix `0<epsilon<1/176`. For indices modulo four, suppose elements `x_i` satisfy

    d(x_i, [x_{i-1}^{-1},x_i]) <= epsilon.             (1)

Then `ell(x_i)<4 epsilon` for all four indices.

We prove this with an induction on the order of `D`.

### The elementary lower bound

Equation (1) and commutator contraction imply

    ell(x_i) - epsilon <= 4 ell(x_{i-1}) ell(x_i).

If `ell(x_i)>=4 epsilon`, division gives `ell(x_{i-1})>=3/16`. Since `4 epsilon<3/16`, repeat around the cycle. Thus a counterexample to the conclusion has

    ell(x_i)>=3/16 for every i.                      (2)

### The minimum-length normal subgroup

The trivial group satisfies the conclusion. For a nontrivial `D`, let

    delta = min{ell(g):g!=1},
    N = <g:ell(g)=delta>.

If `delta>epsilon`, every error word in (1) is the identity. Thus the four elements are an exact Higman tuple. Each has finite order, so the minimum-prime argument in `Cpaths_ALEF_obstruction.md`, Section 2, makes all of them the identity.

We may therefore assume `delta<=epsilon<1/4`. The conjugation invariance of `ell` makes `N` normal. If `g,h` both have length `delta`, then

    ell([g,h])<=4 delta^2<delta.

By minimality of `delta`, they commute. The subgroup generated by all these pairwise commuting elements is therefore abelian. It is nontrivial because an element realizes `delta`.

The quotient `D/N` has the explicitly defined invariant length

    bar_ell(gN) = min{ell(g n):n in N}.

The minimum exists since `D` is finite. It is positive on nonidentity cosets. The quotient map is length decreasing. Subadditivity follows by multiplying minimizing representatives; conjugation and inversion invariance follow by transporting representatives. For the additional inequality, choose minimizing representatives `g,h`; their commutator represents the commutator of the cosets, so

    bar_ell([gN,hN]) <= ell([g,h])
                       <= 4 bar_ell(gN) bar_ell(hN).

The images of the four `x_i` satisfy (1) in this smaller quotient. Induction gives `bar_ell(x_iN)<4 epsilon`. Choose `n_i in N` with

    d(x_i,n_i)<4 epsilon.                            (3)

This also handles the trivial quotient `D/N` by taking `n_i=x_i`.

### The perturbation estimate

The identity

    [p,q s] = [p,q] q[p,s]q^{-1}

gives the exact metric estimate

    d([p,q],[p,q']) <= 4 ell(p) d(q,q').

Inverting the commutators exchanges the two entries, giving the corresponding estimate in the first entry. Consequently, using (3), inversion invariance, the bound `ell<=1`, and commutativity of `N`, we obtain

    ell(x_i)
      <= epsilon + ell([x_{i-1}^{-1},x_i])
      < epsilon + 16 epsilon
          + ell([x_{i-1}^{-1},n_i])
      < epsilon + 16 epsilon + 16 epsilon
          + ell([n_{i-1}^{-1},n_i])
      = 33 epsilon
      < 3/16.

This contradicts (2) for any counterexample. The induction and the claimed estimate are proved.

## 2. The actual ultraproduct homomorphism consequence

Let `(D_s,ell_s)` be any family satisfying Section 1, and let `U` be an ultrafilter on its index set. On the direct product define

    Z = {(g_s): lim_U ell_s(g_s)=0}.

The limit exists because the lengths lie in `[0,1]`. Subadditivity, inversion invariance, and conjugation invariance make `Z` a normal subgroup. Form the actual quotient group

    M = (product_s D_s)/Z.

Every homomorphism from the displayed Higman group `J` in `Cpaths_ALEF_obstruction.md` to `M` is trivial. Indeed choose coordinate representatives `x_{i,s}` for the images of `a,b,c,d`, in their cyclic order. Their exact relations in `M` mean that each of the four error lengths in (1) tends to zero along `U`. For any `epsilon` in `(0,1/176)`, the intersection of the four sets on which these error lengths are at most `epsilon` belongs to `U`. On this intersection Section 1 gives `ell_s(x_{i,s})<4 epsilon`. Letting `epsilon` decrease to zero shows `(x_{i,s})` belongs to `Z` for every `i`. Thus the images of all four generating elements equal the identity in the quotient.

The same conclusion holds for every target with an injective homomorphism into such an `M`: compose the given map from `J` with that injection and use its trivial kernel. Finally the two actual normal-generation maps

    J --j_lambda--> H --i--> G

from A.10 and A.28 propagate triviality exactly as in Section 4 of `Cpaths_ALEF_obstruction.md`. Hence the A.28 group over `S^2` has no nontrivial homomorphism to any such target.

## 3. Source and correction audit

The source prompting this calculation is Andreas Thom, *About the metric approximation of Higman's group*, Journal of Group Theory 15 (2012), 301–310, DOI `10.1515/jgt.2011.102`; the opened primary preprint is

https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0823

The retrieved version is arXiv:1005.0823v1, 5 May 2010, nine pages, web reference `turn433view0`. The definitions, quotient-length calculation, Lemma 2.1, Proposition 3.1, Theorem 3.2 and its proof, and Remark 3.4 were read completely. The old v1 display in Theorem 3.2's proof omits a factor four when it applies Lemma 2.1 to an error bounded by `4 epsilon`. The proof above retains both factors and uses `epsilon<1/176`; it also replaces the extremal compactness argument by a finite-order induction. No claimed numerical bound from that faulty display is used.

This is a proved restriction for the specific lengths and quotient groups just defined. No conclusion for general sofic or hyperlinear approximation is asserted. No third-party file was downloaded for the note.
