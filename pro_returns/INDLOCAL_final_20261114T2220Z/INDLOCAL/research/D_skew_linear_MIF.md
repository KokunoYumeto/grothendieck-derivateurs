# Stable elementary groups over division rings with infinite center

Author: D/retraction collaborator. Written 2026-11-14.

Status: proved directly below; independently checked by root and C/path collaborators.

## Exact conclusion and its application

Let `D` be a division ring whose center contains an infinite field `k`. Let

`E_infinity(D)=colim_n E_n(D)`,

where `E_n(D)` is generated by the elementary matrices `I+a E_ij`, `i!=j`, and the structure maps append an identity block. For every nonidentity element

`w in E_infinity(D) * <x>`,

there is an actual `g in E_infinity(D)` with `w(g)!=1`. Thus this specified group is one-variable mixed-identity-free, in the precise sense used in A.27.

In fact, for each word the witnessing element can be chosen in `SL_N(k)` for a sufficiently large finite `N`, embedded by its literal matrix entries in `E_N(D)`. The proof works with coefficients in the stable general linear group as well.

Consequently every **countable** group `E_infinity(D)` with `D` of characteristic zero is excluded by the existing diagonal block A.27 and the actual tower A.28. Characteristic zero supplies the infinite central subfield `Q`. Countability of `D` implies countability of `E_infinity(D)` because its matrices have finite size and finite lists of entries.

This proves the assertion about the whole displayed stable elementary group. It does not assert that an arbitrary subgroup of a mixed-identity-free group is mixed-identity-free. No conclusion about arbitrary simple subgroups of `D^*` is inferred from the ambient construction.

## 1. A simultaneous central change of basis

Take finitely many nonidentity invertible matrices `c_1,...,c_s` over `D`. Append a common nonempty identity block, and choose the resulting size `N>=3`. Each `c_i` now has a fixed standard basis vector. Each also fails to be a scalar `D`-matrix: a scalar matrix having an identity diagonal entry would be the identity, whereas the original matrix was nonidentity.

There is a single `S in GL_N(k)` such that, for every `i`, all four entries

`(S^(-1)c_i S)_(1,1), (S^(-1)c_i S)_(N,N),`

`(S^(-1)c_i S)_(1,N), (S^(-1)c_i S)_(N,1)`

are nonzero in `D`.

Here is a direct proof of the generic choice, including its coefficient issue. Let `Z` be an `N` by `N` matrix of commuting indeterminates central over `D`. Every entry of

`adj(Z) c_i Z`

is a polynomial in those indeterminates with coefficients in `D`. Its quotient by `det(Z)` is the corresponding entry of `Z^(-1)c_i Z`.

Each required diagonal polynomial is nonzero: choose a permutation matrix taking a fixed tail basis vector to the specified position. At this particular change of basis the diagonal entry is `1`.

Each required off-diagonal polynomial is nonzero as well. If `c_i` has a nonzero off-diagonal entry, a permutation matrix moves its pair of indices to the specified distinct pair. Otherwise `c_i` is diagonal, with two unequal entries `d_a,d_b`, because it is not scalar. Conjugation by `I+E_ab` produces the off-diagonal entry `d_a-d_b`; a subsequent permutation moves it to the required position. All these changes of basis have entries in `k`.

For each of the finitely many nonzero `D`-valued numerator polynomials, take the finite-dimensional `k`-span of its coefficients. Choose a `k`-linear functional on that span which is nonzero on one nonzero coefficient. Applying it coefficientwise gives a nonzero polynomial over `k`. The product of these scalar polynomials and `det(Z)` is nonzero. A nonzero polynomial over an infinite field has a point where it does not vanish: induction on the number of variables proves this from the finite root bound for a one-variable polynomial. Choose such a point `S`.

At this `S`, the determinant and every chosen scalar numerator are nonzero. Therefore the corresponding `D`-valued numerators, and hence all four required entries, are nonzero. This proves the simultaneous assertion without treating a polynomial with division-ring coefficients as an ordinary scalar polynomial.

## 2. The highest Laurent coefficient

Write a nonconstant reduced mixed word in the form

`w=c_0 x^(a_1)c_1 x^(a_2)...c_(m-1)x^(a_m)c_m`,

where `m>=1`, every integer `a_j` is nonzero, every interior coefficient `c_j`, `1<=j<m`, is nonidentity, and the two outer coefficients may be the identity. All coefficients occur in some common finite matrix size; append an identity block as in Section 1. Choose `S` there for the interior coefficients. For `m=1` there are no such conditions and any `S`, for example the identity, suffices.

Use a central indeterminate `t`, and put

`d(t)=diag(t,1,...,1,t^(-1))`,

`g(t)=S d(t) S^(-1)`, and `c_j'=S^(-1)c_j S`.

All entries of the evaluated word are in the central Laurent-polynomial ring `D[t,t^(-1)]`. Define `epsilon_j=1` for `a_j>0` and `epsilon_j=N` for `a_j<0`. In the diagonal matrix `d(t)^(a_j)`, the unique entry having greatest exponent is at position `(epsilon_j,epsilon_j)`, and its exponent is `|a_j|`.

Consider

`M(t)=d(t)^(a_1)c_1' d(t)^(a_2)...c_(m-1)'d(t)^(a_m)`.

Its largest possible exponent is

`L=|a_1|+...+|a_m|>0`.

There is exactly one matrix-index path contributing to this exponent: at the `j`th diagonal factor its index must be `epsilon_j`. Thus the coefficient of `t^L` in `M(t)` is exactly

`((c_1')_(epsilon_1,epsilon_2) ... (c_(m-1)')_(epsilon_(m-1),epsilon_m)) E_(epsilon_1,epsilon_m)`.

For `m=1` the empty product is `1`. For `m>1` every factor is one of the four nonzero entries secured in Section 1. Their ordered product is nonzero because `D` is a division ring. There are no other terms at this exponent with which it could cancel.

The evaluated conjugated word is

`S^(-1)w(g(t))S=c_0' M(t)c_m'`.

The coefficient of `t^L` remains nonzero after multiplication by the two invertible outer matrices. Therefore `w(g(t))-I` is a nonzero Laurent-polynomial matrix.

## 3. An actual elementary-matrix evaluation

Choose a nonzero entry `p(t)` of `w(g(t))-I`. After multiplication by a power of `t`, this is a nonzero polynomial with coefficients in `D`. Apply a `k`-linear functional on the finite span of its coefficients which does not annihilate a nonzero coefficient. The resulting scalar polynomial is nonzero. Only finitely many `q in k` can make that scalar polynomial vanish. Choose `q in k^*` outside this finite set.

Then the original matrix entry satisfies `p(q)!=0`, so the actual finite matrix

`g=S diag(q,1,...,1,q^(-1)) S^(-1)`

has `w(g)!=I` over `D`.

It remains to verify membership in the claimed target. The displayed matrix has entries in `k` and determinant `1`. Gaussian elimination over the field `k` expresses every determinant-one matrix as a product of elementary matrices. The diagonal elimination step is supplied by the exact two-by-two identity

`e_12(q)e_21(-q^(-1))e_12(q) e_12(-1)e_21(1)e_12(-1)`

`=diag(q,q^(-1))`.

The same blocks in any two coordinates, together with elementary row additions, complete that elimination. Hence `g in SL_N(k)=E_N(k) subset E_N(D)`, using the literal coefficient inclusion `k -> D`. This proves the required evaluation. A reduced word with no variable is already a nonidentity coefficient, so its evaluation is nonidentity for every `g`.

## 4. Source status and the division-ring lead

The result above is an elementary proof and has no external theorem dependency. Its application uses the already proved A.27 block.

The primary Fisher--Sanchez-Peralta paper, *Division rings for group algebras of virtually compact special groups and 3-manifold groups*, Journal of Combinatorial Algebra 10 (2026), 153--193, DOI `10.4171/JCA/89`, was opened at <https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/52395>. Its Proposition 3.14 is the source of the possible Higman division-ring representation discussed in the research thread. The dependent Hughes-free embedding inputs have not all been audited here, and the proof above neither requires nor asserts them.

A separate primary preprint, Bradford--Ersoy--Schneider--Thom, *Mixed identities for simple locally finite groups*, arXiv:2608.14537v1, <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14537>, was checked in its introduction and Section 5.2.3. Its infinite-field argument addresses finitary classical groups over locally finite fields. No extension of those hypotheses to division rings is inferred; Sections 1--3 supply the needed division-ring statement directly.

No countable coefficient-preserving solver for all the required infinite boundary-isomorphism systems has been obtained from either source. The concrete whole-group candidate `E_infinity(D)` is eliminated by the proof just given.
