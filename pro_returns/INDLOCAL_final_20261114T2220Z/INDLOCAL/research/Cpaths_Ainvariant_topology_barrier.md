# A precise obstruction to deriving an invariant metric from the A.29 topology

Author: C/path collaborator. Written 2026-11-14.

Status: complete direct proof. This note concerns the proposed implication using only a nondiscrete group topology and the failure of uniform simplicity.

Let `Omega=N`, and let `A` be the group of all even permutations of `Omega` having finite support. Products of permutations act right-to-left. For `g in A`, write `supp(g)={n:g(n)!=n}`.

We prove all of the following for this one specified group:

1. `A` is countably infinite and nonabelian simple.
2. `A` has an explicit nondiscrete Hausdorff metrizable group topology.
3. Every positive definite conjugation-invariant length on `A` has a positive gap at the identity. Hence every metric obtained from such a length is discrete.
4. There is no finite uniform simplicity bound, even when the element whose conjugates are used is one fixed three-cycle.

## 1. An exact commutator computation

Fix any `g!=1` in `A`. Choose `a` moved by `g`, and put `b=g(a)`, so `a!=b`. Choose distinct `c,d` outside `supp(g) union {a,b}`. Put

    tau=(a c d).

This is an even finitary permutation. Since `g` fixes `c,d`, one has the literal permutation identities

    g tau g^{-1} = (b c d),
    [g,tau] = (b c d)(a d c) = (a b c).

The final equality follows by evaluating on the four displayed points: the product sends `a` to `b`, `b` to `c`, `c` to `a`, and fixes `d`; all other points are fixed. In particular `[g,tau]` is a three-cycle.

Every three-cycle in `A` is conjugate to every other by an element of `A`. A bijection between their ordered triples extends to a permutation of a finite set. If this extension is odd, compose it with a transposition on two additional fresh points. This changes its parity without changing its effect on either ordered triple.

Fix once and for all a three-cycle `h`. The computation therefore supplies `v in A` such that

    h = v[g,tau]v^{-1}
      = (v g v^{-1}) ((v tau) g^{-1} (v tau)^{-1}).       (1)

Thus the same `h` is a product of two conjugates of `g` and `g^{-1}`, for every nonidentity `g`.

## 2. Countability and simplicity

The group is the union of the finite alternating groups on the initial finite subsets of `N`, so it is countable. Disjoint three-cycles on fresh triples give infinitely many elements; overlapping three-cycles give noncommuting elements.

Let `N` be a nontrivial normal subgroup of `A`, and choose `g!=1` in `N`. Its commutator with `tau` from Section 1 belongs to `N`, because

    [g,tau] = g (tau g^{-1} tau^{-1}).

It is a three-cycle. Normality and the explicit conjugacy argument put every three-cycle in `N`.

For completeness these three-cycles generate `A`. An even permutation of a finite set can be written as a product of an even number of transpositions. Pair those transpositions in order. A pair of identical transpositions is the identity; a pair sharing exactly one point is a three-cycle or its inverse; a disjoint pair has the exact expression

    (a b)(c d) = (a c b)(a c d).

Hence each paired product lies in the subgroup generated by three-cycles. This proves `N=A`, and proves simplicity.

## 3. The nondiscrete pointwise topology

For each finite `F subset N`, define the subgroup

    U_F = {g in A : g(n)=n for every n in F}.

These groups give a neighborhood base at the identity. Their intersections satisfy `U_F intersect U_K = U_{F union K}`; they are closed under products and inversion; and the exact conjugation formula is

    g U_F g^{-1} = U_{g(F)}.

These formulas verify the continuity requirements for a group topology. Equivalently this is the restriction of the pointwise-convergence topology on permutations of the discrete set `N`.

The intersection of all `U_F` is the trivial subgroup, so the topology is Hausdorff. Every `U_F` contains a three-cycle on fresh points outside `F`, so the topology is nondiscrete.

Number the points `0,1,2,...`. An explicit compatible metric is

    d_pt(g,h)=0                         if g=h,
    d_pt(g,h)=2^{-min{n:g(n)!=h(n)}}     otherwise.

The metric is symmetric, positive definite, and ultrametric: agreement of both pairs `(g,h)` and `(h,k)` on the first `m` points implies agreement of `(g,k)` there. Its balls are precisely the conditions of agreement on finite initial segments, giving the topology just specified. It is left invariant, since an injective permutation `u` satisfies

    u g(n)=u h(n) if and only if g(n)=h(n).

The group-operation continuity follows from the subgroup and conjugation formulas above. Thus this is a concrete nondiscrete Hausdorff metrizable group topology, with an actual compatible left-invariant metric.

## 4. Every conjugation-invariant length has a positive gap

Let `ell:A -> [0,infinity)` have `ell(1)=0`, be positive on nonidentity elements, satisfy inversion symmetry and the triangle inequality, and be invariant under conjugation. Apply it to (1):

    ell(h) <= ell(v g v^{-1})
                + ell((v tau)g^{-1}(v tau)^{-1})
           = 2 ell(g).

It follows for this arbitrary length that

    inf_{g!=1} ell(g) >= ell(h)/2 > 0.                (2)

The metric `d_ell(g,k)=ell(g^{-1}k)` therefore has an isolated identity, and translation makes every point isolated. This proves the assertion for every conjugation-invariant length, not just for the example metric in Section 3.

## 5. Explicit failure of a uniform simplicity bound

For finite-support permutations,

    supp(g k) subset supp(g) union supp(k),

because a point fixed by both factors is fixed by their product. Hence a product of at most `M` conjugates of the fixed three-cycle `h` or its inverse has support of size at most `3M`.

Choose `z_M` to be a product of `M+1` disjoint three-cycles. It belongs to `A` and has support size `3(M+1)`. Thus it cannot be a product of at most `M` conjugates of `h^{±1}`. This gives an explicit obstruction for every finite `M`, and proves the absence of a uniform simplicity bound.

In particular the support size itself is an unbounded conjugation-invariant length; (2) explains why unboundedness of an invariant length does not provide arbitrarily small nonidentity values.

## 6. Exact relevance to A.29 and A.30

This single group has both a nondiscrete Hausdorff metrizable group topology and no finite uniform simplicity bound, while every conjugation-invariant length has the positive gap (2). It therefore disproves the implication from just those two hypotheses to the length condition (A.30.1).

Every element of `A` has finite order, because it is a permutation of a finite support. The torsion-image restriction in A.28 already excludes `A` as a nontrivial image. The example therefore leaves the remaining simple unimodularly closed targets undecided; it identifies the additional argument required to pass from the topology furnished by A.29 to the invariant-length hypothesis used in A.30.
