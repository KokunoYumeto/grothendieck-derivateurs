# Exact remaining norm calculation after A.31

**Status: 14 November 2026, 22:12 UTC.** This note records the verified
calculations used in the last attempt at the countable simple-target problem.
It makes no assertion that the remaining class is empty or nonempty and
adds no unproved theorem to the manuscript.

## 1. The residual norm already proved in A.31

For a remaining simple countable quotient \(E\), put

\[
 \rho_b(g)=\min\{r:g\text{ is a product of }r
                 \text{ conjugates of }b^{\pm1}\},\qquad
 N(g)=\sup_{b\ne1}\rho_b(g).
\]

A.31 proves that \(N(g)\) is finite for each \(g\), that it is a positive
integer for \(g\ne1\), and that it is a conjugation-invariant norm. It is
unbounded on \(E\), since the uniformly simple quotients have already been
excluded. For every fixed \(d\ne1\), the exact comparison is

\[
 \rho_d(g)\le N(g)\le N(d)\rho_d(g).
\]

The second inequality follows by replacing every conjugate of \(d^{\pm1}\)
in a shortest expression for \(g\) by an expression of length at most
\(N(d)\) in conjugates of an arbitrary \(b^{\pm1}\), and then taking the
supremum over \(b\ne1\).

## 2. Whole norm balls lie in bounded commutator products

For \(a\ne1\), define

\[
 S_a=\{v[a,u]v^{-1}:u,v\in E\},\qquad
 B_k=\{g\in E:N(g)\le k\}.
\]

Then, for every positive integer \(k\),

\[
 B_k\subseteq S_a^k\qquad(a\ne1).
\]

Indeed \(Z(E)=1\), so choose \(u\) with \(c=[a,u]\ne1\). Every \(g\in B_k\)
has an expression of length at most \(k\) in conjugates of \(c^{\pm1}\),
since \(\rho_c(g)\le N(g)\le k\). Both signs belong to \(S_a\):

\[
 [a,u]^{-1}=u[a,u^{-1}]u^{-1}.
\]

Conjugation preserves \(S_a\), and \(1\in S_a\) permits padding. This
proves the displayed inclusion with the actual product map

\[
 (E\times E)^k\longrightarrow E,
 \quad ((u_1,v_1),\ldots,(u_k,v_k))
       \longmapsto\prod_{j=1}^k v_j[a,u_j]v_j^{-1}.
\]

For a fixed nonidentity \(d\), its entire conjugacy class lies in
\(B_{N(d)}\) and generates \(E\). Consequently every \(a\ne1\) fails to
commute with some element of that fixed ball: commuting with the whole
conjugacy class would mean commuting with the group it generates.

## 3. An explicit bound on solutions of unimodular equations

Let

\[
 w(x)=c_0x^{n_1}c_1\cdots x^{n_r}c_r,
 \qquad n_1+\cdots+n_r=1.
\]

Put \(p_j=n_1+\cdots+n_j\), with \(p_0=0\). Collecting the powers of the
single element \(x\) gives the exact identity

\[
 w(x)=\left(\prod_{j=0}^r x^{p_j}c_jx^{-p_j}\right)x.
\]

Thus

\[
 N(w(x)x^{-1})\le\sum_{j=0}^rN(c_j).
\]

Every solution of \(w(x)=1\) therefore satisfies

\[
 N(x)\le\sum_{j=0}^rN(c_j).
\]

For exponent sum \(-1\), the same collection gives the final factor
\(x^{-1}\) and the same bound on a solution. These are bounds for the
actual values of solutions; no new solvability assertion is used here.

## 4. What the Higman doubling pair directly controls

For elements satisfying \(bab^{-1}=a^2\), every integer \(m\) satisfies

\[
 [b,a^m]=a^m,
 \qquad N(a^m)\le2N(b).
\]

The first equality is a direct substitution in the commutator definition.
The second follows by writing the commutator as the product of \(b\) and
a conjugate of \(b^{-1}\). Each cyclic subgroup generated by a Higman
generator is therefore bounded in \(N\). This calculation supplies no
bound for all products of those cyclic subgroups or their conjugates.

## 5. First unresolved step of this attempt

The inclusion \(B_k\subseteq S_a^k\) supplies boundedly many commutator
factors, but its proof does not bound the norms of the variables \(u_j,v_j\)
which represent a given element. In particular it does not supply a fixed
bounded set of witnesses as \(a\) varies. After one root-dependent
substitution in the A.27 diagonal construction, repeating that substitution
requires precisely such control, or a separate proof that the new word
images remain nonconstant. Neither conclusion has been proved here.

An elementary question arising in the conjugacy-class attempt is this
(write \(c^g=gcg^{-1}\)): the commutator identities used here do not show that
commuting with \(c\) and with all \(c^{d^h}\), \(h\in E\), forces an element
to commute with every conjugate of \(c\). The available formulas do not
propagate that commutation assertion through arbitrary products of the
conjugacy class of \(d\). No such propagation is used in A.27--A.31.

The demonstrated bounds above also do not prove that unimodular closure
makes \(N\) bounded. A.31's finite-but-unbounded norm alternative is the
exact current conclusion.
