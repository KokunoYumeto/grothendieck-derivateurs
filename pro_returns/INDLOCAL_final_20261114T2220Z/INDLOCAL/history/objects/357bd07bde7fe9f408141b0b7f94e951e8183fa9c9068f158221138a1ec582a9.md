# Finite root projections of unimodular equations

Author: Part A collaborator. Written 2026-11-14 22:13 UTC.

This note records an exact consequence of the finite interpolation proof in
`A_finite_interpolation.md`. It does not remove the universal-root obstruction
in the remaining countable-target construction.

## 1. Statement and proof

Let E be a nonabelian simple group in which every one-variable equation of
total exponent +1 or -1 has a solution. Fix a finite number m of auxiliary
variables and a word

    f(x; z_1,...,z_m) in E * F(x,z_1,...,z_m)

whose total x-exponent is +1 or -1. Define its root projection by

    P_f = {c in E : there is a tuple z in E^m with f(c;z)=1}.

When P_f is finite, it contains an element c such that

    f(c;z)=1 for every z in E^m.

Here all evaluations fix the coefficient copy of E. No assumption on the
exponents of the auxiliary variables is required.

To prove the assertion, first fix any auxiliary tuple. The resulting
one-variable word has exponent +1 or -1, so has a root; hence P_f is
nonempty. Suppose that every c in the finite set P_f admits a tuple
z(c) with f(c;z(c)) not equal to 1. For every j=1,...,m, finite interpolation
supplies a word q_j(x) in E * <x> of total x-exponent zero such that

    q_j(c)=z_j(c) for every c in P_f.

Explicitly, that interpolation theorem is the surjectivity of the evaluation
map from ker(E * <x> -> Z) to E^{P_f}; its proof in the cited authored note
uses the diagonal copy of E and its coordinatewise conjugate by the distinct
elements of P_f. Thus the substitution homomorphism

    E * F(x,z_1,...,z_m) -> E * <x>
    a |-> a  (a in E),  x |-> x,  z_j |-> q_j(x)

sends f to v(x)=f(x;q_1(x),...,q_m(x)) with the same x-exponent +1 or -1.
The exponent calculation follows by applying the exponent homomorphism to
this displayed substitution: every z_j has image of exponent zero.

Let c be a root of v, supplied by the assumed equation-solving property.
The tuple (q_j(c)) shows c is in P_f. But the interpolation identities give

    v(c)=f(c;z(c)) != 1,

a contradiction. Consequently one element of P_f is a universal root as
asserted. The same proof covers m=0, when the conclusion is immediate.

In particular, a word with no universal root has infinitely many possible
root values. This conclusion is about the projection of its solution set,
not about the image of a word map.

## 2. Exact remaining construction problem

The root-dependent reset construction uses finite equations of this form.
It would be useful to preserve the absence of universal roots during every
substitution. A substitution of exponent-zero words for the auxiliary
variables preserves the required x-exponent. It can, however, turn the
evaluation at a particular c into the constant identity function on all new
auxiliary tuples. The preceding argument then allows c as a universal root
and does not remove it.

The available finite function N(g)=sup_{b != 1} rho_b(g) lets one choose a
finite reset width large enough to preserve any prescribed finite list of
failing evaluations. No width has been proved to preserve a failing
evaluation simultaneously at every possible c. Increasing widths in later
substitutions does not undo a universal root already introduced: evaluating
that earlier identity after any further substitution still gives 1.

Thus this lemma does not yet yield a new unsolvable countable system, a new
actual two-dimensional relative block, or a residual-countability theorem.
